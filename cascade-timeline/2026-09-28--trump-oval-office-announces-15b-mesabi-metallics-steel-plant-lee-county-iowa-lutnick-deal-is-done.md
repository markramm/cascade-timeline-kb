At an Oval Office event on the day of the announcement (event date), Iowa Public Radio reported that President Donald Trump "announced an $18 billion investment in a fully integrated American steel system, including a major steel plant coming to southeast Iowa's Lee County." The plant "will be built by Mesabi Metallics, a Minnesota-based company belonging to the Indian conglomerate Essar Group." Iowa Public Radio also reported that "A slate of Iowa Republicans, including Gov. Kim Reynolds, Sen. Joni Ernst, Rep. Ashley Hinson and Rep. Mariannette Miller-Meeks joined Trump at the White House."

## "This deal is done" and "They've already started building"

A reporter asked whether the project was a done deal or contingent on Iowa tax breaks. In a third-party transcript, the answer is labeled as Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's: "Is it contingent on tax breaks within Iowa? No. I think this deal is done. This deal is done. They’ve already worked it out." The same answer ends: "You guys know that already. They’ve already started building." In this transcript both lines sit inside Lutnick's labeled answer, not Trump's. The transcript is a secondary copy; the video was not reviewed here. The transcript does not say what "started building" refers to. The pieces read for this entry report no construction at a Lee County site, and Mesabi's own timeline, as CBS reports it, is that production "won't be before 2030."

## What the company and the state said

Iowa Public Radio reported that "Mesabi expects the plant to begin operations in a few years and offer at least 1,700 permanent high-paying jobs." Iowa Senate Democratic Leader Janice Weiner said in a statement that the Statehouse had "no details regarding a potential incentive package." CBS News reported that "lawmakers in Iowa still hadn't received details to review or timing for the special session held Friday." In that one-day session the Iowa legislature approved $1.36 billion in incentives over 10 years; CBS reports "The Iowa House passed it 75-17, the Senate 28-19" and Gov. Reynolds signed it into law that Friday night. The tax credits "don't kick in until the plant is running."
