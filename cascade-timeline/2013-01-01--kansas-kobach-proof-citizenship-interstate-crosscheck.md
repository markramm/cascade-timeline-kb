Kansas Secretary of State Kris Kobach implements one of the nation's strictest voter registration laws, requiring documentary proof of citizenship to register to vote, and runs the Interstate Voter Registration Crosscheck Program, a state-to-state matching program for registrations.

## Proof-of-Citizenship Requirement

The federal court's findings count 31,089 total applicants who were denied registration for failure to provide DPOC. Canceled or suspended applicants represented 12.4% of new voter registrations between January 1, 2013 and December 11, 2015.

## Interstate Crosscheck Program

Twenty-two states participated in the 2013 Crosscheck matching program, according to Virginia's own list-maintenance report. Following the matching process in early 2013, Virginia received approximately 308,579 potential duplicate registration records from the other participating states. Those are records on a match list, not people.

Crosscheck's own participation guide, as the researchers Goel and colleagues describe it, has administrators return to each participating state a list of registrations in that state that share the same first name, last name, and DOB as a registration in another participating state. At Kobach's presentation, per the Brennan Center, participating states upload their voter registration databases to a server run by the Kansas secretary of state.

The Brennan Center reports that the program's methods lead to false positives, as in Pennsylvania, where the system reported more than 300,000 potential double voters. A Rolling Stone investigation, as the Brennan Center reports it, found the system flagged one in six Latinos, one in seven Asian Americans, and one in nine African Americans as potential double registrants in the states examined. As of late 2013, a total of 14 had been referred to prosecutors. Goel and colleagues estimate that, in the consortium data, 99.5% of votes cast with the same name and date of birth were cast by distinct individuals, and that removing the earlier of two same-name, same-birthdate registrations could impede approximately 300 legitimate votes for each double vote prevented. KCUR reports that Kobach's office acknowledged that it issued many "false positives."

### Suspension

KCUR reports the system had been on hold since 2017, when it was shut down by the U.S. Department of Homeland Security for data breaches. On December 10, 2019, the ACLU of Kansas announced a settlement of its 2018 lawsuit over the exposure of the information of nearly 1,000 Kansas voters. Under it, Secretary of State Scott Schwab agreed not to resume operating Crosscheck until all security upgrades recommended by the Department of Homeland Security have been implemented, industry-standard encryption practices are adopted, and participant states agree to a penalty of expulsion for any negligent, reckless or intentional disclosure of information.

## The Voter Fraud Question

Despite claiming to have located instances of noncitizen registration in Kansas, Kobach filed zero criminal complaints against noncitizens for registering to vote. The court drew its conclusion in the findings: "there is no iceberg; only an icicle, largely created by confusion and administrative error."

## Contempt and the Court's Ruling

NPR reports that the judge found Kobach in contempt of court for disobeying her order to allow some potentially ineligible voters to remain eligible to cast a ballot under a preliminary injunction; the ACLU dates its comment on the contempt ruling April 18, 2018. According to ProPublica, the judge cited Kobach's "history of noncompliance" with the order and ordered him to pay the ACLU's legal fees for the contempt proceeding. In her findings the judge wrote of Kobach's "well-documented history of avoiding this Court's Orders," and held that the documentary-proof-of-citizenship law and its regulation violate § 5 of the NVRA and infringe on the right to vote under the Fourteenth Amendment.

KCUR reports that Kobach later led President Trump's Presidential Advisory Commission on Election Integrity, which disbanded after roughly a year and failed to find any evidence of Kobach's claims of widespread voter fraud.
