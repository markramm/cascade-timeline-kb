## Opening paragraph

On September 1, 2026, Sen. John Fetterman (D-PA), ranking member of the Senate Homeland Security Subcommittee on Border Management, Federal Workforce, and Regulatory Affairs, sent a letter to David J. Venturella, the Senior Official Performing the Duties of the Director of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, demanding immediate payment of overdue medical-provider invoices tied to ICE detention. The letter names Moshannon Valley Emergency Medical Services (MVEMS), a rural Philipsburg, PA nonprofit ambulance service, as owed **$236,124.91** in overdue payments. Fetterman requested a response no later than **September 15, 2026**. The letter was copied to DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin. The office's press release publicizing the letter did not go out until ten days later, on September 11, 2026 — the date under which most secondary coverage indexes the story, but not the date on the letter itself.

## What Happened / Key Facts

Per the letter PDF itself (primary source, verified directly, not via the press release or corpus summaries):

- **Recipient and framing**: Addressed to "The Honorable David J. Venturella, Senior Official Performing the Duties of the Director, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement." Fetterman writes: "I am concerned about the non-payment of medical service provider invoices for care rendered to individuals in the custody of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), and I demand that ICE immediately pay these bills."
- **Mechanism context (already in canon, restated in the letter)**: For more than two decades, ICE paid the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) to process medical-service reimbursement claims for immigration detention, at no cost to the VA. On **October 3, 2025**, VA and ICE "abruptly terminated this decades-long contract," leaving ICE "with no mechanism to process invoices and pay medical providers serving ICE facilities and detainees." On **January 13, 2026**, ICE Health Service Corps (IHSC) announced a new electronic provider portal, which DHS and ICE expected to launch **April 30, 2026**. As of the letter date, "It is now September and providers are still unable to submit reimbursement claims for 911 call-initiated responses and other care."
- **ICE's own characterization of the stakes**: The letter quotes ICE's own October 2025 contracting justification, filed via SAM.gov and made public in November 2025: "It is an absolute emergency for ICE to immediately procure claims processing support because lack of this support will delay critical medical care for [illegal aliens] such as dialysis, prenatal care, oncology, chemotherapy, etc."
- **MVEMS scale** (all figures from the letter): serves an area of **almost 800 square miles**; provides EMS to **41,000 Pennsylvanians**; staffed by **10 full-time and 35 part-time employees**; operates **5 ambulances**; also serves the Moshannon Valley Processing Center, "capable of holding nearly 1,900 detainees."
- **The dollar figure and its date anomaly**: The letter states, verbatim: *"As of August 20, 2025, ICE owes MVEMS $236,124.91 in overdue payments, because of the reimbursement freeze."* This as-of date — August 20, **2025** — precedes by six weeks the October 3, 2025 VA/ICE contract termination that the letter itself identifies as the cause of the freeze. The anomaly is in the primary document, not introduced by any relay: MyChesCo's September 12 pickup drops the year entirely ("as of Aug. 20"), which is consistent with either reading but resolves nothing. **This entry does not silently correct the letter's own arithmetic.** The most probable explanation is a typographical error for August 20, **2026** in the Senate office's drafting, but the letter as issued and publicly posted says 2025, and it should be quoted that way going forward with the anomaly flagged, because subsequent citations (including RAMM's own Moshannon draft) will otherwise repeat an unflagged error.
- **The three requests**, quoted from the letter: (1) "A detailed accounting of the total amount currently owed by ICE to medical service providers, EMS providers, hospitals, and other health care entities for care provided to individuals in ICE custody," with a fallback to "a detailed estimate ... based on historical averages adjusted for recent increases in ICE's enforcement and detention activities" if unavailable; (2) "A statistical impact report addressing how these payment delays have affected detainee medical care and medical and EMS providers"; (3) "A detailed update on the status of ICE's new payment system, including when it will be fully operational, when outstanding claims will be paid, and what steps ICE is taking to prevent future payment disruptions."
- **Deadline**: "I hope to receive your response no later than September 15, 2026." Signed by John Fetterman, Ranking Member, Subcommittee on Border Management, Federal Workforce, and Regulatory Affairs. Copied to Hon. Markwayne Mullin, Secretary, Department of Homeland Security.

## The letter's recipient, in context

David J. Venturella is not a neutral bureaucratic addressee. Per [[2026-05-13--venturella-geo-group-executive-named-acting-ice-director]], he spent 12 years at GEO Group — the same company that owns and operates the Moshannon Valley Processing Center generating the EMS calls at issue — before being named acting ICE director on May 13, 2026. Per [[2026-06-04--venturella-memo-eliminates-post-release-death-reporting-ice-accountability-erasure]], he separately issued a June 4, 2026 memo ending ICE's post-release death-reporting requirement, reversing a 2021 transparency rule. Fetterman's demand for a "statistical impact report" on payment-delay harms therefore goes to the same official who, three months earlier, narrowed what the agency is required to publicly report about detainee harm. This entry does not allege a causal connection between the two acts — it notes the structural fact that the transparency-narrowing official and the accountability-demand recipient are the same person.

## Why This Event Matters

This is a named, dated, dollar-denominated congressional demand landing directly on the mechanism this corpus has tracked since the October 2025 VA/ICE contract termination: a federal detention system that generates emergency medical calls it has no functioning system to pay for, pushed onto rural providers with no scale to absorb the float. MVEMS's $236,124.91 receivable is not an isolated billing dispute — it is the same fiscal-asymmetry pattern documented in [[2026-09-09--clearfield-county-tables-moshannon-six-month-extension-vote-sept-22]], where Clearfield County absorbs uncompensated EMS costs while receiving a flat $200,000 annual intermediary fee. Fetterman's letter converts that pattern into a specific, falsifiable federal accountability question — a number, a deadline, a named recipient — that ICE either answers or does not.

## Broader Context

The letter sits inside a nine-month arc already in canon: VA/ICE contract termination (2025-10-03) → IHSC portal announcement (2026-01-13) → missed April 30, 2026 launch → still non-functional as of September 2026 → this letter (2026-09-01) → public announcement (2026-09-11) → deadline (2026-09-15, unmet as of this writing). The Clearfield County commissioners' own six-month extension vote was tabled to September 22, 2026, the same week this letter's deadline lapsed — placing local officials' decision on whether to keep signing for the facility directly alongside a live, unanswered federal accountability demand about the facility's unpaid medical bills.
