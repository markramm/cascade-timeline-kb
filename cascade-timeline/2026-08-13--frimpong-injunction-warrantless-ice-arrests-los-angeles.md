U.S. District Judge Maame Ewusi-Mensah Frimpong (C.D. Cal.) signaled August 13, 2026 that she will issue an injunction requiring ICE and Border Patrol agents to make individualized pre-arrest probable-cause determinations of flight risk before any warrantless arrest across the Los Angeles metropolitan area — roughly 19 million people. The proposed order bars agents from citing "illegal presence" alone as justification and requires an affirmative assessment of community ties before a warrantless arrest. The ACLU and Public Counsel represent the plaintiffs; the case arises from the LA-area interior enforcement surge conducted under Border Patrol commander Gregory Bovino. If finalized, this would be at least the fifth federal court nationwide to block the administration's warrantless-arrest practices, forcing an operational change to the mass-deportation campaign's interior enforcement model in the nation's second-largest metro area.

This extends the judicial pushback against Bovino-era LA enforcement tactics the timeline has already documented — Judge Ellis's finding that Bovino lied under oath and her sweeping use-of-force injunction in a separate case ([[2025-11-06--judge-ellis-finds-bovino-lied-preliminary-injunction]]), and the earlier 2025 indiscriminate-raids block that SCOTUS later stayed in part ([[2025-07-11--federal-judge-blocks-indiscriminate-ice-raids-los-angeles]], [[2025-09-08--scotus-stays-noem-v-perdomo-la-immigration-enforcement]]). Frimpong's expected order is narrower and more specific than the earlier raids injunction — targeting the individualized-suspicion requirement directly — making it a live test of whether courts can hold given the SCOTUS stay already narrowed one prior LA ruling in the same enforcement campaign.
