An ICE agent shot **Wilber Rafael Garces Perez**, 28, a Venezuelan asylum seeker, in the torso on **September 20, 2026** in **Austin, Texas**, while he was making a food delivery. Video captured gunshots at the intersection of Anderson Square and Research Boulevard; subsequent footage showed Garces Perez handcuffed on the ground with a gunshot wound in his back. His attorney, **Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch**, said he is married to a woman with legal status. DHS said he entered the United States during the Biden administration and was subject to a final order of removal. **Homeland Security Investigations** and the **FBI** opened a federal investigation; the **Travis County District Attorney** separately called for an independent probe. Austin's mayor said ICE was "fomenting" chaos.

**The torso-versus-back discrepancy is the load-bearing detail and should not be smoothed over.** Reporting describes an entry wound to the torso and video showing a gunshot wound in his back. Those are reconcilable — a person turning, or a through-and-through — but they are also the exact factual question a use-of-force review turns on, because a wound in the back is hard to square with a frontal threat. **This entry records both descriptions as reported and asserts neither as the established mechanism.** No official account of the shooting's justification has been published.

**The immigration status DHS supplied answers a different question than the one at issue.** A final order of removal establishes that the government may deport someone; it does not establish that an agent may shoot them during a civil arrest. The juxtaposition in the coverage — attorney says he has a spouse with legal status, DHS says he has a final order — is a fight over sympathy, not over the lawfulness of the force used. The structural question is the force.

**Two investigations with different incentives are now running, and that divergence is itself the pattern.** HSI is a component of the same department as the agent; the FBI is within DOJ; the Travis County DA has asked for something independent of both. Canon records the one available comparison where this sequence has progressed: ICE officer **Christian Castro** was indicted on six false-statement counts over a Minneapolis shooting during Operation Metro Surge ([[2026-09-04--ice-officer-castro-pleads-not-guilty-six-false-statement-counts-minneapolis-shooting]]) — and what produced charges there was not the shooting itself but the **false statements about it**, after independent video contradicted the agent's account. That is the precedent worth holding: the accountability hook in these cases has run through the paperwork, not the wound. Video exists here too.

**The Aurora comparison marks the boundary of the pattern.** A GEO Group employee shot a protester in Colorado ([[2026-07-17--geo-group-employee-shoots-protester-aurora]]) — a contractor, not a federal officer, and therefore a different liability regime entirely. Conflating the two would obscure which accountability mechanisms are even available.
