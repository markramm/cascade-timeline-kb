On August 25, 2026 (opinion filed), a three-judge Eleventh Circuit panel (Rosenbaum, Lagoa, Wilson) vacated the Northern District of Georgia's judgment in the consolidated *Alpha Phi Alpha*, *Pendergrass* and *Grant* cases (Nos. 23-13914, 23-13916, 23-13921) and remanded. The district court had held that Georgia's 2021 redistricting plans violated Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act and had permanently enjoined them. The panel did not rule on the merits. It held that *Louisiana v. Callais* ([[2026-04-29--scotus-callais-guts-section-2-vra-reworks-gingles-standard]]) changed the Section 2 framework after briefing closed and that the district court should apply it first. The opinion is marked not for publication.

Two things follow, and one does not. The injunction against the Georgia maps no longer stands, and the case returns to Judge Jones. The opinion's text does not say which maps run on November 3; press reports (WRDW/Atlanta News First, Bloomberg Law) say the Secretary of State's office said the decision would not affect the November election and that the lines stay in place. I did not locate a statement of that from the Secretary's own page, so treat it as reported.

Georgia's legislature had already declined to redraw. Governor Kemp called a special session starting June 17, 2026 (press accounts; his proclamation text was not retrieved) for congressional and legislative maps to take effect for 2028. On June 17 House Republican leaders wrote that they would not take up redistricting in that session (WRDW). That is a legislature choosing not to act on a Callais opening. Georgia is therefore not a state where the ballot map was redrawn after Callais; the 2021 enacted congressional plan (SB 2EX) is the one in force, and what is pending is the remand, which could matter for 2028.
