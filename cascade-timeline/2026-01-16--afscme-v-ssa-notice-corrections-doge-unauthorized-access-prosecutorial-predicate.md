# DOJ Files Notice of Corrections in AFSCME v. SSA — DOGE Team Actions Outside SSA Policy

On January 16, 2026, the U.S. Department of Justice filed a **Notice of Corrections to the Record** in *AFSCME et al. v. Social Security Administration et al.* (D. Md.), telling Judge Ellen Lipton Hollander that SSA had identified DOGE Team actions that were "potentially outside of SSA policy and/or noncompliant with" the March 20, 2025 temporary restraining order. The Notice revisits earlier sworn declarations; for the Dudek declaration it states that "SSA believed that statement to be accurate at the time it was made." It uses the word "potentially" and does not concede a violation of the Privacy Act or the TRO.

## What the Notice Disclosed

- **Cloudflare.** DOGE Team members used links to share data through the third-party server Cloudflare beginning March 7, 2025 and continuing until March 17, about a week **before** the TRO. SSA states "Cloudflare is not approved for storing SSA data" and that it cannot determine what data were shared.
- **PII searches on the morning the TRO was certified.** One DOGE Team member ran PII searches on SSA's copy of the Numident on the morning of March 24, 2025; access was cut at about noon that day.
- **Post-TRO access grants.** One member was granted access to EDW schema containing PII on March 26, 2025 (SSA says it was never used); a call-center profile that allowed PII access was granted on April 9, 2025 (revoked June 11; SSA does not know whether PII was accessed).
- **Encrypted file to DHS.** On March 3, 2025, before the TRO, a DOGE Team member emailed DHS an encrypted, password-protected file, copying a senior DOGE advisor; SSA believes it held PII on approximately 1,000 people and has been unable to open it.
- **Voter Data Agreement.** One DOGE Team member signed a "Voter Data Agreement" in his capacity as an SSA employee with an unnamed political advocacy group, whose stated aim was to find evidence of voter fraud and to overturn election results in certain States. He sent the executed agreement on March 24, 2025. The agreement was not reviewed through SSA's data exchange procedures. SSA says it "has not yet seen evidence that SSA data were shared with the advocacy group."
- **Hatch Act referrals.** In late December 2025 SSA made two Hatch Act referrals to the Office of Special Counsel relating to the voter-data activity.

## What the Notice does not say

It does not name the advocacy group, does not concede that any person violated the Privacy Act or a computer-crime statute, does not state that anyone defied the TRO, and says nothing about residents of particular states. Readings of the Notice as a "prosecutorial predicate" for state charges, including the dual-sovereignty framework attributed to Christopher Armitage, are third-party characterizations that were not re-read for this correction; the legal theory belongs to them, not to the filing.
