US forces carried out a raid on Caracas before dawn on Saturday and took Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, into US custody. The operation was named "Absolute Resolve". Attorney General Pam Bondi said the two had been indicted in the Southern District of New York; PBS reports the indictment accused them of a role in a narco-terrorism conspiracy. President Trump said the US will "run the country" until a "judicious transition" can happen.

**Timing, as Al Jazeera reports it.** US helicopters touched down at Maduro's compound at 2:01am local time (06:01 GMT). Al Jazeera says Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth described the action as a "massive joint military and law enforcement raid" and that it lasted less than 30 minutes. Maduro was put on board a US ship at 4:29am, two and a half hours after the touchdown.

## Who was killed, by whose count

Every count of the dead on file is a named party's, given at a stated time, and none is independent.

- An official, speaking to The New York Times on condition of anonymity, said at least 40 people had been killed (Al Jazeera, the day after the raid).
- Cuba revealed on Monday that 32 of its citizens were killed, whom it described as carrying out missions for Cuba's armed forces and interior ministry (CNBC, reporting a statement posted by Cuba's presidential office).
- Two senior Venezuelan officials told MS Now that more than 70 people were killed.
- On Wednesday Diosdado Cabello, Venezuela's interior minister, said "So far — and I say so far — there are 100 dead", and a similar number injured. CNBC reports this was the first time a Venezuelan government official publicly gave a toll.

The sources read do not say where the 32 Cubans were killed, whether Cabello's figure includes them, or who the other dead were. A Pentagon official told MS Now that seven US service members were injured, and that the US assessment of casualties on the ground was still ongoing, led by the US intelligence community. No US government count of the dead is on file.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

Checked against the sources above by the recheck-canon task. The earlier text gave a toll above one hundred, and included 32 Cuban military personnel in it, with no counting party and no date. The figure above one hundred is the Venezuelan interior minister's, given on the Wednesday after the raid. The 32 is Cuba's own count, which describes them as carrying out missions for Cuba's armed forces and interior ministry; no source read places them at Maduro's compound. The earlier text also carried two quotations attributed to Hegseth, a 1989 legal memo by a former Attorney General, a quotation from the UN Secretary-General, a comparison with the 1989 capture of Panama's leader, Delta Force as the capture unit, and a capture lasting under 30 minutes. None is in the sources read in this pass and none is restated here until bound to a source. The under-30-minutes figure is Al Jazeera's description of the raid, not of the capture; touchdown to Maduro on a ship was two and a half hours. Venezuela is four hours behind GMT, so 2:01am is local time (06:01 GMT), not Eastern.
