The Meta newsroom post of November 5, 2018 announcing the BSR report says: "The report concludes that, prior to this year, we weren’t doing enough to help prevent our platform from being used to foment division and incite offline violence." It adds: "We agree that we can and should do more." The findings about Facebook's role come from BSR, the UN fact-finding mission and Amnesty International, set out below in their own words.

## What the company said

The Meta newsroom post (originally Facebook's) quotes the company's reading of the BSR report: "The report concludes that, prior to this year, we weren’t doing enough to help prevent our platform from being used to foment division and incite offline violence." The company's next sentence: "We agree that we can and should do more." It also relays BSR's point that, in the post's words, "Facebook alone cannot bring about the broad changes needed to address the human rights situation in Myanmar."

## What BSR found

BSR's report is dated October 2018. It states: "This HRIA was funded by Facebook, though BSR retained editorial control over its contents." On the platform's role, BSR writes: "Though the actual relationship between content posted on Facebook and offline harm is not fully understood, Facebook has become a means for those seeking to spread hate and cause harm, and posts have been linked to offline violence." It also writes: "The Facebook platform in Myanmar is being used by bad actors to spread hate speech, incite violence, and coordinate harm."

## What the UN fact-finding mission found

The mission's report to the UN Human Rights Council is dated September 2018. On the platform it says: "Facebook has been a useful instrument for those seeking to spread hate, in a context where, for most users, Facebook is the Internet." It continues: "Although improved in recent months, the response of Facebook has been slow and ineffective." And: "The extent to which Facebook posts and messages have led to real-world discrimination and violence must be independently and thoroughly examined." It adds: "The mission regrets that Facebook is unable to provide country-specific data about the spread of hate speech on its platform, which is imperative to assess the adequacy of its response."

The phrase "determining role" is not in that report. It is from March 2018: the mission's chairman, Marzuki Darusman, told reporters in March 2018 that Facebook had played a "determining role" in Myanmar, in a report dated March 13, 2018.

The mission counted the harm to the Rohingya as follows. It wrote that by mid-August 2018 nearly 725,000 Rohingya had fled to Bangladesh. On deaths it wrote that information it collected suggests the estimate of up to 10,000, an estimate the report footnotes to a March 2018 report by Doctors Without Borders, is a conservative one. On genocide, the mission concluded: "the mission concludes that there is sufficient information to warrant the investigation and prosecution of senior officials in the Tatmadaw chain of command, so that a competent court can determine their liability for genocide in relation to the situation in Rakhine State." That conclusion concerns the Myanmar military's chain of command, not the company.

## What Amnesty International found, September 2022

As PBS NewsHour reported on Sep 29, 2022, Amnesty found that Meta's algorithms "proactively amplified and promoted content" on Facebook which incited violent hatred against the Rohingya. AP quotes the report: "Meta — through its dangerous algorithms and its relentless pursuit of profit — substantially contributed to the serious human rights violations perpetrated against the Rohingya." These are Amnesty's findings. A Meta spokesperson declined to answer AP's questions about the report. The company said it "stands in solidarity with the international community and supports efforts to hold the Tatmadaw accountable for its crimes against the Rohingya people."

AP also reports a detail from the Amnesty report about a 2020 Facebook investigation of one video by an anti-Rohingya figure: over 70% of the video's views came from "chaining", meaning the platform suggested it to people who played a different video.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

The earlier text of this entry called the company's statement an admission of facilitating genocide, gave "determining role" as a finding of the UN mission's 2018 report, and said the company made no structural changes and faced no accountability. The statement is the company's account of what BSR concluded; the findings are BSR's, the mission's and Amnesty International's, each as set out above. The entry was retitled and renamed, and its date moved to November 5, the date of the company's post. Details the sources read for this correction do not carry were cut from the earlier text and are listed in the research ticket's work log.
