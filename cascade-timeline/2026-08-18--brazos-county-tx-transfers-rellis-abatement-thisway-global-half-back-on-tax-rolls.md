Brazos County commissioners voted **4-0 on August 18, 2026** to transfer an existing tax abatement agreement to **ThisWay Global, Inc.**, which acquired the bankrupt **$700 million RELLIS campus data center**. The commissioners had **tabled the same vote on August 5**, two weeks earlier — the delay is part of the record, not a formality. The transfer carries a fiscal consequence the county gains rather than gives: it returns **half the project's value to the tax rolls**, reversing a prior non-taxable status that attached to the land through the **Texas A&M University System**.

That reversal is the reason this is worth recording. The dominant pattern in the datacenter-abatement fight runs the other way — counties granting exemptions to attract projects and then discovering, as Bell County's commissioners have argued, that the abatement was their only source of leverage over noise, water use, and generators ([[2026-08-17--bell-county-tx-tables-data-center-abatement-repeal-blackburn-leverage-sept-8-vote]]). Here a bankruptcy and a change of owner did what a policy fight has not: it reopened a settled tax status and moved half a $700 million project from untaxed to taxed. The mechanism was insolvency, not local government asserting itself, which is worth holding precisely rather than reading as a win for county leverage.

The transfer lands inside a Texas policy environment that tightened over the same summer. Governor **Abbott** directed the PUCT and ERCOT to address who bears data-center infrastructure costs ([[2026-06-10--abbott-directive-puct-ercot-data-center-infrastructure-costs]]) and then froze new grid connections pending an ERCOT audit ([[2026-08-03--abbott-freezes-texas-data-center-grid-connections-ercot-audit]]). A distressed campus changing hands under those conditions is a datapoint on what happens to speculative buildout when the financing assumptions fail: the physical asset persists, the tax treatment is renegotiated, and the county's position is determined by bankruptcy proceedings rather than by the abatement policy it debated.
