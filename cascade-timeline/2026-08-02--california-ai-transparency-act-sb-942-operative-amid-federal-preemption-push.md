California's **AI Transparency Act (SB 942)** became **operative August 2, 2026**. It reaches
generative-AI providers whose publicly accessible systems have **more than one million monthly
users or visitors** in California, and requires three things of them: a **free public AI-detection
tool**, an **option for visible manifest disclosures** on AI-generated content, and **embedded
latent disclosures** — a hidden, machine-readable provenance watermark — in AI-generated image,
video, and audio. The civil penalty is **$5,000 per violation, with each day a discrete violation.**

The latent-disclosure requirement is the load-bearing part. A visible label can be cropped; a
machine-readable provenance mark travels with the file, which makes it the first US mandate that
attaches origin to synthetic media as a property of the artifact rather than of its presentation.

The operative date arrives while a **federal preemption push** is live. The **Great American AI Act
discussion draft** ([[2026-06-04--great-american-ai-act-discussion-draft-three-year-state-preemption]])
would block state AI laws for three years, and no comprehensive federal AI statute has passed —
so the preemption vehicle would clear the field without replacing it. The same week, the **EU AI
Act's Article 50** transparency obligations became enforceable
([[2026-08-02--eu-ai-office-gains-full-penalty-powers-gpai-article-50-chatbot-disclosure]]),
putting a US state and the EU on the same disclosure principle on the same date while the US federal
posture moves the other way.

**Citation note.** The statute is **SB 942** (signed September 19, 2024), **as amended by AB 853**
(signed October 13, 2025), which moved the operative date from January 1, 2026 to August 2, 2026.
The ledger capture identified the law as "AB 853"; AB 853 is the amending bill, not the Act.
