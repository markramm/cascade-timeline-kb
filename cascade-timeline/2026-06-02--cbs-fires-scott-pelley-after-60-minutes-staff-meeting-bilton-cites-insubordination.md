CBS News fired veteran 60 Minutes correspondent Scott Pelley on a Tuesday night, the day after a Monday staff meeting with new executive producer Nick Bilton. Bilton told Pelley the reason was insubordination at that meeting (NPR). The termination notice, obtained by the Associated Press, says Pelley "hijacked" Bilton's first meeting with staff. Pelley's own statement, shared with NPR late Tuesday, accused CBS's new news leadership of pushing him "to inject falsehoods and bias into a politically sensitive story." NPR reported that CBS had not yet responded to those accusations. The event date is the firing, Tuesday night; PBS and NPR ran the story the next day, Wednesday.

Sequence: the correspondents Alfonsi and Vega and executive producer Tanya Simon were fired the Thursday before ("Black Thursday," see the Black Thursday entry in related_events); the Pelley confrontation with Bilton was on the Monday; the firing came on the Tuesday. Anderson Cooper's departure from 60 Minutes was reported on Feb. 16, 2026 (Reuters); he said he wanted more time with his young children. NPR's story says Cooper "left last month, concerned over the direction of the network's coverage"; that is NPR's characterization, not Cooper's stated reason.

NPR's David Folkenflik described the dispute as "part of a broader fight over the direction of CBS News," given CBS's acquisition by a billionaire family "whose business interests have become intertwined with the political interests of President Trump." That framing is NPR's.

This entry was dated a day late (it gave Wednesday for a Tuesday firing) and misplaced the Black Thursday firings on the Monday.
