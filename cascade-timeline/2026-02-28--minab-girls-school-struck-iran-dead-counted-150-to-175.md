On February 28, during the opening hours of the assault on Iran, a missile struck a girls' school in southern Iran, killing more than 170 people, according to Al Jazeera's explainer. The count of the dead differs by who counted and when, and none of the counts below has been independently confirmed:

- UN News: the missiles "reportedly destroyed a girl’s primary school in Minab, southern Iran, killing around 150 and wounding almost 100".
- Iranian state media, as of March 4: 168 dead. Human Rights Watch "has not been able to independently verify that number".
- Time, Mar 11, 2026: "Some 175 people were killed, at least 108 of them children," per a representative of the Coordinating Council of Iranian Teachers' Trade Associations.
- Al Jazeera: at least 170 people killed.
- Representative Jason Crow's press release of March 13, 2026: at least 175 civilians killed.

Time reported that "neither Israel nor the United States military claimed responsibility" in the days after. Time also reported that a video released by Mehr News and first geolocated by Bellingcat "appears to show a U.S.-made Tomahawk missile hitting a clinic near the school", and that two munitions experts it interviewed "identified the munition in the video as a Tomahawk". Time reported that The New York Times said "the preliminary findings of a military investigation into the strike have determined that the U.S. was responsible", citing unnamed U.S. officials. Trump said "In my opinion and based on what I've seen, that was done by Iran"; Al Jazeera wrote that there was "no evidence then, nor now, suggesting any Iranian role in the attack". Al Jazeera reported that "Israel has denied any involvement". Time reported that the Department of Defense and U.S. Central Command "declined to provide comment", and quoted White House Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt: "The investigation is still ongoing". Al Jazeera reported that Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the U.S. "would not intentionally target a school".

The calls for investigation: Human Rights Watch said the February 28, 2026 attack "should be investigated as a war crime". UN News reported that UNESCO called the bombing "a grave violation of humanitarian law". Al Jazeera reported that a letter to Hegseth signed by 46 senators called for a "swift investigation" and said "The majority of those killed in the strikes were girls between the ages of 7 and 12 years old."
