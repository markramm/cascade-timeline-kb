MS NOW reported on Nov. 8, 2026 that 11 Iranian nationals — 10 men and one woman — were scheduled to be flown by Immigration Customs and Enforcement from Mesa, Arizona, to Islamabad, Pakistan, on Sunday, despite a federal court order requiring five business days' advance notice. Yasmine Chubin, the executive director of the group that raised the alarm, told MS NOW: "We have received credible information from multiple independent sources indicating that the U.S. government is proceeding with plans to deport Iranian nationals, including asylum seekers, to Iran, via Pakistan, despite a federal court order requiring five business days' advance notice"

In a court hearing on Oct. 15, U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes said deporting Iranians to their native country was the equivalent of sending them to Nazi Germany during World War II, and warned: "If anything happens that violates either of those two orders, I will hold every single person associated with violating it". Earlier reporting on Iran-bound flights is at [[2026-08-18--intercept-ice-coordinated-iran-deportation-flights-during-twelve-day-war]].

**The case.** The order is in *Iranian American Legal Defense Fund v. Rubio*, (D.D.C.), before Judge Reyes. Her sealed ex parte order, since unsealed, requires "five business days' written advance notice" before removing any Iranian to Iran or to a third country where Iran will coordinate onward travel.

**Who filed what.** The sealed notice of Nov. 3 that MS NOW attributed to "a party" was the government's: the plaintiff's later filing calls it "Defendants' ex parte notice filed Tuesday, November 3, 2026," and the government asked the court to keep it sealed. IALDF's own filing came on Nov. 8, a "Notice of Apparent Violation" that says a removal beginning at 4:00 a.m. on Sunday, November 8 "would follow only three business days' notice." IALDF said it had not seen the government's notice and asked the court to compare the two in camera. It asked for no relief and reserved its rights.

**What happened to the flight.** ABC News reported that the Sunday attempt was canceled while the plane sat on the tarmac in Arizona, and that a second flight left Mesa on Tuesday and turned back, with Pakistan declining to receive the group "according to two sources familiar with the situation." ABC also reported "There has been no public notice to the federal judge about the last two deportation attempts." Neither flight is shown to have removed anyone.
