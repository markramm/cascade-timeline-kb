Activists rallied at the NC General Assembly on July 22, opposing ICE's plan to reopen the shuttered Rivers Correctional Institution in Winton, Hertford County as a GEO Group-operated 1,400-bed detention center by the end of 2026 — ahead of the New York Times's national story on the internal ICE planning documents, published July 24 ([[2026-07-24--ice-plans-1400-bed-rivers-correctional-hertford-nc]]). A separate local protest was organized in the county itself. One Hertford County commissioner expressed reservations, citing recent fatal ICE shootings in other states. ICE publicly stated it has "no new detention centers to announce at this time" — a denial issued even as internal planning documents obtained by the New York Times describe an active fast-track plan.

Local and statewide mobilization against the plan was already underway on July 22, two days before the New York Times carried the internal ICE planning documents nationally on July 24 ([[2026-07-24--ice-plans-1400-bed-rivers-correctional-hertford-nc]]) — the sequence runs local awareness first, national disclosure second, not the reverse. This registers the same jobs-versus-detention local division the timeline has documented at other GEO Group and CoreCivic reopening sites through 2026 — the facility was previously operated by GEO Group as a federal Bureau of Prisons contract prison before closing in 2021.
