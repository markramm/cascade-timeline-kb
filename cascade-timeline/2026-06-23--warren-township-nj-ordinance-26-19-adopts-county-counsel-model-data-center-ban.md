Warren Township, Somerset County, adopted **Ordinance 26-19** in late June 2026, defining data centers, designating them a prohibited use, and adding supplementary standards. It is the clearest documented instance of a New Jersey municipality adopting circulated model ordinance language, and the measurement is unambiguous: **Ordinance 26-19 reproduces 47.3% of the model ordinance's 8-gram word sequences** (651 shared sequences, Jaccard 0.2009), with a **longest contiguous shared run exceeding 115 words**. By comparison, Jackson's and Pemberton's ordinances each reproduce **0.1%** of the same model — boilerplate only.

The model was drafted by **Joseph J. Bell IV, Assistant Warren County Counsel**, in February 2026, and circulated to municipal governing bodies by Warren County Commissioner James R. Kern III ([[2026-02-26--warren-county-counsel-model-data-center-ban-ordinance-circulated-nj]]). The model's distinctive factual recitals all appear in Warren Township's adopted text — the "3 to 5 million gallons" of daily water use, the "300 MW" comparison, the "twenty percent" energy-cost increase, the "subterranean aquifers" language. **Warren Township is in Somerset County, not Warren County**, which is worth stating plainly because the name collision invites error: the ordinance's textual parent originated with Warren *County's* counsel, but the municipality that adopted it most faithfully is Warren *Township* in a different county. The template traveled beyond the county whose lawyer wrote it.

Warren did not copy passively. It made three substantive additions the model does not contain:

- **A threshold.** "Any use with a power density of 2 to 4W or more per acre shall be considered a data center." The units are apparently garbled as printed — watts per acre is not a meaningful density for a facility drawing hundreds of megawatts — and this looks like a drafting error worth flagging rather than quoting as a functional standard. The model has no threshold of any kind.
- **A warehouse exclusion.** "**A data center is not a warehouse.**" This is the third New Jersey municipality documented drawing that line in its own code, alongside Jackson's definitional exclusion and Pemberton's carve-out of data centers from its warehouse permissions — three towns, three independent drafting lineages, the same distinction.
- **Supplementary performance standards**, plus a provision treating transformers, electrical substations, and utility infrastructure as separate uses rather than as accessory components.

Structurally, Warren folds the prohibition into an existing prohibited-uses list at §16-5.25 — alongside drive-throughs, adult bookstores, and cannabis establishments — and separately strikes data centers from the permitted uses of the GI General Industrial district.

**What this case establishes and what it does not.** It establishes that model ordinance text originating in a county counsel's office is being adopted, at scale, into binding municipal code — a supply-side coordination channel the corpus had not previously documented in the 2026 moratorium wave, and one running through local-government legal infrastructure rather than activist drafting. It does **not** establish that the wave is template-driven: three of the four New Jersey ordinances text-compared in this pass share nothing but boilerplate with the model, and the wave contains at least three drafting lineages and five distinct mechanisms. The accurate reading is **partial template diffusion inside a broader convergent wave**. See [[research-new-jersey-datacenter-ban-wave-cran-and-developer-litigation-2026-08]].
