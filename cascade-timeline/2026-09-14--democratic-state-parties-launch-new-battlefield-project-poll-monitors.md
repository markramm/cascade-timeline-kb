Democratic state parties announced the **"New Battlefield Project"** on **September 14, 2026**, a volunteer mobilization aiming to place **10,000 blue-vested monitors** at **25 percent** of the roughly **95,000** election-day polling places, concentrated in competitive districts. The effort is framed explicitly as a counter to federal interference: the administration has not ruled out deploying federal agents to cities during the election, and Trump allies have publicly urged sending immigration agents to polls. ICE and CBP leadership told a congressional committee in mid-September they have **"no plans"** to guard polling places. Five Democratic-led states — **California, Colorado, Connecticut, Maryland, and Washington** — have separately enacted statutory shields against federal interference at polling sites.

This is the private half of a two-track response, and the tracks answer different problems. The five state shield laws create legal exposure for a federal agent who shows up; 10,000 volunteers create a witness where the law's enforcement would otherwise depend on the account of the person being intimidated. Neither prevents a deployment, and the volunteer track's specific bet is that documentation is the binding constraint — which is a reasonable read of a cycle in which the FBI director declined under oath to rule out agents at the polls ([[2026-09-15--patel-refuses-under-oath-rule-out-fbi-agents-polling-places-midterms]]) and LULAC and UnidosUS had to sue DHS over armed ICE presence at Denver polling sites to establish what happened there ([[2026-09-10--lulac-unidosus-denver-sue-dhs-armed-ice-polling-places]]).

*Figure note: a separate account cites 50,000 monitors rather than 10,000. This entry carries 10,000, the figure attributed to the state parties' own announcement in U.S. News; the discrepancy is unreconciled and the number should be re-checked against the parties' materials before it is used in published work.*
