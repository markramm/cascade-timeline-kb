In a **6-3 ruling on June 29, 2026**, the Supreme Court overturned *Humphrey's Executor v. United States* (1935), holding that President Trump's firing of FTC Commissioner **Rebecca Kelly Slaughter** without cause was lawful. The decision grants the president sweeping at-will removal authority over roughly two dozen independent agencies Congress designed to insulate from direct presidential control — the FTC, CFPB, NLRB, and others — vindicating the unitary-executive theory and removing a structural check that stood for 91 years. Chief Justice Roberts wrote for the majority; Justice Sotomayor dissented that the ruling converts the duty to faithfully execute the laws into a license to defy them.

This is the merits ruling that the timeline anticipated through the cert grant ([[2025-12-08--supreme-court-independent-agency-firing-humphreys-executor]]) and the shadow-docket stay in [[2025-05-22--scotus-stays-trump-v-wilcox-nlrb-mspb-removal]]. It completes a doctrinal arc running from [[1926-10-25--myers-v-united-states-taft-presidential-removal-power]] through Scalia's *Morrison v. Olson* dissent ([[1988-06-29--morrison-v-olson-independent-counsel-scalia-dissent]]). Decided the same week as the nationwide-injunctions ruling, it forms a structural pair: independent agencies can now be captured by removal, and courts lose the broad tool to halt the resulting cascade — the Court-rigging and accountability-elimination categories operating in tandem.

## The same-day Fed carve-out, and what the ruling has actually produced (added 2026-08-27)

**A companion decision bounds this one, and the corpus was missing it.** On the same day, the Court decided ***Trump v. Cook*, No. 25A312 (June 29, 2026), 5-4**, upholding the Federal Reserve's for-cause protection as a **Fed-specific carve-out**. Governor **Lisa Cook** — whose removal was attempted in August 2025 — **remains in office** under an active district-court injunction. Canon has the January oral argument ([[2026-01-21--scotus-hears-trump-v-cook-fed-independence-case]]) but had no entry for the decision.

So the unitary-executive holding is broad but not total: it reaches roughly two dozen independent agencies and **stops at the Fed**.

**What the ruling has produced in ~60 days, measured** ([[research-independent-agency-removals-post-slaughter-census-2026-08-27]]): **exactly one agency's leadership has been removed citing it** — the Election Assistance Commission, where the White House expressly named *Slaughter* as the legal basis. Commissioners **Hicks** and **Hovland** were fired July 9; **Christy McCormick resigned** under pressure in the same window. That is a sourced causal link, not an inference from timing.

**Everything else commonly attributed to this ruling predates it.** The FTC (Slaughter, Bedoya), NLRB (Wilcox), MSPB (Harris), EEOC (Burrows, Samuels), CPSC (Trumka, Hoehn-Saric, Boyle), NRC (Hanson) and FEC (Weintraub) removals all occurred in **2025** — they are the **predicate litigation this ruling resolved**, not evidence of its effect. Powell was not fired (term expired; Warsh confirmed normally), and FCC's Anna Gomez is a term-expired holdover, not removed.

**The structurally important finding is the mechanism, not the roster.** Where agencies have been disabled, it is by **quorum loss** rather than capture: the EAC now has zero commissioners and cannot certify voting equipment, update the national mail-in registration form, or distribute HAVA grants. The FEC has been below quorum since before the ruling. Agencies that were captured-but-staffed — FTC, CPSC, NRC — keep operating under new direction. **A quorum-blocked agency is a quieter outcome than a captured one, and on this evidence the more consequential one.**

No lawsuit has been filed over the EAC firings as of 2026-08-27.
