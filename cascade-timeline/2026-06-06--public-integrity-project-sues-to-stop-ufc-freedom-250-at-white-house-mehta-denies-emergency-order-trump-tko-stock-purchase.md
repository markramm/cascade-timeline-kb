A federal lawsuit filed Saturday asks a court to stop the UFC fight night planned for the White House South Lawn on Trump's birthday. The Public Integrity Project filed it on behalf of a political activist and an Air Force veteran (CBS News); AP calls the plaintiffs an activist and a Vietnam War veteran. The suit calls the plans for the June 14 event "deeply corrupt" and argues that they seek to enrich the president and his allies. Its claims are allegations. The National Park Service and the Interior Department are named as defendants in the lawsuit; neither Paramount nor UFC is.

**Who the suit says benefits.** The lawsuit contends that those who stand to benefit financially from the event include the president, who has investments in UFC's parent company (TKO Group Holdings), White, and the company's broadcast partner Paramount Skydance, which is the parent company of CBS News, and its advertisers.

**The ruling.** A federal judge ruled on Friday, June 12, 2026, that the White House could stage the event. Mehta concluded that the plaintiffs likely don't have legal standing and failed to prove irreparable harm, and wrote that "it is fair to say Plaintiffs unreasonably delayed bringing suit, undercutting their claims of irreparable harm." He noted the $60 million that UFC has spent to produce the event. He decided on the written briefs: "he would not be holding a hearing on the petition, and would instead rule based on the written briefs." He wrote that "the court does not reach the merits." The claw's disassembly was to begin Monday morning, which the judge treated as making the claimed aesthetic harm temporary. This entry holds no ruling on the merits; none was made.

**The stock purchase.** Trump purchased between $15,001 and $50,000 of stock of TKO Group Holdings, the parent company of UFC, on March 25, according to a HuffPost review of his May 12 financial disclosure filing. The disclosure gives a range, not a share count.

**Paramount+.** UFC and Paramount always planned to have the White House card streamed exclusively on Paramount+, per Sports Business Journal. UFC and Paramount struck a media rights deal worth $7.7 billion. Whether Paramount+ paid anything separately for this event is not established.
