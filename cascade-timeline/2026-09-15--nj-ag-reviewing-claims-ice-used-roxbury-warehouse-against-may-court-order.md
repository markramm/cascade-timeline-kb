The **New Jersey Attorney General's office** confirmed in mid-September 2026 that it is **"carefully reviewing"** allegations that ICE transported a person arrested in Dover on **September 4** to the **Roxbury warehouse** in Morris County — a 470,000-square-foot site barred from housing detainees under a **May 12 court agreement** requiring completion of a NEPA environmental review before any ICE use. The **Project No ICE North Jersey Alliance (Project NINJA)** sent a **September 11 letter** to the AG's office documenting the incident, including video collected by activists of ICE vehicles entering and leaving the facility. ICE has neither confirmed nor denied the allegation. A contempt motion would be the first such enforcement action at the site.

This entry is `status: reported` rather than confirmed, and the reason is the evidentiary posture: the central claim — that a detainee was brought inside a barred facility — rests on community-collected video and a resident advocacy group's letter, with no agency confirmation and no filing yet. The AG's "carefully reviewing" is the only official language on the record.

The structural interest is what a court agreement is worth once the litigation attention moves on. Roxbury is the site DHS abandoned after suit in June ([[2026-06-29--dhs-abandons-roxbury-nj-warehouse-ice-detention-after-lawsuit]]), then declined to foreclose in a July status report that kept its options open ([[2026-07-20--roxbury-nj-dhs-flip-flop-continuing-assess-status-report]]). If the allegation holds, the sequence is that a warehouse removed from the detention network by consent was used anyway, which would say something specific about the durability of the state-by-state wins that stalled the Detention Reengineering Initiative program-wide ([[2026-08-19--ice-detention-reengineering-initiative-stalls-program-wide-11-purchased-sites]]) — namely that a stalled acquisition and an unusable building are not the same thing.
