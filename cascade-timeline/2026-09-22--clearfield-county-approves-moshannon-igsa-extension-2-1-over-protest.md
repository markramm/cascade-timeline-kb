The **Clearfield County Board of Commissioners** voted **2-1** on **September 22, 2026** to approve a **six-month extension** of the county's Intergovernmental Service Agreement with ICE and **GEO Group** for the **Moshannon Valley Processing Center** — the largest immigration detention facility in the Northeast. **Tim Winters** and **John Sobel** voted in favor; **Dave Glass** voted against. Dozens of people from across Pennsylvania protested at the meeting. The expiring agreement was a five-year IGSA; the extension **modifies the staffing plan and changes the contracting officer**, while, per a copy of the document obtained through a public-records request, "all other terms and conditions remain unchanged."

**The majority's stated reasoning is the substance of this entry, because it is an argument about what a county intermediary is for.** Sobel and Winters said rejecting the contract would cost the **Philipsburg-Osceola Area School District** tax revenue and would **eliminate whatever oversight the county has** over Moshannon. That is the intermediary position stated plainly: the county's leverage exists only while it holds the contract, so refusing the contract forfeits the leverage along with the money. Glass, who had publicly committed to voting no before the meeting, cited ICE officer conduct and opposition to federal immigration policy.

**Whether that oversight is real is the question the vote does not answer, and the record cuts against it.** The county receives roughly **$200,000 a year** as the pass-through — a fee, not a regulatory instrument. Nothing in the reported terms gives the county inspection rights, conditions standards, or a mechanism to act on a violation; the extension explicitly leaves all terms but staffing and the contracting officer unchanged. Canon already records a Moshannon detainee placed in solitary and then transferred to Louisiana after a protest ([[2026-06-04--moshannon-ice-detainee-solitary-then-transferred-louisiana-after-protest]]) — an exercise of exactly the discretion county "oversight" did not touch.

**The alternative the majority was weighing against is documented, and it is not hypothetical.** ICE had already been reported as weighing an outright purchase of Moshannon as the contract neared expiry ([[2026-08-05--ice-weighs-buying-moshannon-valley-clearfield-county-contract-expires]]), and in July it published a request for information on **turn-key detention services in central Pennsylvania** — part of the four-AOR turn-key solicitation canon tracks at [[2026-09-11--ice-turnkey-detention-solicitation-four-aors-deadline-extended]]. So the county faced a live structural fact: a no vote plausibly removes the county from the arrangement without removing the facility, and federal ownership would end both the $200,000 and the property-tax base. **Either outcome takes the county's vote away** — which makes this a six-month deferral of that choice, not a resolution of it.

**This is the fiscal-dependency mechanism operating at the moment of decision.** The corpus documents the IGSA intermediary model as a structure that converts a county's budget into a reason to keep a detention facility open; here two commissioners said so on the record, naming the school district. The tax-base argument is the same one that recurs at warehouse-conversion sites where federal purchase would remove the parcel from local rolls.

**Sourcing note.** The 2-1 tally, the three commissioners' votes, the staffing-plan-and-contracting-officer scope with the "all other terms and conditions remain unchanged" quotation, the school-district and oversight rationales, and the protest are carried across four tier-1 outlets reporting the same day (Spotlight PA, WPSU, WESA, Pennsylvania Capital-Star). **The ledger captured this story before the vote occurred** — its own summary said "outcome not yet confirmed" — and the outcome was verified independently on 2026-09-22 before this entry was written. **The $688,000 property-tax figure carried in the capture is deliberately excluded here:** prior corpus work established it traces to an economic-impact release from a booster organization with no published methodology, and that the county's own assessment office has never published a defensible figure. The commissioners' tax-base argument is recorded as their argument, not as a quantified loss.

**Open:** the executed extension document (its actual expiry date, and whether the staffing-plan change alters conditions-of-confinement obligations); which contractors responded to the July central-Pennsylvania turn-key RFI and at what capacity; and what happens at the six-month mark, when the same choice returns without the pre-expiry leverage.
