ICE confirmed on August 12, 2026 that Jose Chajon-Raxon, a Guatemalan man arrested July 18, died weeks after being released from Delaney Hall in Newark following a medical emergency on July 19. He was the third Delaney Hall death of 2026. ICE disclosed the death only after advocacy pressure, and explained the delay by citing an internal policy that no longer requires death reporting for individuals who die after leaving ICE custody. Newsweek independently confirmed the non-reporting policy; NBC News confirmed the death and the medical-emergency timeline.

The policy is the story. A death-count that excludes anyone who dies after release creates a discretionary lever over the statistic: release a detainee in medical crisis and the subsequent death leaves the official ledger. This operationalizes the Venturella memo eliminating post-release death reporting (2026-06-04--venturella-memo-eliminates-post-release-death-reporting-ice-accountability-erasure) and matches the undercount Mother Jones documented at Stewart (2026-08-05--mother-jones-ice-death-undercount-stewart-detention-trejo-ricano). Delaney Hall, operated by GEO Group under a $1.2B contract, had already logged Edwin Lopez-Cornejo's death on August 1 (2026-08-01--edwin-lopez-cornejo-dies-delaney-hall-second-death-geo-1-2b-contract) and a third death ICE never announced (2026-08-06--ice-42-ai-tools-23-disclosed-delaney-hall-third-hidden-death), alongside a hunger strike and a state-police kettling crackdown on protesters. New Jersey is separately suing GEO over health-inspector access at the same facility (2026-06-02--nj-sues-geo-group-delaney-hall-health-inspector-access). The count of deaths in ICE custody is not a measurement; it is an output of definitional choices ICE controls.
