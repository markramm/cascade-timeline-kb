The Washington County Board of County Commissioners voted unanimously at its August 20, 2026 regular meeting to impose a one-year moratorium on the acceptance, processing, review, and approval of applications for data centers and large-scale data processing facilities in unincorporated Washington County. The stated rationale is a gap in the county's own rules: the ordinance recites that the Comprehensive Plan and Land Development Code "do not adequately address the infrastructure and public safety impacts associated with data centers," naming demands on electricity, backup generation, water, telecommunications, and transportation. County Attorney Clay Milton and County Planner Dawn McDonald handled the ordinance; Commissioner David Corbin pressed for speed — "We don't need to kick the can down the road. We need to get it in." Neither source identifies a specific pending application or named developer that triggered the vote.

Washington County is a Florida panhandle county of roughly 25,000 people, and the scale is the point. The moratorium reaches only unincorporated territory; the county's incorporated municipalities retain authority over what happens inside their own limits, which leaves an intact path for any developer willing to site within a city. Resident Ken Attard supported the pause but pressed commissioners on exactly that structural weakness, arguing it should hold until enforceable rules actually take effect rather than lapsing after a year of study: "The pause should remain until enforceable regulations take effect." That is the recurring failure mode in this corpus — a moratorium is a stopgap that buys drafting time, and it converts into durable protection only if the county finishes and adopts the code amendment before the clock runs out. DeSoto County, Florida is the cautionary comparator: its moratorium passed with a carve-out intact and was approved in final form with the loophole preserved ([[2026-06-25--desoto-county-fl-data-center-moratorium-dcip-carve-out]], [[2026-07-29--desoto-county-fl-moratorium-approved-loophole-preserved]]).

The underlying fact (a unanimous August 20 vote on a one-year unincorporated-area moratorium) is consistent across both, but the record here is thinner than most entries in this file, and no primary ordinance text has been pulled. What the corpus does independently document is that county-level moratoria have been arriving continuously through the summer across several states: Citrus and Lake Counties in Florida ([[2026-06-18--citrus-county-fl-data-center-moratorium-holder-rezoning-denied]], [[2026-06-23--lake-county-fl-commissioners-draft-data-center-moratorium-july-vote]]), Linn County, Iowa ([[2026-07-01--linn-county-ia-18-month-data-center-moratorium-vote]]), Walker County, Georgia ([[2026-08-11--walker-county-ga-datacenter-moratorium-180-days-study-committee]]), an eleven-county wave in North Carolina ([[2026-08-03--nc-moratorium-wave-11-counties-durham-hearing-aug24]]), and back-to-back votes in Fort Worth and Louisville ([[2026-08-13--fort-worth-louisville-consecutive-datacenter-moratorium-votes]]). A county of 25,000 acting on the same concerns as Fort Worth is the signal worth recording: the buildout's siting pressure has reached jurisdictions with no zoning staff to absorb it, and the moratorium is what a small county reaches for when the application arrives before the ordinance does.
