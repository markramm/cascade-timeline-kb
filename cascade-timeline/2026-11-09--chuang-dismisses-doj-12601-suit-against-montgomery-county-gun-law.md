On November 9, 2026, Judge Theodore D. Chuang (D. Md.) granted Montgomery County's motion to dismiss the United States' civil enforcement action under the police pattern-or-practice statute and denied the Government's motion for a preliminary injunction; the docket entry directs the clerk to close the case. The suit, filed on August 3, 2026 over the County's Expedited Bill 23-26E, which restricts firearms within 100 yards of listed places of public assembly (parks, places of worship, schools, libraries, recreational facilities, government buildings, polling places and others), was signed by the Second Amendment Section of DOJ's Civil Rights Division under Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon.

The opinion holds that the statute reaches a "pattern or practice of conduct by law enforcement officers" and that the complaint alleges no instance of enforcement of the bill. It says DOJ did not dispute that it has used the statute only against police misconduct, and calls the Government's reading "a breathtakingly broad expansion of federal power" over state and local governments. The Court assumed without deciding that standing exists and did not reach the Second Amendment merits. It cites the only other federal court to have addressed the question, the Central District of California in the Government's suit against that state; California filed the Montgomery opinion there as supplemental authority the next day.

The Court's order does not say whether the dismissal is with or without prejudice. The County enacted the bill on July 27, 2026, a week before DOJ sued, so the absence of enforcement describes a law only days old when challenged.
