# Hennepin County Attorney Mary Moriarty Charges ICE Agent Gregory Donnell Morgan Jr. with Two Counts of Second-Degree Assault

On April 16, 2026, Hennepin County Attorney Mary Moriarty announced that her office had charged Gregory Donnell Morgan Jr., an ICE agent, with two counts of second-degree assault. NPR reported that the charges "appear to be the first against a federal immigration officer for actions allegedly taken while on duty during the immigration enforcement crackdown in Minnesota earlier this year." The charges are allegations; Morgan has not been convicted.

## The allegation

The county attorney's release says that on Feb. 5, 2026, Morgan "brandished and pointed his duty weapon at two individuals while traveling alongside them in a black rental vehicle." NPR reported, from the complaint, that Morgan told state authorities in a voluntary interview that he feared for his safety when the other car pulled in front of him, so he drew his gun. The release does not describe a defense; Morgan's account is his own.

## Status (as of the 2026-11-17 correction)

- Morgan is fighting to move the two charges to federal court under Supremacy Clause immunity, per the KMSP-TV (Fox) report of October 14, 2026; a federal judge, Nancy Brasel, held a two-day evidentiary hearing.
- KMSP-TV (Fox) reported on November 13, 2026 that the judge "has not indicated when she will make a final ruling on if the trial will take place in state or federal court."
- The county attorney's office opposes the move, and argues that pointing a gun at motorists does not meet the threshold required for a federal immunity defense, per KMSP-TV's account of its briefs.
- The charge is pending. No court has ruled on the immunity defense in the sources read.
