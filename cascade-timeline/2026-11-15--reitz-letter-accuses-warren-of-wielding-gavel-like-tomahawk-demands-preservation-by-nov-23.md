Aaron Reitz, the United States attorney for the Southern District of Texas, posted a preservation letter to Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., on X on Sunday, Nov. 15. His post said Warren "is on the warpath to wield her committee gavel like a tomahawk, seeking the scalps of those who associate 'too closely' with President Trump." The letter, read from its page images, says "In sum, you appear eager to wield your future gavel like a tomahawk, hunting for the scalps of President Trump’s private associates." It gives formal notice of an obligation to preserve documents relevant to Warren's alleged intent to "weaponize congressional investigations and deprive citizens of their core constitutional rights in violation of 18 U.S.C. §§ 241 and 242," and asks her to confirm in writing by 6:00 p.m. Eastern Time on Nov. 23, 2026 that she received the letter and has taken steps to preserve the materials. Fox News reports the letter does not establish that Warren violated federal law.

The letter opens with her Nov. 8 "Face the Nation" appearance. It says CBS News host Margaret Brennan noted Warren had said "you want to bring big tech CEOs, Wall Street executives, and giant corporations who may have broken the law to face consequences," and that Warren agreed, adding these people "better get ready for some investigations—serious ones." The words about corporations that may have broken the law are the letter's quotation of Brennan; the "serious ones" words are Warren's. The letter also says that Warren, with Blumenthal and Garcia, "wrote to a private citizen demanding sweeping information about her and other private citizens and entities who associate with President Trump." It does not name her in the text; a footnote cites a Warren press release whose title, in the image, names Meredith O’Rourke. NBC identifies the recipient as Meredith O’Rourke, a National Park Foundation board member and top fundraiser for Trump, and reports that Trump reposted the letter on Truth Social later Sunday. NBC says the letter contains numerous references to Native Americans, such as "scalps," "war cries" and "tomahawk," which it calls a dig at Warren over her past claims of Native American descent; the characterisation is NBC's.

The letter tells Warren that Sens. Blumenthal and Slotkin were "each already recipients of preservation demands from my office" ([[2026-11-11--reitz-letter-tells-blumenthal-to-preserve-documents-citing-18-usc-241-242-over-ballroom-donor-inquiry]], [[2026-11-12--reitz-letter-tells-slotkin-to-preserve-documents-over-go-hard-on-accountability-comments]]). Warren had not responded to NBC's request for comment when it published. The letter text is as transcribed from the X images, which are screenshots, not a DOJ-hosted original.
