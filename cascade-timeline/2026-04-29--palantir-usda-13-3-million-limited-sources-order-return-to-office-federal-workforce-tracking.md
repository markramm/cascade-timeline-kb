USDA signed a delivery order with Palantir Technologies to design, configure, deploy and manage a tool to track USDA employees' return to the office, with insights into employee and facility mapping, facility utilization and office capacity. The order's ceiling is $13,304,802.99, with $8,608,990.17 obligated.

The order is a limited-sources order. USDA's justification supports "firm fixed-price delivery order under the GSA Multiple Award Schedule (MAS)" and states that "Palantir is the only vendor capable of providing the required capabilities in Section 3 above without unacceptable delays and significant duplication of effort to the Government." Its market-research section names Databricks, Snowflake, IBM, SAS, SalesForce and Alteryx and says "none offer the combination of capabilities" that Palantir provides. The USAspending record carries the same posture in its own fields: "ONLY ONE SOURCE - OTHER," one offer received, and, alongside, "FULL AND OPEN COMPETITION" "SUBJECT TO MULTIPLE AWARD FAIR OPPORTUNITY." The justification form's own guidance notes that orders under schedule contracts are "exempt from the competition requirements of FAR Part 6," so the schedule contract was the competition and the order was a one-source placement under it.

"No-bid" is therefore loose: one vendor answered, by USDA's own account because it judged only one vendor capable. The precise statement is a one-source order under an existing contract vehicle, justified on only-one-source grounds.

The $75 million that circulated earlier is not the order's value. It traces to a State of Surveillance line, "The contract value ranges from less than $750,000 to over $75 million," and the same numbers appear in the justification form as approval tiers for who must sign, such as "if over $15 million, but not exceeding $75 million" for the Head of the Procuring Activity.

The order extends the federal-data consolidation pattern documented across the IRS ([[2026-04-24--intercept-palantir-irs-130-million-data-mining-contract]]) and other agencies, and it ties to the finding in [[2026-04-23--propublica-disclosure-database-144-trump-appointees-palantir-holdings]] on administration appointees with Palantir ties. When the vendor that serves immigration enforcement also assigns desks for the workforce that administers those systems, the surveillance architecture instruments its own administrators.
