A Texas jail released ICE agent Christian Castro on Thursday morning from the Cameron County detention center near Brownsville, and Minnesota's extradition request for him had not been granted or refused. Al Jazeera reported that under Texas law the state cannot hold the subject of an extradition order for more than 90 days. Castro is charged in Hennepin County, Minnesota, with four counts of second-degree assault and one count of falsely reporting a crime, CBS News Minnesota reported.

On the governor: U.S. District Judge Fernando Rodriguez Jr. wrote that "The record reflects only that Governor Abbott has neither agreed nor refused to sign the rendition warrant as to Castro," and found that Minnesota's dispute "does not present an issue ripe for adjudication," denying its request for a temporary restraining order (CBS News Minnesota). PBS NewsHour reported that Abbott said the week before that he would not respond to the extradition request, citing the ongoing investigation. The governor's press secretary, Andrew Mahaleris, gave CBS News Minnesota this statement: "Laws require that Mr. Castro be released while the State completes its investigation into his fugitive status" (CBS News Minnesota). Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison's characterization of the same facts is that "Abbott's inaction unlawfully sidestepped federal extradition to bolster Trump's deportation campaign" (as quoted by Al Jazeera). That is Ellison's statement; the Texas side's stated position is that its investigation of Castro's fugitive status was still under way.

A senior Homeland Security official said Castro "has been suspended without pay as the investigation continues" (CBS News Minnesota).
