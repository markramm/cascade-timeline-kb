On April 23, 2026, Senators Maria Cantwell, Tammy Duckworth and Edward Markey released a letter asking the Department of Transportation's deputy inspector general to open an investigation. They asked whether FAA Administrator Bryan Bedford "profited from deliberately violating his ethics agreement". The letter asks for an investigation.

The Office of Government Ethics wrote the Senate Commerce Committee on December 8, 2025 that Bedford "has failed to timely comply with certain terms of the ethics agreement." OGE said he was required to comply by October 7, 2025. OGE added that "OGE has not been informed that Mr. Bedford has divested."

According to the senators' letter, Bedford's October 7, 2025 extension request said his work demands had "created an unusual hardship for me" in meeting the required timeframe. According to the letter, Bedford told the committee on December 17, 2025: "My intention was always to complete the merger and to sell the shares in the market."

According to the senators' letter, Bedford acknowledged holding 16,733 shares of Republic. The letter says the Republic-Mesa merger turned them into "at least 652,475 shares". The senators write that Bedford sold after the merger closed "for potentially more than $25 million". The letter says the Department of Transportation told the committee he had "completed the sale of all Republic shares on February 20, 2026".

Bedford's periodic transaction report lists transactions in Republic stock in dollar ranges rather than exact amounts. This entry records no response from the inspector general; the senators' letter is a request, and whether an investigation was opened is not established by the documents read.
