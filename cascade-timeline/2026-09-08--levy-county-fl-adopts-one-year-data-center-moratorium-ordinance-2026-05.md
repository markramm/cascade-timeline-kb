The Levy County, Florida Board of County Commissioners voted **September 8, 2026** to approve **Ordinance 2026-05**, a moratorium on new data centers and large-scale power-intensive facilities in unincorporated areas, running **October 1, 2026 through September 30, 2027**. Staff will study impacts and draft proposed regulations during the pause.

The ordinance's stated concerns are worth listing in full, because they are the specific externalities a county has to zone for and the generic "economic development" frame does not address: **electricity, water, infrastructure, noise, vibration, lighting, heat, and construction traffic**. Vibration and heat are the tell — those are not objections to a building, they are objections to what the building does continuously once operating.

Note the structure of the pause. Unlike Dougherty County's renewable 180-day freeze, Levy's is a **fixed one-year window tied to a deliverable**: the moratorium ends September 30, 2027 whether or not regulations exist. That is the weaker instrument for a county that wants leverage and the stronger one for a county that wants rules — it forces the staff work to happen on a schedule, but it also hands developers a date to wait out.

**Three other Florida counties** are simultaneously advancing one-year data-center moratoriums. Arizona's legislature passed a **three-year pause on tax incentives** in July 2026 — a different lever entirely, aimed at the subsidy rather than the permit.
