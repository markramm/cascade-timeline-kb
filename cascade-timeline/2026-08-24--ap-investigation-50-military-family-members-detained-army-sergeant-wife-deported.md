## Opening

An **Associated Press** investigation, published **2026-08-05**, documented that the administration
had detained **more than 50 spouses and parents of active-duty U.S. military service members**. As of
that publication, **at least 6 had been deported.** On **2026-08-24**, the **wife of a U.S. Army
sergeant was deported to Honduras** — bringing the documented total to **at least 7**. A
**congressional probe** into the deportations was reported on **2026-08-10**.

## Date and count discipline — read before citing any number

**The "6 vs. 7" is not a contradiction; it is two different dates and both must be stated as such.**

| Figure | As of | What it counts |
|---|---|---|
| **50+ detained** | AP publication, **2026-08-05** | spouses and parents of active-duty service members |
| **at least 6 deported** | AP publication, **2026-08-05** | a floor at that date |
| **at least 7 deported** | **2026-08-24** | the Aug 5 floor plus the Army sergeant's wife |

**Every one of these is a floor, not a total** — "at least," from an investigation that necessarily
found what it could find. **Never report any of them as a complete count**, and never cite a figure
without its as-of date. This corpus has repeatedly destroyed findings by flattening
publication-date, event-date, and press-pickup-date into a single `date:` field.

**The AP investigation's own publication date is 2026-08-05; this entry is dated 2026-08-24 because
the deportation is the discrete event.** The investigation is the context, not the occurrence.

## The named person

**The Army sergeant's wife deported to Honduras on 2026-08-24 is a named individual in the source
reporting.** This entry deliberately does not print her name: the timeline record does not require it,
and re-publishing the name of a deported person alongside her spouse's unit and rank compounds
exposure for a family that did not choose coverage.

**Any piece that uses this story must go to the source and use her own words** — the
witness-not-illustration standard. **She is a witness to a mechanism, not an illustration of a
statistic.** ABC News and the Latin Times both carry the sergeant speaking on the record
(2026-08-27); that is where the human account lives, and a drafter should read it there rather than
paraphrasing from this entry.

## Why This Event Matters

**The category is the finding.** Military-family status has historically operated as a form of
protection in immigration enforcement — through parole in place and prosecutorial discretion for the
immediate relatives of service members. **50+ detentions and at least 7 deportations indicate the
protection is not being applied**, and the Guardian's framing (2026-08-25) describes it as a
**rollback of military-family protections**.

**This is the class of harm with a nameable victim**, which is rare in enforcement data and is why it
carries an importance of 9 despite being a small absolute number against total removals. It also
produced **congressional oversight within five days**, which most enforcement reporting does not.
