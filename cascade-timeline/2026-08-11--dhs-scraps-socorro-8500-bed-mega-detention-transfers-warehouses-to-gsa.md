DHS abandoned its plan to convert a nearly one-million-square-foot warehouse complex in Socorro, Texas into an 8,500-person immigration detention center and ICE training facility — what would have been the largest ICE detention center in the country — and transferred the three-warehouse industrial park to the General Services Administration for sale, removing it from federal detention use entirely. The government had purchased the Socorro property and adjoining land for $123 million in February 2026. Community leaders and residents had raised sustained objections over the area's limited water and wastewater infrastructure, including whether the facility would strain water resources, reduce water pressure, or raise residents' water bills. Socorro is at least the ninth of eleven warehouse complexes — bought for roughly $1 billion under a plan initiated by then-Secretary Kristi Noem — to be scrapped.

This closes a loop the corpus opened in January, when Flint Development sold the Eastwind Logistics complex to DHS for $123 million (2026-01-17--flint-development-sells-eastwind-logistics-dhs-123m-socorro-tx-ice-detention). The warehouse-to-detention strategy is now failing at scale: ICE had already offloaded seven of eleven warehouses by June (2026-06-21--ice-offloads-seven-of-eleven-warehouses-detention-reengineering), against the 100,000-bed reengineering initiative that produced the acquisitions in the first place (2026-06-23--ice-detention-reengineering-initiative-100k-warehouse-network). The mechanism of failure is worth naming precisely, because it is not federal restraint: Salt Lake City and county sued over a 13,000-bed warehouse conversion (2026-06-08--salt-lake-city-county-sue-dhs-ice-warehouse-detention-conversion), Arizona's AG obtained a stipulation pausing the Surprise conversion (2026-07-01--arizona-ag-stipulation-pauses-surprise-warehouse-ice-conversion), and Newburgh's town board opposed a warehouse lease (2026-07-14--newburgh-ny-town-board-opposes-ice-warehouse-lease). Buildings designed to store goods lack the water, sewer, and medical infrastructure to hold people, and local utility capacity turned out to be the binding constraint that neither appropriations nor executive will could override. Nine failures out of eleven, at a billion dollars of federal acquisition, is the most consequential reversal in the detention-expansion record — and it was produced by municipal water math, not by immigration policy.
