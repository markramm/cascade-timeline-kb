The White House published a Genesis Mission release on July 22, 2026. The release lists the National Science and Technology Challenges.

According to the release, VA will "combine its vast electronic health records and genomic data from the Million Veteran Program with DOE’s supercomputing to train AI models".

The release says more than 15 federal agencies will contribute datasets and facilities to the challenges.

The release names no consent terms, no privacy notice and no non-federal access terms for the veterans' data. The research note [[genesis-mission-va-mvp-data-at-ornl]] holds the agreements, the notices and the participant statements.

## Update (2026-07-22, announcement date): Challenge list announced the same day; DOE’s PDF names health and genomic data

The White House release of July 22, 2026 says the challenges were announced that day.

DOE’s earlier press release says DOE "announced 26 science and technology challenges of national importance".

DOE’s challenges PDF names the chronic disease, drug discovery, pediatric cancer and veterans challenges.

The chronic disease challenge names longitudinal health outcomes among its data.

The drug discovery challenge names "clinical, and real-world evidence data sources from government and private partners".

The pediatric cancer challenge names "electronic patient and family health records, claims, and other data".

The veterans challenge names "VA’s vast electronic health record data (EHR)" and genomic data from the Million Veteran Program.

The research note [[genesis-26-challenges-data-table]] compares the February and July lists.

## Update (2026-11-24, fact sheet date): DOE's one-year fact sheet counts 20 federal agencies for the same period

DOE's one-year Genesis Mission fact sheet says that by July 2026, 20 federal agencies had joined the mission.

The White House release this entry records says more than 15 federal agencies will contribute to the challenges. The documents give different counts, and neither reconciles them.
