The SEC unveiled a proposed Regulation Crypto Assets on August 18, 2026, creating tiered registration exemptions: offerings up to $5 million exempt for four years, and offerings up to $75 million exempt for 12 months with ongoing financial reporting, subject to a 60-day comment period. The most consequential provision is a safe harbor under which a crypto asset may be deemed no longer subject to securities law after a qualifying offering — a sunset on securities status rather than an exemption from registration. The proposal follows months of the Commission withdrawing enforcement actions initiated under the prior administration.

A sunset clause on securities status is a different instrument from an offering exemption, and the distinction is the whole point. An exemption says a particular sale need not be registered; a safe harbor terminating securities status says the ongoing disclosure, fraud-liability, and fiduciary architecture of the Securities Exchange Act simply ceases to attach to the asset — the protections that make secondary-market trading legible expire on a timetable rather than on the facts. This is the endpoint of a trajectory the corpus has tracked from the SEC's declaration that meme coins are not securities (2025-02-27--sec-says-meme-coins-not-subject-to-securities-regulations) through the enforcement-division shakeup (2025-04-02--us-securities-exchange-commission-shakes-up-enforcement-divi) and Atkins being grilled in Congress over the enforcement pullback (2026-02-11--atkins-grilled-congress-crypto-enforcement-pullback). It also runs in parallel to the CLARITY Act's stalled attempt to move the same assets to CFTC jurisdiction by statute — the agency is now proposing by rule much of what the Senate has not managed to pass, which is the familiar sequence: when the legislative vehicle stalls, the captured agency delivers the substance administratively.
