On October 22, 2026, the Justice Department sued Alaska, its elections director and its election and ballot-review boards in federal court in Anchorage (No. 3:26-cv-00376), asking the court to bar certification of the U.S. Senate result in one case only: if "Senator Sullivan does not win the election" and the votes cast for a second candidate, Daniel J. Sullivan, would have elected him had they gone to the incumbent. The complaint says "it is vital that Defendants not certify any election result that may be impacted by voter confusion," calls the second candidate's run a "frivolous candidacy," and asks the court to hold certification "pending an investigation to determine whether votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan were meant to be cast for Senator Sullivan." Its one count rests on the National Voter Registration Act's statement of purpose, to "protect the integrity of the electoral process." It is signed by Associate Attorney General Stanley E. Woodward Jr. Trump told reporters, "I'm the one that said 'do it,'" CNBC reported.

On 10/23/26, Alaskans for Mary and the DSCC moved to intervene, and the League of Women Voters of Alaska, Common Cause and individual voters moved to intervene as defendants. The suit sets up a post-election certification fight before any ballots are counted, through the Justice Department's own civil enforcement power.

## Update (2026-10-25, filing date): Alaskans for Mary and the DSCC file a proposed motion to dismiss and ask the court to expedite

On October 25, 2026 (the filing date), Alaskans for Mary and the DSCC, still proposed intervenors, moved for leave to file a motion to dismiss, attached as an exhibit. That proposed motion argues "The NVRA provides no legal basis for DOJ’s claim" and "DOJ has no other legal authority to interfere with Alaska’s election." A companion filing, styled an "emergency motion to expedite," asks the court to order DOJ to respond to the intervention motion, the leave motion and the proposed motion to dismiss "by 5:00 p.m. AKDT on Tuesday, October 27" and to "dismiss the case this week." These are a proposed party's arguments, not rulings.

## Update (2026-10-26, filing date): Alaska moves to dismiss, and Judge Gleason expedites the intervention motions

On October 26, 2026 (the filing date), the State defendants, represented by the Alaska Department of Law, moved to dismiss the complaint "with prejudice, because the complaint has no legal basis." The motion argues "The United States lacks standing to bring this suit," that it "fails to state a claim for violation of the National Voter" Registration Act, and that "This lawsuit and the relief requested violate the Elections Clause." It says the United States sued to block certification "but only if a specific, favored candidate" loses. The State also moved to expedite its motion.

In an order dated the 26th day of October, 2026, Judge Sharon L. Gleason granted the expedite motion and the League of Women Voters' joinder. The United States must respond to the motions to intervene "by Tuesday, October 27, 2026, at 5:00 p.m." Proposed intervenors may file replies by Wednesday, and the order says "The Court intends to issue a ruling by 5:00 p.m. on" Wednesday. If intervention is granted, the United States must answer the proposed motions to dismiss "by Thursday, October 29, 2026, at 5:00 p.m." The order's background notes the suit was filed "After Alaskans had already begun casting their ballots for the November" election.

## Update (2026-10-27, order date): Gleason expedites the State's motion to dismiss; Alaska does not oppose the Democratic intervenors

Judge Sharon L. Gleason signed an [order](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.akd.80263/gov.uscourts.akd.80263.19.0_4.pdf) on 10/27/26 granting the State's motion to expedite its motion to dismiss. The United States must respond "by Thursday, October 29, 2026, at 3:00 p.m." and the State may reply by "Friday, October 30, 2026, at 12:00 p.m."

The same day, the Division of Elections [told the court](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.akd.80263/gov.uscourts.akd.80263.20.0.pdf) it "does not oppose the intervention of Alaskans for Mary" and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee, and it "takes no position on intervention by the League of Women Voters." The United States' own response to the intervention motions, due that evening at 5:00 p.m. Alaska time, was not on the RECAP docket when this update was written. Neither motion to dismiss has been ruled on.

## Update (2026-10-27, filing date): DOJ opposes the intervention motions: the State already seeks "expeditious dismissal"

The United States filed a [brief](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.akd.80263/gov.uscourts.akd.80263.26.0.pdf) on October 27, 2026 with the heading "PLAINTIFF’S OPPOSITION TO MOTIONS TO INTERVENE". It argues that the State defendants already seek what the would-be intervenors want, "expeditious dismissal," and it concludes: "Both motions should be denied." The brief calls the second Senate candidate named Daniel J. Sullivan "a sham candidate" and quotes the Wall Street Journal editorial board. No ruling on intervention is on the docket yet.

## Update (2026-10-28, publication date (AP report of the New York Times report)): Trump calls for prosecution after NYT reports Peltola campaign was involved in recruiting the second Dan Sullivan; campaign and DSCC deny it

On October 28, 2026, AP reported that President Trump "called for the criminal prosecution" of Democratic Senate candidate Mary Peltola after a New York Times report that her campaign appeared to be involved in recruiting the second Dan Sullivan. "I think they should be prosecuted," Trump said. Per AP, the Times obtained text messages between members of Peltola's campaign team, and its story rests partly on interviews with her former campaign manager, who "tried to retract her comments last week." Peltola's campaign said it "has no involvement with either Sullivan campaign," and the DSCC said it "is not involved with Dan J. Sullivan." AP reported that legal experts said the report "will have no impact on a lawsuit filed recently by the U.S. Department of Justice."

## Update (2026-10-28, order date): Court grants intervention to the Peltola campaign, the DSCC and League of Women Voters intervenors

Judge Sharon L. Gleason's ORDER ON MOTIONS TO INTERVENE, (Document 35 Filed 10/28/26; dated the 28th day of October, 2026), says the Campaign intervenors' motion to intervene "is GRANTED" (Alaskans for Mary and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee), and so is the League of Women Voters intervenors' motion. The order notes that the United States had "withdrew its opposition to the Campaign's Motion to Intervene" (at Docket 33).

## Update (2026-10-28, order date): Gleason's intervention order: Campaign in as of right, League of Women Voters permissively; both motions to dismiss accepted; DOJ response due Oct. 29

Judge Sharon L. Gleason's order (filed 10/28/26) found that the Campaign's "interests would be inadequately represented by the existing parties" and that the Campaign intervenors "are entitled to intervene as of right," citing the non-opposition of the United States and the State. For the League of Women Voters intervenors, the court said it "need not determine whether League of Women Voters are entitled to intervene as of right" because "their permissive intervention is warranted under Rule 24(b)." The United States' main argument against them was that the State already represents their interests; the order answers that the Ninth Circuit has held government representation "may not be 'identical to the individual parochial interest'" of a group, and notes the League "have advanced legal arguments in their proposed Motion to Dismiss that the State has not raised or developed." The order accepts both intervenors' motions to dismiss "as filed" and directs the United States to respond "by Thursday, October 29, 2026, at 5:00 p.m." and lets the intervenors reply "by Friday, October 30, 2026, at 2:00 p.m."  Intervention was decided on the papers: "Oral argument on the Motions to Intervene was not requested and was not necessary for the Court's determination." No motion to dismiss has been ruled on in this order.

## Update (2026-10-28, filing date): DOJ withdraws its opposition to the Peltola campaign's intervention and says it will seek expedited discovery

On 10/28/26 the United States filed a notice (Document 33) withdrawing its opposition to intervention by Alaskans for Mary and the DSCC. It gives as the reason "the New York Times reporting that Proposed Intervenor Alaskans For Mary and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee orchestrated a sprawling conspiracy to recruit and prop up a sham candidate for the purpose of deceiving Alaskan voters" (the filing's description of the Times report; the campaign and the DSCC deny involvement, per the AP update above). The notice says the United States "intends to seek expedited discovery from the Campaign regarding the reported conspiracy," including whether the Campaign sought an expedited ruling "when the Campaign may have been aware of the New York Times reporting before it was published." It "maintains its opposition" to intervention by the League of Women Voters of Alaska, Common Cause and individual voters, whom the court allowed to intervene the same day (see the order update).

## Update (2026-10-29, filing date): DOJ amends its complaint: a Supremacy Clause count and the New York Times story, with the same requested relief

On October 29, 2026 the United States filed an amended complaint (Document 43 Filed 10/29/26), citing Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 15(a)(1). The original had one count, under the National Voter Registration Act. The amended complaint adds a second, and asks for a declaration that certification would be "preempted under the Supremacy Clause." On that count it says that "certifying a result that may be impacted by the scheme would frustrate Congress’s objectives in seeking to protect the integrity of the electoral process." Its new factual material is the New York Times report: the complaint says "The New York Times has reported that operatives aligned with Peltola spent months recruiting candidates to take votes away from Senator Sullivan," while conceding that "what the New York Times reports is not always true." A new section alleges that Alaskans for Mary and the DSCC "appear to have turned to misleading this Court," and it cites Governor Mike Dunleavy's announcement that he is "directing the Department of Public Safety to investigate whether this scheme violated Alaska’s laws." The relief is unchanged in substance: the same trigger, "Senator Sullivan does not win the election," and the same hold "pending an investigation to determine whether votes cast" for the second candidate were meant for the incumbent.

## Update (2026-10-29, filing date): DOJ says its amended complaint moots all three motions to dismiss; the State calls that gamesmanship and asks for a ruling by Nov. 2

The United States filed a brief on October 29, 2026 (Document 46 Filed 10/29/26) arguing that "All three motions to dismiss (ECF Nos. 6-1, 8-1, 18) should be denied as moot," because the amended complaint supersedes the original and "None of the motions to dismiss address the United States’ claim under the Supremacy Clause." It did not answer the motions on their merits, though the Court had ordered it to respond "by 3:00 p.m. today" to the State and "by 5:00 p.m. today" to the intervenors. The State's lawyers say the United States filed the amended complaint at 12:55 p.m. and the mootness brief at 2:25 p.m. The State moved the same day (Document 53 Filed 10/29/26) for expedited consideration of a renewed motion to dismiss (Document 55 Filed 10/29/26), saying "the Court should not tolerate this gamesmanship." It asks that the United States oppose by 10 a.m. Friday, October 30 and that the Division reply by midnight, noting the United States asked for until 1:00 p.m. It asks for a decision "by Monday, November 2." The renewed motion says the amended complaint rests on the same "meritless theory that the National Voter Registration Act (NVRA)—a voter registration law—governs this case."

## Update (2026-10-29, filing date): The Campaign and DSCC, the League of Women Voters side and an amicus all file against the amended complaint

Alaskans for Mary and the DSCC moved to dismiss the amended complaint (Document 57 Filed 10/29/26), arguing that "The NVRA provides no legal basis for DOJ’s claim" and that DOJ "fails to state a claim under the Supremacy Clause." They also joined the State's motion to expedite (Document 59 Filed 10/29/26). The other intervenors, including the League of Women Voters of Alaska and Common Cause, filed their own motion to dismiss (Document 60 Filed 10/29/26), which opens: "The U.S. Department of Justice may wish to have a junkyard brawl with a partisan political campaign in the days before an election." An amicus lodged a proposed amicus brief supporting the State (Document 48-1 Filed 10/29/26) arguing that "Laches bars the federal government’s attempt to interfere with Alaska’s certification process."

## Update (2026-10-30, order date): Judge Gleason expedites three motions to dismiss the amended complaint

On October 30 Judge Sharon Gleason GRANTED expedited consideration of three motions to dismiss DOJ's amended complaint, with replies due October 31 at noon. The State's motion, filed 10/29/26, asks for a decision by November 2. The court has not ruled on dismissal.
