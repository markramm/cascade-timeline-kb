The Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights opened the investigations Friday, saying the decision was based on credible reports that several programs have offered sex or race-specific recruitment, support, and financial opportunities in potential violation of federal civil rights laws, Inside Higher Ed reports. The programs named include the NYC Men Teach Program at the City of the University of New York; the Maryland Higher Education Commission Teacher Quality and Diversity Grant Program and the EdTerps CARES Network at the University of Maryland's School of Education; the Education Tuition Assistance Program administered by the Minnesota Department of Education; and the Minnesota Aspiring Teachers of Color Scholarship Pilot Program.

Assistant Secretary for Civil Rights Kimberly Richey accused many states and universities of prioritizing "recruiting and training 'minority' candidates rather than evaluating candidates based solely on merit." According to National Center for Education Statistics data, roughly 80 percent of teachers at public schools were white.

Context: the probes extend the administration's campaign against race-conscious programs in education; see [[2025-04-03--department-of-education-dei-certification-dear-colleague]].
