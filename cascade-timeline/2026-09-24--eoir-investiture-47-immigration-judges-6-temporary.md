## Opening paragraph

On September 24, 2026, the Executive Office for Immigration Review (EOIR) swore in 47 new immigration judges and 6 temporary immigration judges at an investiture held at the Department of Justice's National Advocacy Center in Columbia, South Carolina. EOIR Acting Director Sirce E. Owen and U.S. Attorney for the District of South Carolina Bryan P. Stirling delivered remarks; Chief Immigration Judge Teresa L. Riley administered the oath of office. Attorney General Todd Blanche appointed all 53 adjudicators.

## Why This Event Matters

This is the most recent dated data point in the corpus's judicial-capture thread on EOIR hiring, which previously stopped at the May 21, 2026 swearing-in of 82 judges ("largest class in EOIR's history," per Reuters, unconfirmed independently — see [[2026-eoir-immigration-judge-firing-record]]). Combined with TRAC's August 31, 2026 finding that the administration has hired 192 new judges plus 53 temporary judges cumulatively through FY2026 ([[2026-08-31--trac-report-279-immigration-judges-fired-resigned-38-percent-turnover]]), this Sept. 24 cohort of 47+6 postdates TRAC's cutoff and is additive to that cumulative count, not included in it.

## Broader Context

Part of a sustained pattern: EOIR is simultaneously removing sitting judges (see [[2025-12-08--immigration-judge-purge-100-judges-fired]] and [[2026-08-31--trac-report-279-immigration-judges-fired-resigned-38-percent-turnover]]) and installing new appointees at a pace exceeding attrition, changing the composition of the bench that decides asylum and removal cases. TRAC's August 2026 report found only 43% of the 743 judges deciding asylum cases in FY2026 had served before Trump took office.
