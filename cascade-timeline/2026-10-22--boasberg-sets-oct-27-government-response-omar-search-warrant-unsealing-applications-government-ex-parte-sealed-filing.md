The government did not respond to the Omar search-warrant unsealing applications by the deadline the court had set. It asked for more time, and on October 22 Chief Judge James E. Boasberg granted that motion in part and denied it in part.

**What the court ordered.** The government's response, in each of three related cases, is now due October 27, 2026, and the response "may argue in part that the Court should not address the matter yet." Applicants' reply is due October 30, 2026. Any party opposing consolidation of the three cases must file by October 27, 2026. The same day the court allowed the government's supporting memorandum to be filed under seal and ex parte, and said that if it later believes a redacted version belongs on the public record, it will so order.

**What the government asked for.** Its motion sought to extend the deadlines "until after the subject search warrant has been fully executed," with a status report to follow. Its fallback was a single deadline of November 9, 2026 for all three. The court adopted neither. The reasons are in the sealed memorandum, which is not public. The motion records that the applicants oppose it. The New York Times Company's position, attached to the motion at its request, states that "the Government executed the search warrant on October 12, 2026, and subpoenas have been served on Rep. Omar's family members"; that is the Times' statement to the government, not a court finding.

**Where the record stands.** The docket entries read on the day of this pass list no order unsealing the warrant and no ruling on the applications. This entry makes no finding about purpose.

## Update (2026-10-27, filing date): Government files redacted public opposition to immediate unsealing

The United States filed a redacted public version of its consolidated memorandum opposing access to the sealed search-warrant records, due October 27 under the Court's order. The government writes that the Court should deny the Applications without prejudice. The government also writes that it does not necessarily oppose unsealing the records, with appropriate redactions, after the warrant is fully executed. No ruling on access is reflected in the filing.

## Update (2026-10-28, order date): Boasberg seals the government's opposition for now; applications consolidated

The government's Oct. 27 opposition is on the public docket in redacted form (above); it also moved to file its memorandum under seal (Dkt. 11). On 10/28/2026 Chief Judge James E. Boasberg granted the motion to seal, adding: "In the event that the Court, after review, believes that a redacted version of the Government's Memorandum should be filed on the public record, it will so order." A 10/29/2026 docket entry consolidates 26mc145 and 26mc147 into this case. Press applicants, Judicial Watch and The New York Times filed replies on 10/30/2026 (Dkt. 18, 19, 20). Read as docket text through CourtListener's API on docket 74969880; hand update, because the sourcing gate can't read docket text.

## Update (2026-11-12, order date (minute order)): Boasberg sets a Nov. 17 hearing and asks the government why more than the affidavit cannot be unsealed

On November 12, 2026, Chief Judge James E. Boasberg ordered the parties to appear for a hearing on November 17, 2026, at 11:00 a.m. on the applications to unseal. His minute order adds: "The Government shall be prepared to address why materials beyond the Affidavit itself cannot be unsealed." The docket shows no order unsealing any of the warrant materials as of this pass.
