DHS's termination of Temporary Protected Status for Ethiopia took effect on August 18, 2026, ending protection from removal and work authorization for more than 5,000 Ethiopian nationals. The department announced the termination in a Federal Register notice dated December 15, 2025, recording its determination that Ethiopia "no longer continues to meet the conditions for the designation." The termination was originally set to take effect February 13, 2026. On January 30, 2026, a judge in the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts stayed it. The August 18, 2026 effective date reflects a later implementation timeline following a Supreme Court decision affecting the case. USCIS confirmed on that date that A12 and C19 employment authorization documents issued under the Ethiopia designation are no longer valid.

This entry's core claim was independently verified beyond its original sourcing: the capture ledger carried a single tier-3 advocacy source, and the chronology and the effective date recorded here come instead from the Federal Register notice and the USCIS release, both primary. That substitution also corrected the framing. The ledger characterized the termination as coming despite no change in conditions on the ground; the administrative record contains DHS's contrary determination, and advocacy groups' dispute of that determination is a contested characterization rather than a settled fact, so it is attributed here rather than asserted.

The Ethiopia termination fits the documented pattern of country-by-country TPS wind-downs whose timing is set less by conditions abroad than by how fast litigation can be cleared. The corpus records the same sequence for Yemen ([[2026-02-13--noem-terminates-tps-yemen-deportation]]), Venezuela — where the Supreme Court stayed a lower-court block ([[2025-05-19--scotus-stays-noem-v-ntpsa-venezuelan-tps-termination]]) before the Ninth Circuit held the termination unlawful ([[2026-01-28--ninth-circuit-affirms-tps-venezuela-unlawful]]) — and Haiti and Syria, where the Court's decision in Mullin v. Doe barred judicial review of TPS terminations outright ([[2026-06-25--scotus-mullin-v-doe-ends-haiti-syria-tps-judicial-review-barred]]), narrowing the district-court stay that had held Ethiopia's termination for six months.
