PJM Interconnection asked the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission on Monday (the filing date; the report ran Tuesday) to approve a plan that PJM says would "give states transparent access to the costs of transmission improvements driven by data centers, as directed by FERC, empowering states to bill Large Loads for those costs," according to a Daily Caller News Foundation report republished by BizPac Review. Large Loads are customers drawing 50 megawatts or more at one site. The plan would also halve the expected review period for proposed power plants and big electricity users, and PJM asked FERC to rule within 120 days. A PJM spokesman said PJM "does not control how those costs are allocated"; utilities that own the power lines propose how to split up and recover them. The filing says data centers account for 30 of the 32 gigawatts of peak demand growth PJM's 2025 forecast projects between 2024 and 2030.

PJM sent FERC a separate proposal the same Monday that would require data centers to stay on the grid when disturbances hit rather than switching over to backup power. On July 22, nearly 3.9 gigawatts of data centers dropped off utility power at once, which PJM said was the biggest such event in its history. PJM asked FERC to rule on the ride-through proposal by Jan. 15, 2027.
