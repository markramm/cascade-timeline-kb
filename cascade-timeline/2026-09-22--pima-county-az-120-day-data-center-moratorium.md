The Pima County Board of Supervisors voted 3-2 on September 22, 2026 to impose a 120-day moratorium on new data center development in unincorporated Pima County, under Resolution 2026-56. The Board's meeting summary records that Supervisor Heinz moved, and Supervisor Cano seconded, approval of the resolution as amended; Supervisors Steve Christy (District 4) and Rex Scott (District 1) voted no. The Board first amended Section B 4-1 (Heinz moved, Scott seconded; Christy no), so the moratorium applies to future development of new data centers "where permits have not been submitted for the site" — the agenda-packet draft had said "where permits have not been issued". The exemption therefore reaches projects that had submitted permits for site improvements — grading, paving, flood control, construction — before the vote, not only those that had been issued. KJZZ reported on October 9 that Project Gravel, a proposed 160-acre data center in Sahuarita, is currently exempt "because the application came prior to the pause"; the same coverage notes the other project in the pipeline, Project Blue, has already begun construction (AZPM). The board took up the resolution after an executive session of more than an hour with County Attorney Laura Conover.

The stated purpose is to give county staff time to draft permanent zoning regulations for large-scale data centers, addressing water consumption in an already water-stressed desert region, energy demand and reliability, air pollution, noise, and waste-heat dispersion; staff hopes to bring the full policy to the board for approval by December 10, 2026. No specific hyperscaler project was named as the proximate cause in the reporting reviewed. Pima County's action follows Arizona's legislature-imposed three-year pause on state data-center tax incentives and lands the same week as parallel county-level moratoriums elsewhere — this timeline separately documents Marshall County, Iowa's six-month moratorium adopted the following day ([[2026-09-23--marshall-county-ia-six-month-data-center-moratorium-unanimous-vote]]) and St. Joseph County, Indiana's vote the same day to begin a data-center ban process ([[2026-09-22--st-joseph-county-in-council-votes-6-3-to-begin-data-center-ban-process]]) — part of a broader 2026 wave of county governments moving to pause hyperscale development pending zoning review.

Corrected 2026-10-14: the entry said the moratorium exempted projects with permits "in hand" (issued); the Board amended the text to "submitted". It also named Chair Allen as moving and Heinz as seconding; the meeting summary records Heinz moving and Cano seconding.
