Common Cause, joined by the ACLU, Protect Democracy, and other groups, sued on April 21, 2026 (per the ACLU's own press release, the entry's cited source) to block a DOJ national voter surveillance and purge database that the release describes as still being compiled rather than completed — "is compiling that information into a single record system" — the first federal consolidation of confidential voter registration data from all 50 states and DC into a single record system, now cross-referenced against SAVE, an immigration-verification database with a documented history of false positives. The lawsuit argues the cross-reference creates substantial risk of mass wrongful purges of eligible voters. The threat is time-critical: the National Voter Registration Act's 90-day blackout on systematic roll purges before a federal election begins August 5, 2026, ahead of the November midterms — while Republicans are simultaneously pushing the Supreme Court to gut that same blackout protection via an Arizona case (*RNC v. Mi Familia Vota*) that, per OPB, is "not expected until next year."

This is a legal-challenge stage of the database-building campaign the timeline first documented in September 2025 ([[2025-09-09--doj-demands-voter-data-centralized-database]]), when DOJ began demanding voter files from more than 30 states. Roughly seven months of state-by-state data collection and litigation (including DOJ's subsequent suits against holdout states) preceded this suit, which — per the ACLU's press release — targets a database the agency itself describes as still being compiled rather than finished. DOJ's subsequent escalation to criminal threats against election officials ([[2026-07-07--doj-threatens-criminal-prosecution-election-officials-50-states]]) and Trump's EAC commissioner firings ([[2026-07-10--trump-fires-eac-commissioners-hicks-hovland-after-humphreys-executor]]) extended the same pressure campaign against election administration in the following months.
