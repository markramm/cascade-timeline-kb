Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act — the authority underlying PRISM downstream
collection — **expired at midnight on Friday, June 12, 2026**, after Congress failed to enact a full
reauthorization.

**The sequence.** RISAA (April 2024, **Pub. L. No. 118-49**) had reauthorized Section 702 through
April 2026 and expanded the "electronic communication service provider" definition to reach data
centers and cloud storage. As the April 2026 deadline approached, Congress passed a **~10-day stopgap
extension (~April 17, 2026)**, then a second **~45-day extension** in late April. Neither produced a
full renewal. Senate action stalled on **June 5**; Chairman **Grassley** urged passage from the floor
on **June 8**, four days out; the House **rejected a last-ditch extension on June 11**; the authority
lapsed the following day.

**What reporting attributes the collapse to.** Contemporaneous accounts tie the Senate stall to a
fight over the **Bill Pulte** nomination rather than to disagreement over 702's substance or its
civil-liberties provisions. That is what reporting says about the *cause*; this entry records it as
such and asserts no independent causal claim of its own.

**Status as of 2026-08-31: still lapsed.** No reauthorization was located between the June 12 lapse
and the end of an early-to-mid-August search window. **A dateline caution applies**: Grassley's
Senate Judiciary release re-indexed on **August 6, 2026** is a re-dated version of the **June 8**
floor speech, not a new statement — an RSS index date read as a remarks date would manufacture a
false "still contested in August" picture.

**What is NOT established here, and matters.** No source fetched in full text addresses **what
operationally changed at the moment of lapse** — the status of existing FISA Court-approved
certifications, or whether PRISM collection stopped, continued under a wind-down, or was re-sourced
to other authority. **A statute expiring is not the same event as collection ceasing**, and this
entry documents only the first. Anyone citing this for the second needs a separate source.
