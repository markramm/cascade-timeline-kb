Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, the authority underlying PRISM downstream
collection, reached its statutory repeal date of **June 12, 2026**, after Congress failed to enact a
reauthorization. Public Law 119-87 had set that date; the statute names a date, and the sources read
give no clock time (CNBC described the earlier April 30 date as the end of that day).

**The sequence.** The original April 2026 deadline was extended twice. A 10-day patch passed April 17
and ran to April 30 (CNBC); Public Law 119-87, approved April 30, then moved the repeal date to June 12
(45-day extension, CNBC). On June 11 the House failed, 198-218, to pass a three-week extension (ABC), a
vote needing two-thirds. The authority reached its repeal date the next day. So the two stopgaps
passed; it was a third that failed, in the House.

**What reporting attributes the collapse to.** Contemporaneous accounts tie the failure to House
Democrats' refusal to back an extension while Bill Pulte stood as Trump's choice for acting Director of
National Intelligence (CNBC), rather than to disagreement over 702's substance. That is what reporting
says about the *cause*; this entry records it as such and asserts no independent causal claim of its own.

**Status as of 2026-08-31: still lapsed.** No reauthorization was located between the June 12 lapse
and the end of an early-to-mid-August search window. **A dateline caution applies**: a Senate Judiciary
release re-indexed on August 6, 2026 is a re-dated version of Chairman Grassley's June 8 floor remarks,
not a new statement (see the first source's note).

**What the lapse did and did not stop.** A statute reaching its repeal date is not the same event as
collection ceasing. Collection under existing FISA Court certifications is reported to be authorized to
continue until March 17, 2027, because the law grandfathers those certifications and the directives under
them (Brennan Center, an advocacy source). We have not read the FISA Court certifications or the
transition provision itself, and found no source reporting that PRISM collection stopped.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

The earlier text said two stopgaps failed; two passed and a third failed. It placed the failure in the
Senate and called Pulte a nominee; the failed vote was the House's, and Pulte was Trump's choice for
acting DNI. It said the authority expired at midnight on June 12; the record gives the June 12 repeal
date and no hour. See the corrections field.
