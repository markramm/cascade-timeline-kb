Kyle Shideler, Director for Homeland Security and Counterterrorism at the Center for Security Policy (CSP) — a Frank Gaffney-founded think tank the Southern Poverty Law Center has designated an anti-Muslim hate group — served as the Department of Justice's expert witness on "antifa" in the Prairieland material-support-for-terrorism prosecution ([[2026-02-23--prairieland-trial-begins-fort-worth]]). The Intercept reported on March 9, 2026 that CSP's involvement predated the trial: Shideler authored a September 2025 article, "How to Dismantle Far-Left Extremist Networks: A Roadmap for the Trump Administration," and conferred with prosecutors in October 2025 — a month before the original indictment — before testifying at trial that he had supplied prosecutors the definitional language for "antifa" that appeared in the indictment itself.

At trial, defense attorneys challenged Shideler's qualifications on cross-examination and were overruled by Judge Mark Pittman. The credentials record established at trial: Shideler holds a bachelor's degree (in English) and has never held a government or academic research position; his analytical work has not been peer-reviewed, does not employ verifiable/empirical data methods, and has not been presented to a critical academic audience. Anne Speckhard, director of the International Center for the Study of Violent Extremism, filed a report with the court stating Shideler "lacks the academic training, professional credentialing, and empirical research background required for expertise in criminology, extremism studies, social-movement analysis."

Shideler's pre-CSP career ran through pro-Israel advocacy and Islamism-focused organizations rather than counterterrorism scholarship or government service: he worked at StandWithUs (the Los Angeles pro-Israel group, managing its database of "extremist groups and individuals"), then at the Endowment for Middle East Truth (EMET, self-described as operating from "an unabashedly pro-America and pro-Israel stance") studying the Muslim Brotherhood, before joining CSP in 2014. This career trajectory is independently corroborated by Shideler's own author bio at emetonline.org and CSP's public biography, which confirms the bachelor's-only academic credential and the absence of any government or peer-reviewed academic post — both consistent with the Klippenstein report's framing, though Klippenstein is not the source of record here; each element above is drawn from tier-1 reporting (The Intercept, Texas Observer, In These Times/Type Investigations) or primary organizational bios.

In its post-verdict statement, the Department of Justice cited Shideler's testimony and the "North Texas Antifa Cell" language he supplied nearly a dozen times. Attorney General Pam Bondi stated "Antifa is a domestic terrorist organization" and that the Prairieland case marked the start of an effort to "systematically dismantle" the group, explicitly invoking Trump's September 2025 executive order designating Antifa a Domestic Terrorist Organization. The Prairieland convictions and sentencing ([[2026-06-23--prairieland-defendants-sentenced-30-100-years-antifa-terrorism]]) thus function as the DOJ's founding precedent for prosecuting the antifa designation — built substantially on testimony from a think-tank analyst with no government or academic credential, who had a documented hand in drafting the charging language he then validated as an expert witness. This is a textbook capture-structure instance: a movement-advocacy think tank (Islamophobia-focused, itself an SPLC-designated hate group) supplies both the definitional predicate for a domestic-terrorism prosecution and the "expert" testimony validating it, with no independent academic or governmental check in the loop.
