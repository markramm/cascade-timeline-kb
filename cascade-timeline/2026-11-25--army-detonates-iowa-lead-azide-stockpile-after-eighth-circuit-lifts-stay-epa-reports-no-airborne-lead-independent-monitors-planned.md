The Army detonated its stockpile of degraded lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant on November 25. The Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club filed a petition for review on November 19, 2026, challenging an EPA permit covering 68 drums of lead azide. The Eighth Circuit denied the emergency motion for stay and lifted its administrative stay, in a published order.

The readings so far come from the agencies running the operation. EPA said it had collected over 1,200 samples before the blast. EPA said pre-detonation sampling detected no lead in the air. EPA's administrator said its monitors had not detected lead particles. These are EPA's statements, and this entry records no independent post-blast reading.

The University of Iowa team said it would use air quality sensors, topsoil samples, and wipes of dusty surfaces. The team said it would share findings with the community as soon as it could. One of its researchers said having more independent data is always better. The University of Nebraska-Lincoln is coordinating a community science initiative on soil samples. Missouri state agencies activated their own monitoring team on Nov. 20. The plaintiffs said their court declarations question the Army's and EPA's claims that all alternatives were given appropriate consideration.
