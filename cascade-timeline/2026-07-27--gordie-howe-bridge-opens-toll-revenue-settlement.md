## Opening paragraph

The Gordie Howe International Bridge, the publicly-funded Detroit-Windsor crossing that Trump had threatened to block five months earlier following a $1 million donation from Ambassador Bridge owner Matthew Moroun to MAGA Inc., opened to traffic on July 27, 2026, following a ceremonial inauguration on July 24. The opening followed a toll-revenue settlement announced July 10, 2026, that split first-15-years toll profits between Canada and a U.S. "regional economic development fund."

## What Happened / Key Facts

Major construction on the bridge finished in February 2026 — the same month Trump publicly threatened to block its opening after Moroun's donation and a same-day meeting with Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick (see [[2026-02-20--ambassador-bridge-owner-1m-maga-donation-gordie-howe-attack]]). Canada funded the bridge's entire CA$6.4 billion construction cost. The dispute that emerged from the February 2026 episode was addressed via a new toll-governance arrangement **announced** July 10, 2026 (confirmed 2026-09-01 against a primary WDBA/Housing Infrastructure and Communities Canada news release, dateline "Windsor, Ontario, July 10, 2026" — this is the announcement date, not a signing, effective, or press-pickup date, and no other date-kind for the settlement has been located): Canada and the U.S. agreed to "cooperative measures focused on toll governance and transparency," including the establishment of a **15-year economic development fund tied to a portion of profits from bridge operations**, plus a U.S. concurrence role on future "non-market related toll changes." **The specific "Canada retains half" 50/50 split is not stated in this primary document** — it appears to originate in secondary (tier-3 Wikipedia) reporting not independently verified this pass; treat it as unconfirmed pending a document that states an exact split. The bridge opened to traffic at noon local time on July 27, 2026.

## Why This Event Matters

The settlement structure — directing a share of Canadian-funded bridge toll revenue to a U.S. fund rather than blocking the bridge outright — is a resolution that neither fully vindicates Trump's threatened action nor leaves the Moroun family's competing monopoly interest unaddressed. It closes the acute phase of the February 2026 episode but does not resolve the underlying structural conflict: Ambassador Bridge continues to compete directly with a now-operational public alternative, and Matthew Moroun's federal political spending continued through the settlement period — a confirmed $150,000 contribution to the "Grow the Majority" joint fundraising committee, transaction-dated June 23, 2026, roughly five weeks before this opening (FEC bulk data, verified 2026-09-01; see [[detroit-international-bridge-company]] for a correction to a prior pass's larger "$300,000 to NRCC" figure, which FEC records do not support as stated).

## Broader Context

This event closes the specific "will Trump block the bridge" question raised by the February 2026 attack, but the underlying monopoly-competition dynamic between the Ambassador Bridge and the Gordie Howe Bridge is now a live, ongoing commercial and political relationship rather than a one-off dispute — consistent with Mark's 2026-08-06 flag that this is "an ongoing Trumpland issue, not a one-off."
