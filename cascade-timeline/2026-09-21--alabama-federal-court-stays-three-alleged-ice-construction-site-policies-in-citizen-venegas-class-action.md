Chief Judge Jeffrey Beaverstock of the Southern District of Alabama granted in part the preliminary-injunction motion of Leonardo Garcia Venegas, a construction worker who alleges he is a U.S. citizen wrongly detained by immigration agents. The court ruled that the challenged DHS policies "will be temporarily stayed" under the Administrative Procedure Act, writing that Venegas "is likely to succeed on the merits." The policies, as the order describes them, are a warrantless-entry policy for construction sites, a preemptive-detention policy without particularized suspicion, and a continued-detention policy after a worker shows evidence of citizenship or lawful presence. A companion order the same day gave "conditional certification" to a class.

The federal defendants do not concede the policies exist. Their later motion to reconsider or clarify says "The United States denies that these purported policies exist" and that the order "functions as a de facto injunction". That motion is pending on the docket, with Venegas's opposition filed in early November.

This is a preliminary ruling on a stay motion in one district, not a merits judgment, and whether the policies exist remains contested.
