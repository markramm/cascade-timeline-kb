The House failed on June 11, 2026, 198-218, to pass a three-week Section 702 extension (ABC), well short of the two-thirds needed under suspension. Section 702's repeal date, as set by Public Law 119-87, was June 12, 2026, so the authority lapsed the next day, not at midnight after the vote. The statute states a date, not an hour; CNBC described the previous date, April 30, as the end of that day. NBC reported it as the first time Congress had let Section 702 lapse since the law passed in 2008.

Reporting attributes the failure to House Democrats, who opposed any short-term extension while Bill Pulte, the Federal Housing Finance Agency director, served as Trump's choice for acting Director of National Intelligence (CNBC; Democrats' demand was that Trump drop him). That attribution is the reporters' account of the Democrats' stated position, not an independent causal finding here. The same day as the vote, Trump said he would nominate Manhattan U.S. Attorney Jay Clayton, a former SEC chair, as permanent DNI, with a Senate intelligence committee confirmation hearing planned for June 17 (CNBC, published June 11).

**What the lapse did and did not stop.** Under the law's grandfathering of FISA Court certifications, collection under existing certifications is reported to continue until March 17, 2027 (Brennan Center, an advocacy source; NBC quotes the same point). The date of the statute's repeal is not the date collection ended.

Two readings of ours, not found in the sources. **First: the succession/loyalty-capture vector.** Pulte as acting DNI would sit atop the intelligence community; we read this as an instance of the loyalty-over-expertise succession pattern this timeline tracks. CNBC reports that Pulte used his FHFA post to attack several of the president's opponents; the entries on that record are ([[2025-08-15--pulte-sends-doj-criminal-referral-of-fed-governor-cook-over-2021-mortgage-documents]], [[2025-08-20--pulte-mortgage-fraud-campaign-against-democrats]], [[2025-12-04--gao-accepts-senate-democrats-request-to-review-fhfa-director-pulte-mortgage-fraud-referrals]]); we read his installation as the "capture the clearinghouse" move, in the same escalation arc as Ratcliffe's retraction of 19 CIA intelligence products ([[2026-02-20--cia-ratcliffe-retracts-19-intelligence-products-political-independence]]) and Gabbard's firing of career analysts who contradicted White House positions ([[2025-05-14--gabbard-fires-venezuela-analysts]]). **Second: surveillance paradox as counter-pattern.** Democrats withholding 702 votes as leverage is unusual; our reading is that it is a case where opposing the DNI appointment produced a lapse in a surveillance authority, as a side effect and not as a stated aim. It cuts against the usual "Democrats acquiesce on surveillance" pattern documented across the FISA arc from [[1978-10-25--fisa-executive-power-statute-bargain]] through [[2008-07-09--fisa-amendments-act-passed-telecom-immunity]] to [[2026-04-29--congress-passes-fisa-702-45-day-extension-short-term]]. That April 45-day extension had bought Congress until mid-June specifically for the Patel-firing bargaining dynamics ([[2026-04-28--bloomberg-government-fire-patel-fisa-reauth-bargaining-chip]]); the substitution of Pulte-appointment leverage for Patel-firing leverage is the same Democrats-withhold-702-votes tactic, now applied to a DNI appointment rather than an FBI director. Supporters of reauthorization warned of operational harm (NBC reports Rep. Don Bacon saying expiration would be dangerous); critics of the program said collection could continue under existing certifications, and the Brennan Center says it can until March 2027. We have not found a source reporting that collection stopped.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

The earlier text had the program lapsing at midnight after the June 11 vote, put the Clayton announcement on June 12, said the lapse had immediate operational consequences, and called Pulte a political-retribution operative. The record read supports: lapse on the June 12 repeal date (Public Law 119-87), the Clayton announcement on June 11, collection continuing under existing certifications, and Pulte's use of his FHFA post against opponents as CNBC's characterisation.
