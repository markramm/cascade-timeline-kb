Detainees at the Folkston D. Ray James ICE Processing Center in southern Georgia began a hunger strike on October 6, a spokesperson for the Campaign to Shut Down FIPC told the local broadcaster WSAV, per Mediazona; the Guardian says the strike was launched this week. The Guardian reports that advocates estimate at least 73 detained immigrants are participating; the sentence names no advocate, and the figure is an estimate, not a count by ICE, GEO Group or the facility. The Folkston facility, a former state prison, is run by the private prison company GEO Group under a contract with ICE.

According to the Guardian, some of the men participating released a video and an open letter, requesting they be released while they continue pursuing their asylum and immigration cases; the letter was signed this summer by 45 detained immigrants. Two Russian asylum seekers detained there also released a document outlining what they claim to be "violations" inside, including allegations of substandard medical care, threats by GEO Group guards, moldy food and a lack of due process; these are allegations. GEO Group referred all questions to ICE. The Department of Homeland Security did not respond to detailed questions about the allegations or requests for comment, and GEO Group and ICE did not respond to questions about these allegations.

Mediazona, not the Guardian, reports that ICE's own inspectors rated the facility "good" after their first visit in June 2026, found it compliant with 22 of 24 detention standards, interviewed 28 detainees, and that the facility had been given four weeks' notice of the inspection. The inspection report itself has not been read.
