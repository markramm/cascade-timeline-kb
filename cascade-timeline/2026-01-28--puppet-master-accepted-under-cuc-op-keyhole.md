## Opening

The Operation Puppet Master case-opening report closes with one sentence that the Minnesota
surveillance coverage has almost entirely passed over:

> **"This case has also been accepted under CUC Op Keyhole."**

Verified directly in our archived copy of the primary document (Doc. 212-1, text layer, not
OCR). The same page records that **AUSA Kristian Weir** accepted the investigation for federal
prosecution.

If Keyhole is what HSI's own terminology says it is, **Puppet Master is not the program. It is one
investigation inside a program.**

## What "CUC" means

"CUC" is ICE terminology for **Certified Undercover Operation** — not a case, but a formally
administered framework through which individual investigations are authorized, funded, equipped
and managed.

ICE's Undercover Operations Handbook (April 14, 2008, public) describes what a CUC can carry:
undercover identities, covert communications, leased surveillance vehicles, recordings, bank
accounts, business credit cards, and other technical and operational resources maintained
specifically for the operation. Certification requires an Undercover Review Committee and
higher-level approval. Individual investigations can be *placed within* a CUC and draw on its
authorization and resources.

So the sentence is not decorative. It states that Puppet Master was slotted into a pre-existing,
certified, resourced structure — one whose name, scope, authorization date, and other member
investigations are all undisclosed.

## Why this is the "much bigger" question

**Keyhole's boundaries are unknown, and that is the finding.** DHS has not publicly confirmed
Keyhole's relationship to Puppet Master or Whipple Shield, and no public ICE or HSI document
explains its mission. Nothing disclosed so far establishes that Keyhole was a national political
surveillance operation — and that must be stated plainly rather than implied.

But three things make the undisclosed boundary consequential rather than merely curious:

1. **A certified operation is by construction larger than any one investigation inside it.** That
   is what certification is for. If Puppet Master was accepted *under* Keyhole, Keyhole predates
   it and was built to hold more than it.
2. **Undercover activity already crossed state lines.** The Ann Arbor church operation
   ([[2026-04-08--hsi-undercover-operation-zion-lutheran-church-ann-arbor]]) shows the Minnesota
   investigation tasking a Michigan field office. Whether that ran on Keyhole's authorization is
   not established — but the question is now unavoidable.
3. **The undercover presence PREDATES Puppet Master.** HSI records show undercover monitoring
   before the case formally opened on January 28. At a **publicly advertised ICEWatch training on
   December 3, 2025**, an undercover agent reported that participants were being taught to
   document ICE activity, notify others of agents' presence, and lawfully observe enforcement.
   An agent later volunteered for an "action" team involved in planning protest activity.

   Point 3 is the structural one. If agents were already inside these groups in December, the
   January 28 case opening did not *start* the surveillance — it gave existing surveillance a
   case number. And the thing that existed before the case number is Keyhole.

## The predicate, in the government's own words

The case-opening report states its purpose without euphemism. It is not opened to investigate a
crime. It is opened:

> "to document the integration of Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) Undercover Agents (UCAs)
> into groups, organizations, or other gatherings identified by intelligence information and other
> sources as potentially threatening to law enforcement"

Note the standard: groups **identified by intelligence information** as **potentially threatening**.
Not groups suspected of a crime.

The synopsis dates the predicate to "Since November 28, 2026" — an evident typo for 2025, but it
places the origin **before Renee Good was killed on January 7, 2026**, which cuts against the
tidy four-days-after-Pretti framing (including in our own earlier entries). The Minneapolis
killings accelerated something already running.

## The administrative trail that should exist

Certified Undercover Operations are designed to leave records. The handbook describes formal
applications, certification memoranda, and periodic review. ICE's **Office of Professional
Responsibility said as recently as May 2026 that it conducts biennial financial audits of the
agency's Certified Undercover Operations.**

That matters for what can be asked for: Keyhole should not be an ephemeral codename. There should
be an application, a certification decision, an authorization scope, financial audit records, and
a list of investigations accepted under it.

Defense attorney **Kevin Riach** has specifically asked the government to produce additional
records concerning Keyhole after finding the reference in the case-opening report.

## What we do NOT know — state this before publishing

- What Keyhole authorized, when it was certified, and by whom
- What other investigations were accepted under it, in Minnesota or elsewhere
- Whether the Ann Arbor operation, the Clearview searches, or the church recordings ran on
  Keyhole's authorization
- Whether Keyhole is regional or national
- Whether the "action team" volunteering constitutes inducement — the records do not establish it

Every one of those is a question the documents raise and do not answer. The honest claim is that
the named operations sit inside an unnamed structure, not that the structure is nationwide.
