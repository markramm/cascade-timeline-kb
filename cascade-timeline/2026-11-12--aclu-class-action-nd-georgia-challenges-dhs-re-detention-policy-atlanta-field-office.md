A class action filed on Nov. 12, 2026 in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia (1:26-cv-06895) challenges what the plaintiffs say is an ICE policy that lets agents detain immigrants again after they have been released, even when nothing significant has changed in their cases. ACLU organizations in Georgia, North Carolina and South Carolina represent the plaintiffs, and the proposed class covers the states under ICE's Atlanta field office. The suit says DHS reversed its earlier practice in May 2025; under that practice, according to ACLU of Georgia staff attorney Raneem Ashrawi, officials had to show a change in circumstances before re-detaining someone. It argues the policy violates the Fourth Amendment and challenges it under the Administrative Procedure Act, naming DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, acting ICE Director David Venturella and acting Atlanta Field Office Director Kristen Sullivan as defendants. The complaint also alleges that a woman suffered a miscarriage during her re-arrest and re-detention in South Carolina. DHS did not respond to a request for comment.

The complaint cites a California federal court that found the policy likely violated federal law and temporarily stayed its use within the San Francisco ICE field office's jurisdiction. The Georgia suit seeks the same relief across the Atlanta field office's jurisdiction.
