On **June 15, 2026**, the Supreme Court agreed to hear *Genalo v. Black*, No. 25-886, in which the Trump administration challenges a Second Circuit ruling requiring bond hearings for noncitizens held for prolonged periods. The case involves two green card holders detained for seven months and nearly two years without a hearing.

The case will not be decided: the Court dismissed it on September 11, 2026, under Rule 46, on a joint stipulation filed by the petitioners; it had been set to be argued on October 13, 2026. On Sept. 21, 2026 the government filed a new petition for certiorari before judgment, *Genalo v. D.C.*, No. 26-379.

The law at issue requires mandatory detention for noncitizens convicted of a list of crimes, a different question from the one in the circuit split recorded in [[2026-04-28--second-circuit-rules-ice-mandatory-detention-policy-illegal]] and [[2026-05-08--eleventh-circuit-rejects-trump-no-bond-detention-policy-circuit-split]]. The case sits at the intersection of the judicial-capture and detention-pipeline lanes the timeline tracks.
