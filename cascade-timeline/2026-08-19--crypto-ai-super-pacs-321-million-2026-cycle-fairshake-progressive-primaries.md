Super PACs funded by the cryptocurrency and artificial-intelligence industries have amassed more than **$321 million** in the 2026 cycle across fourteen federal and state groups, according to The Nation's review of FEC filings. Actual spending so far runs well below the war chest: crypto groups have made roughly **$51 million** in independent expenditures this cycle, and crypto, AI, and AIPAC-linked super PACs together have spent more than **$180 million** — **$69.9 million** from crypto groups, **$52.8 million** from AI industry PACs, **$57.6 million** from AIPAC-linked groups. The overwhelming share lands in Democratic primaries: over half of the crypto money, nearly three-quarters of the AI money, and the vast majority of the AIPAC-linked money. **Fairshake**'s top donors remain **Coinbase**, **Ripple Labs**, and **Andreessen Horowitz**; the AI counterpart **Leading the Future** launched in January with $125 million in announced funding. For scale, the Fairshake network's 2024 total of more than $133 million set the record for independent expenditures by an industry-funded group.

The targeting is specific and legible. Crypto money spent **$10 million** opposing Illinois Lieutenant Governor **Juliana Stratton** — and lost — and **$6.5 million** supporting Representative Christian Menefee in Texas. In the March Illinois primaries, **$16.1 million** moved through three secretive super PACs including "Elect Chicago Women," funded by AIPAC's United Democracy Project. The named targets are progressive insurgents: Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan, Nida Allam in North Carolina, Robert Peters and Junaid Ahmed in Illinois, Harry Dunn in Maryland. The Nation describes the three interests as aligned in helping centrists push back against progressive challengers, and describes their messaging as deliberately obscuring the policy agendas underneath.

This is the continuation of a documented model rather than a new development. Fairshake's method was proven in 2024, when crypto interests put more than $40 million into a single Ohio Senate race to defeat **Sherrod Brown**, chair of the Senate Banking Committee and the industry's most effective critic ([[2025-10-11--crypto-pac-141-million-war-chest]]). The AI side has been running the same play ([[2026-03-12--ai-industry-super-pacs-flood-2026-midterms]], [[2026-02-27--ai-super-pac-leading-the-future-targets-pro-regulation-midterms]], [[2026-03-03--ai-tech-pac-millions-block-alex-bores-congressional-race]]), with mixed returns in Illinois ([[2026-03-18--illinois-primary-ai-super-pac-mixed-results]]) and a parallel state-level operation in Florida ([[2026-08-10--meta-forge-future-pac-buys-florida-legislative-leaders]]). What distinguishes this cycle is not novelty but convergence: two industries whose regulatory fates are decided by the same committees, financing the same primaries against the same candidates. The alignment The Nation documents is directional, not evidence of unlawful coordination, and the sources support "aligned" rather than "coordinated."
