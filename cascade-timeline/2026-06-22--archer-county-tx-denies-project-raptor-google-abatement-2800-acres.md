The Archer County Commissioners Court voted unanimously on June 22, 2026 to deny a tax abatement request for Project Raptor, a Google data center development spanning roughly 2,800 acres along Three Way Road. The request had been submitted June 3 by Dynamo Ventures LLC, the entity fronting the project; more than 30 residents packed the Commissioners Court to oppose the incentive. County Judge Randy Jackson said commissioners wanted to see what regulations and incentives might come from the Texas Legislature before considering any reduction in local tax revenue. The denial does not stop the project — it removes the subsidy, not the permission.

The ledger captured this as an "apparent" Google project on a single tier-3 source; independent local reporting confirms the Google connection directly and supplies the project name and acreage. The special-purpose-entity pattern is the recurring structure worth noting: hyperscalers approach counties through shell developers like Dynamo Ventures, so the abatement request arrives without the name of the company that will actually own the load, and residents must reverse-engineer who they are subsidizing. Texas has become the densest theater for this fight — Leon County rejected a $34B Crusoe abatement (2026-07-14--leon-county-tx-rejects-crusoe-34b-tax-abatement), Guadalupe County residents sued over Cloudburst (2026-07-22--guadalupe-county-tx-residents-sue-cloudburst-tax-abatement), Bell County residents moved against Meta abatements (2026-07-07--bell-county-tx-residents-demand-end-meta-data-center-abatements), and Governor Abbott subsequently froze grid connections pending an ERCOT audit (2026-08-03--abbott-freezes-texas-data-center-grid-connections-ercot-audit). Judge Jackson's stated reason — wait for the Legislature — is the tell that county officials understand they are negotiating without the information or leverage the counterparty has.
