More than 1.2 million Texas voter registrations — roughly 6% of the state's approximately 19 million registered voters — are on the state's "suspense" list heading into the November 2026 midterms, with over 150,000 in Bexar County alone. Counties place a registration on suspense when they cannot confirm a current address, typically after election mail is returned undeliverable or other information suggests the voter moved. Voters have until October 5 to update their registration; those still on suspense at the polls can generally vote after confirming their address has not changed.

The accurate framing matters more than the number, and Votebeat states it directly: suspense-list voters have not been removed from the rolls and are not ineligible, and a list of this size is not atypical for Texas before an election. This entry records the count and its context rather than a purge, because the structural question is different from the DOJ voter-database campaign it sits beside ([[2026-08-06--aclu-doj-voter-database-12-states-complied-5-suits-dismissed]], [[2026-07-15--aclu-sues-doj-national-voter-database-nvra-blackout-deadline]]) — Texas is one of the twelve states that voluntarily complied with the DOJ's data demands. What suspense lists supply is administrative friction at scale: an extra step imposed on 1.2 million people in a specific window, distributed by whose mail gets returned, which correlates with moving frequency and therefore with renters and lower-income voters. Friction of that kind requires no purge to have an effect and no intent to be measurable, which is why the count belongs in the record whether or not anyone engineered it.
