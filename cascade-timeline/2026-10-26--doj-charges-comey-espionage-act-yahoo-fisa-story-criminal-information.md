The Justice Department brought two criminal counts against former FBI Director James Comey, unsealed on October 26, over an October 2016 New York Times story. The charges came by criminal information rather than a grand jury indictment. Prior investigations of the same conduct closed without any charges filed. AP describes the case as among the department's politically charged investigative actions before the midterms. These are charges, not findings.

## Update (2026-10-23, filing date (government's opposition brief)): The information does not name the article; the government's brief does

The information itself, filed Oct. 5, says only that a co-conspirator was directed to disclose national defense information to a reporter for a national news publication. It names no article. The government's Oct. 23 opposition to Comey's motion to dismiss supplies the account the information leaves out: that an Oct. 5, 2016 New York Times article about Yahoo's role in U.S. email surveillance contained first-time disclosures of classified information. Comey's motion argues that Section 19 of the Internal Security Act extends the limitations period to ten years only for grand jury indictments. The government argues that the ten-year period can be satisfied by an information.
