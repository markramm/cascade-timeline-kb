Between **September 22, 2025** and **March 30, 2026**, four ICE detainees died at the **GEO Group-operated Adelanto ICE Processing Center** in San Bernardino County, California, making it the highest single-facility death cluster in the 2025–2026 period at a confirmed-operator facility and — per the **California Department of Justice's May 2026 state-inspection report** — state's **highest ICE-detention death toll since California began inspecting the centers seven years ago**: six people died in California immigration detention centers between September 2025 and March 2026, four of them at Adelanto (CalMatters, May 15, 2026). The four decedents at Adelanto are: **Ismael Ayala-Uribe** (39, Mexican / DACA, September 22, 2025); **Gabriel Garcia-Aviles** (56, Mexican, October 23, 2025); **Alberto Gutierrez Reyes** (48, Mexican, February 27, 2026); and **Jose Guadalupe Ramos-Solano** (Mexican, died March 25, 2026; March 30 is the date ICE's release was updated). The Adelanto facility — GEO-owned, ICE-contracted under a December 19, 2019 agreement with an option exercised October 2024 running through December 2029 at approximately $85M/year — accounts for **4 of the 12 confirmed deaths at GEO-operated facilities (33% of GEO's confirmed mortality cohort) in the 16-month window January 2025 – April 28, 2026**. The cluster's documented care-collapse substrate is severe: Adelanto's medical-care capacity in 2025–2026 is documentably below what the facility delivered in 2021 — CNN reported fewer doctors serving a population of 2,000+ patients (basis unstated — average daily population vs. design capacity vs. cumulative individuals served are three different denominators, and which one CNN used is not established in this entry) in 2026 than served 79 patients in 2021. **GEO Group and DHS declined CNN's request for current Adelanto staffing numbers, meaning the actual 2026 physician count is undocumented, not merely uncited.** [CORRECTED 2026-08-28: this entry previously stated "a per-detainee physician-coverage ratio that has collapsed roughly 25-fold" as a documented finding. **Structural significance.** The Adelanto cluster is the most granular, factually defensible substrate available for the **deadliest-detention-year framing** of 2025–2026, because it ties a named-facility mortality cluster (4 deaths in 7 months) directly to a named-vendor financial expansion (GEO Group: ~700% net-profit increase 2024 → 2025; $32M → $254M; ~$520M in new or expanded annualized ICE contracts in 2025; ICE contracts ≈ half of GEO's ~$2.6B total revenue) and to a documentable per-detainee care-delivery collapse at a single named facility. The broader national context: By CalMatters' count, 48 people had died in ICE detention since the start of the Trump administration, 18 of them in 2026, as of May 15, 2026; KFF counted 33 deaths in calendar 2025; NPR (June 2026) confirms 2026 is "the deadliest year in immigration detention since DHS was founded"; the Austin Kocher tracker shows a pace of approximately **one death every six days** in 2026 (17 deaths from January 1 to April 12), and projected that ICE was "on predictable track to report 60 detention deaths by the end of the year" (Kocher, April 17, 2026). The Adelanto sub-pattern stands inside that national pattern as the cluster at which the structural mechanism (warehouse-scale buildout + private-contractor occupancy-quota financial model + documented care-delivery collapse + opacity-by-default) is most concretely traceable to specific named decedents at a specific named facility on a specific named-vendor's books.

## Update (2026-11-25, publication date): Experts say an Adelanto detainee's death might have been prevented, citing ICE's own mortality report

A report published November 25, 2026 says experts who reviewed documents on the death of a man who showed alcohol-withdrawal symptoms after his detention at Adelanto concluded that his death might have been prevented had he received more timely care. The article says the internal ICE report shows a four-hour delay in care, and even then he was taken to the hospital in a custody vehicle rather than an emergency vehicle. It also says the Chronicle received documents from ICE through a public-records request. The article calls this the second known instance of ICE admitting medical care failings at Adelanto preceding a detained person's death under the second Trump administration.
