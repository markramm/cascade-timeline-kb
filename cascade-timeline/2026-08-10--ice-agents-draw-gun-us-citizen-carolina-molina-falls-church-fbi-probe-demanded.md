On August 10, 2026, ICE agents in Falls Church, Virginia boxed in the vehicle of **Carolina Molina**, a U.S. citizen, and pointed a firearm at her head while she sat in her own car. Six members of Congress subsequently sent a formal letter demanding an immediate FBI investigation and a DOJ Civil Rights Division review of the agents' conduct, giving the Justice Department and DHS until **August 31** to respond. The signers were Virginia Representatives **Don Beyer**, **Suhas Subramanyam**, and **James Walkinshaw**, joined by three ranking members: **Robert Garcia** on House Oversight, **Bennie Thompson** on Homeland Security, and **Jamie Raskin** on Judiciary. The letter went to Attorney General **Todd Blanche**, FBI Director **Kash Patel**, DHS Secretary **Markwayne Mullin**, and Acting ICE Director **David Venturella**. The lawmakers' framing, as reported, is that agents drew a loaded gun on a citizen in her own car and then misrepresented why — the misrepresentation, not only the weapon, is what they ask investigators to examine.

The structural fact is where the demand had to be routed. Three of the four recipients are the officials whose own departments would be investigating themselves, and the ranking-member signatures indicate the minority side of the three committees with jurisdiction — which is to say the signers hold oversight portfolios without subpoena power over the agency in question. The request is therefore an ask, not a compulsion, and the August 31 deadline is one Congress cannot itself enforce. The vehicle-containment tactic Molina experienced is not novel: it has been documented as a recurring method in civil immigration enforcement ([[2026-07-11--eyes-on-ice-documents-vehicle-containment-tactic-civil-enforcement]]), and the broader surveillance and enforcement apparatus turned on citizens and protesters alike is on the record ([[2026-07-01--wapo-investigation-ice-surveillance-stack-immigrants-protesters]]).

This entry rests on a single tier-2 outlet. What is well sourced is the letter, its six signers, its four recipients, its demands, and its deadline. The underlying incident is reported through that same letter and its accompanying press coverage; no agency account, body-camera record, or Falls Church police record is on the public record as of this writing, and the agent involved has not been publicly named. Whether DOJ or FBI responds by August 31 — and whether a response is anything more than acknowledgment — is the open question.
