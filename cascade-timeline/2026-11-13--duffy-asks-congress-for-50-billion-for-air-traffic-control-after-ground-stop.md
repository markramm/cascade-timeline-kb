Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy asked Congress for emergency air traffic control money after the [[2026-11-13--faa-nationwide-ground-stop-all-airports-network-instability]]. Fox Business quotes him: "This is an emergency. I want Congress to give us emergency funding," Duffy said. "This is bipartisan. This is American. I want $50 billion."

Reuters, as carried by Devdiscourse, reports that Duffy and the White House had asked for $30.1 billion earlier on Friday. The request was for money to upgrade airports and the air traffic control system. Fox Business reports that Duffy noted the administration had already secured $12.5 billion toward modernization. Duffy described the failed equipment this way: "We have old, old switches, old routers. We use 1980s little copper lines," Duffy said.

In August 2025 AvBrief reported Duffy's words on NewsNation: "I could spend my time the next three and a half years fighting over privatization. I'm not going to do that."

On the bill, AOPA reported that the House passed a spending bill on January 22 that would fund the Department of Transportation through September 30. AOPA says the bill prohibits the use of funds to "plan, design, or implement" privatizing the ATC system. The Congressional Research Service reports that the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 (P.L. 119-75) was signed into law on February 3, 2026, with the Transportation, HUD, and Related Agencies Appropriations Act as Division D. The CRS passage read for this entry does not mention the privatization language, so this entry does not say that the enacted text carries it.
