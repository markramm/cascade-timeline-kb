NBC News reported that ICE agents are equipped with smartphones loaded with facial recognition software, enabling them to photograph anyone during street encounters and receive identity matches in real time without prior suspicion — what the article describes as "an unprecedented escalation," noting that "with the exception of air travel, Americans have not previously had their faces routinely scanned by the government." Senator Ed Markey introduced legislation that would ban ICE and CBP's use of facial recognition technology. Separately, the Brennan Center for Justice has called for the bipartisan Fourth Amendment Is Not For Sale Act, which would bar government purchase of geolocation and biometric data from commercial brokers, to be incorporated into Section 702 reauthorization legislation.

This is the field-deployment layer of the surveillance-infrastructure buildout documented the same month in the $513M ICE/CBP surveillance-spending report ([[2026-07-14--palantir-ice-surveillance-513m-immigrationos-elite]]) — the office-side data infrastructure (Palantir's ImmigrationOS/ELITE) and the street-level biometric capture tool are two components of the same enforcement architecture, developed and deployed in the same reporting window with no warrant requirement governing either.
