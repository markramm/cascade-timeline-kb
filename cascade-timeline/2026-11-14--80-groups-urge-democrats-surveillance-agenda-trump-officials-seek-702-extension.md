Mother Jones reported that 80 civil rights groups sent a letter to Democratic leaders outlining an anti-surveillance agenda for January, citing priorities that range from the 120,000 Flock cameras on US roads to electronic monitoring of anti-ICE protesters. Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act lapsed in June, and the National Security Agency can continue operating under the law until March 2027. Incoming House Intelligence Committee head Rep. Jim Himes told Republicans this week to "get this thing done" and renew the authority, joined by Attorney General Todd Blanche, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, and CIA Director John Ratcliffe, whose joint letter said President Trump asked them to extend Section 702 now because a renewal next year by the Democrat-controlled Congress would likely impose stronger warrant requirements.

The lame-duck push follows the lapse recorded in [[2026-06-12--section-702-fisa-lapses-after-reauthorization-fails]].
