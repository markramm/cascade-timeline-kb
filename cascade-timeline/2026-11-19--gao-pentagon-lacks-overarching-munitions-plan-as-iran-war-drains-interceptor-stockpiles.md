A Government Accountability Office report published late Thursday found that without an overarching munitions plan, DOD and the services "are not well-positioned to address persistently low munitions inventories" (as quoted by Stars and Stripes). Citing interviews with Pentagon officials, the report concluded that the services acquire munitions in ways that "can be reactive to global events rather than driven by total requirements," per Al-Monitor. Al-Monitor reports that the Pentagon has depleted the large majority of its THAAD interceptors, with reported estimates nearly as high as 80%; that figure is not from the GAO report itself. The earliest scheduled full contract delivery, for $35.3 billion worth of Lockheed THAAD interceptors, is not scheduled for completion until June 2032. The Army has spent about $4.5 billion on new production lines, according to the DOD inspector general, and the Pentagon gave Raytheon a $22.9 billion Tomahawk contract in August.

Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao told the Senate Armed Services Committee on Tuesday that some of the munitions the Navy has used in strikes on Iran this year were previously classified as unserviceable: "code echo, code foxtrot, which means they're expired."
