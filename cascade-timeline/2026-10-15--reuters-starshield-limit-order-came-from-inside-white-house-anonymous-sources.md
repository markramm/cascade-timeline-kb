- Report, Oct 15: the order "came from inside the White House" (Reuters, Erin Banco and Gram Slattery).
- Instruction as described by the officials: Defense Department leaders "received a message that the SpaceX Starshield service for the Ukrainian military needed to be slowed".
- Who is not named: the officials "did not specify who in the White House made the decision or who was responsible for implementing the order".
- Denial: a senior US official, unnamed, in response to a request for comment: "No such request was ever made by the president."
- Not answered: the White House "did not respond to questions about whether other administration officials might have made the request".
- Ukrainian side: a senior Ukrainian official said "Kyiv was told it was a White House decision".
- Musk, as paraphrased by Reuters: "any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military or the president".
- SpaceX: "SpaceX did not respond to a request for comment."
- Current ceiling: a Defence Ministry person said the receptors work in motion "only at speeds of up to 100 miles per hour (160 kph)".
- Fedorov, per BNO News: "removing Starshield’s speed limit entirely remained critical".
- Miami meeting: Kushner and Witkoff "struck a tough tone with Ukraine".
- Intelligence: American officials "threatened to stop providing intelligence to Kyiv about Russian missile launches".

Not established here: who in the White House gave the instruction, and why. Unverified at the original: Interfax-Ukraine, quoted second-hand by Michael Sellers, attributes the limit to SpaceX acting alone.
