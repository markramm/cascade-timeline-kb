A resident group — the Data Center Action Coalition, joined by Guadalupe County residents Kara
Finlayson, Barry Lindsey and Torrie Martin, represented in part by Save Our Springs Alliance — filed
suit **July 20, 2026** in Guadalupe County district court (state court; exact court number/cause number
not located in press coverage as of this pass — see Research Gaps) against Guadalupe County, its
Commissioners Court, County Judge Kyle Kutscher, and Commissioners Jacqueline Ott, Drew Engelke, Jim
Wolverton and Stephen Germann, each in their official capacities. The suit challenges county approvals
for **two separate data-center projects** — CloudBurst Texas and Palomino Alpha — alleging violations of
the Texas Open Meetings Act, the Texas Tax Code, and state conflict-of-interest law. Filing-date
reporting differs by a few days across outlets (July 20 per Herald-Zeitung/Community Impact/SOS
Alliance; July 22-23 per Law.com/Seguin Today's own report of the filing) — treated here as the same
filing, first reported July 20-23.

## The $500M figure — what kind of number it is

The Guadalupe County Commissioners Court approved a tiered property-tax abatement and 10-year
development agreement for **CloudBurst Texas LLC** on **April 22, 2026**, by a **3-2 vote**: Judge Kyle
Kutscher, Commissioner Drew Engelke and Commissioner Jim Wolverton voting yes; Commissioners Jacqueline
Ott and Stephen Germann voting no. **Kutscher voted in favor of this abatement and no source reviewed
reports a recusal on this specific vote** — a distinction that matters because several outlets discuss
"the judge's conflict" in the same breath as this vote, when the recusal and land-relationship
allegations attach to the *other* project, Palomino Alpha (see below).

**$500 million is the minimum capital-investment/capital-expense commitment on the buildings subject to
abatement** — not the abated tax value, not the assessed value, and not forgone county revenue. Per
Seguin Today's own report of the vote: "$500 million: capital expense and investment on buildings
subject to tax abatement." A developer-side figure reported separately claims $488 million in projected
county tax contribution over 10 years plus $4.5 million annually for roads — a different, larger
"benefits to the county" framing that should not be confused with the $500M abatement-trigger figure.
Total project investment is reported inconsistently across outlets as either $10.0 billion or $14.5
billion — not resolved by this pass. The abatement schedule itself: 90% years 1-3, declining stepwise to
40% by year 10, full taxes due from year 11. **As of 2026-09-17, neither CloudBurst Texas LLC nor
Palomino Alpha, LLC appear in the Texas Comptroller's Chapter 312 or Chapter 380 bulk abatement
registries** (`ch312-abatement.csv`, `ch380.csv`, `ch312-abatement-detail.csv`, pulled 2026-09-17 per
[[texas-comptroller-chapter-312-380-abatement-registries]]) — meaning the agreements are not yet reflected
in the state's compliance-reporting registry, whether from reporting lag or a not-yet-triggered filing
duty. This is a registry absence, not a claim that no agreement exists — the county's own April 22 vote
and its own Chapter 312.207 public notice (for the separate Palomino Alpha zone) are the primary evidence
the agreements exist.

## The conflict allegation — handle as ALLEGATION

The lawsuit alleges County Judge Kyle Kutscher has a financial conflict of interest tied to
**Palomino Alpha**, a 942.29-acre reinvestment zone (per the county's own Chapter 312.207 notice, filed
with the county clerk and dated for a hearing no sooner than 30 days after filing) in Precinct 2, ABS 147
SUR Charles Henderson — a *separate* project from CloudBurst. Plaintiffs' attorney Bobby Levinski is
quoted with three different acreage figures across outlets: "over 100 acres between he and his family"
(KENS5), ~186 acres owned by Kutscher and family under contract for sale to the developer (Community
Impact), and ~200 acres "adjacent to the project site, held since the 1930s" (centexdatacenters.org
tracker — which frames the land as merely adjacent, not under contract for sale to Palomino Alpha, a
materially different claim than the lawsuit's). **These are not reconcilable from press coverage alone,
and "alleged" is the right word for all of them pending the actual land documents.**

The lawsuit further alleges Kutscher participated in an October 2024 executive session with the
applicant regarding tax-abatement policy changes, and that despite recusing/abstaining from Palomino
Alpha votes (December 2024, February 2026, and the March 25, 2026 development-agreement vote that failed
2-2), he "lobbied behind the scenes." **This is the plaintiffs' characterization in a verified complaint,
which attests to good faith, not to accuracy** ([[feedback_party_filing_not_authoritative_for_another_docket]]).
It has not been independently verified in this pass and should not be written as established fact.

**What the public record shows, checked directly (2026-09-17):** the Guadalupe Appraisal District's free
public property search (esearch.guadalupead.org, owner-name search "Kutscher," current appraisal roll)
shows **Kyle W & Lori R Kutscher as record owners of two parcels totaling ~10.1 acres** in ABS 147 SUR
Charles Henderson (Property ID 140259, 1.1 acres, 14394 N State Hwy 123; Property ID 139579, 9.0 acres,
N State Hwy 123) — the same abstract/survey as the Palomino Alpha reinvestment zone. Other Kutscher-surname
owners hold substantially larger adjacent parcels in the same survey (Warren M Kutscher: 175.29 acres +
1.0 acre; likely a relative, relationship not established here) — which may be the source of press
figures describing "100+" or "~200" Kutscher-family acres if reporters aggregated across the surname
rather than the judge's own recorded holding. **This is a meaningful gap between the judge's own directly
recorded ~10.1 acres and the ~100-200 acre figures reported in coverage of the lawsuit** — one this pass
flags rather than resolves.

**The appraisal-district roll cannot confirm or deny "under contract for sale."** A sale contract predating
closing would not yet appear as a change of recorded owner; only a closed, recorded deed (Guadalupe County
Clerk's official records) or the private contract itself would show that. **This pass could not reach the
county clerk's deed-record search** (`roam.guadalupetx.gov`) — it is a Granicus/GovOS portal requiring
account registration, not a plain public search; the county's historical pre-1970 index
(`stage.kofilequicklinks.com`) was not checked as it is out of scope for a 2020s-era transaction. **This is
the one load-bearing document this ticket asked for and it remains unretrieved — recorded here as an open
gap requiring either account registration or a records request, not as a verified absence**, since no
search of the actual grantor/grantee index was completed (distinct from the appraisal-roll ownership check
above, which was completed and is reported as of 2026-09-17).

No source reviewed in this pass reports that a TRO was sought. Plaintiffs seek to invalidate the county's
approvals and halt implementation, per Seguin Today's July 23 report.

## Why this event matters

The specific legal theory around the reversal itself — a rejected deal that comes back for a second vote
and passes months later, absent a disclosed change in circumstances — is the same fact pattern the
timeline should watch for at other contested abatement sites, since it raises an inference of undisclosed
lobbying or inducement between the two votes rather than a routine change of commissioner mind. Note this
applies most cleanly to the CloudBurst vote (rejected in a prior vote, reversed and approved April 22);
Palomino Alpha's development agreement, by contrast, has not yet passed as of the most recent vote located
(failed 2-2, March 25, 2026).
