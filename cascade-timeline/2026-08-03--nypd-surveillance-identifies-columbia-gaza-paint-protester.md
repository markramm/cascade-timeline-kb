Documents obtained by The Intercept, published August 3, 2026, show the NYPD deployed advanced surveillance capabilities to identify a student protester who splattered paint near Columbia University during a pro-Palestine demonstration highlighting the university's ties to the Israeli military. The department used its surveillance toolset to match and identify the individual, illustrating how counterterrorism-grade infrastructure is being repurposed for political-protest policing rather than the threats it was built to address.

The case extends a documented pattern of protest-surveillance infrastructure being redirected at political speech: NYPD's Palantir counterterrorism deployment ([[2010-01-01--nypd-adopts-palantir-counterterrorism]]) and its earlier secret Clearview AI facial-recognition trial ([[2018-12-06--nypd-begins-secret-clearview-ai-facial-recognition-trial]]) built the capability base; Dataminr's real-time monitoring of Gaza-war protests for LAPD ([[2023-10-09--dataminr-monitors-gaza-protests-lapd]]) shows the same capability applied to pro-Palestine demonstrations elsewhere. The Columbia crackdowns of spring 2024 catalyzed the current expansion of NYC protest-policing resources; this case is the first documented instance of that expanded capability being used to unmask an individual protester over a single act of political expression rather than a security threat.
