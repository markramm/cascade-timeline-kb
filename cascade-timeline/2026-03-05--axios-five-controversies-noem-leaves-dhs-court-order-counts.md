Axios reported that Trump announced Thursday that he plans to tap Sen. Markwayne Mullin to replace DHS Secretary Kristi Noem.

## The five controversies Axios listed

Axios listed the controversies that led up to the DHS Secretary's departure. On the ads, Axios wrote that DHS spent millions. On the jets, it reported that DHS Secretary Noem purchased two Gulfstream G700 luxury jets and that a Boeing 737 was being leased with plans to buy it for about $70 million. At the Capitol Hill hearing, Sen. Thom Tillis said the DHS Inspector General's office has "10 different instances under Ms. Noem's leadership where they've been misled and not allowed to pursue investigations that they think are critically important."

## Court records on order violations, kept separate

Axios's list does not include court-order violations. Two court documents give counts, and they count different things.

**D. Minnesota.** In a supplemental order, Chief Judge Patrick J. Schiltz wrote that ICE violated 97 orders in 66 cases, and that Appendix B documents 113 additional orders that ICE has violated in 77 additional cases. He wrote that the Court is not aware of another occasion in the history of the United States in which a federal court has had to threaten contempt to force the government to comply with court orders.

**D.N.J.** The Declaration of Jordan Fox in that case reviewed approximately 547 immigration habeas matters and reported violations between December 5, 2025, to February 12, 2026. Its categories, which the Office counted under a broad interpretation, are six missed answer deadlines, 12 missed bond hearing deadlines, 17 post-injunction transfers, one post-injunction removal, three late releases, three instances in which ICE imposed alternatives, 10 instances of incomplete evidentiary production and four miscellaneous instances. Slate reported that the office had violated at least 56 court orders since December alone. Those categories add to 56, a Justice Department tally under its own broad interpretation and not a count by the court.
