At a Wednesday confirmation hearing to serve as general counsel to the Office of Director of National Intelligence, White House attorney Gary Lawkowski, who The Intercept reports helped craft NSPM-7, was asked whether the law allowed people to give the president a $1 million bribe in exchange for a pardon. He said it was a "complicated legal question."

Democratic senators pressed him on his role in Trump's pardon spree and the executive orders on the left and antifa ([[2025-09-25--nspm-7-countering-domestic-terrorism-and-organized-political-violence-signed]]). Sen. Mark Warner, vice chair of the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence, said: "You were just the lawyer. But so many times, 'I was just the lawyer' kind of becomes the default answer." Senate Intelligence Committee Chair Tom Cotton sought to rush Lawkowski's confirmation before the end of the year, when Democrats take over Congress.
