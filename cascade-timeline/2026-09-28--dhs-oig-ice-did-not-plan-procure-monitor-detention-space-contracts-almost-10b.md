DHS OIG's audit report, dated **September 28, 2026**, covers the detention space contracts and agreements ICE executed in fiscal years 2020 through 2023: ICE executed **46** new contracts, agreements and purchase orders, and OIG found the portfolio, valued at **almost $10 billion**, was not always managed in accordance with federal and departmental requirements.

Findings include that ICE did not document justifications for **nearly 60 percent** of the detention space requests OIG reviewed, valued at **$2.4 billion**, and that it did not adequately justify issuing **two** contracts without full and open competition. OIG made **six** recommendations.

**The audit stops where the current buildout starts.** OIG states that since it completed fieldwork, ICE "received $45 billion for detention space," and that the report does not address ICE's warehouse procurement, on which OIG began a separate review in **May 2026**. OIG also reports that the DHS Office of the Chief Procurement Officer "denied OIG back-end access to its contract management system," giving it only select data extracts, which prevented OIG from validating the data's reliability. The warehouse-purchase records are tracked in [[2026-09-24--gao-ice-no-strategic-plan-45b-detention-expansion-waste]].
