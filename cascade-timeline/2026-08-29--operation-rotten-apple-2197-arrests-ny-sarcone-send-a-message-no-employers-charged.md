A 30-day federal enforcement surge across New York State running **July 27 to August 29, 2026** — named by the government **"Operation Rotten Apple"** — produced **2,197 arrests**, a more than **40% increase over June** and the highest monthly immigration-arrest total in the state since the round-ups began. DHS Secretary **Markwayne Mullin** announced the results on September 1.

The operation's purpose was stated by the government itself, and it was not the one usually given. Speaking for the Justice Department, attorney **John Sarcone** acknowledged the raids "didn't capture '**the worst of the worst**,'" defending them instead as a way to "**send a message**" to employers.

**No employers were arrested. None face penalties for hiring undocumented workers.**

That pairing is the entry. The stated target of the message and the population actually seized are different groups: the message was addressed to employers and delivered to their employees. Where enforcement claims a deterrent rationale aimed at one party while the entire cost falls on another, the deterrent explanation does not describe what the operation did — it describes what it was for. Judged by function rather than stated purpose, this removed roughly 2,200 workers and left every hiring relationship that produced them intact.

The geography shows the same thing. The surge hit low-population northern New York hard, and the sweeps **targeted businesses in Lake Placid** — hotel and restaurant staff, roofers, and other laborers taken **during the busy tourist season**. These are the workers a resort economy runs on, arrested at its peak.

**The lawyer's own standing is contested.** Sarcone serves as Acting First Assistant United States Attorney for the Northern District of New York. On **August 21, 2026** — eight days before the operation ended — the **Second Circuit ruled he was not lawfully an Acting U.S. Attorney**. The government's public justification for a 2,197-arrest operation was voiced by an attorney a federal appeals court had just held was not lawfully holding the office he had been exercising.

This lands amid New York's 287(g) fight, which this corpus tracks: Hochul's May 2026 law limiting ICE cooperation, the Attorney General's July order terminating agreements, and Nassau and Madison counties refusing to comply.

**Sourcing note.** The 2,197 figure, the July 27–August 29 dates, the 40%-over-June increase, the Sarcone quotes, the absence of employer arrests, the Lake Placid sweeps, and the August 21 Second Circuit ruling are from New York Almanack, with the arrest total corroborated at "over 2,100" by DHS's own release and ABC7. Two things in the capture ledger are **not carried**. First, the ledger placed Sarcone's remarks in "a court proceeding"; **the reporting does not state the setting**, and the quote should not be attributed to a hearing or filing without one — the words are his, the venue is unestablished. Second, the ledger framed the operation as DHS's retaliation for Hochul's 287(g) directive; **the Almanack account does not make that connection**, and while DHS's own release emphasizes sanctuary policies, causation between the directive and this operation is the government's framing rather than a documented fact.
