A Senate Banking Committee minority report, released ahead of acting CFPB Director Russell Vought's oversight hearing, found the Trump administration's rollback of CFPB rules and enforcement has cost Americans up to $26.5 billion. The largest component, $22.5 billion, comes from scrapping two rules: abandoning the cap on credit-card late fees at $8 accounts for up to $15 billion, and repealing the overdraft-fee rule (which would have limited many banks to $5 overdraft charges) accounts for another $7.5 billion. The remaining roughly $4 billion stems from dropped enforcement actions and abandoned settlements that would have provided direct consumer relief. The hearing also surfaced an allegation that the agency recently removed 15 years of consumer complaint data from the CFPB website.

This is originally an attributed political claim, but the underlying figure is a formal Senate Banking Committee report, not solely Warren's own analysis as first captured — the committee's methodology is public and the report predates its citation by CNBC, UPI, and other tier-1 outlets. It follows an earlier, smaller estimate ($19 billion) cited during the DC Circuit's en banc hearing on CFPB mass layoffs five months earlier ([[2026-02-24--cfpb-dc-circuit-en-banc-hearing-mass-layoffs]]) — the escalation from $19B to $26.5B over that window tracks the CFPB's continued deregulatory rollback, not a single one-time cost.
