On Sunday, March 30, 2025, at a town hall in Green Bay, Musk gave $1 million checks to two Wisconsin voters, declaring them spokespeople for his political group, CBS reported from AP. Minutes before the rally, a unanimous Wisconsin Supreme Court refused to hear a last-minute attempt by Attorney General Josh Kaul to stop the payments: it declined to take the case as an original action and gave no rationale for its decision. CBS/AP wrote that two lower courts had already rejected the challenge. Kaul argued in his filing, "Wisconsin law prohibits offering anything of value to induce anyone to vote"; the court's order, as reported, does not say whether the payments were lawful. CBS/AP also noted that five of the court's seven justices had endorsed a candidate in the race, raising potential conflicts for them to hear the lawsuit. The filing that began the challenge is on [[2025-03-27--musk-announces-1-million-for-wisconsin-voter-in-supreme-cour]]; the open seat is on [[2025-03-31--musk-funded-political-group-spends-big-and-goes-do]].

Two AP-written stories of that weekend give Musk-related spending on different bases. The Sunday CBS/AP story says Musk had pumped more than $20 million into the race, and puts total spending at more than $81 million; AP's March 31 story, on PBS, says groups linked to Musk had spent more than $17 million to support Brad Schimel, a candidate for the seat of retiring Justice Ann Walsh Bradley in the April 1 election. Neither names a tallier or a period, no state filing was read, and the figures are not added here.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

The earlier text said total spending by Musk-linked groups exceeded $20 million, and gave the recipients' names. The sources give more than $20 million as what Musk had put into the race (CBS/AP, no tally named) and more than $17 million for groups linked to Musk (AP via PBS, March 31); they differ and are not reconciled. The recipients are private people and are not named here. Two other sources the entry listed, NPR and Al Jazeera, were not both used: Al Jazeera is bound for the date and NPR was not read. The seat was open; neither candidate is an incumbent.
