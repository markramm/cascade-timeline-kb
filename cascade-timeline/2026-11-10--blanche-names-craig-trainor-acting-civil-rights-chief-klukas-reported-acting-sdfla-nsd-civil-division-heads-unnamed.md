Attorney General Todd Blanche announced late Tuesday that President Trump had appointed Craig Trainor, a senior Department of Housing and Urban Development official, as acting head of the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division, Yahoo News reported. CBS News gave the title as acting assistant attorney general. Trainor replaces Harmeet Dhillon, who resigned the week before.

In the Southern District of Florida, Bloomberg Law reported the day Quiñones resigned, citing one person familiar with the matter, that Yara Klukas, the first assistant U.S. attorney, will take over in an acting capacity and that the department plans for the "grand conspiracy" probe to continue under her. That is a single anonymous source; NTD reported that Blanche did not name a successor to Quiñones, and no department announcement was found.

No reporting found names a successor or acting head at the National Security Division (John Eisenberg, resigned the Friday after the election) or the Civil Division (Brett Shumate, resigned the following Monday, the fourth departure after Dhillon, Quiñones and Eisenberg, per ABC News in the update on the entry for the Quiñones and Eisenberg resignations).

## Update (2026-11-16, page date): the Civil Division has a named acting head

The Justice Department's own Civil Division pages name Jonathan D. Guynn as Acting Assistant Attorney General, and his staff profile says he was appointed Principal Deputy Assistant Attorney General on November 9, 2026. The profile does not say whom he replaces. The sentence above that no reporting names a Civil Division head reflects news reporting only, not the department's pages. The National Security Division's page lists M. Scott Proctor as Principal Deputy Assistant Attorney General and names no acting head.
