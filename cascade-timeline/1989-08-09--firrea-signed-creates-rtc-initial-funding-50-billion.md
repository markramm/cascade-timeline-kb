The Financial Institutions Reform, Recovery, and Enforcement Act of 1989 (FIRREA) was enacted in August 1989. Its vehicle for cleaning up insolvent thrifts was the Resolution Trust Corporation (RTC). The Congressional Research Service reports that the RTC received about $50 billion in initial funding: "$30 billion" from the Resolution Funding Corporation (REFCORP, which issued Treasury bonds), "$18.8 billion" from the U.S. Treasury and "$1.2 billion" from Federal Home Loan member banks. Later legislation raised the RTC's funding to about $105.1 billion.

The cost of the cleanup is a separate figure from what the Act provided. CRS reports that estimates of the direct and indirect cost of the whole RTC process "have ranged from $100 billion to as high as $500 billion", with the most widely reported estimate "in the area of $150 billion (the majority being taxpayer financed)". The Federal Reserve History essay says the RTC closed 747 S&Ls and that "The ultimate cost to taxpayers was estimated to be as high as $124 billion."

## Correction (2026-11-16)

This entry was filed under the title that named a dollar figure for a taxpayer bailout, described as an amount the Act authorized, with a share said to come directly from taxpayers. Neither figure appears in the CRS report or the Federal Reserve History essay read for this correction, and the earlier text is replaced with what those sources give: the initial funding, the later total, and cost estimates with their basis. The entry was renamed from its earlier id, kept in `previous_ids`.
