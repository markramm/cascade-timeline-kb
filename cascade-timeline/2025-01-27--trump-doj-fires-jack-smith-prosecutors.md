On Monday, Jan. 27, 2025, the Justice Department fired career lawyers who had worked on Special Counsel Jack Smith's investigations of President Trump. NBC News reported that the department "fired several career lawyers involved in prosecuting Donald Trump".

**How many, and by whose count.** The count differs by source and by date. NPR, on the day: "At least 12" officials. CBS, the same day: "About a dozen". The class complaint filed by fired FBI employees, *Garman v. Patel* (filed 03/31/26), pleads that the department fired "at least 14" attorneys on or about the same day; that is the plaintiffs' pleading, not a finding. No source read for this entry gives a higher figure than these.

**Reason given.** A Justice Department official told NBC: "the Acting Attorney General does not trust these officials to assist in faithfully implementing the President’s agenda". NBC also reported, from parts of the termination letter read to it, that the letter said "Given your significant role in prosecuting the president, I do not believe that the leadership of the department can trust you to assist in implement the president's agenda faithfully." Both are the government's stated reason, attributed to the acting attorney general, James McHenry.

**Who.** An official familiar with the matter told NBC that those let go included Molly Gaston, J.P. Cooney, Anne McNamara and Mary Dohrmann; no source read has any of them putting their own name on the record.

## Correction (2026-11-17)

Earlier text: a count above every figure in the sources read, and a quoted sentence credited to McHenry directly. Now: each count with its source and date, and the sentence credited to the official who gave it to NBC.
