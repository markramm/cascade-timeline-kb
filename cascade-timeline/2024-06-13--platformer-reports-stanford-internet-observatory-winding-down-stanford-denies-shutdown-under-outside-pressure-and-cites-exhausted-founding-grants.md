**What happened, by source.** Platformer reported that "the Stanford Internet Observatory is winding down," that "Renee DiResta, its research director, left last week after her contract was not renewed," and that "the lab will not conduct research into the 2024 election or other elections in the future." NPR followed and credited Platformer with the first report. Stanford spokesperson Dee Mostofi told Inside Higher Ed: "To correct the record, the Stanford Internet Observatory is not being dismantled." Stanford's Cyber Policy Center then posted an update saying SIO continues under Jeff Hancock's leadership.

**Who stated a cause.** Platformer wrote that the change came amid "a sustained and increasingly successful campaign among Republicans," and, as its own inference, that "the university seems to have calculated that the lab had become more trouble than it is worth." Stanford, in a statement emailed to Platformer after publication, "strongly disputed the fact that SIO is being dismantled" and said: "Stanford remains deeply concerned about efforts, including lawsuits and congressional investigations, that chill freedom of inquiry." Stanford's own update reads: "Stanford has not shut down or dismantled SIO as a result of outside pressure." It names funding as the problem: "SIO does, however, face funding challenges as its founding grants will soon be exhausted." It also gives Stamos "who previously stepped back for personal reasons" as remaining a lecturer. NPR reports that Rep. Jim Jordan posted "Free speech wins again!" on X; Stamos and DiResta said that "The politically motivated attacks against our research on elections and vaccines have no merit."

**Open.** The accounts differ and neither is a finding: the reporters tie the wind-down to political pressure; the university denies a dismantling and names exhausted founding grants. I did not read a document on what the observatory's work became afterward. The Washington Post's account was not used because of its paywall, and Platformer's post was read only through its free preview.
