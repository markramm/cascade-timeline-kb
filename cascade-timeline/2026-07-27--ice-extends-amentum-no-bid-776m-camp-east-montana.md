ICE's justification for continuing its no-bid, sole-source contract with Virginia-based Amentum for Camp East Montana, the largest ICE detention facility in the country, sited at Fort Bliss in El Paso, Texas, was posted in July and reported on the 27th. The notice, as PBS and the AP describe it, runs through September 30, 2027 and carries a "potential additional cost" of $776 million. That figure is a ceiling: the justification prices it as the combined estimate for two option periods after the base period, taken from the government's own cost estimate because the March award was a letter contract. None of it was obligated. The award record shows $657,555,435.04 obligated and a period of performance ending 2026-11-30. Combined with the facility's prior contract history (the original Army WEXMAC task order, with a ceiling of $1,342,742,059.48 and $253,178,228.81 obligated, and the March 2026 sole-source award documented in [[2026-03-11--amentum-services-no-bid-contract-camp-east-montana]]), the notice puts what Amentum stands to be paid for March 2026 through September 2027 at more than $1.2 billion. That is a projection, not obligations, and it is separate from the Army order to Acquisition Logistics.

The extension came over the explicit objections of Democratic members of Congress and advocacy groups who had called for the facility's closure, following a joint Human Rights Watch/ACLU report documenting routine beatings, denial of medical care, filthy conditions, and inedible food since the Army-run camp opened in August 2025. The extension also proceeds notwithstanding the facility's own documented record: three deaths, 49 deficiencies found in ICE's Office of Detention Oversight inspection of February 2026, a June 2026 GAO finding that the program had "wasted millions" ([[2026-06-19--ice-megajail-program-retreats-gao-finds-camp-east-montana-wasted-millions]]), and a homicide ruling in the January 2026 death of Lunas Campos ([[2026-01-21--lunas-campos-el-paso-medical-examiner-rules-homicide-ice-camp-east-montana]]).

The no-bid procurement mechanism forecloses any competitive re-bid or closure review — the same pattern documented across the facility's contract history, where Amentum became the third contractor in roughly 13 months (following Deployed Resources and Acquisition Logistics) without the facility's operational or safety record ever triggering a competitive process.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

The Army order for Acquisition Logistics carries a ceiling of $1,342,742,059.48 and total obligations of $253,178,228.81. The notice put what Amentum stands to be paid for March 2026 through September 2027 at more than $1.2 billion, which is a projection and not an obligation. The Amentum award carries total obligations of $657,555,435.04. The 49 in this entry counts deficiencies, not violations, from one February inspection by ICE's Office of Detention Oversight.

## Correction (2026-11-16, second)

The title and first paragraph called $776 million an "additional cost" and said the extension ran "through 2027." The July justification shows $776M as the combined estimate for two option periods, not an obligation. The USAspending record for the award shows $657,555,435.04 obligated and a period of performance ending 2026-11-30. "Through September 30, 2027" is the date PBS and the AP attribute to the notice; the justification gives durations and no calendar end date. An October revision of the justification restructured the periods and raised the estimate; see [[2026-10-19--ice-ja-rev1-camp-east-montana-amentum-sole-source-2-8-billion-subaward-records-show-amentum-akima-under-acquisition-logistics]].
