OpenAI announced a 3.2 gigawatt, $20 billion data center campus in Effingham County, Georgia on July 22, 2026. Nearly 1,000 residents confronted OpenAI officials at a packed open house the next day and again at the August 5 Board of Commissioners meeting, where commissioners nonetheless voted to approve a zoning ordinance establishing a unified industrial district for the project's Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub site. Commissioners denied signing NDAs and said they had limited authority over the state-level review process, formally advancing the land-use framework despite the community pressure. A dedicated public forum is planned for late August.

This is one of the largest single-site energy demands in Georgia history, and the pattern — public backlash contained by commissioners citing limited local authority while state-level approval mechanisms move ahead unchecked — mirrors the county-level land-use fights this corpus tracks nationally, from Florida's rejected and moratorium-held rezonings ([[2026-06-18--citrus-county-fl-data-center-moratorium-holder-rezoning-denied]], [[2026-07-15--palm-beach-county-rejects-project-tango-data-center]]) to Maryland's zoning reversal ([[2026-07-21--charles-county-md-scraps-data-center-zoning]]). At 3.2 GW, Project Camellia's grid interconnection will require new transmission infrastructure whose ratepayer cost allocation is not yet public — the same Channel-B cost-shift question driving Texas's grid freeze the same week ([[2026-08-03--abbott-freezes-texas-data-center-grid-connections-ercot-audit]]).
