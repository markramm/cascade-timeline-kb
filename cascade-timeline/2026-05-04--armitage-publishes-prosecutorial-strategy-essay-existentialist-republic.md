# Armitage's Essay on State Prosecution of Federal Officials

Christopher Armitage published the essay *This is way bigger than RICO* on his Substack, The Existentialist Republic, in May 2026. What follows is the essay's own account, quoted from the page. Court-opinion readings and descriptions of named people below are the essay's, not findings.

## The essay's argument

The essay holds that a federal officer is subject to a state's criminal law for conduct in that state. In its words: "A federal officer doesn't get a federal force field that turns off state criminal codes when he crosses the state line." It says that "State prosecution is structurally pardon-proof." It proposes the method of the 1985 Commission case, charging soldiers and working up the chain, and asks governors, attorneys general, district attorneys and legislators to begin state investigations.

## Three cases as the essay cites them

- **Gamble.** The essay says "State prosecution following federal non-action remains constitutionally available under Gamble v. United States, 587 U.S. 678 (2019)."
- **Trump v. United States.** The essay says "Justice Barrett confirmed it specifically for presidents in Trump v. United States in 2024." Later it describes the Barrett concurrence, Trump v. United States, 603 U.S. 593 (2024), as confirming "the Constitution doesn't authorize a President to seek or accept bribes, and the Government may prosecute him if he does so." The opinion's own text governs; this entry did not check these lines against it.
- **Neagle.** The essay says In re Neagle, 135 U.S. 1 (1890), "doesn't protect federal officers from state prosecution for corrupt conduct because no federal duty includes accepting a bribe, taking a kickback, selling a pardon, or trafficking confidential data for personal gain." 

## Examples the essay gives

From the essay, as examples of conduct a state could charge. None is a charge or a finding.

- **Aryam Investment 1.** On January 16, 2025, Eric Trump signed a contract with Aryam Investment 1, an Abu Dhabi entity, transferring forty-nine percent of World Liberty Financial for five hundred million dollars, and that One hundred eighty-seven million went to Trump-family entities.
- **1789 Capital.** The essay says Donald Trump Jr.'s portfolio companies "received over seven hundred thirty-five million dollars in Trump-administration contracts" including a $620 million Pentagon loan to Vulcan Elements. The essay's cited basis is a March 2026 press release from a Democratic member of Congress, which says at least four companies in the firm's portfolio "received federal contracts or awards from the Trump administration in 2025, totaling more than $735 million." That is a contracts-and-awards total, led by the loan, as one member of Congress counts it; it is not a finding that any contract was improper.
- **Social Security data.** The essay says DOGE engineers "accessed Social Security Administration data without authorization," conduct the essay places in a Department of Justice notice in a January 16, 2026 notice in a Maryland federal case.
- **Further examples** in the essay: meme-coin fees, a Commerce Secretary's Tesla recommendation, Pentagon awards to SpaceX and xAI, Kushner's Affinity Partners, immigration-enforcement incidents.
- **Moriarty and Morgan.** The April 16, 2026 Hennepin County charging of an ICE agent under Minn. Stat. § 609.222 "established that state prosecution of federal officers on duty is operationally viable." The charge is a county attorney's; the agent has not been convicted, and the case's status is in [[2026-04-16--moriarty-charges-ice-agent-morgan-assault-minnesota]].

## The essay on named people

The essay assigns roles in a mob hierarchy to named officials, lawyers and relatives of the president. These labels are the essay's. On Boris Epshteyn the essay has him "under active indictment in Arizona for the fake-elector scheme." That was the essay's line when published; the Arizona case was dismissed in June 2026 (see [[2024-06-18--epshteyn-pleads-not-guilty-arizona-fake-elector-nine-felony-counts]]). The corpus has not corroborated the essay's further line about an internal investigation.

## Coalition the essay counts

The essay counts "Twenty-four Democratic Attorneys General have authority over some part of the Trump enterprise, twenty-three Democratic governors can direct that authority," and fifteen Democratic state legislatures "can fund and structure the work."

## Correction (2026-11-17)

The earlier version of this entry stated some of the essay's readings in the entry's own voice (the Barrett concurrence, the Neagle holding), gave a total for 1789 Capital as a flat fact, and described the Social Security matter as confirmed. Each is now attributed to the essay or restated with its base, and the Social Security court-document link is removed.
