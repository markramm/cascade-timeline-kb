The docket randomly reassigned Trujillo v. DHS (Civil Action No. 1:26-cv-03072) to Chief Judge Boasberg: "Case randomly reassigned to Chief Judge James E. Boasberg as the case is not related." After a scheduling conference, his minute order of October 21, 2026 set the sequence: a superseding TRO motion from plaintiffs, the government's opposition by October 26, 2026, a plaintiffs' reply, and a Zoom hearing on October 30, 2026, at 10:30 a.m.

RECAP lists a memorandum in opposition on the docket as of the due date but does not hold the document, so its content was not read. The opposition remains to be read, and the hearing date remains to be checked.

## Update (2026-10-30, order date): Boasberg orders the government to give plaintiffs the 20,000-person list by 5:00 p.m.

After the hearing on October 30, 2026, Chief Judge Boasberg ordered that "the Government shall disclose to Plaintiffs the 20,000-person list at issue" by 5:00 p.m. that day. Plaintiffs may file a supplement on standing, and the government then has 24 hours to respond. The order directs disclosure to the plaintiffs, not to the public. On the amended motion for a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction, the minute entry says "an order is forthcoming."

## Update (2026-10-30, filing date): Government moves to stay the list-disclosure order pending mandamus

On October 30, 2026, the government moved (Document 41) "for a stay pending mandamus of the Court's Minute Order entered October 30, 2026," or for "an administrative stay pending resolution of this motion." It says "Plaintiffs lack standing" and that "the only two individuals Plaintiffs named as recently naturalized citizens likely to be harmed by this list have been shown by sworn declaration not to be on the list." The motion calls the order one "requiring the public disclosure of sensitive law enforcement information"; the minute order directs disclosure "to Plaintiffs." As fallbacks, it asks the court to take the list "in camera" or to limit it to "Plaintiffs' counsel of record in this case only." These are the government's positions.

## Update (2026-10-30, order date (minute order)): The stay motion is withdrawn; Boasberg limits the list to plaintiffs' legal team

In a further minute order on October 30, 2026, after a telephone conference, Chief Judge Boasberg ordered that the list "may not be disseminated outside of Plaintiffs' legal team" and that plaintiffs "shall neither disclose names on the list to anyone nor inform any person that his or her name is on the list." Public filings must redact names, and for now plaintiffs may use it "in relation to their standing arguments only." The order records: "The 41 Motion to Stay is withdrawn."

## Update (2026-10-31, filing date, as reported): Plaintiffs' experts say 42% of DOJ's California list is not on the state's voter file

The AP reported that a filing Saturday by the Coalition for Humane Immigrant Rights said 8,880 of the 20,930 names, or 42%, do not correspond to anyone in California's current voter file. The analysis is by two members of UCLA's Voting Rights Project, who sued with the group, so it is a party's expert report. It came after a judge ordered the list shared with the plaintiffs. Bill Essayli, who runs the U.S. Attorney's office in Los Angeles, called it "bogus."

## Update (2026-10-31, filing date): The filing itself: what the 42% does and does not say

Read directly by OCR from Dkt. 43 and Exhibit 17 (Dkt. 43-1); hand update, because these are scans the sourcing gate can't read. The Barreto and Rios declaration says that of 20,930 names, 4,350 fully matched the voter file on name, exact address and date of birth, a further 7,700 matched without address, and "8,880 names — or 42% — on the DOJ list were not anywhere to be found on the current California voter file." The three figures sum to 20,930.

Three limits. The declarants say matching "could have been more precise" had the voter ID number been included, and that this field and 20 others were "reduced" in the file; they also say the file they received "does not appear to be the identical file" given to Los Angeles County. Their 22 are "entries on the CHIRLA citizen list that fully match," from the plaintiff's own member and client list. And the 3,743, 7,486 and 348,238 figures are stated as "extrapolating" that match rate to 3.8 million naturalized voters. They are projections, not people identified. A name absent from the file is not shown here to be a citizen or a noncitizen. The declarants are the plaintiffs' experts; the government's response had not been read when this was written.

## Update (2026-11-01, filing date): DOJ's motion to strike and for a show-cause order

The government moved to strike part of the plaintiffs' expert declaration and for an order that the plaintiffs show cause why they did not violate the court's minute order, which it says limited the list to standing arguments. The filing says the plaintiffs used the list for non-standing purposes. This is the government's motion, not a finding.

## Update (2026-11-01, filing date): The motion also asks for sanctions and raises a criminal contempt referral

The same motion says the court should impose civil sanctions, for example "dismissing Plaintiffs’ complaint", and should also consider referring the matter to the Department of Justice for investigation into "possible criminal contempt by Plaintiffs and their counsel". The government's filing says plaintiffs' counsel oppose. No ruling was on the docket read.

## Update (2026-11-01, filing date): Government tells Boasberg CHIRLA still has not shown standing and objects to an ex parte, in camera table

In its response to plaintiffs' supplemental standing brief, filed 11/01/26, the government says that "Despite now having a third court-granted opportunity to shore up its deficient standing," CHIRLA "still fails to meet its necessary jurisdictional burden." It objects to plaintiffs' "unilateral submission of an ex parte," in camera "Table of CHIRLA entries matched to DHS list," and asks that plaintiffs "be compelled to immediately provide Defendants with the list of alleged matches." These are the government's arguments in a brief, not findings. The document is the government's response only; plaintiffs' supplemental brief and its appendix were not read for this update.

## Update (2026-11-01, filing date): Plaintiffs answer DOJ's sanctions motion: the order contemplated a public filing

In their response, filed November 1, 2026, plaintiffs ask that the court "deny, in full," the motion. They write that "the Order specifically contemplates a public filing" and that "No entry on the DOJ list has been contacted, directly or indirectly." They argue "these errors increase the chances that the wrong" people will be targeted. A party's filing, not a finding.

## Update (2026-11-02, order date): Boasberg bars demands that California set aside ballots of listed voters; relief narrower than asked

Chief Judge James Boasberg granted CHIRLA's motion in part. Defendants are "preliminarily ENJOINED from issuing demands to California elections officials to compel the segregation of ballots, or other deviations from existing state elections procedures, based on voters' inclusion on the lists of suspected non-citizens." He wrote that the Executive Branch "has likely violated the Constitution's Elections Clause" and that "the lists are flawed." The government's separate motion to strike remains pending.
