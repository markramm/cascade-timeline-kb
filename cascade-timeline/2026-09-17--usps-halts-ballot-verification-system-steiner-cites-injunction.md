On **September 17, 2026**, Postmaster General **David Steiner** told the Associated Press that the
Postal Service has **stopped work** on the voter-verification computer system central to the
Trump administration's mail-ballot rules, three days after the Supreme Court denied the
government's stay application in *USPS v. California* (No. 26A305) 7-2
([[2026-09-14--scotus-blocks-trump-mail-ballot-eo-7-2-kavanaugh-timing-midterms]]). "There's an
injunction so we're not doing anything," Steiner said. A memo sent to all Postal Service employees
the same week and obtained by the AP stated the agency will **"not be enforcing any of these new
rules in the 2026 federal election"** and that "for Election Mail it is business as usual."

## What Happened / Key Facts

- Steiner declined to assess the Supreme Court's ruling and declined to take a position on the
  underlying policy, describing himself as leading an "independent agency of the executive branch"
  whose role was limited to "the execution side."
- He said he had read only "portions" of the Blumenthal whistleblower report alleging the system
  was built in a rushed, error-prone process
  ([[2026-09-01--usps-whistleblower-alleges-work-continued-after-talwani-order]]), and said he had
  planned to unveil the system around September 15 — the week the Supreme Court blocked it.
- Steiner denied resignation rumors amid what he described as pressure from people who "would like
  to see me out of a job," without naming a source.
- He said USPS will continue to **recommend** (not require) the barcode/envelope design changes
  going forward, with a price differential: lower postage for compliant envelopes, higher for
  noncompliant ones needing manual processing — a voluntary, price-incentive version of the design
  requirement the enjoined rule would have mandated.
- Per the same reporting, Justice Kavanaugh's concurrence is characterized as leaving open that he
  "might rule in favor of the administration if the issue comes back before the court at a later
  time" — consistent with the canon entry's own reading of the concurrence as timing-based rather
  than authority-foreclosing.
- Separately noted in the same report: mail-ballot rejections for late postmarks have risen in
  Washington and Oregon; Steiner said no operational changes are planned and encouraged early
  voting ("Everything is solved if people vote early").

## Why This Event Matters

This is the concrete "administration response" signal the canon entry's own analysis called for:
not a refiled rule with more lead time, and not a post-election revival attempt, but an operational
stand-down for 2026 with the pricing-incentive substitute left in place. It is consistent with —
not contradictory to — the canon entry's finding that the ruling foreclosed timing, not authority:
USPS is complying with the injunction as narrowly construed (no *enforcement* this cycle) while
preserving the underlying design preference through a non-mandatory price lever that requires no
judicial authorization to operate. Whether a mandatory version returns for 2028 remains the open
question the canon entry identifies; this entry documents the interim posture, not an answer to it.
