The Cumberland County, Maine Board of Commissioners voted 3-1 on Tuesday, April 21, 2026 (commissioner James Cloutier and two others in favor, Stephen Gordon opposed, Tom Tyler absent) to drop Immigration and Customs Enforcement from the county jail's contract with the U.S. Marshals Service. **Correction to prior version of this entry**: the county's detention arrangement is a single USMS contract, not a separate ICE-specific IGSA layered atop a distinct USMS agreement. Per the Portland Press Herald, "the jail's federal contract with the U.S. Marshals Service, which pays the jail $150 per day for each federal inmate held at the facility, includes those held for ICE" — the April vote strips the ICE-detainee provision out of that one USMS vehicle, rather than severing a standalone ICE IGSA. (WGME/Bangor Daily News and Yahoo/AP wire coverage independently corroborate the USMS-contract framing; Maine Public's coverage does not specify which federal vehicle covered ICE detentions.) The distinction from Orange County, FL's same-day vote ([[2026-04-22--orange-county-fl-terminates-ice-section-igsa-caps-stays-48-hours]]) is structural: Orange County split ICE out of its IGSA while keeping a separate USMS agreement in force; Cumberland County had no separate ICE IGSA to begin with — ICE housing rode entirely under the county's USMS contract, and the vote removes ICE's access to that contract.

**Context substantially predates the vote.** ICE had already pulled all ~50 of its detainees from the Cumberland County Jail on January 23, 2026 — DHS itself canceled its arrangement to hold ICE detainees there after Sheriff Kevin Joyce publicly criticized ICE's "bush-league" tactics in arresting one of his corrections officers (an Angolan national, in the country on an unexpired work authorization, during the "Operation Catch of the Day" enforcement surge). The commissioners' April vote is therefore best read as a formal, board-level ratification/closure of a contractual relationship ICE/DHS had already vacated on the ground three months earlier, not the initiating event — though it forecloses ICE resuming use of the jail without a new vote. The board had considered ending the contract once before and rejected it: a November 2025 vote to terminate failed 3-2. Roughly eight months of sustained public comment (from single digits to 50-100+ commenters per meeting) and an April 13, 2026 state-law change (Gov. Janet Mills signing LD 2058, clarifying counties may decline detainees held solely on civil immigration violations while remaining obligated on criminal holds) preceded the reversal to 3-1.

Sheriff Joyce, who runs the jail, said the vote does not fully exclude ICE — the jail remains obligated under state law to accept federal detainees facing criminal charges — and warned of a hit to jail funding built around the $150/day federal per-diem ("our county, for 30-some-odd years, has built funding the jail around federal revenue"). Commissioner Stephen Gordon cast the lone "no" vote on cost-shifting-to-taxpayers grounds, not in ICE's defense.

This is a county-government counter-move against the same contractual lever (a federal per-diem detention contract) that drives the detention buildout elsewhere in the corpus, and — alongside Orange County FL's same-day vote — is one of a small number of documented 2026 instances of local government narrowing rather than expanding ICE detention capacity. Other counties have moved in both directions on the same underlying mechanism in 2026: Otero County, NM renewed a 5-year ICE IGSA in defiance of the state's detention ban ([[2026-07-01--otero-county-nm-renews-igsa-defies-state-detention-ban]]), while Rockingham County, NH tabled a proposed ICE deal indefinitely (Feb 2026, not yet in this KB) — the pattern is contested county-by-county, not a uniform withdrawal wave.
