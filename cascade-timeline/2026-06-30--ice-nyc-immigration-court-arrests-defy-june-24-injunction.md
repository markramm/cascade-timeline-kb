On the Thursday and the Monday before The Intercept's report, ICE agents arrested three men at New York City immigration courts: an Ecuadorian man at 26 Federal Plaza and a man from the Dominican Republic at 290 Broadway on the Thursday, and a man from Guatemala at 290 Broadway on the Monday. Lawyers told The Intercept this appears to be the first grave violation of two orders barring such arrests. Both orders came before the arrests. Judge Kevin Castel's May 18 stay covers only the three Manhattan immigration courthouses, 26 Federal Plaza, 201 Varick Street and 290 Broadway. The nationwide order is a separate one: on June 23, Judge P. Casey Pitts of the Northern District of California vacated ICE's and EOIR's courthouse-arrest policies under the Administrative Procedure Act, a vacatur that applies nationwide and, like Castel's stay, restores the earlier limits. Make the Road New York filed habeas corpus petitions for the men arrested on the Thursday. All three men have since been transferred to detention, according to ICE records. An ICE spokesperson denied the agency had violated any court order and did not explain how the arrests fit the orders' exceptions.

This is a discrete instance of the pattern the timeline tracks as ICE treating adverse court orders as advisory rather than binding — the enforcement arm acting past the point where a court has said stop. It sits directly in the documented ICE-defies-the-judiciary cluster: the New Jersey criminal-contempt findings against ICE transfers ([[2026-03-03--new-jersey-judge-farbiarz-criminal-contempt-ice-transfers]]), the Minnesota chief judge's contempt threat over 96 violations ([[2026-02-26--chief-judge-schiltz-criminal-contempt-threat-ice-96-violations]]), the re-detention of a 77-year-old Palestinian in defiance of a federal judge ([[2026-06-10--ice-defies-federal-judge-redetains-77yo-palestinian-omar-louisiana]]), and Judge Bryan's contempt holding over transfer violations ([[2026-02-24--judge-bryan-holds-government-contempt-ice-transfer-violations]]). The open leads: which field office and supervisors authorized the arrests, whether DOJ was aware, and whether emergency habeas follows for the three detained men.
