New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill signed legislation on Wednesday banning local law enforcement agencies from partnering with federal immigration authorities, which Bolts, dated March 25, 2026, counted as making New Jersey the 10th state with such a law. Bolts said the new law codifies a 2018 order by then-Attorney General Gurbir Grewal, known as the Immigrant Trust Directive. It listed nine other states as prohibiting participation in the 287(g) program: California, Connecticut, Delaware, Illinois, Maryland, Maine, New Mexico, Oregon and Washington. New York was not on that list. Bolts also said four states governed by Democrats, including neighboring Massachusetts and New York, still had local or state agencies with 287(g) contracts.

Sherrill also signed the Privacy Protection Act and a law requiring ICE agents to show their faces and provide identification before making an arrest, according to Bolts. Bolts added that New Jersey already banned local jails and sheriffs from entering into Intergovernmental Service Agreements, the contracts used to rent space for ICE detention. The kind of law is a ban on agreements.
