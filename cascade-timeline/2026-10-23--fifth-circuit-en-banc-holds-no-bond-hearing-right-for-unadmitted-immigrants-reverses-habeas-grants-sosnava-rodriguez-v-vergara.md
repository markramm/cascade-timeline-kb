Sitting en banc on Oct. 23, 2026, the Fifth Circuit held that three Texas men, "three aliens who had been living unlawfully in the United States for many years," were not entitled to individual hearings on dangerousness or flight risk before being detained pending removal proceedings, and reversed the district courts' decisions and vacated their grants of habeas relief. Judge Cory Wilson wrote for the majority; the Texas Tribune reports the vote as 10-7 and quotes Judge Leslie Southwick's dissent describing an "impenetrable wall between the Constitution and these long-term residents." The ruling displaces the panel decision, which had found a right to individualized hearings.

This is the merits ruling the en banc order (see [[2026-07-10--5th-circuit-en-banc-reinstates-mandatory-detention]]) set up: the court that vacated the panel's due-process holding has now rejected it. The Tribune reports the Trump administration announced its no-bond policy in July 2025 and that the Supreme Court has agreed to hear a separate but similar challenge ([[2026-10-01--scotus-grants-review-rhoney-v-barbosa-da-cunha-mandatory-detention-no-bond]]).

## Update 2026-10-26: what the opinion says, who dissented, what is still pending

Checked against the slip opinion (Doc. 306-1, 5th Cir. No. 26-50183, consolidated with 26-50219 and 26-50221), not the press accounts.

- **The 10-7 count, from the opinion itself.** Nine judges joined Judge Wilson's opinion (Elrod, Jones, Smith, Richman, Willett, Ho, Duncan, Engelhardt, Oldham), but a footnote says Judge Richman "concurs in the judgment" only. Her separate opinion agrees petitioners are "applicants for admission" and that due process does not require a pre-detention bond hearing under section 1225, expresses no view on whether ICE could instead proceed under section 1226, and notes that the petitioners did not challenge the length of detention. Judges Ho and Oldham each wrote separate concurrences.
- **Seven dissenters, in three opinions.** Southwick (joined by Stewart, Graves, Higginson, Douglas and Ramirez); Douglas (joined by Stewart, Graves, Higginson and Ramirez); Higginson (joined by Stewart, Southwick, Graves, Douglas and Ramirez); and a short dissent by Judge Haynes, who would limit relief to people like the petitioners (more than ten years' residence, no criminal history, close American relatives). Southwick writes that the Fifth Circuit's reading of the statute "is the minority position among the circuits."
- **What the court held and did not hold.** The majority's two holdings are that no individualized dangerousness or flight-risk hearing is constitutionally required, and that the length of these three detentions (three months, about two months and three weeks) raised no constitutional problem. It expressly leaves open challenges where detention "no longer served its immigration purpose," where proceedings are a pretext, or where removal is not "practically attainable," and notes petitioners did not raise duration. The 90-day rule from the vacated July 2 panel opinion is gone.
- **Argument and history.** En banc argument was September 24, 2026, so the September sitting this ticket tracked did happen. The court ordered rehearing sua sponte, then stayed the district court judgments pending rehearing.
- **Supreme Court posture.** The dissents cite *Rhoney v. Barbosa da Cunha*, No. 26-104 (cert granted Oct. 1, 2026), and list nine other circuits on the other side. Fox News reports (Oct. 23) that no argument date has been set. We found no cert petition in *Sosnava Rodriguez* itself. The statutory ruling this opinion builds on is the earlier *Buenrostro-Mendez v. Bondi*, 166 F.4th 494 (5th Cir. 2026).
- **Not found as of Oct. 26.** CourtListener's RECAP copy of the Fifth Circuit docket (id 72762866) shows termination on Oct. 23 and nothing after; a RECAP search for post-Oct. 23 filings returned zero, which says only that nobody has pulled newer entries. No district-court order applying the en banc decision was located, and no DOJ or DHS statement. Open items: mandate and any rehearing or cert filing in the Sosnava docket, how W.D. Tex. and other circuit district courts treat pending habeas petitions, and the Rhoney argument calendar.
