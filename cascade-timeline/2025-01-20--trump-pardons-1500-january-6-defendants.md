On his first day in office, President Trump issued pardons for some 1,500 January 6 defendants (NPR's count; the proclamation states no total) and commuted 14 sentences — the largest mass clemency action related to political violence in modern American history.

**Scope of clemency:**
- Pardons for some 1,500 people convicted of January 6 offenses (press count)
- 14 named Oath Keepers and Proud Boys defendants had sentences commuted to time served; ten of the 14 were convicted of seditious conspiracy and four were not
- 300+ pending cases subsequently dismissed by DOJ
- 20+ DOJ employees who worked on January 6 prosecutions were fired
- The DOJ's January 6 prosecution database was scrubbed

**Key commutations included:**
- Stewart Rhodes (Oath Keepers founder, sentenced to 18 years for seditious conspiracy)
- Enrique Tarrio is not a commutee: he received a full pardon under the clause covering all others (see the correction below)
- Other Oath Keepers and Proud Boys defendants: nine Oath Keepers and five Proud Boys in all, convicted of seditious conspiracy (ten of them), conspiracy to obstruct, assault on officers and obstruction

**Significance:**
The mass clemency reversed four years of the largest federal prosecution in American history. Over 1,200 defendants had been charged; hundreds had been convicted at trial or pled guilty; dozens of federal judges had imposed sentences. The blanket pardons — including for those convicted of seditious conspiracy and violent assault — sent a clear signal that political violence on behalf of the president would be rewarded rather than punished.

This action set the stage for the DOJ weaponization that followed: the same department that dropped cases against insurrectionists would proceed to prosecute Comey, James, Powell, Bolton, and Lemon for opposing Trump.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

1. **The "1,500" is a press count of pardons, not of pardons and commutations.** Old text: "~1,500 pardons and commutations total." Proclamation 10887 (90 FR 8331-8332) states no total. NPR reports pardons for "some 1,500" [c3] and commutations for 14 separately. The title and body now say so. The id keeps "1500" because the figure is the reported pardon count and the URL is already in use.
2. **Tarrio was pardoned, not commuted.** Old text: "Key commutations included ... Enrique Tarrio (Proud Boys leader, sentenced to 22 years for seditious conspiracy)." The commutation clause names 14 people and Tarrio is not among them [c1]. Judge Kelly's July 10, 2026 memorandum says the order commuted the sentences of Nordean, Biggs, Rehl and Pezzola and granted Tarrio a full pardon [c6]. The 22-year figure was not re-checked here and was removed.
3. **Not all 14 were convicted of seditious conspiracy.** The court counts 14 commutees [c2]. Ten were convicted of seditious conspiracy: Rhodes and Meggs [c8], Minuta, Hackett, Moerschel and Vallejo [c9], Nordean, Biggs and Rehl [c4], and Bertino by guilty plea [c7]. Four were not: Watkins, Harrelson and Caldwell, acquitted of it [c8], and Pezzola [c5].
