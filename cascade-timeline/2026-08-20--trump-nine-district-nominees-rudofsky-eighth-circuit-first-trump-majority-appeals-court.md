Trump named **nine new judicial nominees to federal district courts** across six states —
**Oklahoma, Alaska, Texas, Kentucky, Louisiana, and Florida**. The announcement lands alongside a
separate and structurally larger nomination made a week earlier: the elevation of **Judge Lee
Rudofsky** (E.D. Ark., appointed 2019, previously Arkansas solicitor general) to the **Eighth
Circuit**, filling the seat vacated by Judge **Bobby Shepherd**.

Rudofsky's confirmation would make the **Eighth Circuit the first federal appeals court with a
majority Trump-appointed bench** — the threshold that converts appointment counts into durable
control of a circuit's law. The Eighth Circuit covers Arkansas, Iowa, Minnesota, Missouri,
Nebraska, North Dakota, and South Dakota.

The constraint is the calendar, not the votes. The GOP-led Senate faces a compressed timeline
across the summer break and October midterm campaigning, and a Rudofsky confirmation vote is not
expected until after the election. That places the circuit-flipping vote in a lame-duck window,
after the electorate has voted and before any new Senate is seated — the same structural window
that has repeatedly carried judicial confirmations that would be harder to take to voters first.

The nominations come after the pipeline had already slowed
([[2026-08-07--trump-judicial-pipeline-slows-53-judges-49-vacancies-remain]]), which reported all
circuit vacancies filled with 49 district seats left; the Shepherd vacancy reopens circuit-level
business and the nine district picks work the remaining backlog.
