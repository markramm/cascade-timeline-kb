POLITICO reported on September 17, 2026 that Democratic consultants are "privately encouraging their battleground candidates to avoid talking about artificial intelligence regulations out of fear that powerful tech groups will unleash a tidal wave of spending against them ahead of November." The guidance came from "strategists in three separate top-tier House races, according to people involved in those races, who were granted anonymity", and the article draws on "interviews with more than a dozen Democratic lawmakers and strategists". The anonymous sources are POLITICO's, not independently checked here.

POLITICO describes Leading the Future as a pro-AI industry super PAC that "spent over $25 million in primary races this year and is backed by investors and executives of OpenAI", and Public First Action as "pushes for AI regulation and is funded by OpenAI's chief competitor, Anthropic." Said Leading the Future spokesperson Josh Vlasto: "The only people that have both threatened and carried out spending and intimidation tactics against Democratic members of Congress over their positions on AI are Public First and other groups funded by Anthropic and effective altruists," and "Our agenda is and has always been Congress passing a strong and smart national regulation which has broad and building support in Congress and across the country." Public First co-founder Brad Carson said the network will "absolutely" get involved in the general election; its first stated test is "strong support for export controls and strong opposition to the preemption of state laws regarding AI, absent a federal framework".

On the Democratic side, Abdul El-Sayed, the Michigan Senate nominee, is advocating for a "full pause at the frontier of research", told POLITICO he would "rather be on the truth" than dodge AI industry money, and "hasn't committed to running ads on the issue". Rep. Pramila Jayapal said that "these AI groups — they come out and they say that they're concerned, that they want regulation. But then they spend huge amounts of money on defeating anybody who wants legislative regulation,". POLITICO also cites a new poll, in which "About two-thirds of Americans say there's a risk advanced AI could destroy humanity."
