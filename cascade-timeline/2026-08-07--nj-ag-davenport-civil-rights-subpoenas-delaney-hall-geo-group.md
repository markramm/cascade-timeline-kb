New Jersey Attorney General Jennifer Davenport announced on August 7, 2026 that the state's Division on Civil Rights had issued investigative subpoenas concerning Delaney Hall, the roughly 1,000-bed ICE detention facility in Newark operated by GEO Group under a 15-year, $1 billion contract. The subpoenas mark an escalation in kind, not merely degree: the state moved from litigating a specific access dispute — the June 2 civil suit filed after GEO barred health inspectors from sleeping areas and medical units during a May 28 visit — to a standing civil rights investigation of the facility's practices as such, with compulsory process attached.

Delaney Hall is the densest single-site accumulation of the detention-accountability pattern in the corpus, and the sequence is the argument: a hunger strike and pepper-ball clashes ([[2026-05-26--delaney-hall-newark-hunger-strike-geo-group-protests-pepper-ball-clashes-sen-andy-kim]]), state police mass arrests and kettling of protesters ([[2026-05-30--delaney-hall-nj-state-police-mass-arrests-kettling-protest-crackdown]]), the health-inspector access suit ([[2026-06-02--nj-sues-geo-group-delaney-hall-health-inspector-access]]), a GEO employee charged with driving into a protester ([[2026-06-21--geo-group-employee-charged-driving-into-delaney-hall-protester]]), and then three deaths in custody — Edwin Lopez Cornejo ([[2026-08-01--edwin-lopez-cornejo-dies-delaney-hall-second-death-geo-1-2b-contract]]) and Chajon Raxon, whose death ICE never announced under a post-release non-reporting policy ([[2026-08-12--chajon-raxon-third-delaney-hall-death-post-release-non-reporting-policy]]). The subpoenas issued five days before that third death became known. The facility also operates without proper local permits according to ongoing Newark litigation — a jurisdictional gap that recurs across the warehouse-conversion program, where federal contracting is used to route around the municipal permitting that would otherwise supply the only local check.
