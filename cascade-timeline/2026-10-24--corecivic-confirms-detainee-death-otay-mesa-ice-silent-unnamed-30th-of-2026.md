A person in ICE custody died on Oct. 24, 2026 at the Otay Mesa Detention Center in San Diego. The operator, CoreCivic, gave Daylight San Diego this account by email: a staff member found a detainee unresponsive a little after 11:30 a.m., staff attempted life-saving measures, and emergency medical professionals pronounced the person dead at the facility.

At the time of KGTV's report, the station had reached out to ICE and DHS for more information and had yet to hear back, and Aguirre was awaiting more information about the cause and the person's identity. This entry therefore binds to the operator's account as relayed by San Diego outlets, not to an ICE notification; ICE's own notice follows in the update below.

San Diego County Board of Supervisors Chair Pro Tempore Paloma Aguirre, statement of Monday:  "We don’t yet know their name." She asked ICE and CoreCivic to reach the family, cooperate with an investigation and share findings with the family and the public. She pointed to what county public health staff found when they got inside after going to federal court: "tuberculosis cases no one told the County about, no vaccines in stock, and a man who had waited 18 months for a medical device." That is the county's characterization, not an adjudicated finding.

Daylight reports that the death would bring the known number of deaths this year in ICE custody to 30. That is a reporter's count, not reconciled against canon's own tally ([[2026-09-17--marchena-dies-winn-correctional-25th-ice-custody-death-2026]]).

DHS's purchase of Otay Mesa from CoreCivic is in canon ([[2026-07-02--dhs-buys-corecivic-otay-mesa-california-city-1-5b-oversight-shield]]).

## Update (2026-10-27, publication date (ICE detainee death notification)): ICE posts its death notification for the Otay Mesa detainee: a 43-year-old man from Guatemala

ICE posted a [detainee death notification](https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/criminal-illegal-alien-guatemala-dies-ice-custody-california) on October 27, 2026. It names him as Roberto Jimenez Gutierrez, 43, from Guatemala, and says he "died Oct. 24 while in the custody of U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement at the Otay Mesa Detention Center in San Diego." The notice gives a timeline of his last hour, says the death is under investigation and an autopsy will be done.

## Update (2026-10-27, publication date (KPBS and KGTV reports of the supervisors' Tuesday statements)): His wife says he had no previous health conditions; San Diego County supervisors demand records, video and inspection access

KPBS reports that his wife said he left behind five children and that he had no previous health conditions, and that the family is trying to fly his body back to Guatemala. The agency told KPBS it arrested him in Escondido on Oct. 9 during an enforcement stop.

San Diego County Supervisor Terra Lawson-Remer and Supervisor Paloma Aguirre called on Tuesday on ICE, the Trump administration and CoreCivic to cooperate with investigators and turn over records and video. Aguirre asked for "a timeline of this person's care from intake to death". The supervisors also sent a letter to ICE and DHS demanding access and information on whether anything has been fixed.

On the count that the entry above carried as a reporter's figure: KPBS reports that ICE's own reporting shows 29 deaths in custody so far this year, while the National Immigrant Project put the number at least at 30.

## Update (2026-10-28, identification date (county medical examiner)): County medical examiner identifies the Otay Mesa decedent under a different name than ICE released

San Diego County's medical examiner late Wednesday officially identified the man who died in ICE custody at Otay Mesa Detention Center. The name it released was different from what ICE initially released: in its Oct. 27 notification, ICE said his name was Roberto Jimenez Gutierrez, and as of Thursday the agency still referred to him by that name. The office said it verified his identity by running his fingerprints through the Department of Justice and California ID databases, and it also matched that information against his Guatemalan passport. An ICE spokesperson said the detainee provided false information and used several aliases to mislead law enforcement.

ICE's release had said he had a criminal history; his family insists those charges are not true. KPBS reported that ICE had not responded to its questions about how it confirms the identity of people in its custody.
