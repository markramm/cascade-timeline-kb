Federal judge Adam Abelson issued a preliminary injunction on August 12, 2026 vacating Howard County, Maryland's revocation of building permits for 6522 Meadowridge Road in Elkridge, allowing construction to resume on the warehouse conversion being built by Genesis GSA Strategic One LLC under a Department of Homeland Security contract. County Executive Calvin Ball had revoked the permits in February 2026, citing failures of public notice in the original approval. Abelson's ruling is the disposition of the arguments he heard on July 2, when the county and the Department of Justice argued for over two hours over whether the revocation was lawful ([[2026-07-02--howard-county-md-elkridge-ice-facility-ruling-imminent]]). Howard County is appealing. The facility is intended to replace the Baltimore ICE field office and has drawn sustained opposition from residents and elected officials since early 2026.

The procedural shape of the loss matters more than the loss itself. Howard County did not lose on whether an ICE detention facility belongs in Elkridge — that question was never reached. It lost on whether a county may withdraw a permit it had already issued, which converts local land-use authority into a one-way ratchet: once the permit is out the door, the county's remaining leverage is an appeal that runs slower than the construction schedule. That asymmetry is the recurring feature of the corpus's warehouse-conversion fights. Maryland has been the more aggressive test bed for the counter-move, having banned state and local ICE partnerships by emergency law ([[2026-02-17--maryland-bans-state-local-ice-partnerships-emergency-law]]) and having seen a state judge extend a restraining order against a separate ICE facility in March ([[2026-03-19--maryland-judge-extends-ice-facility-restraining-order]]). The procedural hook that has actually held elsewhere is federal environmental review rather than local zoning — a Maryland judge halted ICE construction at Williamsport on NEPA grounds in April ([[2026-04-15--judge-halts-williamsport-ice-construction-nepa]]), and that precedent then traveled to a Bexar County, Texas warehouse ([[2026-04-15--hurson-maryland-nepa-precedent-bexar-tx-warehouse-pause]]). Elkridge is the case where the county reached for the zoning tool instead, and the zoning tool did not hold.
