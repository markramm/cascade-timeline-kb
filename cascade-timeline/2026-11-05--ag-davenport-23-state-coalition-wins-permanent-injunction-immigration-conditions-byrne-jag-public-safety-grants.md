New Jersey Attorney General Jennifer Davenport and a coalition of 23 states reported a federal court ruling in which Chief U.S. District Judge John J. McConnell Jr. in Rhode Island declared the administration's immigration conditions on federal public safety grants unlawful and permanently prohibited their enforcement. Among the affected programs was the Edward Byrne Memorial Justice Assistance Grant Program, commonly known as Byrne JAG. Davenport said: "Today's victory in court makes clear that the federal government cannot unlawfully politicize law enforcement and public safety by threatening our access to critical funding." The account is the coalition's, as relayed by local outlets; the order text was not read for this entry, and the entry is dated to the day of the report, not the order.

The conditions in dispute would have required states to give federal immigration authorities access to people in state custody and notice of their release as a condition of receiving grant funding. The ruling follows an earlier coalition challenge that led the federal government to remove similar conditions from more than $1.3 billion in VOCA funding. The outlets count the coalition differently (attorneys general plus a governor in one, states in the other), so the headline number is the coalition's own framing.
