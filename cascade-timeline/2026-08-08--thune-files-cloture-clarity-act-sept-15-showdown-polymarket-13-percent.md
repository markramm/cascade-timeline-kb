The Senate missed its self-imposed August 7 deadline for the CLARITY Act and left for recess without a vote. Majority Leader John Thune filed cloture on the motion to proceed on August 8, setting a September 15 procedural vote for the day senators return. The 600-page market-structure bill would assign most digital assets to CFTC oversight and remove them from SEC reach. Democrats withheld support pending ethics, conflict-of-interest, and illicit-finance guardrails, with unresolved fights over how decentralized exchanges fit under AML reporting and how stablecoin yield is treated. Polymarket's odds of 2026 passage had collapsed to 13 percent.

The conflict-of-interest provision is the actual obstacle, and it is unusual in being about the President's own holdings rather than an abstract principle — which is why it has proven unresolvable rather than negotiable. The corpus documented the ethics provision collapsing once already over a World Liberty Financial loophole (2026-07-23--clarity-act-ethics-provision-collapse-wlf-loophole), and senators had raised the same conflicts during the GENIUS Act stablecoin fight (2026-03-10--senators-stablecoin-genius-act-trump-crypto-conflicts). The jurisdictional move underneath the ethics fight deserves equal attention: routing most crypto regulation to the CFTC, an agency with a fraction of the SEC's enforcement resources, accomplishes deregulation through venue assignment rather than through any change in the substantive rules. Meanwhile the SEC has begun delivering much of the same outcome by rulemaking (2026-08-18--sec-proposes-regulation-crypto-assets-75m-exemption-securities-safe-harbor), which lowers the stakes of the September vote considerably — if the bill dies, the agency route remains open, and it requires no Democratic votes at all.
