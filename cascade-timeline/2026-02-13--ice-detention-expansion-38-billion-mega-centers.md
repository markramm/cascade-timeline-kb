An internal ICE memo dated Feb. 13, 2026, as Fox News reported it, describes a nationwide detention buildout: capacity of 92,600 beds, eight mega-centers each holding up to 10,000 detainees and slated to be fully operational by Nov. 30, 2026, 16 regional processing sites holding between 1,000 and 1,500 detainees for stays of three to seven days, and the acquisition of 10 existing "turnkey" centers where ICE Enforcement and Removal Operations already operates. The memo states that the initiative will be funded through congressional allocations under the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act," and says ICE has added 12,000 new law enforcement officers through a surge hiring effort. Bisnow reported that the federal government plans to spend $38.3B to acquire industrial buildings and convert them into detention centers. WBUR's On Point described a $38 billion plan to convert commercial warehouses into massive detention centers.

Bisnow's tally of the purchases showed what DHS had paid: $102.4M for a warehouse near Hagerstown, Maryland; $70M to Rockefeller Group for one in Surprise, Arizona; $87.4M in Berks County, Pennsylvania; and almost $123M for a Socorro, Texas warehouse.

The Brennan Center, writing about the 2025 budget law and not this memo, wrote that detention and surveillance contracts, rapid enforcement hiring and new monetary incentives will create a "deportation-industrial complex."
