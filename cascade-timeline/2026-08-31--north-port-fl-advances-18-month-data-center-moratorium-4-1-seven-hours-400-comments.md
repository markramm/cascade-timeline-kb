The North Port, Florida City Commission convened **Monday, August 31, 2026 at 6 p.m.** for the first reading of an 18-month data-center moratorium ordinance. Public comment alone ran roughly six to seven hours, drawing about 400 speakers; the commission's **4-1 vote came just before 2 a.m. on Tuesday, September 1** — meaning the vote itself falls after midnight, on the calendar date after the meeting convened. Commissioner Demetrius Petrow cast the lone dissenting vote. Commissioner Phil Stokes, voting yes, said: "In good conscience, I can't stand by and support this project moving forward without regulation."

**This entry records a first reading, not adoption.** A first reading advances an ordinance; it does not enact one. The **second and final reading is scheduled for September 22, 2026** — a date that itself moved: earlier coverage (as of the August 31 meeting) had flagged September 8 as a likely second-reading date, but by the time of the vote the city had set September 22 instead. As of this writing (September 17, 2026), the September 22 vote has not occurred.

**The moratorium reaches the pending application, but only partway.** The trigger was a filing by **North Port Holdings 300 LLC** for an approximately 896,000-square-foot, seven-building data-center and office campus (about 123,000 sq ft of office space, roughly 4.5 acres of wetland impact) in North Port's Yorkshire area, submitted July 20, 2026 under an NDA the developer had first requested in August 2025. The ordinance as advanced on first reading freezes new applications for 18 months and blocks **final approval** of the pending North Port Holdings 300 application during that window, but the applicant may continue through the city's processing and technical-review stages. Commissioner Petrow's dissent was specifically that this did not go far enough — he wanted the moratorium to halt the pending application's processing outright, not just its final approval. The ordinance also carries a hurricane-recovery exemption (page six, tied to Florida Senate Bill 180, which bars municipalities from "burdensome" post-hurricane development restrictions for property affected by Hurricanes Debby, Helene, or Milton) whose applicability to this specific project the city attorney said he could not yet clarify.

**The public-participation record is the densest in this timeline's Florida entries**, and the September 3 workshop the day before the vote featured presentations from **Florida Power & Light** and the **Peace River Manasota Regional Water Supply Authority** — the utility and the regional water authority, on the record, before the vote, on the two constraints that decide whether a project of this size is feasible. Most jurisdictions in this timeline have made these decisions without that testimony in hand.

Florida had two data-center moratoria move in the same window: **Levy County** adopted its own one-year moratorium by ordinance on September 8. North Port's neighboring **Sarasota County** — the jurisdiction that voted 5-0 in July to direct staff toward its own hyperscale freeze — has not held its promised August/September follow-up vote on a permanent-vs-temporary posture as of September 17, 2026; no agenda item or press report located shows that vote occurring. North Port and Sarasota County are separate governments (city vs. county) responding to separate, unconnected applicants; see [[2026-07-08--sarasota-county-fl-hyperscale-moratorium-directed]] for the county's action and why the two should not be conflated.
