The Walker County Board of Commissioners voted August 6 to extend its data center moratorium by 180 days, through roughly February 2027, and created a Data Center Development Standards Study Committee with a December 4 reporting deadline. The county's original 30-day moratorium, approved July 10, was preemptive — no data center application had been filed at the time. The study committee will review noise, water, electricity demand, setbacks, and land compatibility. No hyperscaler has been publicly identified in Walker County.

Walker County's preemptive freeze — moving before any application exists, let alone a disclosed developer — is the same undisclosed-siting pattern seen the same week in Warrick County, IN ([[2026-08-07--warrick-county-in-project-zora-1200-acres-undisclosed-hyperscaler]]). Together the two counties illustrate how the moratorium wave has shifted from reactive (blocking a known project) to anticipatory (closing the door before a project can even be filed) — itself a sign that county governments now treat "hyperscaler seeking land" as a predictable, recognizable threat pattern rather than a one-off local dispute.
