Weeks after the Supreme Court struck down his original attempt to end birthright citizenship, President Trump signed new executive orders on August 6, 2026 attempting to restrict birthright citizenship through different legal mechanisms. Cato Institute immigration analyst David Bier called the orders "blatantly ignoring the Supreme Court's decision." The move forces courts to re-litigate a constitutional question the Court had already settled 6-3 at the end of its term ([[2026-06-30--scotus-strikes-down-trump-birthright-citizenship-executive-order]]), which itself followed the original January 2025 order ([[2025-01-20--birthright-citizenship-order]]) and the Court's grant of cert ([[2025-12-05--supreme-court-grants-review-birthright-citizenship]]).

The re-issuance sits inside a documented pattern of post-ruling defiance across immigration, deportation, and court-order enforcement more broadly — separate reporting cited in the same coverage put the administration's cumulative record at 96 violations of federal court orders across 74 cases since January 2026. Whether the new EOs attempt the same "jurisdiction" theory SCOTUS rejected through a different administrative vehicle, or a genuinely distinct legal mechanism, is the open question that determines how quickly a new injunction can issue.
