The Virginia State Corporation Commission ordered Dominion Energy on July 31, 2026 to file within 90-120 days a policy directly assigning transmission infrastructure costs caused by large-load customers — principally data centers — to those customers, rather than socializing them across the full ratepayer base. The Commission found that data centers are driving "massive transmission line costs," and set a floor requiring new large-load customers to pay at least 85% of contracted distribution and transmission demand charges. The immediate effect on households was measurable: the residential bill increase Dominion had proposed fell from +$2.90/month to roughly +$0.94/month.

This is the cost-shift channel of the data-colonialism pattern made explicit by a regulator: the extraction is not only of land, water, and power but of the capital cost of the grid itself, billed to the residents of the territory hosting the build-out. Virginia is the highest-concentration data center market in the United States — Northern Virginia's "Data Center Alley" — which makes the SCC the venue with the most load-bearing precedent, and this the clearest state cost-allocation holding in the country to date. It lands alongside contested proceedings in the same posture elsewhere: Wisconsin's PSC forcing ATC to restart the Port Washington interconnection application ([[2026-08-11--wisconsin-psc-forces-atc-restart-port-washington-datacenter-grid-app]]), Oracle suing the Wisconsin PSC over exactly this cost-shift question ([[2026-06-19--oracle-sues-wisconsin-psc-data-center-ratepayer-cost-shift]]), and Abbott's directive to PUCT and ERCOT on data center infrastructure costs ([[2026-06-10--abbott-directive-puct-ercot-data-center-infrastructure-costs]]). It also follows Virginia's own HB 892 SCC load-forecast review ([[2026-04-30--virginia-hb892-scc-load-forecast-review-dominion-pjm]]). The operative fight moves to Dominion's compliance filing due October-November 2026, where the cost methodology — not the principle — will be litigated.
