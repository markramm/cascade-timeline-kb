Alex Pretti, a 37-year-old intensive care nurse at the Minneapolis VA hospital and a U.S. citizen, was shot and killed by Border Patrol agents on Saturday morning near 26th Street and Nicollet Avenue in Minneapolis. It was the second fatal shooting in Minneapolis by immigration agents that month, after the shooting of Renee Good.

His family said: "Alex was a kindhearted soul who cared deeply for his family and friends and also the American veterans whom he cared for as an ICU nurse at the Minneapolis VA hospital." Local officials said he was an American citizen and a legal gun owner with a permit to carry.

NPR reported that multiple bystander videos and witness testimony contradict the federal account. In the videos Pretti can be seen holding only a phone before at least six officers tackle him, pin him face down and shoot him in the back, firing what sounds like 10 shots. One eyewitness wrote in a court document that "It didn't look like he was trying to resist, just trying to help" a woman up. NPR also reported that it had verified no evidence that Pretti brandished his handgun at any time, and that one video appears to show an officer take away his gun just before another shoots him.

The official account, attributed. On Saturday Gregory Bovino, the commander at large for Border Patrol, said agents attempted to disarm the man "but he violently resisted," and speculated that he intended to "massacre law enforcement." Trump administration officials called Pretti a "domestic terrorist." Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche, on NBC's Meet the Press, called it an "incredibly split-second decision" and said Pretti was "interrupting an ICE operation." At a press conference on Sunday, asked whether he had already concluded the shooting was justified, Bovino said: "I have not concluded anything, I have not given you a conclusion." CBS News described that as a walk-back of his Saturday claims.

Governor Tim Walz called the federal narrative "nonsense" and "lies," said the federal government cannot be trusted with the investigation, and said "there will be justice to Minnesotans"; Minnesota is creating a log of evidence for possible future prosecution of immigration agents. Mayor Jacob Frey called the federal surge "an invasion" and told residents: "Stand up for America. Recognize that your children will ask you what side you were on." In a court filing the Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension wrote of the federal handling of the scene: "From a law enforcement perspective, this is astonishing." A federal judge ordered the U.S. Department of Homeland Security to preserve evidence.

Pretti was a member of AFGE Local 3669. Its parent union's president, Everett Kelley, said: "Our union is heartbroken. An AFGE member is dead. And a family’s life has been forever changed." Kieran Knutson, a Minneapolis CWA local president, wrote "Never Forget, Never Forgive" on Facebook from the streets near the site, and Payday Report reported that on Saturday night many online were calling for an expanded strike.

The Intercept, citing anonymous eyewitnesses, reported that the man was acting as a civilian observer.
