The Senate Judiciary Committee's Democrats said that Sen. Dick Durbin led the release of the Government Accountability Office's newly-completed report on ICE detention inspections. GAO's central finding was that ICE had not adequately assessed how the inspection changes affected its ability to oversee facilities. From April 2025 to April 2026, the average daily detained population jumped by more than 50 percent as active facilities expanded from 128 to 172, per the release, which says ICE received over $45 billion for immigration detention. An IANS report says the average daily population of the facilities covered reached 61,817 in April 2026.

Durbin's release says the DHS Office of the Immigration Detention Ombudsman, an independent office with statutory authority to conduct surprise inspections, has been shut down entirely, and that a provision barring funding for a contract with a facility after two consecutive less-than-adequate ODO ratings was left out of the FY26 DHS Appropriations Act. The release says ICE told GAO that failing ratings now will no longer block continued funding. Earlier GAO work is at [[2026-09-24--gao-ice-no-strategic-plan-45b-detention-expansion-waste]].

The report is GAO-27-109299, a Q&A report to congressional requesters dated November 5, 2026. It puts the growth in average daily population at 52 percent, from 40,587 in April 2025 to 61,817 in April 2026, in facilities that detain people for over 72 hours, and counts over 40 added facilities. GAO reports that two of four DHS inspecting entities decreased or eliminated their inspections as of July 2026. ICE's Office of Detention Oversight conducted 194 inspections in fiscal year 2024 and 102 in fiscal year 2025, and generally did not conduct follow-up inspections; facilities with an average daily population of 49 or less now conduct self-inspections. The Immigration Detention Ombudsman's office did not receive a fiscal year 2026 appropriation and has stopped conducting inspections; its last inspection was in January 2026. The DHS Inspector General was appropriated $20 million, and its contractor plans approximately 96 inspections, begun in August 2026. DHS agreed with GAO's two recommendations to ICE.

On the penalty, ICE officials told GAO in August 2026 that two consecutive ODO inspections rating a facility's performance inadequate would no longer preclude it from receiving further funds. They said the FY2019 provision was not applicable because the fiscal year 2026 DHS Appropriations Act omitted it. The report names no operator, so GEO Group and CoreCivic appear only in the Senate release's wording, and it lists no facilities rated inadequate.
