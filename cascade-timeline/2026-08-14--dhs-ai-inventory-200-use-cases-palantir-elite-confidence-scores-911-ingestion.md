Tech Policy Press documented in August 2026 that DHS's public AI use-case inventory had grown to more than 200 active or in-development deployments, roughly 40 percent above its July 2025 level. The catalog includes Palantir's ELITE platform generating "confidence scores" used in deportation targeting, a system ingesting the full stream of 911 call data to build enforcement heat maps, and phone-based biometric scanning applications for field agents. EFF and 140 organizations called for an end to AI use in immigration decisions absent due-process safeguards. DHS provides no mechanism by which an individual or their attorney can learn that AI was used in a decision affecting them, let alone contest the algorithmic output.

The absence of a notice mechanism is the operative fact, and it compounds against a defiance record already established in the corpus: Chief Judge Schiltz found ICE violated 96 court orders across 74 cases in Minnesota (2026-01-28--judge-schiltz-ice-violated-96-court-orders-minnesota), escalated to a criminal contempt threat (2026-02-26--chief-judge-schiltz-criminal-contempt-threat-ice-96-violations), and then issued a written order documenting 210 violations (2026-02-27--chief-judge-schiltz-written-order-210-ice-violations). An agency that disregards explicit judicial orders at that rate is not going to be constrained by the softer requirement to disclose its models. ELITE is not new to the record either — an ICE agent testified about ELITE quotas in Oregon (2025-12-02--ice-agent-testimony-elite-quota-kasubhai-oregon), which reframes "confidence score" as a throughput instrument rather than a precision one. This is the analytics tier sitting on top of the acquisition tier the corpus has been mapping: the $1B sole-source Palantir contract (2026-02-19--dhs-signs-billion-dollar-palantir-ice-surveillance-contract), the $2.9B surveillance architecture (2026-08-12--brennan-center-dhs-29b-domestic-surveillance-architecture), and the LexisNexis pipeline feeding 82 billion data points into Palantir (2026-08-10--ice-lexisnexis-palantir-bulk-facial-recognition-contract). Bulk 911 ingestion is the boundary crossing worth marking: emergency calls are made by everyone, citizen and non-citizen alike, under an implicit promise that summoning help is not surveillance.
