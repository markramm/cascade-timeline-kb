The Intercept reports, from ICE records it obtained exclusively, that GEO Group valued the Northwest ICE Processing Center in Tacoma at $67 million this spring. This month the federal government paid GEO $453 million, almost seven times that, for the facility. The Intercept reports that ICE had also awarded GEO a $1.5 billion contract to continue its detention services there. GEO told shareholders it plans to use the $340 million in net profits to buy back shares. Sen. Maggie Hassan said, "These deals raise serious questions about pay-to-play schemes and corruption at DHS." Former DHS Inspector General John Roth told The Intercept, "I think this is what happens when you move too fast without sufficient internal controls."

This adds the appraisal record and the oversight response to the sale already recorded in [[2026-11-18--geo-group-sells-tacoma-northwest-ice-processing-center-to-us-for-453m-keeps-operating-it]] and the contract in [[2026-10-28--ice-awards-geo-group-1-5-billion-10-year-contract-tacoma-northwest-ice-processing-center-single-offer]]. The Intercept also reports that Hassan opened a probe into ICE's $495 million purchase of a detention center that CoreCivic had previously offered to the state of Minnesota for $75 million, and that ICE officials told GAO investigators the purpose of buying facilities was not to save money, but to avoid issues with local governments. The Intercept reports the $67 million figure from ICE records; neither ICE nor GEO's response is reported here.
