## Opening

**Nothing turns on whether these eight defendants are guilty; the question is what the government did around the case.** That is the Whipple Shield discipline — see [[2026-02-15--project-whipple-shield-hsi-financial-surveillance-minnesota-unions]] — and it governs this entry the same way.

On the morning of June 10, 2026, the FBI raided homes in Michigan, Illinois, and Wisconsin — including a residence in Ypsilanti — and unsealed a 63-page federal indictment, **Case No. 5:26-cr-20306-JEL-EAS** (E.D. Mich., Southern Division, assigned to Judge Judith E. Levy, referral to Magistrate Judge Elizabeth A. Stafford), charging eight people connected to the University of Michigan. **The indictment itself was filed under seal a month earlier, on May 20, 2026** — signed by Assistant U.S. Attorney Margaret Smith. The sequence — sealed indictment, then a same-morning raid, then unsealing — is documented on the face of the filing (filing date May 20 vs. the June 10 raid/unseal reported contemporaneously) and is the entry's structural spine.

**The defendants**, all named on the indictment's caption:

- **D-2 Amatullah Aliasgar Hakim** ("A"), Ann Arbor, 21
- **D-3 Paige Elizabeth Feyock**, Ann Arbor, 26
- **D-4 Ahmet Kerem Korkaya** ("Met"), Milwaukee, 28
- **D-5 Jonathan Hongru Zou**, Ann Arbor, 22
- **D-6 Alexander Matthew Sepulveda**, Chicago, 23
- **D-7 Mariam Muhammed Odeh**, Dearborn, 24
- **D-8 Colin Hunter Weger** ("Beams"), Ann Arbor, 24

(Ages/cities per contemporaneous reporting — FOX 2 Detroit — cross-checked against the indictment's defendant list, which gives names and aliases but not ages/addresses.)

**The charges**, count by count, per the indictment: Counts One through Eight each charge **conspiracy to transmit threats in interstate and foreign commerce** (18 U.S.C. §§ 371, 875(c) — up to 5 years), distributed variously across the eight defendants (e.g., Count One: Amatullah Hakim and Sepulveda; Count Eight: Zainab Hakim, Feyock, and Korkaya). **Count Nine** charges **Zainab Hakim and Feyock** with **conspiracy to tamper with a witness** (18 U.S.C. § 1512(b)(3), (k) — up to 20 years). **Count Ten** charges **Sepulveda alone** with **destruction of property to prevent seizure** (18 U.S.C. § 2232(a)).

**The Count Ten allegation is not about the vandalism.** It charges that on April 23, 2025, while law enforcement attempted to execute a search warrant at Sepulveda's residence, Sepulveda "attempted to and did knowingly remove, delete, transfer, and clear the contents of his cellular phone and computer laptop" to prevent the government from taking the devices into custody (indictment ¶ 69). This is the same April 2025 device episode the sibling ticket [[ypsilanti-raid-participation-and-referral-um-police-armored-vehicle-nessel-fbi-briefing]] investigates for its evidentiary link to this indictment — that connection is not chased further here.

**Agencies**: the FBI led the June 10 raids. FBI Director Kash Patel publicly announced the case the same day the raids occurred, characterizing the conduct as a "coordinated campaign of violent, criminal acts seeking to pressure University of Michigan leaders and other businesses in the Eastern District of Michigan to cut off all ties with Israel" (quoted in CounterPunch's republication of an Adi Saleem piece originally in Common Dreams). U.S. Attorney Jerome Gorgon stated: "In America, we rule by law not by fear. These alleged threats and attempts to terrorize government officials, businesses, and the Jewish Federation are anti-American. We will counter intimidation with justice" (FOX 2 Detroit). Michigan State Police is visible in Mark's own photograph from the scene.

## Tier 2 — advocacy-sourced. Attributed, not stated as fact

**Everything about how the June 10 raid was physically executed traces to one source chain, not several.** Katz and Ward's Mondoweiss piece describes agents rolling into the neighborhood "in an armored vehicle with guns drawn." A separate account — Ypsilanti neighbor **Grace Viscito**, speaking on the Working People podcast (June 24, 2026), quoted via The Real News Network — describes flashbangs thrown "into the upstairs bedrooms," "battering rams attached to vehicles" used to break down doors, and residents made to "walk across the broken glass to get to the street." CounterPunch, Common Dreams, Electronic Intifada, and Palestine Uncensored recirculate this same Katz/Ward-and-Viscito account without independent reporting of their own. **That is one source chain — Mondoweiss's synthesis plus Viscito's eyewitness account — repeated across five outlets, not five independent sources.**

**Mark's own photographs from the scene corroborate the armored vehicle and residents standing on the sidewalk. They do not corroborate flashbangs.** That distinction is deliberate: the flashbang detail rests on Viscito's account alone and has no independent visual or documentary confirmation in this corpus.

**U-M police participation** is asserted in the same Katz/Ward account ("with help from local and state police departments, including the University of Michigan Police") but is **not confirmed by U-M or the FBI** — see the sibling ticket, not chased further here.

## The framing trap — what the indictment alleges is not "property damage"

**Movement coverage characterizes the underlying conduct as property damage** (broken windows, spray paint, glass jars). **The indictment alleges considerably more, and it is worth being precise about what "considerably more" means — because these are allegations, quoted selectively by the prosecution from what is presumably Signal or encrypted-app traffic, and none has been tested at trial.**

Per the indictment's overt-acts sections:

- **The "hit list" allegation**: on or about May 21, 2024, Korkaya wrote to Feyock, "[V-4]'s entire family is now on my hit list." Feyock replied, "Let's get [V-4's] kids bruh and [V-2's] too" (indictment ¶¶ 21(a), 33(a)). A separate paragraph frames the same exchange as referring to a "hit list" where people "gotta die" (¶ 21(b)).
- **The "has to be neutralized" / strip-search allegation**: on or about August 5, 2024, Feyock allegedly messaged co-conspirators that a person identified as V-6 "has to be neutralized," and that "Zainab and I are getting coffee with him tomorrow evening / ima strip search [V-6] tomorrow to see if he is wearing a wire / not taking no chances with him" (¶ 54(e)). The indictment alleges Zainab Hakim and Feyock in fact confronted V-6 at a coffee shop the next day (¶ 54(f)).
- **The jars-into-homes allegations**: on November 27, 2024, the indictment alleges Zainab Hakim, Korkaya, and others vandalized a fifth victim's (V-5) house — spray-painting inverted red triangles and throwing "two clear mason jars containing, among other things, a noxious chemical," one of which "broke through a plate glass window and landed in V-5's house" (¶ 59(i)). Separately, on March 15, 2025, the indictment alleges Zou and Sepulveda, provided a vehicle by an unindicted co-conspirator, threw "two glass jars containing an unknown blue substance and food compost" through a large window at the home of University of Michigan Provost V-7, "one of which landed in a bedroom of V-7's house" (¶ 66). The indictment does not itself state that a child was in that bedroom at the time — that detail, where it appears in DOJ's public characterization, is not present in the indictment text as read.

**"Property damage" is not a defensible summary of what is charged.** A conspiracy count naming a hit list, a "gotta die" characterization, a plan to strip-search a suspected informant for a wire, and jars of unknown chemicals thrown through windows into occupied homes is a materially different allegation than vandalism — regardless of whether any of it is ultimately proven. Adopting the "property damage" frame from movement coverage would hand away any downstream piece built on this entry.
