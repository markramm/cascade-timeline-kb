ICE has paid **no third-party medical provider for detainee care since October 3, 2025**, when the
Department of Veterans Affairs abruptly ended its role processing detainee medical claims. The
replacement is a contract with **Acentra Health**, valued at **up to $67.5 million**, to stand up a
new claims-processing system. As of the reporting window, **Acentra has processed zero claims.**

The failure is documented at both ends of the contract. Acentra had received **$44.6 million of the
$67.5 million as of June 4, 2026**, against a contract term set to end July 31. Processing was to
begin **April 30, 2026**; when that date passed, payments to third-party providers did not resume,
and as of **August 25** the IHSC provider portal still described itself as "coming soon." On the
provider side, **UCHealth is owed roughly $4 million** for detainee admissions it has already
absorbed.

Federal contracting documents themselves warned that payment delays could cause community
providers to stop accepting patients in federal custody — the predicted consequence, recorded in
advance, of a system that pays a contractor most of its money for a service it has not delivered.
That consequence lands on a detention population whose medical outcomes are already the subject of
a documented death record ([[2026-06-12--eighteenth-ice-detention-death-of-2026-denny-adan-gonzalez-deadliest-year]]
is one marker of the trend). The structural point is not that care was denied by policy: it is that
the payment rail was privatized, the privatized rail failed, and no mechanism forced either
correction or refund while the contract ran its term.

**Sourcing note.** The ledger capture reported the Acentra contract at **$36 million** from a
single source. Independent tier-1 reporting (NOTUS, Healthcare Finance News) puts the contract
value at **up to $67.5 million**, with $44.6M disbursed as of June 4; the $67.5M figure is used
here and the ledger's $36M figure is not carried forward.
