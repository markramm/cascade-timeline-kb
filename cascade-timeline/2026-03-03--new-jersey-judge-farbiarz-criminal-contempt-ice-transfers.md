In an opinion filed on 03/02/26 in *Kumar v. Soto* (D.N.J.), Judge Michael E. Farbiarz wrote that no-transfer injunctions issued by New Jersey district judges had been violated 17 times by the Respondents, "around three every two weeks," and ordered that his future no-transfer injunctions in immigration-habeas cases carry two declarations, one from the U.S. Attorney's Office and one from ICE.

## The Kumar transfer

Baljinder Kumar, held at Delaney Hall, filed a habeas petition seeking a bail hearing before an immigration judge. The court issued its no-transfer injunction at 7:58am on January 26. A letter from the U.S. Attorney's Office told the court that ICE had transferred him to the Port Isabel Service Detention Center in Los Fresnos, Texas on January 31, 2026, after that order. The supervising Assistant U.S. Attorney swore that ICE's Newark operations office had missed the injunction e-mail "Unfortunately and inadvertently."

## What the court counted

The court had ordered the U.S. Attorney's Office to list every violation of a court order in the case. The Office's declaration reviewed approximately 547 immigration habeas matters filed during the relevant period, defined as December 5, 2025 to February 12, 2026. The declaration says the Office takes a broad interpretation of what constitutes a violation of a Court order, so its categories include the government lawyers' own missed deadlines as well as ICE conduct. The 17 is the Justice Department's own count of post-injunction transfers, repeated by the judge; it is not a court finding.

The Office also reported that it had obtained no indication that the Respondents intentionally engaged in the transfers. The judge wrote that repetition might begin to inch closer to looking intentional, and added that he was making no bottom-line finding.

## The remedy: declarations, not contempt

The court said it will not go down that road toward finishing a criminal contempt inquiry, and that the declarations requirement is less intrusive than further steps toward a possible criminal contempt proceeding. One declaration comes from the U.S. Attorney's Office. The other will be executed by the Newark ICE Field Office Director or the Deputy Director.
