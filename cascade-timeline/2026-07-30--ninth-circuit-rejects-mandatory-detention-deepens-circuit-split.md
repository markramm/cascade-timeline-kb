The U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit ruled 2-1 on July 30 that immigrants must receive bond hearings, rejecting the Trump administration's expansion of mandatory no-bond detention — with a Trump-appointed judge joining the majority. The ruling deepens a circuit split now standing at 4-2: the Second Circuit ([[2026-04-28--second-circuit-rules-ice-mandatory-detention-policy-illegal]]), Eleventh Circuit ([[2026-05-08--eleventh-circuit-rejects-trump-no-bond-detention-policy-circuit-split]]), and now the Ninth Circuit have rejected the policy, while the Fifth Circuit's en banc panel ([[2026-07-10--5th-circuit-en-banc-reinstates-mandatory-detention]]) and the Eighth Circuit have upheld it.

A Trump-appointed judge joining the majority against the administration's own position substantially raises the odds the Supreme Court will grant certiorari to resolve the split — a cert petition is already pending in a related case ([[2026-06-15--scotus-cert-genalo-v-black-prolonged-immigration-detention-bond]]). A SCOTUS ruling favoring bond hearings could reshape detention economics broadly, since the administration's capacity build-out (targeting 92,600 beds, [[2026-07-29--ice-issues-emergency-solicitation-5500-beds-four-cities]]) assumes prolonged custody without individualized hearings.
