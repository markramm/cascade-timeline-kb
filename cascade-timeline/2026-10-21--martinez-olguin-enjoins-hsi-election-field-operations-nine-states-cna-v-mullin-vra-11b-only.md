On October 21, Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin (N.D. Cal.) granted a preliminary injunction in *California Nurses Association v. Mullin* (Dkt. 87). It bars DHS and those acting with it from conducting elections-related field operations and field interviews in California, Connecticut, Georgia, Missouri, Nevada, New York, Pennsylvania, Washington and Wisconsin that involve or are likely to involve people the officers know, suspect or should reasonably know or suspect are U.S. citizens, and from conducting such field operations based wholly on the TECS records the "Voter Eligibility Matching Policy" generated against those same people. By its terms it runs to November 3 at 8:00 p.m., or when polling places close on Election Day, or as the court otherwise directs, and is stayed until 5:00 p.m. on Friday, October 23, which CBS reports gives the government a chance to seek appeal. The order also sets a $100 bond for the plaintiffs, due by 5:00 p.m. Thursday, October 22.

This is a preliminary ruling with a narrow legal footing, not a merits decision. The court found only the nurses' associations (CNA/NNU) had shown standing, and only on intimidation of voter assistance and mobilization under Voting Rights Act section 11(b). It held the San Francisco and Santa Clara County plaintiffs and Voto Latino had not clearly shown standing, did not reach the Administrative Procedure Act claims for want of an adequately shown final agency action, and found CNA/NNU likely to succeed on the section 11(b) claim. The court says plainly it does not find that investigating unlawful voting is itself unlawful, and that it makes no findings on the extent of voter fraud. On the record, it points to a high likelihood of error in the review of target lists because no federal database holds the citizenship status of U.S. residents. Context: the injunction reaches the nine-state surge documented in [[2026-09-01--dhs-hsi-voter-fraud-surge-nine-states-midterms]], the government's own account of it in [[2026-09-28--cna-v-mullin-government-says-hsi-surged-voter-fraud-investigations-after-uscis-leads]], and the HSI broadcast in [[2026-08-28--hsi-domestic-operations-broadcast-to-all-sacs-voter-fraud-surge-requested-by-ad-krol-in-california-nurses-amended-record]]. It does not touch the California voter-list fight in [[2026-10-19--doj-seeks-tro-us-v-california-sworn-dhs-declarations-describe-how-20000-voter-list-was-built]].

## Update (2026-10-26, filing date): Government appeals the preliminary injunction to the Ninth Circuit

On October 26, 2026, the defendants filed a notice of appeal (Document 89, "NOTICE OF PI APPEAL") stating that they "hereby appeal to the United States Court of Appeals" for the Ninth Circuit from Judge Martínez-Olguín's October 21 order granting the preliminary injunction. A notice of appeal is not a ruling: the injunction stays in effect unless a court stays it.

## Update (2026-10-27, filing date): DHS asks the court to stay the injunction and says it will ask the Ninth Circuit to rule by Oct. 29

In an ex parte motion filed Tuesday, October 27, the government asks the court to stay the preliminary injunction pending appeal. Citing "the imminence of the election," it asks for a decision "as quickly as possible" and says it plans to file a motion to stay in the Ninth Circuit "today, Tuesday, October 27," asking that court to decide by Thursday, October 29, if the district court has not already granted a stay. The motion argues that DHS "may be substantially chilled in enforcing federal voting law in the run-up to a nationwide election." These are the government's arguments, not a ruling.

## Update (2026-10-29, order date): Court denies the government's ex parte motion to stay the injunction pending appeal

In an order filed 10/29/26 (order date) the court "DENIES Defendants’ motion to stay the preliminary injunction pending appeal", the motion the defendants filed on October 27, 2026. It found the defendants had not shown irreparable harm, writing "Compliance with the law does not constitute a legally cognizable harm." It noted that the defendants "consistently represented that the field operations investigate" "allegations of past voting and do not relate to voting in the upcoming election", and faulted the delay: the defendants "did not notice their appeal until five days after the Order issued".

## Update (2026-10-29, order date): Martinez-Olguin refuses to stay the injunction on HSI election field operations

In an order filed 10/29/26, Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin DENIES a stay of her October 21 preliminary injunction pending appeal. She rested on irreparable harm and did not reach the other factors, writing: "Compliance with the law does not constitute a legally cognizable harm." She also wrote that DHS's argument about stopping a noncitizen voting on November 3 "contradicts" its earlier account of the operations. This denial is not a ruling on the merits; the appeal is No. 26-7139 at the Ninth Circuit.

## Update (2026-10-30, order date): OCA case related to this one; the SEIU suit is not

In a related-case order dated 10/30/2026, Judge Martinez-Olguin determined this case "is related to Case No. 26-cv-12617," the OCA-APAA suit, and "is NOT related to Case No. 26-cv-12800," the SEIU suit, which stayed with Judge Thompson.

## Update (2026-10-30, order date): Ninth Circuit panel refuses a stay

In a per curiam order, a Ninth Circuit panel wrote: "The request for stay pending appeal is denied." It said the federal defendants "have not carried their burden to show that they likely will be irreparably harmed," and that "The existing injunction is properly drawn and narrowly defined." The panel said the union "has associational standing to pursue an intimidation claim." It said lawfulness will "be resolved at the merits stage of this case."

## Update (2026-10-30, order date): En banc vote fails, over a dissent

A judge made "a sua sponte request for an en banc vote" the same day, and the government asked the court to "grant rehearing en banc and issue an immediate stay." In an order for publication, the court wrote that "en banc proceedings with respect to the panel’s order are concluded." Judge Tung wrote a dissent from the denial, joined by other circuit judges named in the order. The appeal itself remains pending.
