USAspending lists a definitive contract, number 70CMSD26C00000001, signed in March 2026 by Immigration and Customs Enforcement. The award description is "THIS AWARD IS FOR PENLINK PLX INVESTIGATIVE SOFTWARE SUITE AND SUPPORT SERVICES FOR HOMELAND SECURITY TASK FORCE." The awarding office is "INVESTIGATIONS AND OPERATIONS SUPPORT DALLAS" and the funding office is "HOMELAND SECURITY INV HOMELAND SECURITY TASK FORCE". The recipient is not Penlink: it is "CHENEGA DEFENSE & AEROSPACE SOLUTIONS, LLC", of HUNTSVILLE, Alabama, which is the reseller of Penlink's software here.

The record, as pulled for this correction, gives obligated to date as 2899162.98 dollars and the total potential value (base and all options) as 8310148.26 dollars. The period of performance starts in April 2026 and ends in March 2027, with a potential end date in March 2029. The first figure is an obligation to date; the second is a ceiling that depends on options being exercised.

The record codes the instrument as a sole-source award: solicitation procedures "ONLY ONE SOURCE", set-aside "8(A) SOLE SOURCE", extent competed "NOT AVAILABLE FOR COMPETITION", and 1 offer received. The stated basis for limiting competition is "AUTHORIZED BY STATUTE", with a Federal Acquisition Regulation section cited in the record; the full citation is in this entry's claim records. We did not read a written justification: the SAM.gov notice pages render only in a browser and were not read, so the reasoning behind the sole-source choice is not in this entry.

Prism Reports first reported the award on Apr 29, 2026 as a "no-bid" contract. Prism's description of the product is its own characterisation: it writes that PLX "intercepts, aggregates, and analyzes live communications from phone calls, texts, and internet activity" and "could enable DHS to track people’s movements in real time", and also that "it’s unclear how Penlink PLX collects location data or other personal information". Prism quotes Mario Trujillo, an Electronic Frontier Foundation attorney: "In the past, to get location data, law enforcement had to use a warrant approved by a judge". That is the attorney's account of tools like this one, not a finding about this contract.

Penlink disputes Prism's description. In a statement Prism printed, a Penlink representative said law enforcement uses PLX "solely to store, organize and analyze their own investigative data", that the data is "lawfully obtained by law enforcement through established legal processes, such as subpoenas, court orders, or warrants", and that "PLX does not provide additional data to law enforcement, such as the real-time communications or location data that this article alleges".

Penlink's own product page, in the Internet Archive copy, lists "Live Collection from Intercept Delivery Standards: J-STD-025, T1.678, T1.IAS" and also states "PLX enables the live collection of phone data, ensuring investigators can track and intercept important communications without delay." That is vendor marketing copy. The legal process for ICE's use of PLX is not in the award record or on that page. The one government account of PLX we read is not ICE's: the Securities and Exchange Commission's 2025 privacy impact assessment notes an application that converts phone records the SEC enforcement division "receives from telecommunication companies via subpoenas".

Whether a government purchase of location or communications data is a Fourth Amendment search is an open question, and no court has decided it (see the question on purchases in [[fourth-amendment-and-location-data-law]]). The contract record is a software and support purchase through a reseller, not a purchase of data, and we found no source on how the task force uses PLX in the field. The same vendor's other ICE products appear in [[2026-08-20--maryland-ag-complaint-penlink-thomson-reuters-geolocation-ice]], and the purchase-instead-of-warrant mechanism in [[2026-03-25--npr-documents-data-broker-loophole-purchase-instead-of-warrant-fisa-702]].

## Correction (2026-11-17)

The entry first ran under a title that called the contract no-bid and described warrantless real-time interception. Several statements did not survive a check against the award record. The headline and text carried a no-warrant-requirement line with no source, and it is removed. A sentence on circumventing Fourth Amendment warrant requirements is removed, because the law is undecided. The entry named Penlink as the contract holder; the award went to Chenega. It also gave interception as the contract's function; that is Prism's description and the vendor's own marketing, not a term of the award. A further line, that ICE cancelled a briefing for a senator's office, was not re-verified and is removed. The lawmakers' demand for an inspector general investigation is covered in [[2026-03-03--72-lawmakers-demand-dhs-oig-probe-warrantless-location-purchases]]. The Padilla and NPR links that supported the removed lines left the sources list with them. The filename changed with the title, and the old id is in previous_ids.
