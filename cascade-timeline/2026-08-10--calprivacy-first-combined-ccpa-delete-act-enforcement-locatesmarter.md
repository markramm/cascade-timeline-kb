The California Privacy Protection Agency (CalPrivacy) announced a decision imposing a $116,490 fine (the law-firm blog Data Protection Report dates the announcement Aug. 10; the agency's release is dated Aug. 11, 2026) on LocateSmarter LLC, an Iowa-based data broker, in the agency's first enforcement action brought under both the California Consumer Privacy Act and the state's Delete Act simultaneously. The release says LocateSmarter failed to register timely as a data broker and required consumers seeking to opt out of data sales to provide partial Social Security numbers — more sensitive data than the opt-out process is legally permitted to demand. CalPrivacy later announced separate orders against two other brokers, Cybba (Aug. 13) and SalesIntel Research (Sept. 1), per Data Protection Report.

California's Delete Act data broker deletion requirements took effect on August 1, 2026, per Data Protection Report. The LocateSmarter case establishes that CalPrivacy will pursue brokers who never registered at all, not only brokers who registered but processed deletions poorly — a meaningfully different and harder-to-detect violation.

## Update (2026-08-11, agency release date): CalPrivacy's release: fine followed a mere handful of opt-out requests

The agency's release, dated August 11, 2026, quotes executive director Tom Kemp saying the fine came "even though a mere handful of consumers submitted requests to opt out". The fine was $116,490.
