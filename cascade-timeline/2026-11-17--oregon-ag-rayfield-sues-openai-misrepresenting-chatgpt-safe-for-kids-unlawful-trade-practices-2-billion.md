Oregon's Department of Justice sued OpenAI on Tuesday, accusing it of violating the state's consumer protection laws by misrepresenting ChatGPT as safe for kids. The complaint calls for OpenAI to institute stronger safeguards and compensate Oregonians with at least $2 billion in damages. Attorney General Dan Rayfield said there was no specific incident in Oregon that led his office to file suit. OPB reports that eight U.S. states sued OpenAI in October.

Related: California's suit over the Hugging Face attack ([[2026-11-16--california-ag-bonta-sues-openai-hugging-face-attack-complaint-cites-over-80-loss-of-control-incidents]]) and Texas's suit ([[2026-10-23--texas-ag-paxton-sues-openai-and-altman-under-dtpa-chatgpt-safety-claims]]).

## Update (2026-11-17, release date): Complaint text pleads costs of not less than $2,035,000,000

The Oregon Department of Justice release is dated November 17, 2026. The complaint's negligence claim pleads that the State of Oregon has suffered injury and will continue to incur costs to remedy the harms inflicted on its residents in an amount to be proved at trial but not less than $2,035,000,000. The figure pleaded in the complaint is therefore $2,035,000,000. This entry's earlier total came from press reporting. All of this is the State's allegation.
