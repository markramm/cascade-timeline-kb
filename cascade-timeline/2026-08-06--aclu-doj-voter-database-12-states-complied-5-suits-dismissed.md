The ACLU and Protect Democracy's suit on behalf of Common Cause and four individual voters to block DOJ's national voter surveillance-and-purge database is proceeding toward a ruling, with new numbers clarifying the fight's scope: of all 50 states DOJ demanded confidential voter data from, 12 have complied voluntarily, while federal courts in Michigan, Oregon, California, Massachusetts, and Rhode Island have dismissed DOJ's own suits seeking to compel data from resistant states. Competing summary-judgment motions — plaintiffs filed May 19, DOJ's counter-motion June 2 — remain pending, with a ruling likely before the November 2026 midterms. The Department has sued thirty states to compel handover; the plaintiff coalition, initially the ACLU and Protect Democracy on behalf of Common Cause and four individual voters, was reported in mid-August as also including Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington.

This is a status update on the suit this corpus first captured at filing ([[2026-07-15--aclu-sues-doj-national-voter-database-nvra-blackout-deadline]]), following the broader DOJ voter-data campaign that began with 18-state lawsuits demanding Social Security numbers and driver's license data ([[2025-12-12--doj-expands-voter-data-lawsuits-to-18-states]]) and the Sixth Circuit's first appellate loss for DOJ on Michigan voter rolls ([[2026-06-24--sixth-circuit-doj-first-appellate-loss-voter-rolls-michigan]]). The 12-complied/5-dismissed split maps a genuine partisan and judicial fault line months ahead of the midterms this database could be used to influence. The litigation record has since run to 0-23 against the Department ([[2026-08-17--minnesota-judge-dismisses-doj-voter-roll-suit-fishing-expedition-0-23]]), including a with-prejudice dismissal in Colorado ([[2026-08-04--doj-voter-roll-colorado-dismissed-with-prejudice-dc-dismissed]]) and a 0-9 dismissal by a Trump appointee in Maryland ([[2026-06-23--doj-voter-roll-maryland-dismissed-0-9-trump-appointee]]) — a near-total losing streak that has not slowed the database, because the aggregation half never depended on winning the compulsion half: the twelve voluntary compliances came from request letters, not court orders ([[2025-09-09--doj-demands-voter-data-centralized-database]]).
