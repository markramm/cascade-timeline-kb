# Boris Epshteyn Pleads Not Guilty to Nine Felony Counts in the Arizona Fake Elector Case; the Case Was Dismissed in June 2026

On **Jun 18, 2024**, Boris Epshteyn, a Trump adviser, pleaded not guilty to nine felony charges in the Arizona fake elector case, per AP. His hearing was the last of 18 arraignments. Epshteyn was charged, not convicted, and the charges below are allegations: AP wrote that he "is accused of assisting Giuliani" in the scheme to submit fake electors and obstruct congressional certification on Jan. 6, 2021.

## Indictment and defense funding

Epshteyn was among those indicted in April 2024 on state charges, per NBC News. NBC reported that Trump's leadership PAC, Save America, made two payments of $40,000 and $10,000 to Tully Bailey LLP, the Phoenix law firm representing him.

## Where the case stands (as of the 2026-11-17 correction)

- **Dismissed.** On Jun 18, 2026, AP reported that Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes was dismissing the case against Mark Meadows, Rudy Giuliani and others. AP said the decision "marks the third such fake elector case filed by states to be dismissed," and that "the Democratic attorney general is vowing to bring it back to a grand jury in hopes of securing another indictment." AP gave the reason as a deadline: "The legal maneuver is aimed at getting around a Friday deadline for starting new grand jury proceedings" after Mayes lost an appeal. KJZZ's later account says "failed to provide relevant evidence to the grand jurors who indicted the defendants" (a Superior Court judge's finding, as KJZZ reports it).
- **Not re-indicted, as far as the sources say.** KJZZ's later story says Mayes "has repeatedly said she plans to bring the case back to a grand jury to seek new indictments" and does not report that a new indictment exists. None of these 2026 sources names Epshteyn; the dismissal covered the whole case (18 defendants, per AP).
- **Not known from the sources read:** whether the dismissal was with or without prejudice (AP's story does not say), whether a new grand jury has been convened, and whether Epshteyn would be named in one.
- A dismissal is not an acquittal. Epshteyn was not convicted of anything in this case, and no charge against him is pending in it as of this writing.
