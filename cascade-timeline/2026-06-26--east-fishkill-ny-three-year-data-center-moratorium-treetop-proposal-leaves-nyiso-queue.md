The East Fishkill Town Board in **Dutchess County, New York** voted **unanimously in late June 2026** to impose a **three-year moratorium** on new data centers, running until **July 1, 2029**. Dozens of residents turned out in support.

The trigger was **Treetop Development's** proposal for a campus described as **more than ten times the scale of the largest existing data center in New York**. That proposal has since **disappeared from the NYISO interconnection queue.**

**The queue departure is the part worth holding.** A moratorium governs what a town will consider; the interconnection queue governs whether a project can get power at all. New York's grid operator processes those requests on its own timeline, independent of any town board. A project can clear every local hurdle and still be years from energization — or, as here, exit the queue without the town having denied anything.

This is the constraint the rest of this lane keeps gesturing at without naming. Counties across the corpus have been negotiating over water, noise, setbacks, and tax treatment — the terms visible at a zoning hearing. **Interconnection is the one that actually gates the buildout**, it is decided at the grid-operator level where no county has standing, and a project's disappearance from a queue is not a public decision anyone can point to. East Fishkill got the outcome it wanted; the record does not establish that its moratorium produced it.

Three years is also long. Against Pierce County's six-month extensions (Washington law bars perpetual moratoria) and Dougherty County's renewable 180-day freeze with "no ceiling," East Fishkill bought a single durable window — and one that will likely outlast the current board.

Councilmember **Marianne Flores** framed it as an opportunity to form a **task force** with planning, utility, environmental, and engineering experts before any future approval. That is the same instrument Bulloch County, Georgia adopted in September with its 21-person citizen group: pause plus a body to write the rules, rather than a permanent ban — a shape that also avoids the litigation posture a flat ban invites, which this timeline records being tested in **Monroe, New Jersey**, where a developer sued over one on First Amendment grounds.

**Sourcing note.** The unanimous vote, the three-year term to July 1, 2029, the Treetop trigger, the ten-times-largest characterization, the NYISO queue departure, and Flores's task-force framing are from **WAMC** and **Data Center Dynamics**, both tier-2; **no tier-1 source covers this**. **The vote date is recorded here as June 26** per WAMC; Spectrum Local News gives June 27 for what appears to be the same board meeting, and the discrepancy is **unresolved** — the town's minutes would settle it. **The "more than ten times" comparison is the reporting's characterization**, not a figure traced to a filing, and no megawatt or square-footage number for the Treetop proposal is recorded here. **Why the proposal left the NYISO queue is not established** — withdrawal, rejection, and lapse are different events with different meanings, and the sources do not distinguish them; that is the obvious check, and NYISO publishes queue data. The Flores quote reached this entry through a local blog cited in the capture ledger and is **paraphrased here rather than quoted**.
