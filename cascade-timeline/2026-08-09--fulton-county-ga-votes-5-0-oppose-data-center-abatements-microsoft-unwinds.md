The Fulton County Board of Commissioners voted 5-0 on August 9, 2026 to oppose tax abatements for data center projects, instructing all county development authorities to stop approving such incentives. Commissioner Bridget Thorne, who sponsored the measure, cited $150 million in abatements granted since 2020 and an estimated $1.1 billion in statewide sales tax revenue forgone in 2026 alone. The vote came the same week Microsoft moved to unwind existing incentive agreements on three Atlanta-area sites.

The convergence is what distinguishes this from the moratorium votes accumulating elsewhere. Moratoria pause siting; this attacks the subsidy, which is the actual transfer mechanism — the county is not saying data centers may not be built, it is saying the public will not pay for them. And the hyperscaler retreat arriving simultaneously suggests the economics were already softening independent of the politics, which is the more interesting signal: Microsoft unwinding three sites is not a concession to Fulton County, it is a portfolio decision that happens to coincide. The corpus has tracked the subsidy fight separately from the siting fight throughout — Guadalupe County TX residents suing over a Cloudburst abatement (2026-07-22--guadalupe-county-tx-residents-sue-cloudburst-tax-abatement), Leon County TX rejecting a $34B Crusoe abatement (2026-07-14--leon-county-tx-rejects-crusoe-34b-tax-abatement), Columbia County GA putting Google tax relief to referendum (2026-07-28--columbia-county-ga-google-datacenter-tax-relief-referendum), and Bell County TX residents demanding an end to Meta abatements (2026-07-07--bell-county-tx-residents-demand-end-meta-data-center-abatements). Whether the resolution binds the Development Authority of Fulton County or merely instructs it is the load-bearing legal question — development authorities are precisely the semi-independent bodies designed to grant incentives at arm's length from the elected officials who would face voters for them.
