The North Port, Florida City Commission voted **4-1 on first reading**, reported **September 4, 2026**, to advance an **18-month moratorium** on new data center applications. WGCU described the hearing as running **seven hours** with roughly **400 public comments** in a packed City Hall. **The final vote is scheduled for September 22.**

**This entry records a step, not an outcome.** A first reading advances an ordinance; it does not adopt one. The 18-month pause is not in effect, and the September 22 vote is where it either takes effect or does not.

The trigger was a developer's filing for an approximately **896,000-square-foot** data center and office campus in North Port's **Yorkshire** area.

**Two things make this the densest public-participation record in the lane.** Seven hours and 400 comments is an order of magnitude past the "dozens of residents" that appears in the East Fishkill, Elkhart, and Barber County accounts. And the September 3 workshop the day before featured presentations from **Florida Power & Light** and the **Peace River Manasota Regional Water Supply Authority** — the utility and the regional water authority, appearing before the city on the two constraints that decide whether a project of this size is feasible.

That is the sequence other jurisdictions in this timeline said they could not get: the power and water providers on the record, in public, *before* the vote. Most of the counties in this corpus have been deciding these questions without them.

Florida now has two in this timeline within days — **Levy County** adopted its own one-year moratorium by ordinance on September 8 — with the reporting indicating several more Florida counties advancing similar pauses.

**Sourcing note.** The 4-1 first-reading vote, the seven-hour hearing, the ~400 comments, the 18-month term, the September 22 final vote, the September 3 workshop, and the FPL and Peace River Manasota participation are from **WGCU**, a PBS/NPR affiliate, corroborated by Sarasota Magazine.
