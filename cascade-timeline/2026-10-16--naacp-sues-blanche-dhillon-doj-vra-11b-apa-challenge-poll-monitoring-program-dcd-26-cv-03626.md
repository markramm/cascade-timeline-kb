The NAACP, the Rev. Andre Miller, Nicholas Little and Daniella Pierre sued Attorney General Todd Blanche (official capacity), Assistant Attorney General Harmeet Dhillon (official capacity) and the Department of Justice in the D.D.C. (No. 1:26-cv-03626); the complaint is stamped Filed 10/16/26 (the filing date). It is a different case from the FOIA suit NAACP v. Dhillon ([[2026-10-02--naacp-sues-dhillon-foia-mandamus-doj-election-monitor-legal-authority-dcd-26-cv-03461]]): it asks the court for relief about the monitoring program itself, not for records.

Claims. Count I is voter intimidation under Section 11(b) of the Voting Rights Act, 52 U.S.C. 10307(b), and 42 U.S.C. 1983. Count II is under the Administrative Procedure Act, 5 U.S.C. 706(2)(A): that DOJ rescinded, without reasoned decision-making, its policy against overt criminal investigations in connection with an election before certification, and that deploying criminal prosecutors as monitors itself violates 10307(b). The complaint also alleges (plaintiffs' allegations, not findings) that "There is no federal statute that gives Defendants the authority to turn polling places into interrogation rooms," that "Federal observers are authorized by statute; the deployment of election monitors is not," and that monitors "have no independent statutory authority to enter polling places without consent from state or local election officials."

Relief sought. The prayer does not ask the court to bar all monitors from polling places. It asks the court to enjoin "recruitment and deployment of criminal prosecutors, DOJ law enforcement agents, and any other criminal enforcement personnel as DOJ election monitors"; to enjoin the "operational plan" to use monitors for law-enforcement purposes, to curtail access to voting locations, or to challenge voter qualifications; to order the government to cease threats to use monitors to intimidate the named plaintiffs and similar voters; and to order a report to the court listing the jurisdictions selected, the notices and any consents, and "a list of the federal civil rights statutes that will be the focus of any such monitoring."

Schedule. Not established from the record read: whether any motion for a temporary restraining order or preliminary injunction has been filed, a hearing date, or an assigned judge. RECAP's copy of the docket, read through the CourtListener API on the evening of the entry's research date, lists the complaint, a notice of appearance by plaintiffs' counsel, a corporate disclosure statement and one later notice of appearance, and nothing else. Treat the absence of a motion in that copy as a dated negative, not a finding that none exists. The complaint alleges (its claim) that the Department has never before deployed more than 600 monitors; the 1,000 figure it attributes to Dhillon's Aug. 17, 2026 announcement.

Function: a suit that asks a court to limit who DOJ may use as monitors, and a complaint that pleads that no statute authorizes staff monitors at all. The government has not yet answered it on the record.
