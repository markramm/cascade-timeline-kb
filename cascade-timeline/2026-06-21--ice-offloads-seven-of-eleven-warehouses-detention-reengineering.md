> **Correction, 2026-08-27 — merged duplicate + labeled dollar figures.** This entry and `2026-06-21--ice-offloads-seven-noem-era-warehouses-mullin-retreat.md` covered the same June 21, 2026 offload announcement with two different dollar totals ($700M here, "$336M-plus" there) and slightly different site framings; the sibling entry has been converted to a stub pointing here, with its Philadelphia Inquirer source folded into this entry's source list. **Neither figure is the program total** — the corpus-verified aggregate acquisition cost across all 11 WEXMAC-TITUS warehouses is **$1.074 billion** (see [[wexmac-titus-warehouse-census]] theme note and the `wexmac-titus-comprehensive-seller-census` research note). The body text below is corrected to state the program total accurately and to characterize "$700 million" as it was reported at the time: an acquisition-cost figure from June 19-21, 2026 reporting that appears to be either a rough approximation of a subset of the eleven purchases or a since-superseded pre-reconciliation estimate — it is not sourced to a line-item breakdown in either underlying article and should not be read as the program total. "$336M-plus," per the Inquirer's June 19 reporting, is closer to (though does not exactly match) the combined purchase price of the specific Pennsylvania and New Jersey sites (Tremont $119.5M + Upper Bern $87.4M + Roxbury $129.3M ≈ $336.2M) named in that day's offload reporting — i.e., a **subset-of-sites acquisition total**, not a program-wide figure, and also not the total of all seven sites ultimately named for offload.

On June 21, 2026, DHS announced it will offload at least 7 of the 11 warehouses it purchased in early 2026 for conversion into mass immigration detention centers, citing community opposition and seven active federal lawsuits. Contemporary reporting cited two different dollar figures for the offloaded warehouses' acquisition cost — roughly $700 million in some accounts, "$336 million-plus" in the Philadelphia Inquirer's June 19 reporting on the Pennsylvania/New Jersey sites specifically — neither of which is the program-wide total; the corpus-verified aggregate cost of **all 11** WEXMAC-TITUS acquisitions is **$1.074 billion**. DHS is retaining only four sites at the time of this announcement — Surprise (AZ), Hagerstown/Williamsport (MD), San Antonio and Socorro (TX). Salt Lake City, which sued in June over the planned mega-detention conversion of its warehouse (up to 10,000 beds per later court filings — see [[2026-06-08--salt-lake-city-county-sue-dhs-ice-warehouse-detention-conversion]]), is among the sites being abandoned. The retreat marks the partial collapse of ICE's "Detention Reengineering Initiative" under new Secretary Markwayne Mullin, reversing a buildout favored by his predecessor Kristi Noem.

This is the first documented reversal in the warehouse-conversion buildout — the centerpiece of ICE's push toward an owned-detention footprint targeting a 100,000-bed network. The collapse was driven by litigation and local resistance, not federal restraint: seven jurisdictions filing in parallel produced enough legal and political cost to force divestiture. Yet the four retained sites still represent a historically unprecedented owned-detention capacity, so the initiative is wounded rather than defeated. The disposition of the seven offloaded properties — whether deed covenants will bar future detention use — remains the open question that determines whether this is a genuine rollback or a tactical regrouping. (As of August 2026, only two of the originally-retained sites — Bexar/San Antonio and Williamsport/Hagerstown — remain actively proceeding; Socorro was scrapped August 11 and Salt Lake was confirmed abandoned August 19, see [[2026-08-19--ice-abandons-salt-lake-city-warehouse-conversion-gsa-excess-property]].)
