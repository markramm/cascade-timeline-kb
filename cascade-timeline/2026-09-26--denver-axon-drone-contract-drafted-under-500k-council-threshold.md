On **September 26, 2026**, 9NEWS reported that the City and County of Denver has drafted a contract with **Axon Enterprise, Inc.** for three **Skydio** drone systems priced at **$496,012.15** — an amount roughly $3,988 below the **$500,000 threshold** above which Denver's Charter and procurement rules require City Council approval by ordinance or resolution before execution. As of this reporting, the document is a **draft, not an executed contract**: Department of Safety spokesperson **Brent Weisberg** told reporters, "The document you submitted is a draft and may not be reflective of final terms or cost," and Police Chief **Ron Thomas** said the city's 2027 budget does not yet include funding for drones, with the department still negotiating final terms with the vendor. **This entry should not be read as reporting an approved or executed contract — verify current status (Legistar filing, committee action, or signed order form) before citing this as final.**

## What Happened / Key Facts

- **Amount and structure**: $496,012.15 for three Skydio drone systems (make/model not specified in the draft as reported), submitted for review to Denver's Citizen Oversight Board at a meeting the Friday before the September 26 report (i.e., on or about September 25, 2026).
- **The threshold**: Denver's $500,000 council-approval threshold governs contracts "which will require or result in the payment or receipt by the City of one-half million dollars ($500,000.00) or more" — Denver Charter provisions on council contract authority, corroborated by the city's own 2019 procurement audit and reinforced by Fiscal Accountability Rule (FAR) 8.1, which bars city employees from "split[ting] purchases to avoid the maximum limits contained in this FAR, Charter, or Revised Municipal Code." The precise charter section (commonly cited alongside §3.2.6, which governs Council resolution procedure) could not be independently confirmed from primary text in this pass — Denver's Municode site is JS-rendered and the city auditor's PDF could not be parsed; flagged as a research gap below.
- **No council record exists yet**: A search of Denver's Legistar system (denver.legistar.com) turned up no bill, resolution, or file number for this drone contract as of this research pass (2026-09-28) — consistent with its draft status and with it never having been submitted to a council committee. Contrast with the *separate, already-approved* Axon ALPR camera contract, Legistar **File #26-0246** ($150,000, 50 cameras, approved 7-6 on March 31, 2026 with Council President Amanda Sandoval breaking a 6-6 tie) — see [[2026-03-31--denver-council-approves-axon-alpr-contract-150k-7-6]] *— needs entry*.
- **Not the first time**: 9NEWS/Yahoo explicitly frame this as a repeat pattern. In 2025, Mayor **Mike Johnston**'s administration extended Denver's prior Flock Safety ALPR contract at **$498,500** — also under the $500,000 threshold — without a council vote, which drew criticism from a majority of council members as "undemocratic" (Denverite, Oct. 29, 2025). When the city later negotiated the $150,000 Axon camera deal, the administration voluntarily sent it to council, "recognizing the controversy" — a step it has not taken (yet) for the drone contract.
- **Oversight pushback**: Citizen Oversight Board member Julia Richman said the sub-threshold structure creates "a middle gray area" where contracts just below the vote line should still get public discussion; board member Alfredo Reyes also raised concerns. The Mayor's Office said it was not involved in the negotiations and that Council will be briefed if the Department of Safety moves forward — but briefing is not a vote.
- **Program context**: Denver Police has run a "drone as first responder" (DFR) pilot since roughly mid-October 2025, using two Skydio X10 drones on a zero-dollar arrangement (contracted through March 2026) launched from the DPD headquarters roof with a two-nautical-mile operating radius, credited with responding to 215 calls for service through December 2025. Flock's competing "Aerodome" drone product was also being piloted on a no-cost trial (through August 2026) as of late 2025. The $496K draft contract would convert (at least part of) this pilot into a paid, Axon/Skydio-only arrangement.

## Why This Event Matters

This is a documented, named instance of the **sub-threshold procurement pattern** — a contract priced just under a legislative-review trigger, so that it needs no public vote — recurring at the same city, with the same vendor family (Axon, having already absorbed the DFR-relevant Skydio integration), within roughly a year of the first instance. The record shows the price and the threshold, not the purpose: no source quotes an official saying the figure was set to avoid a vote, and the Department of Safety says the draft "may not be reflective of final terms or cost" (9NEWS, 2026-09-26). Denver's own Fiscal Accountability Rule 8.1 exists precisely to prohibit this ("purchase splitting"), but FAR 8.1 addresses splitting one purchase into multiple contracts, not structuring a single contract's price under the line — a narrower mechanism this event illustrates.

## Broader Context

Axon's drone push sits inside a broader consolidation: Axon acquired 911-dispatch/data platform **Carbyne** (announced 2025-11-04) and has partnered with **Skydio** to bundle "Axon Air" drone management into its existing body-camera/Evidence.com ecosystem — see [[trump-2-surveillance-procurement-chronology-2025-2026]] and [[thiel-surveillance-defense-portfolio]]. For comparison, neighboring **Douglas County, CO** approved a much larger, fully public **$22.8 million, 10-year Axon contract** (Skydio drones + Axon Outpost + expanded ALPR + a "Drone-First Responder" real-time operations center called "Arctic") by voice vote on July 14, 2026 — demonstrating that a full-scale Axon DFR buildout is an order of magnitude larger than Denver's draft, and that Denver's sub-threshold structuring is a choice, not a reflection of program scope. Separately, **Jefferson County, FL** announced on August 31, 2026 that it would deactivate its 44-camera Flock ALPR network entirely, citing privacy concerns outweighing investigative benefit (Bolts, per Sheriff Mac McNeill) — part of a broader 2026 wave of Flock contract terminations tracked in [[flock-safety-alpr-contract-exits-and-ice-data-sharing]].
