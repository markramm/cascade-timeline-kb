A Brennan Center analysis published August 12, 2026 (data through July 26) documents that DHS has obligated more than $2.9 billion in domestic surveillance contracts since 2021 — $1.1 billion of it in biometrics alone — with the spending rate accelerating sharply: $805 million obligated in 2026 through July 26, already more than any prior full year. The architecture integrates facial recognition, commercial data-broker location feeds, Stingray cell-site simulators, drones, and AI analytics platforms. The report's central finding is that the integration layer, not any single tool, is the threat: networked together, these systems enable persistent identification and tracking of people engaged in lawful activity, and the same infrastructure directly feeds warrantless ICE arrest targeting.

This synthesizes and quantifies a buildout the timeline has tracked piece by piece — the Clearview AI contract with deleted oversight policy ([[2026-06-20--ice-largest-clearview-ai-contract-dhs-deletes-oversight-policy]]), the $513M Palantir-backbone CBP/ICE surveillance spending ([[2026-07-14--ice-cbp-surveillance-spending-513m-palantir-backbone]]), and Mobile Fortify facial recognition rolled out to 1,300 local police departments ([[2026-06-18--dhs-mobile-fortify-facial-recognition-1300-local-police]]). The $805M 2026 pace, annualized, is nearly double the 2025 rate — the acceleration is the story as much as the cumulative total.
