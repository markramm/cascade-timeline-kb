The Bloomington, Illinois city council on Monday unanimously extended its moratorium on data center proposals that use more than 5 megawatts of power, pausing them for at least two years, WGLT reported. The 12-month moratorium proposal was amended by council member Kent Lee to 24 months. The city's planning commission, after hearings on draft data center regulations, had recommended on Nov. 16 that the city extend the moratorium by a year. Speakers pointed to a Nov. 3 advisory ballot question on a moratorium on hyperscale data centers, on which 78% said yes.

City Manager Jeff Jurgens said the city must pursue regulations in case it is not able to ban data centers outright, and suggested state lawmakers could pre-empt home rule. That is the same pattern the timeline tracks elsewhere: local pauses voted in quick succession (Lee County, Georgia, extended its own pause a second time), with the open question being whether a state legislature takes the decision away from the town.
