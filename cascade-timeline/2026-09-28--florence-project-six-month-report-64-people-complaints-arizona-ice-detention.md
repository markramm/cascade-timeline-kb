The Florence Immigrant & Refugee Rights Project, the nonprofit that gives free legal services to people held at three large ICE facilities around Florence and Eloy, Arizona, listed a six-month report on its news page on 09.28.2026.

**Count and period.** The report says it "documented complaints directly from at least 64 people detained in Eloy, FDC, and CAFCC" from January 2026 through June 2026. CALO News (Nov 9, 2026) wrote that people "reported at least 64 cases of abuse and neglect" and gave the period as January to July; the report's own wording is complaints from people, through June.

**Facilities and operators.** The report names Eloy Detention Center, Florence Detention Center and the Central Arizona Florence Correctional Complex. It says Eloy and CAFCC "are private prisons, owned and operated by Corecivic, and FDC is owned and operated by ICE." The Florence Staging Facility is a unit of Florence Detention Center where people are meant to stay no more than 72 hours.

**Accounts included, at class level.** Medical neglect and delayed or denied specialty care for people with chronic illness; dental care limited to waitlists and extraction; three pregnant women and one miscarriage at Eloy; mail and law library failures at Eloy tied to missed court and appeal deadlines; a letter from Eloy signed by 112 other people; and overcrowding, no laundry and no recreation at the staging facility.

**The death the report names.** The report says Emmanuel Damas, a 56-year-old Haitian man, died in March 2026 while in ICE custody at CAFCC. It sets ICE's Detainee Death Report against the Maricopa County Medical Examiner's report. The medical examiner's report is "on file with author" and was not read here.

**Oversight.** The report says ICE oversight mechanisms "have been effectively obsolete or nonexistent" since 2025, that the Office of the Immigration Detention Ombudsman was dismantled in May 2026, and that its complaints to the Office for Civil Rights and Civil Liberties brought no follow-up.

**Responses.** A Department of Homeland Security spokesperson said in a statement to CALO News: "Being in detention is a choice."
