Court filings unsealed in the prosecution of anti-ICE activists in Minnesota show that the Department of Homeland Security surveilled at least five Midwest churches, four in the Twin Cities area and one in Michigan. Plaintiffs in a federal religious-freedom suit say the surveillance at one of them, University Baptist Church in Minneapolis, violated a preliminary injunction. DHS says the injunction did not cover its criminal investigation. No court has found a violation.

## What the record shows

The surveillance itself is not disputed in the filings. Homeland Security Investigations reports, filed in the Minnesota case, describe undercover agents and observation at these churches:

- **December 2025**: an undercover agent at an event at First Universalist Church, Minneapolis
- **April 2026**: an undercover agent at a meeting at St. John the Evangelist Episcopal Church, St. Paul, collecting license plates
- **Between April and June 2026**: agents conducted surveillance around University Baptist Church, Minneapolis, at least three times, recording people entering and leaving and running license plates
- Two more churches affiliated with the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, one in Minnesota and one in Michigan, are listed in the documents as surveilled
- **May 28, 2026**: an undercover agent attended a martial-arts class in the University Baptist gymnasium

During the May 28 session the agent lost a concealed recording device. The HSI report says it "erroneously fell to the floor of the gymnasium near the end of the training," and the agent returned to the gym to retrieve it. The church had posted signs in March saying federal agents were not permitted on the premises under the court order. Church leadership learned of the surveillance only after a defendant in the Minnesota case filed a discovery motion in August.

## The injunction

On **February 13, 2026**, U.S. District Judge F. Dennis Saylor IV entered a preliminary injunction in *New England Synod v. Department of Homeland Security* (No. 4:25-cv-40102-FDS, D. Mass.). Its operative terms are narrower than "no surveillance of churches":

- Paragraph 2: "Absent Exigent Circumstances, Defendants shall not take Immigration Enforcement Action In a Protected Area."
- Paragraph 3: the same bar "Near a Protected Area" (within 100 feet of an entrance) absent Exigent Circumstances or Prior Approval from ICE or CBP headquarters.
- "Immigration Enforcement Action" means any action taken "in whole or in part, to enforce the immigration laws of the United States, including any arrest, seizure, search, interrogation, questioning, interview, or investigation."
- It protects only the plaintiff denominations and their member churches, at locations they list for DHS. It does not bar arrests or searches under a warrant. Paragraph 12 warns that violations "may subject Defendants and all other persons bound by this Order to sanctions and penalties, including contempt of court."

University Baptist belongs to two plaintiff bodies, the Alliance of Baptists and American Baptist Churches USA, and the plaintiffs say it was on each list they gave DHS. The motion at issue concerns University Baptist only. The filings reviewed do not argue that the injunction covered the other four churches.

## The dispute and where it stands

- **August 20, 2026**: the plaintiffs moved for an order to show cause why DHS should not be held in contempt, calling the University Baptist surveillance "egregious violations" of the injunction.
- **August 24**: Judge Saylor granted the motion and ordered DHS to show cause in writing by August 28 why it should not be held in contempt.
- **August 28**: DHS opposed. It argued the University Baptist investigations "were not an Immigration Enforcement Action as defined by the Preliminary Injunction," that HSI has authority to enforce non-immigration laws, and that it "reasonably determined" the order did not apply.
- **September 4**: the court heard the matter by video and took it under advisement. It ordered DHS to file by September 25 a declaration listing every actual or potential violation of the injunction it knew of.
- **September 25**: DHS filed four declarations. It said ICE's enforcement arm identified two potential violations, both raised earlier by the plaintiffs and both at churches elsewhere in the country, and that HSI, CBP's Office of Field Operations and the Border Patrol identified none.
- **September 29**: the plaintiffs answered that DHS's searches were inadequate and urged the court to impose compliance safeguards in its contempt remedy.

As of the docket's November 9, 2026 update on CourtListener, the show-cause motion had not been decided on the docket entries reviewed, and no order finds that DHS violated the injunction. The question of whether an undercover investigation counts as an Immigration Enforcement Action remains open. In October the case moved to a second motion on behalf of plaintiffs the injunction does not yet cover, and an appeal of the injunction is pending in the First Circuit.

## Response

Rev. **Cody Sanders**, University Baptist's interim pastor: "We should not become accustomed to this. We should not let this become sort of the taken-for-granted, status-quo norm that we just expect, because this is wrong."

## Significance

This is the church-facing half of the infiltration posture documented in Operation Puppet Master. The record establishes that surveillance reached houses of worship, including inside a church gymnasium at a location the plaintiffs had designated. Whether it broke Judge Saylor's order is the pending question, and the answer turns on how the court reads "in whole or in part, to enforce the immigration laws."
