- Report, Oct 15 (Reuters, one unnamed US official): the administration is considering easing sanctions on some shadow-fleet vessels to cut record freight rates. Per the relay, the talks "centre on the United States potentially allowing 'legal oil' to flow on previously sanctioned vessels", meaning crude or products Washington has not itself sanctioned, as opposed to Russian and Iranian oil, which stay under sanctions.
- Mechanism, if approved: individual Treasury licences for companies using those tankers. No OFAC action, general license or Federal Register notice has been found; this is a consideration, not a decision.
- Vessels covered: the tracker and the Dawn syndication say vessels that have carried Russian, Iranian and Venezuelan oil; the Ukrainska Pravda relay says Russian and Iranian. The Venezuelan element is not span-confirmed here.
- Wright: Ukrainska Pravda says Energy Secretary Chris Wright "confirmed" in a World Petroleum Congress (Riyadh) video address that easing is under consideration. The Dawn/Reuters syndication, per its summary, instead attributes this to Energy Aspects' account and says Reuters could not obtain the video or a transcript, and the Department of Energy said it was not available. Treat Wright's confirmation as unverified until the video or transcript is read.
- Skeptics, per the relay: Energy Aspects expects little effect, since the old tankers have mostly been trading already and EU/UK insurance rules would still bind.

Not established: the Reuters original was blocked and not read; the "official" is unnamed; nothing on the record from Treasury.
