Five U.S. senators (Alan Cranston, Dennis DeConcini, John Glenn, John McCain and Donald Riegle) met with Keating and with Federal Home Loan Bank Board enforcement officials while regulators were investigating Lincoln Savings and Loan. The Dayton Daily News reported: "Keating donated $1.3 million overall to the senators' campaigns."

The Senate Ethics Committee's findings differed by senator, as the Dayton Daily News gives them. Glenn was "cleared" of improper conduct. The committee called DeConcini's intervention with regulators "inappropriate", and said DeConcini and Riegle's conduct "appeared improper, although it fell short of actual impropriety". It found "substantial credible evidence" that Cranston acted improperly. On November 22, 1991, Reuters reported that the committee reprimanded Cranston for "improper and repugnant" conduct.

The sources read here are press accounts. The committee's own report was not fetched.
