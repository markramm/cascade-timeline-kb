Federal agents from **ICE New Orleans**, operating as **Operation Corral Light**, arrested **121 people** at the El Corralon nightclub in Memphis's Berclair neighborhood on the night of **August 30, 2026**. DHS confirmed the arrest count in a September 1 press release. Immigration advocates describe it as the largest raid in the city's history; by arrest count it ranks second among single-day ICE operations in any U.S. city in 2026, behind a 157-arrest operation in Florence, Arizona. Memphis Mayor **Paul Young** condemned the raid. Subsequent reporting found several detainees were released amid questions about the operation's legal basis.

**The warrant-scope question is the open one.** DHS states agents entered on a warrant to search for drugs and illegal gambling. What followed was the mass arrest of 121 people on immigration grounds. Whether the warrant authorized that is not established on the public record, and the actual warrant has not been obtained — a lead is open to pull it and compare its scope against who was actually detained. The subsequent releases are the tell worth tracking: people released after arrest are people the operation could not sustain a basis to hold.

This continues the pattern documented in Memphis since the SAFE Task Force racial-profiling findings ([[2025-11-04--memphis-safe-task-force-racial-profiling]]) and the ACLU's First Amendment suit against it ([[2026-05-13--aclu-memphis-safe-task-force-first-amendment-lawsuit]]), and it matches the collateral-arrest structure measured in Operation Metro Surge, where 1,300 arrests were of people who were not the named targets ([[2026-04-08--ice-data-reveals-1300-collateral-arrests-operation-metro-surge]]). A venue raid is a collateral-arrest engine by construction: everyone present is screened, and the predicate that opened the door is not the one most arrests rest on.
