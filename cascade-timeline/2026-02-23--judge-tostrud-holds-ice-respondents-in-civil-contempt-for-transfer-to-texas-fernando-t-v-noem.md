U.S. District Judge Eric C. Tostrud signed an opinion and order dated February 23, 2026 in a habeas case in the District of Minnesota, *Fernando T. v. Noem*, No. 26-cv-445 (ECT/EMB). The order states: "Respondents’ conduct constitutes civil contempt," and its decretal paragraph finds the respondents in civil contempt of the January 20 order. The respondents are the federal officials sued in their official capacities, among them the Secretary of Homeland Security and the acting ICE director. The January 20 order enjoined them from moving the petitioner out of Minnesota. The court recorded that they "acknowledge that the Minnesota-to-Texas transfer violated an order that enjoined Respondents from moving Fernando to a location outside of Minnesota." ICE moved the petitioner to Texas and released him in El Paso.

The remedy is compensatory. The court awarded $568.29, the documented cost of the petitioner's return airfare to Minnesota, and ordered the respondents to pay it on or before April 1, 2026, to the petitioner or to counsel if counsel advanced it. The order takes effect March 1, 2026, and gives the respondents until the end of February to ask for an evidentiary hearing; absent a request, judgment is entered on the order. The court wrote: "We do not know which of the Respondents was directly responsible for the January 20, 2026 Order’s violation," and invited a motion identifying the responsible respondent. It is a civil contempt finding against the respondents jointly and severally. It names no individual and orders no jail or fine beyond the compensation.

## Correction, 2026-11-16 — judge, date, findings and remedy

An earlier version of this entry was dated February 24 and credited the finding to Judge Jeffrey Bryan. The order the entry describes is Judge Tostrud's, dated February 23, 2026 (a civil contempt finding in a different case from those Judge Bryan presided over), and the entry is renamed to that date; the old id is kept in `previous_ids`. The earlier text also said ICE had violated a 72-hour out-of-state transfer bar and a one-hour attorney-access requirement, that the finding rested on a transfer to Texas in defiance of Bryan's order, and that the court ordered a U.S. Attorney and ICE representatives to a formal contempt hearing on March 3. None of this is in the Tostrud order, which sets no hearing for the respondents' counsel; the 72-hour and one-hour terms and the hearing are not found in any order read, and the entry no longer states them. The cited MPR News URL returned not-found on 2026-11-16, and the Washington Post and Courthouse News pieces were not read, so they are dropped from the sources.
