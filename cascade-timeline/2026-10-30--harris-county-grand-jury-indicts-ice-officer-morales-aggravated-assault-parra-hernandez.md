A Texas grand jury indicted ICE officer Mario Alberto Morales on a charge of aggravated assault with a deadly weapon, alleging he drove his vehicle into Miguel Parra Hernandez, who was on foot, earlier in October in Houston. The indictment was returned by a Harris County grand jury Friday morning, District Attorney Sean Teare said ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/ice-agent-indicted-striking-injuring-immigrant-vehicle-houston-rcna606370)). An indictment is an accusation.

Morales was hired by ICE in August 2025, his attorney, David Lindenmuth, said, adding that he had more training than other hires. A Texas Department of Criminal Justice spokeswoman said he resigned from TDCJ in 2018 while on probation for falsification of records, documenting security rounds as completed when they had not been conducted ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/indicted-ice-officer-job-little-year-trumps-hiring-surge-comes-scrutin-rcna607033)). An ICE spokesperson said "allegations that ICE struck Miguel Parra-Hernandez intentionally are RECKLESS and FALSE."

## Correction (2026-11-17)

The title of this entry originally stated as fact that the officer was hired in August 2025. The only source for that date in the reporting read is his attorney, David Lindenmuth; the NBC News article carries no confirmation from ICE, DHS or another agency, so the date is attributed to him in the text and left out of the title. Removal status as of the latest docket read: the defense said at his surrender that it anticipates moving the case to federal court (Houston Public Media), and the District Attorney said he also anticipated a removal effort; as of the monitor's last read and a CourtListener search made for this correction (Southern District of Texas, filed after the indictment, no match; RECAP holds only what someone has pulled from PACER), no notice of removal has been found. The next state court hearing is set for December 2, according to the defense. Indicted is not convicted.

## Update (2026-11-18, publication date): A Houston TV station reports personnel records showing two disciplinary probations at the officer's former prison job

A Houston television station reported on Nov. 18 that Morales was disciplined twice at his former job as a Texas correctional officer, according to personnel records it obtained. A warden put him on disciplinary probation for three months for failing to report a use of force. The warden later imposed another 10 months of disciplinary probation after finding he had falsified records. His employment ended April 2, 2018. Morales has pleaded not guilty and is free on a $30,000 bond, according to the report. His defense team says it plans to seek to move the case to federal court. His attorney questioned what the findings have to do with the criminal case. An indictment is an accusation.

## Update (2026-10-30, news conference and release of video and 911 audio): Public media assets (Video, 911 call)

At a news conference on Oct. 30, the Harris County District Attorney's office released public media assets in the case: a video and a 911 call ([DA's office](https://dao.harriscountytx.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/media-assets-grand-jury-returns-indictment-in-ice-related-crash-in-west-houston)). Surveillance footage from a nearby business, the Texas Tribune reported, showed a black SUV driven by Morales pursuing one of the men from behind. The District Attorney's office also shared ICE body camera footage that showed Morales swerve toward Parra-Hernandez at the moment of impact ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/10/30/ice-agent-indictment-criminal-charges-texas/)).

In the 911 call the office released, Morales said, "I tried to get around him, and he hit my car." Morales told another ICE agent nearby, "I was coming and I hit him with the car." Teare said Morales was one of the surge hires in 2025. ICE said agents were searching for a man; that man's daughter later told the New York Times that her father died nearly a decade ago. Teare said Parra-Hernandez is in a rehabilitation facility and still faces deportation. An indictment is an accusation.
