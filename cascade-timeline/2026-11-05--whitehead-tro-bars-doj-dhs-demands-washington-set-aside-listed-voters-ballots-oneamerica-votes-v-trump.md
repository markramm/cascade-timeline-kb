U.S. District Judge Jamal N. Whitehead granted in part the plaintiffs' TRO motion in OneAmerica Votes v. Trump (2:26-cv-04223) on 11/05/26. Defendants DOJ, Attorney General Todd Blanche, DHS, Secretary of Homeland Security Markwayne Mullin and the federal prosecutors who had sent Secretary of State Steve Hobbs a list of roughly 400 registered voters are RESTRAINED from directing or demanding that any Washington state, county, or local election official remove specific registered voters from their registration lists, refuse them ballots, or set aside or not count their ballots, and from threatening officials who refuse.

The court found plaintiffs likely to succeed on their claim that defendants exceeded their authority. Reading the Ninth Circuit's rulings, it held the government may ask a State to set ballots aside but may not compel one to do so. Nothing in this order restrains defendants from providing information or investigating unlawful voting. The order lasts FOURTEEN (14) days unless extended, and the court denied the government's request for an administrative stay. Defendants respond by November 12, 2026, and the court will hear the motion on November 18, 2026.

## Update (2026-11-12, filing date): DOJ opposes a preliminary injunction, saying the matter has ended

In a response entered on the docket 11/12/2026, the defendants opposed the plaintiffs' motion for a preliminary injunction (W.D. Wash. 2:26-cv-04223). They point to Secretary Hobbs' refusals to act on Defendants’ list and to the injunction entered on November 2 in League of Women Voters v. DHS, which they say bars them from sending such lists to election officials, and conclude: "Thus, the matter has ended." They write: "On this record, Plaintiffs lack standing because there is no injury in fact." In the alternative, they argue that "Any TRO Should be Limited, Accompanied by Security, and be Stayed".
