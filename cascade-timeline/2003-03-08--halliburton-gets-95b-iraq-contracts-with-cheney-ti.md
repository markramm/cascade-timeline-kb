Halliburton subsidiary KBR received Iraq War contracts worth more than $1.7 billion by September 2003, including a no-bid 'Restore Iraqi Oil' contract awarded March 8, 2003. Vice President Dick Cheney, Halliburton's former CEO (1995-2000), maintained financial ties through a deferred compensation account valued at between $500,000 and $1 million and stock options comprising 433,333 shares, per his 2001 financial disclosure. Whistleblower Bunnatine Greenhouse, the Army Corps' top procurement official, called it 'the most blatant contract abuse' in her career and was demoted after testifying. KBR was accused of overcharging for fuel and services. The Defense Contract Audit Agency found systematic billing deficiencies. Despite criminal investigations by the Pentagon Inspector General, KBR kept receiving contracts throughout the war. This represented the largest wartime contracting scandal in U.S. history, with minimal oversight of a company whose former CEO was sitting Vice President.
