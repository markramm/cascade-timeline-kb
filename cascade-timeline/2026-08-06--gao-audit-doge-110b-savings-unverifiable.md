GAO report GAO-26-108615, published August 6, 2026, found DOGE's "Wall of Receipts" claim of $110 billion in federal savings is systematically inflated: 96% of claimed grant savings lack any verifiable methodology, 2,503 contracts totaling $27.4 billion in claimed savings show no actual termination action ever taken, and roughly two-thirds of all contract-savings figures are unverifiable or methodologically indefensible. Senate Democrats Gary Peters and Richard Blumenthal, who requested the review, called it proof DOGE misled the public about its results. DOGE had not responded to the findings as of publication.

The audit is the authoritative government reckoning of the efficiency-campaign disinformation the timeline has tracked since DOGE's creation — following reporting that real federal auditors were "horrified" by DOGE's methods ([[2025-03-18--it-s-a-heist-real-federal-auditors-are-horrified-by-doge]]) and Congress's own rejection of DOGE's spending-cuts analysis ([[2026-02-19--doge-defeat-congress-rejects-spending-cuts-analysis]]). It also pairs directly with GAO's finding one day earlier that the White House stonewalled DOGE oversight requests from half of federal agencies ([[2026-08-05--gao-white-house-stonewalled-doge-oversight-half-agencies-refused]]) — an obstruction-plus-fabrication pattern: the administration blocked independent verification of DOGE's claims while GAO's own audit, once conducted anyway, found the claims themselves didn't hold up.
