A three-judge Ninth Circuit panel of Circuit Judges McKeown, Friedland and Miller denied the government's emergency motion for a stay pending appeal of a preliminary injunction in *L.T. v. ICE*, No. 26-4902. The order bears the clerk's filing stamp of August 20, 2026, and appears on the district docket as Doc. 122 with the same filing date, 08/20/26. Migrant Insider's report says the judges ruled Thursday. The panel wrote: "we deny the Government’s emergency motion for a stay pending appeal" and lifted its earlier administrative stay. It also reset the merits briefing, with the appellants' opening brief due September 17, 2026. The class action, brought by Public Counsel, CHIRLA and the Immigrant Defenders Law Center, challenges conditions at the Adelanto ICE Processing Center in San Bernardino County, California. The panel's order points to the district court's memorandum of decision of August 12, 2026 for the reasons the injunction issued. The injunction the government sought to stay was entered by the district court in July 2026 and requires ICE to provide detainees with clean drinking water around the clock, nutritious meals, outdoor recreation, clean clothing, mattresses and hygiene products at no cost.

The load-bearing holding is the panel's reliance on *West v. Atkins* (1988), the Supreme Court decision that a private physician under contract to a state prison acts under color of state law and remains subject to constitutional constraint. The order states that the government is "ultimately responsible for ensuring that the conditions of confinement are constitutionally acceptable" even if it does not run the facility day to day. Contracting the operation of Adelanto to GEO Group does not, on the panel's reading, relieve the federal government of its own duty toward the people it detains. The panel also rejected the government's reliance on *Trump v. CASA*, writing that the injunction sets out obligations to a certified class at a single facility, "not anything universal or nationwide."

A Colorado decision of the same date went the other way for the same contractor: Chief Judge Daniel Domenico enjoined Colorado's expanded health-inspection mandate over GEO's Aurora facility on federal-preemption grounds ([[2026-08-20--federal-judge-blocks-colorado-health-inspection-authority-geo-aurora]]). GEO won the argument that federal contracting displaces state oversight, while the government lost the argument that federal contracting displaces its own constitutional duty. If states may not inspect and the outsourcing does not transfer responsibility, the federal courts hearing conditions claims are the remaining forum. Adelanto is where that matters most concretely. The facility recorded its fourth death in seven months in March, a California record ([[2026-03-30--adelanto-geo-fourth-death-california-record-seven-months]]), and the corpus documented Carlitos Ricardo Parias held roughly 300 days there with an untreated gunshot wound ([[2026-08-16--parias-shot-journalist-300-days-adelanto-untreated-wound]]).

## What followed (read from the orders)

The district court modified the injunction on August 28, 2026 and again on September 11, 2026. On October 13, 2026 the Ninth Circuit denied the government's request for an administrative stay of those revisions: "The request is denied." The panel cited the government's month-long delay and added: "We will rule on the government’s motion for a stay pending appeal in due course." On October 19, 2026 the district court denied the government's ex parte application for a stay pending appeal of the modifications: "the Ex Parte Application is DENIED." In an order filed 10/21/26 the Ninth Circuit ruled on that motion: "We therefore deny the motion for an emergency stay pending appeal." A footnote on the monitors' ex parte communications with the court says "the Government has not identified any harm that this provision has caused."
