The FAA says that on November 13 it issued a nationwide ground stop, which ABC News describes as covering all airports and lasting more than four hours. The FAA says air traffic resumed as of 8:15 p.m. that day and that operators canceled approximately 12,462 flights on November 13. The FAA's account is that legacy equipment became unstable when data flooded its network at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds, with instability at 17 of its 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers. Its initial investigation points to possible origin sites, including a radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where L3 Harris, its network service provider, had deployed a new router. The FAA says it has no indication of a cyberattack, and Administrator Bryan Bedford said "zero evidence of anything nefarious, and zero evidence of anything AI-related", attributing the stop to faulty equipment.

The cause is not settled, and each account comes from a party with a stake in it. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a preliminary investigation found an L3Harris contractor was switching copper wire to fiber while replacing a router, and that the FAA is determining whether this was human error or a faulty router. ABC News reports that the routers believed central to the stop were made by Juniper, owned by Hewlett Packard Enterprise; an HPE spokesperson said the device "functioned as it was configured to by the installer". President Trump accused the Biden administration of making an "old and broken system worse than when they began". CBS News reports this was the first nationwide ground stop since January 2023.
