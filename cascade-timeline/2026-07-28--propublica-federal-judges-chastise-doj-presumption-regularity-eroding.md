A ProPublica investigation published July 28, 2026 documents a nationwide pattern: federal judges — appointees of both parties, including Trump appointees — have repeatedly called out DOJ attorneys for "unlawful," "unethical," and "unseemly" conduct in courtrooms spanning D.C., Rhode Island, and Oregon, among other jurisdictions. The through-line is erosion of the "presumption of regularity" — the foundational judicial assumption that government lawyers litigate in good faith and that the executive branch's representations to courts can be taken at face value.

This is a systemic capture story distinct from any single case: it documents the DOJ's institutional credibility with the judiciary itself deteriorating in real time, connecting to the broader pattern of DOJ leadership actions this timeline has tracked — including the politicization of ethics enforcement ([[2026-01-27--bove-delegates-doj-ethics-authority-political-aides]]) and contested dismissals characterized as unlawful by courts ([[2026-02-09--doj-appeals-comey-james-dismissals-halligan-unlawful]]). Where those entries document individual incidents, this ProPublica investigation is the first to synthesize the pattern across jurisdictions as a named, systemic erosion.
