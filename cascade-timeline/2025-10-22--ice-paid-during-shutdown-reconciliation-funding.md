Federal News Network reported on October 17, 2025 that DHS Secretary Kristi Noem posted on X on Thursday afternoon that more than 70,000 federal law enforcement officials, including employees from Customs and Border Protection, Immigration and Customs Enforcement and the Secret Service, as well as federal air marshals, will be paid for all hours worked during the shutdown. Noem wrote that they will receive a "super check" by Oct. 22 that covers shutdown days already worked without pay, their overtime and their next pay period. Fortune reported on the 22nd, as the shutdown moved into its fourth week, that more than 700,000 federal employees were going without pay, and that a Department of Homeland Security spokesperson confirmed that border patrol agents, ICE deportation officers, Secret Service special agents and TSA air marshals will continue to be paid. Their pay is covered under the One Big Beautiful Bill, which gave ICE an extra $75 billion in funding.

Noem's post said: "President Trump and I will always stand by law enforcement, and we are keeping our promise to always support them by making sure they are paid during the Democrats' shutdown." A DHS spokesperson, not Noem, said in a statement that the One Big Beautiful Bill Act will ensure the employees "will be having their pay covered during the Democrats' government shutdown." Federal News Network adds that the reconciliation bill "gave DHS additional funds and few conditions on how to spend that money." President Donald Trump said at the White House: "We got the people that we want paid, paid."

The decision left others out. The president of the National Treasury Employees Union said it "does not apply to other CBP employees who also do vital work facilitating international trade and travel." A TSA spokesperson said TSA officers are not covered, "as they are not law enforcement officers."
