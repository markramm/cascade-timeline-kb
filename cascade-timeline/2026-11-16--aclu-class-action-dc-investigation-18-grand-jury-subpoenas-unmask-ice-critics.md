Four pseudonymous social media users filed a class action in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia on Monday, November 16 (filing date), challenging grand jury subpoenas that the ACLU says belong to an investigation called "Investigation 18," which targets people who anonymously criticize the Trump administration's immigration enforcement online. The ACLU says the subpoenas for the four users went to Bluesky, X (formerly known as Twitter), and Meta, and that the plaintiffs' initial filings point to 35 subpoenas related to Investigation 18. The case was filed under seal. AP reports that Chief Judge James Boasberg ordered it unsealed on Wednesday, and the ACLU said in a November 27, 2026 release that the unsealed filings were placed on the public docket that day.

According to AP, ACLU lawyers say U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro's office has used the term "Investigation 18," and Pirro, Attorney General Todd Blanche and DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin are named as defendants. The suit argues the investigation violates the First and Fourth Amendments, and the ACLU says the investigation "is a bad-faith fishing expedition initiated to gather intrusive intelligence and facilitate retaliation against dissenters." The plaintiffs are seeking a preliminary injunction to halt the investigation. The ACLU says DHS sent hundreds of administrative subpoenas to tech companies in the last year seeking information about ICE critics, and that, facing pushback from users and tech companies, the government began shifting to grand jury subpoenas, which are subject to secrecy rules. AP reports that spokespeople for DHS and Pirro's office didn't immediately respond to requests for comment.

The suit follows an earlier amicus push in the D.C. Circuit over a grand jury subpoena used to unmask a Reddit critic after DHS withdrew its own administrative subpoena, and HSI's own estimate, in EFF's FOIA suit, of how many administrative subpoenas it has issued. The shift from administrative to grand jury process moves the same unmasking effort behind grand jury secrecy.
