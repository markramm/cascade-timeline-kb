TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC published, on the date its newsroom gives for the post (the entry's date field), its plan for the 2026 midterms. It says the plan "centers on four priorities", and that during the election cycle it expands its protections through "our dedicated U.S. Election Mission Control Center, which operates around the clock", staffed by "cross-functional teams from security, trust & safety, engineering and more". It also says it launched a "U.S. Election Center" that links to Democracy Works, the National Association of Secretaries of State, Ballotpedia and the Associated Press, and that "Paid political ads are prohibited on TikTok in the United States."

The post says no feature or code reaches U.S. users "without first undergoing comprehensive security review", "assisted by our Trusted Security Partner". This page does not name that partner. The company's January 23, 2026 formation announcement does: it describes software assurance "assisted by its Trusted Security Partner, Oracle", and says the venture holds "decision-making authority for trust and safety policies and moderation". So the October post describes election-season moderation and security review as functions of the Oracle-linked joint venture, in the company's own words.

Why it matters: election-content rules and security review on a platform the company says serves more than 200 million Americans now sit with a venture in which Oracle is a 15% investor and the named security partner (the 2025 sale is in the entry tiktok-sale-oracle-ellison-murdoch). Social Media Today, a trade outlet, published its report on Oct. 5, 2026 and wrote that TikTok outlined its preparation measures "on Monday", a day before the newsroom's own date; the entry follows the company's date. The post is a statement of policy, not evidence of how it is applied. The venture's algorithm-licence terms and any formal divestiture determination remain unconfirmed.
