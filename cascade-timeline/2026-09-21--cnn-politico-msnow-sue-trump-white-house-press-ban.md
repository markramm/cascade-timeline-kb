On **September 21, 2026**, three days after President Trump announced he was "banning" **CNN**, **Politico**, and **MS NOW** from the White House complex, the three outlets — together with reporters **Betsy Klein** (CNN), **Akayla Gardner** (Politico), and **Cheyenne Haslett** (Politico) — filed suit in the **U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia**. The case is **Cable News Network, Inc. v. Trump**, No. **1:26-cv-03287**, assigned to **Judge Timothy J. Kelly** (a 2017 Trump appointee). Named defendants are Trump, White House Communications Director **Steven Cheung**, and Chief of Staff **Susan Wiles**; Trump himself was later excused from the operative TRO. Lead counsel is **Theodore J. Boutrous Jr.** of Gibson Dunn, with the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press's **Bruce D. Brown** and **KatieLynn Boyd Townsend** also appearing for plaintiffs.

## What was filed

Docket entry 1 is a **Complaint for Declaratory and Injunctive Relief** (filing fee receipt ADCDC-12686613), filed the same day alongside docket entry 2, a **Motion for a Temporary Restraining Order** supported by declarations from Gardner, Klein, Haslett, and CNN/Politico/MS NOW executives (David Vigilante, J. Marsh, Sudeep Reddy) plus a declaration from Boutrous himself and a certificate of actual notice to defendants. The complaint alleges the credential revocations — Secret Service confiscation of hard passes at the White House gate on or about September 18-19 — violate (1) the **First Amendment**, as retaliation for and viewpoint discrimination against the content of the outlets' reporting, and (2) the **Fifth Amendment's Due Process Clause**, because no notice, stated standard, or opportunity to respond preceded the revocations.

That same day, Judge Kelly issued a standing order and a **minute order** directing defendants to respond to the TRO motion by September 22 and setting a **TRO hearing for 3:30 p.m. on September 23, 2026**, by videoconference.
