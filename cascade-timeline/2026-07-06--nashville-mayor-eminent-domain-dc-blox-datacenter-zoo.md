Nashville Mayor Freddie O'Connell filed late-breaking eminent domain condemnation legislation targeting a 23.5-acre site adjacent to the Nashville Zoo where DC Blox holds a $23M purchase contract set to close in early July. Metro Council votes July 7 on whether to even consider the bill — itself a procedural threshold requiring majority support. A separate data-center regulations ordinance (500,000 sq ft / 100 MW caps, strict setbacks from schools, churches, and zoos, 65 dBA noise limits) is on its second of three readings the same night. More than 500,000 petition signatures oppose the project.

The fight combines two resistance tools simultaneously: executive eminent domain as an emergency brake and council-level zoning regulation as a permanent gate — a more aggressive posture than the moratorium approach taken elsewhere in the current datacenter-siting wave (see [[2026-06-30--washington-county-md-datacenter-moratorium]], [[2026-07-02--santa-fe-county-nm-data-center-moratorium-18-months]]).

## Update (2026-11-10, date of zoning administrator's note): Metro zoning administrator says DC Blox's zoo-adjacent permit falls under pre-moratorium rules; zoning appeals board to hear the zoo's challenge

Nashville's zoning administrator wrote in a note to the Board of Zoning Appeals on Nov. 10 that DC Blox's building-permit submission of May 29, 2026 “was and continues to be subject to the rules applicable at the time of the filing of the application,” and that “There were no rules applicable to data centers at the time of its filing,” the Nashville Banner reported. The board meets Dec. 3 to consider appeals from the zoo and Metro Councilmember Courtney Johnston.

The Banner reports that a federal judge sided with Metro last month in DC Blox's suit alleging the city unfairly targeted the company, and that DC Blox has since filed an amended complaint. The city's moratorium on new data center applications expires and will not be extended, and there has been no update on the mayor's proposed use of eminent domain.
