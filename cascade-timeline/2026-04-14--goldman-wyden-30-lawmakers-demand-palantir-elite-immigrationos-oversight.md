Thirty lawmakers led by Rep. **Dan Goldman**, Rep. **Nydia Velázquez** and Sen. **Ron Wyden** sent DHS a demand letter on **April 14, 2026** seeking transparency on two Palantir-developed ICE systems: **ELITE**, described in court testimony as functioning like Google Maps to identify the neighborhood locations of targeted individuals, and **ImmigrationOS**, a $30 million AI-enabled platform contracted to give ICE near-real-time visibility into what its own procurement language calls the immigration lifecycle.

**The spending figure is the structural fact.** Funding awarded to eleven surveillance-technology firms by ICE and CBP doubled from 2024 to 2025, reaching over **$310 million**, then spiked to **$513 million in 2026** — a jump driven specifically by Palantir contracts and by Anduril's border towers, drones and associated AI systems. Palantir has received over $1.8 billion in federal funding since the start of the second Trump term, including $81 million from ICE in 2025 and $97 million in 2026, followed by a $1 billion purchase agreement.

**Sourcing note — attribution corrected at write time.** The capture ledger credited the $513M and $310M figures to ACLU. That is wrong, and a mechanical title-figure check caught it: **neither figure appears in the ACLU roundup or the Goldman press release.** They originate in a separate contract study by **Mijente, Just Futures Law and the Surveillance Resistance Lab**, tracking ICE and CBP awards to eleven surveillance-technology firms from 2013 to 2026, reported by Truthout on 2026-06-27 and now cited above. The figures have **not** been reconciled line-by-line against USASpending award records, and a lead to do exactly that is open. Cite them as that study's findings — not as ACLU's, and not as government-published totals.

The dates also differ in kind and must not be flattened: the **letter** is dated April 14, 2026; the **spending study** reporting the $513M figure was published June 27, 2026. This entry is dated to the letter.

This is the second such letter from substantially the same members — the first went to Palantir CEO Alex Karp in June 2025 ([[2025-06-17--wyden-goldman-velazquez-letter-karp-palantir-ice-surveillance]]) — and it lands inside a documented pattern in which the oversight request is the visible artifact and the answer never arrives. The ELITE confidence-score methodology the letter asks about remains undisclosed ([[2026-08-14--dhs-ai-inventory-200-use-cases-palantir-elite-confidence-scores-911-ingestion]]), and DHS filed zero privacy impact assessments against the $1 billion no-bid Palantir vehicle ([[2026-05-13--dhs-zero-privacy-impact-assessments-palantir-1-billion-no-bid]]). Congress asks; the procurement record stays blank.
