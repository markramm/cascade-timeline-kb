On November 16, 2026, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security released an interim final rule on remote access to advanced "super intelligence" chips and servers. BIS says the rule was written by and at the direction of the Department of Treasury and the National Security Council, and that it is designed to ensure that existing restrictions on access to sensitive American technology cannot be circumvented through remote computing arrangements involving data centers in third countries. It describes the rule as aligning protections on remote access with existing controls on the physical shipment of that technology.

NPR reports that the move comes amid the fallout at Nvidia, and that before the Nvidia scandal the Trump administration had resisted imposing additional regulations to prevent foreign access to American AI technology. The Nvidia thread is in [[2026-11-08--nvidia-board-opens-investigation-after-reports-ceo-huang-targeted-by-chinese-intelligence-affair]] and [[2026-10-29--nvidia-huang-megaspeed-ties-normal-after-report-executive-under-us-scrutiny-china]].
