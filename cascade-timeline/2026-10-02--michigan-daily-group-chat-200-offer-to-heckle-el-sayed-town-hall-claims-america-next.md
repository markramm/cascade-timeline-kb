The Michigan Daily's story by Glenn Hedin, dated October 2, 2026, rests on group-chat messages.
Students affiliated with the University of Michigan College Republicans were offered $200 each to
travel to Abdul El-Sayed's Saturday town hall in Flint and target him with boos and difficult
questions. The student who posted the offer in the GroupMe chat was "claiming to be working on
behalf of America Next". One message read: "We are going to coordinate questions to ask him and
spread out to increase the chances we’re called on. We’re gonna boo him."

The Daily's evidence is the messages themselves. It has no document or payment showing that America
Next made or funded the offer. America Next did not respond to requests for comment, and the Daily
could find no evidence that Rogers’ campaign had any direct involvement. The paper labels America
Next a political action committee, but in its IRS and FEC filings it is a nonprofit that files
independent-expenditure reports as a non-committee.
