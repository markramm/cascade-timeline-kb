The Michigan Daily's story by Glenn Hedin, dated October 2, 2026, rests on group-chat messages.
Students affiliated with the University of Michigan College Republicans were offered $200 each to
travel to Abdul El-Sayed's Saturday town hall in Flint and target him with boos and difficult
questions. The Daily's headline calls it a conservative PAC's offer, and its text says the student who
posted the offer in the GroupMe chat was "claiming to be working on behalf of America Next, the
conservative political action committee financing the initiative." One message read: "We are going to coordinate questions to ask him and
spread out to increase the chances we’re called on. We’re gonna boo him."

The Daily's evidence, as it describes it, is the group-chat messages, which its headline says seem to show the offer.
America Next did not respond to requests for comment, and the Daily could find no evidence that
Rogers’ campaign had any direct involvement. We found no filing or other document showing that
America Next made or funded the offer, and this entry does not assert it. The paper labels America
Next a political action committee, but in its IRS and FEC filings it is a nonprofit that files
independent-expenditure reports as a non-committee.
