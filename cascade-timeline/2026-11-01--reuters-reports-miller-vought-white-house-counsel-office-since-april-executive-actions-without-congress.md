Reuters reported that the White House has spent months preparing for a possible Democratic takeover of Congress, according to a White House official and two people familiar with the plans. The report says Stephen Miller and Russell Vought have been working with the White House Counsel's Office since April to identify executive actions Trump could pursue without congressional approval, said the three people, who spoke on the condition of anonymity.

Among the executive actions under consideration are new tariffs on imports of critical minerals and wind turbines, tighter rules on visas for international students and temporary foreign workers, and measures aimed at lowering housing and construction costs, the White House official said. Vought, whose US Office of Management and Budget oversees federal spending, is examining which priorities can move forward using money already appropriated by Congress, two of the people said. He also wants to change federal grant rules to give political appointees more say in which government programs receive taxpayer money, they said, potentially giving the White House more influence over federal spending without seeking congressional approval.

The report also says White House lawyers have been briefing political appointees and agency leaders on how to handle congressional inquiries, document requests and subpoenas, one person said. They have advised officials to be careful about what they put in writing.
