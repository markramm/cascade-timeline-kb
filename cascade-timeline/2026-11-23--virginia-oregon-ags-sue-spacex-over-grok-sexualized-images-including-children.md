Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones sued SpaceX, the parent of X and its AI tool Grok, on Nov. 23 in Richmond City Circuit Court. The suit was filed in coordination with Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield, who filed a separate suit in Multnomah County Circuit Court under Oregon's Unlawful Trade Practices Act, asking the court to make the company give up any profits from the practices. Virginia seeks civil penalties of up to $2,500 per willful violation of its Consumer Protection Act and alleges SpaceX derived revenue and "benefits in the form of data collected from consumers" from Grok's use in the state. Jones cited a study reporting that between Dec. 29, 2025, and Jan. 8, 2026, Grok created and posted about three million sexualized images on X, including "23,000 images that appeared to depict children." The figures are the study's, not findings of a court. X's safety team has said "we acted promptly to tighten its protections."

The suits extend state consumer-protection law to generative AI products: a week earlier Rayfield sued OpenAI under the same Oregon statute.
