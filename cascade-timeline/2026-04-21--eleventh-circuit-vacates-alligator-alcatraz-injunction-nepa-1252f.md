On **April 21, 2026** (opinion filed and judgment entered the same day), a divided Eleventh Circuit panel (Chief Judge Pryor, Judge Brasher; Judge Abudu dissenting) **vacated the preliminary injunction** that Judge Kathleen Williams of the Southern District of Florida had entered against "Alligator Alcatraz," the Everglades detention site Florida built at the Dade-Collier airport. The opinion is published and signed. The mandate issued **July 8, 2026**.

**What the injunction actually did, and did not do.** It did not order the facility closed in so many words. It barred new construction work and new arrivals ("any additional persons ... who were not already being detained"), and gave the defendants 60 days to remove fencing, lighting, generators and waste. The district court expected attrition to run the population down. A motions panel had already stayed the injunction pending appeal (the stay is the September 2025 unpublished order Judge Jordan dissented from, as cited in the merits dissent), so the injunction was not operating while the appeal ran. Any account saying the facility was "ordered closed" describes the district court's order as written, not a result that ever took effect.

**The two holdings.** First, the plaintiffs failed to show a likelihood of success on the NEPA claim: no final agency action under the APA, and no federal control that would make the project a "major federal action." The panel's factual premise is that Florida built it ("Florida, not federal, officials constructed the facility"), on state land with state money, with only four federal officers on site when the injunction issued, and that federal standards and 287(g) agreements do not turn a state project into a federal one. Second, and independently, 8 U.S.C. 1252(f)(1) bars an injunction that restrains the Secretary's detention authority "regardless of the merits of the underlying federal claims." The panel left venue for remand, holding the district court was wrong to find the federal and state defendants waived their objection.

**Why the structure matters.** The ruling turns on which side of the state-federal line the facility sits, and the same facility is described the other way elsewhere in the record. DHS told its own inspector general that Florida detained people at the facility under delegated 287(g) authority (see [[2026-09-11--dhs-oig-alligator-alcatraz-inspection-79-detainees-in-18-sq-ft-metal-enclosures]]); the plaintiffs' amended complaint alleges FEMA awarded Florida grant funding for it and that the first disbursement reportedly arrived about May 15, 2026, less than a month after the ruling. That is an allegation in a party's pleading, not a finding. The panel noted that Florida had received no federal funding when the injunction was entered.

**Dissent.** Judge Abudu argued the majority substituted its own reading of the record for the district court's, and adopted Judge Jordan's earlier dissent from the stay order.

**Remand (S.D. Fla. 1:25-cv-22896).** After the mandate, plaintiffs sought a status conference (held August 14, 2026), filed a supplemental amended complaint adding the Interior Secretary, Fish and Wildlife Service and FEMA as defendants and the Miccosukee Tribe amended its intervenor complaint. On **October 2, 2026** the federal defendants moved to dismiss and to transfer the case to the Middle District of Florida, arguing the site is in Ochopee and calling it "a detention facility closed months ago"; Florida's emergency-management division separately says the claims against it are moot. The facility's closure itself is documented by the DHS OIG ("FSSF ceased operations in June 2026") and, per the plaintiffs' complaint, the Governor said on June 25, 2026 that it held no detainees and was being demobilized, and that Florida could resume detention if DHS asked. The district court has not ruled. Oppositions are due **October 30, 2026** and replies **November 20, 2026**.

**Open and unverified.** The reporter citation the federal defendants give for the opinion (174 F.4th 822) is their filing's, not checked against a reporter. Who ordered or caused the June 2026 wind-down is unestablished here: the plaintiffs' complaint records conflicting statements from the state and ICE.
