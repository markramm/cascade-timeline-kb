Two separate events. On March 26, 2026, at CPAC, Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche asked "Why is there objection to sending ICE officers to polling places? Illegals can't vote. It doesn't make any sense." (Democracy Docket, March 26; The Hill, March 27). On July 15, 2026, Blanche, by then Acting Attorney General, declined to explicitly rule out deploying federal agents to polling places (Axios, July 15). Steve Bannon separately called ICE's airport enforcement campaign "perfect training for the fall of 2026." Before the July 15 remarks, on July 13, Judge Howell's minute order in the DNC's FOIA suit noted that ICE, which had represented that no responsive records were found, "has now reversed its position only after plaintiff pointed out" ICE enforcement actions outside polling places and reported 11,103 potentially responsive pages (CourtListener minute order id 485986612). DHS stated ICE has "no current plans" to target polling sites but left the door open based on "available intelligence." Senator Markey led a 12-senator letter demanding explicit assurances from DOJ, DHS, and DOD.

This is the most direct executive-branch endorsement yet of using immigration enforcement as a mechanism of voter intimidation, and connects directly to the broader airport-enforcement expansion documented later in July ([[2026-07-30--ice-widens-airport-arrests-legally-present-migrants-tsa-checkpoint]]) — the same enforcement apparatus Bannon frames as "training" for a polling-place operation.
