Pinal County supervisors voted **4-1 on August 26, 2026** to reject **Vermaland's** proposed **La Osa** data center campus south of Eloy — a **3,300-acre** site that would have been the largest data center project in Arizona.

**What makes this the useful case is that the developer had already conceded almost everything.** Vermaland cut the project from **59 buildings and two gas-fired power plants to 11 buildings and one** — roughly an **80% reduction** — and pledged self-imposed limits on water use. The county rejected it anyway.

Supervisor **Jeff Serdy** gave the reason without dressing it up: "**When 90% of the people are against it, I'm going to vote the will of the people.**"

That is a different mechanism from everything else in this lane. The moratoria this timeline records — Dougherty, Walker, Hall, Levy, Pierce — are procedural: a county pauses because it lacks the rules or the information to decide. Pinal had a specific application, an amended one, with concessions on the two constraints counties most often say they cannot get. It decided on the merits and said no. Residents' concerns were **water, air quality, noise, wildlife, and property values.**

**The concession pattern is worth naming.** An 80% scale-back is a large move, and it reframes what a data-center negotiation is: the opening proposal functions as an anchor, and the "concession" returns to something nearer what the county might have accepted first. A county that treats the amended proposal as a victory has been moved from its own baseline. Pinal did not, and the vote suggests the scale-back was read as evidence the original was unreasonable rather than as good faith.

Set this beside **Barber County, Kansas**, which approved Google's Project Helium four days later with written terms on taxes, water, and noise. Two rural counties, two weeks, opposite outcomes — and the difference is not the presence or absence of concessions but whether the county's own residents were persuaded. Barber's chairman said the county would "continue to die" without it; Pinal's said 90% of people were against.

Arizona is also where this corpus records the **Surprise ICE warehouse** environmental-review fight. The state is absorbing both buildouts at once, in the same counties, competing for the same water.
