On Oct 22, 2026 (hearing and order date), Judge Sparkle Sooknanan denied the NAACP's emergency motion for a temporary restraining order in *NAACP v. Dhillon* (D.D.C., FOIA suit seeking DOJ records on its election monitors). The parties' joint status report says the court cited "among other things, its breadth" and directed the parties to confer on narrowing the request.

The plaintiff proposed production of narrowed categories by October 29, 2026; "Defendants declined the offer", saying they did not anticipate finishing by then. The government's stated position in the report: the jurisdiction list and prosecutor counts are likely exempt because "such information remains under deliberation", and "CRT is not using federal law enforcement officers as election monitors on election day." That is the government's litigating position on one agency's use of such officers on one day; the filing does not define the term.

This suit is separate from *NAACP v. Blanche*, which challenges the monitoring program under the Voting Rights Act and the APA.

## Update (2026-10-27, filing date): DOJ reports 18,993 and 7,842 potentially responsive records, and no final jurisdiction list until Election Day

In a [notice](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.dcd.297278/gov.uscourts.dcd.297278.27.0.pdf) filed October 27, 2026, pursuant to the court's minute entry of that day, the Civil Rights Division said it ran two searches: one for the jurisdictions where monitors will be deployed and the number of prosecutors and investigators serving as monitors, the other for written restrictions on criminal staff serving as monitors and for federal agents deployed as monitors in the primaries. The former "returned 18,993 potentially responsive" records and the latter "returned 7,842 potentially responsive records." The notice calls the results "by their nature, overly inclusive," says the figures count records, not pages, and says CRT can "begin uploading these records into Relativity for manual review."

On the two lists, CRT says it "will not have a finalized list of jurisdictions until" Election Day, and that it "has not collected the position titles of DOJ" employees who signed up as monitors, so it "would likely need to create a record" to say how many are prosecutors or investigators. This is the government's account of its own records; the notice reports search results, not a production.

## Update (2026-10-28, filing date): Voting Section chief Neff's declaration says no FBI or ICE employees will serve as monitors; DEA, Bureau of Prisons and ATF employees will

The Declaration of Eric Neff, acting chief of the Civil Rights Division's Voting Section, was filed in the case in a court filing on Oct. 28 (filing date), as reported by the Epoch Times and by Democracy Docket. As quoted in the Epoch Times account, it says "There will be no Federal Bureau of Investigation or U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement employees, much less agents, serving as monitors." It also says the monitors will include employees from the Drug Enforcement Administration, the Federal Bureau of Prisons, and the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms, and Explosives, who will be prohibited from carrying guns, and that Neff was aware of only one Department of Homeland Security employee participating, from Customs and Border Protection.

The declaration says the division is still identifying all locations it will send monitors to, and that releasing a full list of locations, as sought by the Freedom of Information Act request, would potentially put monitors at risk. Democracy Docket separately reports, citing records Democracy Forward received, that for the July primary in Arizona the DOJ dispatched an FBI agent, as well as several criminal prosecutors, to monitor; that is a different election from the one the declaration covers.
