Judge Jed S. Rakoff of the Southern District of New York wrote in The New York Review of Books: "not a single high-level executive has been successfully prosecuted in connection with the recent financial crisis". The count is his, as of the article, and it is limited to high-level executives and to successful prosecutions.

The record in the sources has one nearby case. AFP reported that "A former top Credit Suisse trader was sentenced to two and a half years of prison" in November 2013: Kareem Serageldin, for inflating subprime mortgage-related bond prices. Whether a Credit Suisse trader counts as a high-level executive is a rank call that the sources do not settle.

The Justice Department's own inspector general found counts of executives to be unreliable in one program. In a 2014 audit of the Department's mortgage-fraud efforts, the inspector general wrote that what counted as an executive "was subjective and left up to the discretion of the participating agency submitting the data", and that 98 of the 172 "executives" in a 2012 press release were labeled other, unknown or no in the underlying spreadsheets. That program concerned fraud against distressed homeowners, not Wall Street banks, so it bears on how executive counts are made, not on the count above.

The date is that of Rakoff's article. The entry's earlier date was an inauguration day, a placeholder.
