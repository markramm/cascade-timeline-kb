In *Trump v. Barbara* (No. 25–365), heard April 1, 2026 and decided June 30, 2026, the Supreme Court affirmed the preliminary injunction against Executive Order No. 14160. The district court had provisionally certified a nationwide class of children who would be denied citizenship by the Order. The Court held that children born in the United States to parents unlawfully or temporarily present are “subject to the jurisdiction” of the United States and are citizens at birth under the Fourteenth Amendment’s Citizenship Clause. The ground the majority gave is the Fourteenth Amendment.

Chief Justice Roberts delivered the opinion of the Court, joined by Justices Sotomayor, Kagan, Barrett and Jackson. The opinion prints these separate writings: Justice Jackson concurred, joined by Justice Sotomayor as to the introduction and Part I; Justice Kavanaugh filed an opinion concurring in the judgment and dissenting in part; Justice Thomas dissented, joined by Justice Gorsuch; Justices Alito and Gorsuch each filed dissenting opinions. Kavanaugh's separate ground is statutory: in his view the Order does not violate the Fourteenth Amendment but does contravene a federal statute, 8 U. S. C. §1401(a). He wrote that Congress could amend that statute or enact new legislation establishing exceptions to birthright citizenship.

The arc this entry sits in runs from the January 2025 order ([[2025-01-20--birthright-citizenship-order]]) through the cert grant ([[2026-01-29--scotus-grants-cert-birthright-citizenship]]) and the April hearing ([[2026-04-01--scotus-birthright-citizenship-oral-arguments]]). It came the day after the *Humphrey's Executor* overturn ([[2026-06-29--scotus-overturns-humphreys-executor-presidential-firing-power]]). The nationwide-injunctions decision of the 2025 term, *Trump v. CASA*, is a different case ([[2025-06-27--supreme-court-restricts-nationwide-injunctions-trump-v-casa]]). The open lead: the status of children born while the EO was nominally in effect.
