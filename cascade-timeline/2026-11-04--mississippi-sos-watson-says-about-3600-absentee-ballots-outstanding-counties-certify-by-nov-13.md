Mississippi Secretary of State Michael Watson said Wednesday that approximately 3,600 absentee-by-mail ballots are still outstanding, and that they must be received within five business days of Election Day to be counted, WLBT and WAPT reported. An unknown number of affidavit ballots must also be processed during the 10-day certification period. As of Wednesday, 70 of Mississippi’s 82 counties had uploaded unofficial results to the Statewide Election Management System, and counties must submit certified results to the Secretary of State by Nov. 13.

The update came as fewer than 100 votes at one point separated Democrat Scott Colom and Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in the U.S. Senate race. Unofficial results could change as outstanding absentee and affidavit ballots are processed and counties complete canvassing and certification. The mail-ballot receipt window is the one at issue in [[2026-06-29--scotus-watson-v-rnc-upholds-mississippi-mail-ballot-window]]; for pre-election polling-place problems in the state, see [[2026-10-31--mississippi-free-press-polling-locator-errors-remain-neshoba-clerk-says-address-cannot-change-before-election]].

## Update (2026-11-05, press conference date (Thursday); NRSC letter reported the same day): NRSC letter asks Mississippi counties to preserve ballots; state GOP chair estimates 20,000 outstanding

The National Republican Senatorial Committee told Mississippi county election officials in a letter that because the ballots and records "are potential evidence in any box examination under § 23-15-911, election contest, or proceeding before the United States Senate, Candidate Hyde-Smith is entitled to their preservation in an unaltered and identifiable state," Democracy Docket reported. During a press conference Thursday in Jackson, Mississippi GOP chairman Mike Hurst estimated approximately 17,000 outstanding affidavit ballots and over 3,000 outstanding mail-in ballots, about 20,000 in all. Democracy Docket notes that Secretary of State Michael Watson flagged 230,000 voter registrations as "inactive" this year and that Mississippi has no recount law for federal elections. Democracy Docket discloses that the Elias Law Group, whose firm chair founded it, represents the DSCC and is active for the Colom campaign.
