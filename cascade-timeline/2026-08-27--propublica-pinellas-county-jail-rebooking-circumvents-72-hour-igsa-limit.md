## Opening

**ProPublica reported on 2026-08-27 that the Pinellas County Jail in Clearwater, Florida — operating
under an intergovernmental service agreement that limits ICE detention there to 72 hours — held
immigrants far longer by REBOOKING them.** Per the headline's own example, **one woman was held for a
total of 26 days.**

## The mechanism, and why it is a mechanism story

**The 72-hour limit is a term of the IGSA — the contract between ICE and the county.** Rebooking
restarts the clock without changing where the person is or who is holding them. **The limit is
honored on paper at every individual booking and defeated in aggregate.**

**That is the shape this corpus has documented repeatedly**: a constraint that binds a *transaction*
rather than an *outcome*, defeated by repeating the transaction. It requires no rule to be broken.
**See [[function-not-intent-the-analytical-rule-2026]] — report the conversion, not a stated purpose,
and do not assert intent the record does not establish.**

## Why This Event Matters

**Pinellas is a 72-hour IGSA county, not a detention center** — the short-term category that has
attracted less scrutiny than the warehouse conversions this corpus tracks. **If a short-term
agreement can be converted into indefinite detention by administrative repetition, the distinction
between "72-hour holding" and "detention" is doing no work.**
