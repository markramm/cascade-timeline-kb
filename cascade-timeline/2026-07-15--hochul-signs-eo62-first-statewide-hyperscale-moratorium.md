Governor Kathy Hochul signed Executive Order 62 on July 14, 2026 (the order's own execution date; press coverage ran July 14-15), directing the state Department of Environmental Conservation to hold in abeyance discretionary permits for the construction or expansion of data centers consuming 50 megawatts or more until the Department of Public Service delivers a final generic environmental impact statement. Law-firm and press summaries call it the nation's first statewide data-center moratorium and describe it as lasting a year; the order text sets no fixed end date for the permit hold (its one-year clock applies to a DEC water-withdrawal report), and it does not reach local-government approvals. The 50 MW threshold targets exactly the facility class driving ratepayer cost-shift concern nationally. New York's market scale — the largest state economy yet to act — makes this the single highest-consequence action in the datacenter-resistance wave the timeline has tracked county-by-county since June.

The order landed inside an unprecedented single-week acceleration: in the July 14-16 window alone, Jackson MS, St. Charles County MO, Haring Township MI, Marietta GA, Hamilton County TN, New Albany IN, Broken Bow NE, Santa Fe County NM, and Valparaiso IN all passed or advanced moratoriums, while St. Joseph County IN rejected a $13 billion unnamed-hyperscaler project outright. This extends the pattern already documented through dozens of individual county actions (see [[2026-07-08--lakeland-fl-bipartisan-opposition-project-swan-data-center]], [[2026-06-08--charlotte-city-council-150-day-datacenter-moratorium]]) but marks the first time the resistance has scaled from municipal/county zoning authority to full state executive action — a structural escalation the county-level wave alone could not achieve, since individual jurisdictions cannot set statewide grid-interconnection or ratepayer-protection standards.

## Verification block — 2026-10-28 (agent:claude-sonnet-5-5-parallel-tick56-a), read against the instrument

- **Date: July 14, 2026.** The EO PDF on governor.ny.gov (`/sites/default/files/2026-07/EO_62.pdf`, a scanned image, OCR'd locally) ends "GIVEN under my hand and the Privy Seal of the State in the City of Albany this fourteenth day of July in the year two thousand twenty-six." July 15 is a press-pickup date (CBS6, this entry's source). The `date:` field and filename stay at 2026-07-15 as an identifier; cite July 14.
- **Threshold: 50 megawatts**, from the EO's definition section ("consume or can consume 50 megawatts of energy or more"), with carve-outs for manufacturing, research, education and medical-care facilities.
- **Scope and duration (the entry's earlier "for up to one year" was press shorthand).** Section 1 directs DEC to hold in abeyance discretionary permits for construction or expansion of a data center that were not yet determined complete before the order, "until DPS submits its report of the final Generic Environmental Impact Statement." No end date is stated for that hold. The only twelve-month clock is Section 5 (DEC water-withdrawal report "no later than twelve months after the date of this order"). Section 1 states it "does not apply to permits, approvals, licenses, or similar forms of permission from local governments."
- **Relationship to S.10642: two separate instruments.** The EO does not mention S.10642. The bill (Gonzalez; legislation passed both chambers early June 2026; 20 MW threshold, per [[2026-06-03--new-york-legislature-passes-statewide-data-center-moratorium-s10642]]) was still unsigned when Spectrum News reported on August 27, 2026 that Sen. Gonzalez was urging Hochul to sign it, noting the EO "is only a one-year pause that impacts new environmental permits" while the bill "would impact existing data centers." A final disposition of S.10642 was not located; check nysenate.gov in a browser tier (Cloudflare blocked a scripted fetch).
- **"First"** is true of an enacted statewide instrument only because the bill had not been signed; the June entry's "if signed, it would be the first" is the correct hedge, and the Desoto County and Imperial County entries that cite "the New York statewide S10642 moratorium" as an accomplished fact should read "the New York statewide moratorium (EO 62)" or "passed S.10642 (unsigned)".
