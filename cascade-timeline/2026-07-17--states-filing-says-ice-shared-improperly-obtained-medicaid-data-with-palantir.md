A motion filed by more than 20 Democratic state attorneys general in their suit over the CMS-ICE data-sharing agreement said ICE gave Palantir the Medicaid data that CMS had first sent to ICE in January 2026; NPR reported it as data Medicaid officials "improperly shared" with ICE, according to new court filings. Palantir operates ELITE, an app ICE agents use to see the addresses of noncitizens who may be subject to deportation. Palantir told NPR the dataset had been purged.

What is on the record in NPR's account: federal officials admitted in court that CMS had shared data with ICE in January that went beyond what Judge Vince Chhabria's order allowed, and one dataset transferred on Jan. 7 covered millions of people, including people in the country legally. A later Justice Department filing said CMS again inadvertently reshared that dataset with ICE, and an ICE declaration said half a dozen users still had a copy after a first deletion. No court has held any of this unlawful, and no source read here uses the word "illegal".

This is the third instance the timeline has documented of benefits data reported to be moving into Palantir's ICE tooling in 2026, after IRS data ([[2026-04-24--intercept-palantir-irs-130-million-data-mining-contract]]) and the ImmigrationOS build-out cross-referencing Medicaid, IRS and DMV records ([[2026-04-01--immigrationos-palantir-tracking-us-citizens-medicaid-irs-dmv]]). The shape is the same each time: data collected under a program with no immigration-enforcement purpose moves to ICE and then to Palantir.
