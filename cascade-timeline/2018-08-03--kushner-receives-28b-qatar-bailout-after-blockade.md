In August 2018, Brookfield took a 99-year lease on 666 Fifth Avenue, the Kushner family's money-losing Manhattan office tower, and paid the rent for the whole term up front. The New York Times reported (in a story WRAL syndicated), citing an anonymous executive briefed on the deal, that Brookfield paid about $1.1 billion in upfront rent and that Charles Kushner negotiated with his lenders to pay less than the company owed. The ground lease filed in property records notes a transfer price of $1.286 billion. The Kushners' mortgage on the office portion was $1.4 billion and due in February. The Financial Times found that the money for the lease came from a fund controlled by Brookfield Property Partners, to which the Qatar Investment Authority, which Bisnow reported held a 9% stake in that Brookfield arm, had sold preferred equity. Brookfield has said that the Qataris had no knowledge of the deal before its public announcement.

The Kushners had spent more than two years searching for a partner. The Intercept reported that in the week of April 24, 2017, Charles Kushner met Qatar's finance minister at a suite at the St. Regis Hotel in New York to discuss financing for the tower; Charles Kushner told the Washington Post he did not ask the government for money. The Intercept wrote that a diplomatic row that followed, ending in the blockade of Qatar, came with Kushner's backing, and it reported separately that Kushner communicated directly with the Saudi and Emirati crown princes on WhatsApp.

Just before the lease became public, Trump dropped his support for the Saudi-run blockade, a reversal Senator Wyden and Representative Castro called a stunning reversal; they opened an investigation into whether the Brookfield deal prompted Trump's earlier public backing of the blockade. The sources do not establish that the lease and Trump's policy were connected; that is the question the inquiry asked.

## Correction (2026-11-17)

The earlier version of this entry, flagged by a reader check, said Charles Kushner flew to Doha; the Intercept places his meeting with the finance minister at a New York hotel. It said Brookfield paid off the Kushners' entire mortgage; the sources above give an anonymous executive's figure for upfront rent, a larger mortgage on which the lenders accepted less, and a separate transfer price in the filed lease. It said the bailout came months after Trump reversed on the blockade; Bisnow places his reversal just before the lease became public.
