On Friday, January 24, 2025, the Trump administration fired 17 inspectors general en masse. The count is the district court's, which treated the facts as undisputed ("The parties do not dispute the relevant facts."); the Associated Press's count that night was about 17. Each inspector general received an email from the Director of Presidential Personnel, Sergio Gor, or his deputy, Deputy Director of Presidential Personnel Trent Morse. The court records the emails as coming without notice to Congress and without the required "substantive rationale".

The 2022 Securing Inspector General Independence Act requires the President to communicate "the substantive rationale, including detailed and case-specific reasons" to both houses of Congress "not later than 30 days before the removal or transfer". Sen. Chuck Grassley (R-Iowa) said "the 30 day detailed notice of removal that the law demands was not provided to Congress". The removal of the USAID inspector general, Paul Martin, is a separate, later event: see [[2025-01-29--usaid-inspector-general-fired-after-warning-about-frozen-aid]]. A Senate Democrats' letter counted "at least 18 offices".

Eight of the fired inspectors general sued; CBS reported the filing on a Wednesday. In an opinion filed 09/24/25, Judge Ana C. Reyes wrote: "President Trump violated the IGA. That much is obvious." She also held that "the Court DENIES Plaintiffs’ Motion for a Permanent Injunction", so no one was reinstated, and wrote "The Court will not decide the merits of Plaintiffs’ claims". The government's position, as the opinion states it, is that the Act is "an unconstitutional intrusion into the President’s removal powers". In a joint status report filed 07/13/26, "Plaintiffs intend to continue pursuing their claims for a declaratory judgment and back pay"; no ruling on the merits or on back pay is in the record read for this entry.

## Correction (2026-11-17)

What changed from the earlier version of this entry:

- The comparison with all earlier removals was dropped; no source read makes it.
- "Violated" is now attributed. The district court wrote that the President violated the Act, in an opinion that denied relief and said it would not decide the merits. No court has ruled on back pay or a declaratory judgment as of the last filing read here. The Supreme Court's decision in *Trump v.
