On his first day in office, President Trump signed **Proclamation 10887**, issuing mass clemency to some 1,500 January 6 defendants (a press count; the proclamation states no total) — the largest executive clemency action for political-violence convictions in modern American history. The proclamation granted a full, complete and unconditional pardon to all other individuals convicted of offenses related to January 6 while commuting the sentences of 14 named individuals (nine Oath Keepers and five Proud Boys) to "time served as of January 20, 2025." See the correction below on who was convicted of what.

## Scope of clemency (Proclamation 10887)

**Full pardons** (unconditional) — all other individuals *convicted* of offenses related to the January 6 Capitol attack, reported by NPR as some 1,500. Defendants with pending indictments were not pardoned by this clause; the proclamation separately directs the Attorney General to pursue dismissal with prejudice of all pending indictments.

**Commutations to time served** — 14 named individuals:
- *Proud Boys* (5): Joseph Biggs, Ethan Nordean, Zachary Rehl, Jeremy Bertino, Dominic Pezzola
- *Oath Keepers* (9): Stewart Rhodes (18-year sentence), Kelly Meggs, Kenneth Harrelson, Thomas Caldwell, Jessica Watkins, Roberto Minuta, Edward Vallejo, David Moerschel, Joseph Hackett

Note: Enrique Tarrio is not on the commutation list. He falls under the proclamation's pardon clause for "all other individuals." The seditious-conspiracy convictions were not excluded from the clemency scope; no carve-out existed for violent-offense or leadership-status defendants.

**Additional DOJ actions following the proclamation**: 300+ pending cases dismissed; 20+ DOJ prosecutors who worked J6 cases were fired; the DOJ's January 6 prosecution database was scrubbed.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

Read against the text of Proclamation 10887 (90 FR 8331-8332) and the court records, this entry had five errors. Each is corrected in the text above.

1. **No count in the proclamation.** The proclamation states no total. The entry's "approximately 1,500" is a press figure. NPR reports pardons for "some 1,500" [c3] and, separately, 14 commuted sentences. Old text: "mass clemency to approximately 1,500 January 6 defendants." New text: "some 1,500 ... (a press count; the proclamation states no total)."
2. **Who was pardoned.** Old text: pardons for defendants "convicted of or awaiting trial." The pardon clause covers "all other individuals convicted" of January 6 offenses [c1]. Pending indictments were handled by a separate direction to the Attorney General to seek dismissal with prejudice.
3. **The 14 commutees.** The proclamation names 14 and the court counts 14 [c2]. The old list named eight and said "additional named Oath Keepers members." The full list is in the body above: nine Oath Keepers and five Proud Boys. Group membership is from the two courts' own case captions (United States v. Rhodes, 22-cr-15, with nine defendants; United States v. Nordean, 21-cr-175, and Bertino's case before Judge Kelly).
4. **Tarrio.** Old text: "Tarrio's clemency was subsequently reported as a full pardon rather than commutation in some sources." Tarrio was never on the commutation list. Judge Kelly's July 10, 2026 memorandum says the order commuted the sentences of Nordean, Biggs, Rehl and Pezzola and granted a full pardon to Tarrio [c6].
5. **Seditious conspiracy was not every commutee's conviction.** Ten of the 14 were convicted of it: Rhodes and Meggs (Nov. 2022 verdict) [c8], Minuta, Hackett, Moerschel and Vallejo (Jan. 23, 2023 verdict) [c9], Nordean, Biggs and Rehl (Proud Boys trial) [c4], and Bertino, who pleaded guilty on Oct. 6, 2022 [c7]. Four were not. Watkins, Harrelson and Caldwell were acquitted of it in the Nov. 2022 verdict [c8], and Pezzola's counts of conviction exclude it [c5]. Any line saying all 14 were convicted of seditious conspiracy is wrong.
