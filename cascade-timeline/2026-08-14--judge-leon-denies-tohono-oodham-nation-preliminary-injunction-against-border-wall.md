Judge Richard J. Leon of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia denied the Tohono O'odham Nation's motion for a preliminary injunction against border wall construction. The order was filed 08/14/26.

According to the order, "plaintiff's Motion for Preliminary Injunction" is denied.

According to the memorandum opinion, the Nation and the federal government had worked together for decades to secure a sixty-two mile stretch of the border.

The opinion gives its reason: "Because plaintiff is unlikely to succeed on the merits of these claims, and because the remaining preliminary injunction factors favor the Government, I will DENY plaintiff's Motion".

On the Nation's claim that a wall would change its reservation boundaries without Congress, the opinion says "First, the Roosevelt Reservation likely exists along the international border."

On the Nation's trespass claim, the opinion says the claim is "unlikely to succeed on the merits because the federal government's sovereign immunity bars its claim".

The opinion also says "significant uncertainties remain about the actual scope of construction activities and the extent to which they may interfere with the Nation's land outside the Roosevelt Reservation".

The ruling denied a preliminary injunction. It did not decide the case on the merits.
