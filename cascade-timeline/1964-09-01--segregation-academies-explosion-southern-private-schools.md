Wikipedia's article on segregation academies gives the scale and the dates. Scholars estimate that "at least half a million white students were withdrawn from public schools between 1964 and 1975 to avoid mandatory desegregation". "The first segregation academies were created by white parents in the late 1950s in response to the U.S. Supreme Court ruling in Brown v. Board of Education (1954)". Southern state legislatures "enacted as many as 450 laws and resolutions between 1954 and 1964 attempting to block, postpone, limit, or evade the desegregation of public schools". The sociologist Jennifer Dyer has argued that the schools' claims to provide a Christian education were simply a guise for the actual objective of allowing parents to avoid integrated public schools.

The IRS text of 1980 gives the agency's side: "The IRS response began in 1965 with the suspension of the issuance of rulings to private schools in order for the Service to consider the effect of racial discrimination on their exempt status". It continues that "the IRS announced in 1967 that racially discriminatory private schools, which were receiving state aid, were not entitled to exemption under IRC 501(c)(3)", and that "Prior to 1970, the Service position was to recognize the exempt status of racially discriminatory private schools that did not receive state aid". The 1970 and 1971 steps are in [[1970-07-10--irs-adopts-non-discrimination-policy-private-schools]] and [[note-tax-trigger-chain-at-source-green-coit-irs-1970-1978-weyrich-dobson-quotations-2026-11-17]].

Wikipedia's article on the Moral Majority says Falwell had founded Lynchburg Christian Academy in 1967.

## Correction (2026-11-17)

The title and the date are the earlier entry's. The date is a placeholder for a span that the body places between the late 1950s and 1975. The title attributed no motive in a source; it is restated as the span. Checked against the Wikipedia articles and the IRS 1980 text.

- The IRS sentence was wrong. The entry had the IRS in 1967 allowing deductions for gifts to any segregated academy. The IRS's own text gives a 1967 ruling that discriminatory private schools receiving state aid were not entitled to exemption; before 1970 it recognized the exemption of those that took no state aid. The 1960s suspension began in 1965, not the early 1960s.
- Falwell's school: the entry placed its opening in the late 1950s and early 1960s, tied to states closing public schools. Wikipedia dates Lynchburg Christian Academy to 1967 (citing the Lynchburg News); no source read gives an earlier date or ties Falwell to a school closure in Alabama, Virginia or Louisiana. The late 1950s date belongs to the first segregation academies in general.
- Cut for lack of a source read: the rise in Southern private enrollment and its concentration in a few states; the Mississippi attendance figures; the Delta paragraphs; the school-choice paragraph on later tax credits and vouchers; the account of the Roe origin story with Criswell, the Southern Baptist resolutions, Falwell's Listen, America! and the quotations from Weyrich and FitzGerald, and Butler's reading of Weyrich's intent. The thesis and its quotations are in the linked note, with their settings.
- The Southern Education Foundation and Encyclopedia of Arkansas pages listed before were not re-read; the sources are replaced by the pages read.
