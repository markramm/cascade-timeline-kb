At least 11 North Carolina counties and 17 towns have enacted data center moratoriums as of August 2026, typically 12 months, in what Carolina Public Press calls a statewide "moratorium fever." Durham City passed a 60-day moratorium in May 2026, since extended to 12 months; Durham County is moving toward a parallel 12-month moratorium, with a public hearing set for August 24 after a contentious August 3 commissioner meeting where 15 speakers opposed hyperscale development. Commissioner Mike Lee has publicly endorsed an outright ban on hyperscale data centers in Durham. Separately, North Carolina's Ratepayers Protection Act repealed electricity tax exemptions for hyperscalers in the state budget, opening a second, fiscal front against the same projects.

North Carolina joins the broader Southeast moratorium cluster this corpus has tracked county by county — DeKalb County GA's extension through March 2027 ([[2026-07-07--dekalb-county-ga-extends-data-center-moratorium-march-2027]]), Loudon County TN's six-month pause ([[2026-06-30--loudon-county-tn-six-month-data-center-moratorium]]) — but the scale here (28 jurisdictions statewide) and the paired tax-exemption repeal make it the most comprehensive state-level squeeze documented to date. Durham's proximity to the Research Triangle's existing hyperscaler pipeline, including the Amazon/Duke Energy air-permit fight in Richmond County ([[2026-06-26--nc-deq-opens-comment-amazon-10b-datacenter-duke-air-permits-richmond]]), raises the stakes of the August 24 hearing — the Hey Durham reporting of the August 3 meeting is tier-3 and should be confirmed against Durham County's official minutes before further reporting relies on Commissioner Lee's quoted position.
