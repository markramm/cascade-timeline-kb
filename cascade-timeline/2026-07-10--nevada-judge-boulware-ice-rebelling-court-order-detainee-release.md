Federal Judge Richard Boulware II issued contempt-adjacent orders in early July 2026 requiring ICE to release 17 people held at the Nevada Southern Detention Center, after DHS refused to comply with his March 2026 ruling vacating mandatory-detention policies under the Administrative Procedure Act. ACLU of Nevada and the Nevada Independent both confirm seven plaintiffs were released under a court order dated around July 8-9, 2026; the remaining ten are attributed to a separate, earlier order discussed in the 2026-08-27 update below, though that update and the two sources above disagree on which date pairs with which count (see Corrected note). Boulware wrote that "the government is rebelling against the basic principle underlying our constitutional order that an order issued by a court must be obeyed." DHS is separately stonewalling Nevada state legislators who requested information about the non-compliance, with state senators describing the agency as "stiffing" their inquiries.

This extends the documented pattern of ICE treating district-court orders as optional, already tracked through the redetention of a 77-year-old Palestinian man in Louisiana in defiance of a court order ([[2026-06-10--ice-defies-federal-judge-redetains-77yo-palestinian-omar-louisiana]]) and the NYC courthouse arrests defying a June 24 injunction ([[2026-06-30--ice-nyc-immigration-court-arrests-defy-june-24-injunction]]). Boulware's explicit "rebelling" characterization — from a sitting federal judge, not an advocacy group — marks an escalation in how courts themselves are now describing ICE's posture toward judicial authority.

**Update, 2026-08-27 — precision on "contempt-adjacent" and the escalation since.** Verified directly against the full docket of the underlying case, Jacobo-Ramirez v. Mullin, 2:25-cv-02136 (D. Nev.): the word "contempt" does not appear anywhere in the docket's 186 entries as of this date — no contempt finding, no order to show cause on contempt, no contempt hearing has been entered. The "contempt-adjacent" characterization above is accurate and should not be upgraded. What has escalated: **plaintiffs filed a Motion for Sanctions on August 4, 2026 (Doc. 177)**, fully briefed by August 25 (defendants' response Aug. 18, plaintiffs' reply Aug. 25) and **still pending, unruled, as of August 27, 2026**. The two "rebelling"/"lawless conduct" orders referenced above are properly counted as **two formal noncompliance findings within one case** (July 2 and July 8/9, 2026 — 7 and 10 detainees respectively, totaling the 17 cited), not as evidence of a wider multi-instance Nevada pattern; the case's companion habeas actions (Sira v. Mattos, 2:26-cv-00828; Reyes Campos v. Blanche, 2:26-cv-00850) are the same underlying class dispute in the same courtroom, not independent instances. Nevada legislators (state Sen. Edgar Flores's interim committee) did **not** issue a subpoena after DHS's July no-show — verified absence; Flores said the committee would "invite them back" rather than compel attendance, and no subsequent reporting shows escalation to compulsion.
