On February 28, 2026, the first day of the U.S.-Israeli campaign against Iran, a missile struck a girls' primary school in Minab, in southern Iran. The people killed were mostly children. Their number is reported differently by source and date, and none of the counts has been independently confirmed:

- UN News, March 1: "around 150" killed and almost 100 wounded.
- Iranian state media, as of March 4: 168 dead; Human Rights Watch said it could not independently verify the figure.
- Time, March 11: "Some 175 people were killed, at least 108 of them children," according to a representative of a network of Iranian teachers' unions.
- The March 12 explainer on aljazeera.com: "more than 170 people - most of them schoolgirls."

A letter from 46 Senate Democrats to Defense Secretary Hegseth said "The majority of those killed in the strikes were girls between the ages of 7 and 12 years old." No outlet read for this entry gives the dead by name.

Who fired is attributed, not settled. Time reported that video from Iran's Mehr News, first geolocated by Bellingcat, appears to show a U.S.-made Tomahawk striking a clinic near the school, and that two munitions experts it interviewed identified the weapon as a Tomahawk. The New York Times, citing unnamed U.S. officials, reported that preliminary findings of a military investigation found the U.S. responsible. President Trump said "In my opinion and based on what I've seen, that was done by Iran"; the same explainer noted no evidence supported this. Israel denied involvement. Hegseth said the Pentagon was investigating; the White House said the investigation was ongoing; U.S. Central Command declined to comment. Secretary of State Rubio said the U.S. "would not intentionally target a school." CNN's headline reported that sources said the strike likely hit the school because of outdated intelligence; the CNN and Washington Post articles could not be fetched and are not summarized here.

The calls for investigation: Human Rights Watch said on March 7 that the attack "should be investigated as a war crime" and addressed the call to the U.S. and Israel, neither of which had claimed responsibility. UNESCO called the bombing "a grave violation of humanitarian law," per UN News. UN human rights experts, per the title of their release, called for an independent investigation. The Senate Democrats asked Hegseth for a "swift investigation." No court or government body is reported to have opened a war-crimes proceeding.
