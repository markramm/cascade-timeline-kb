The Marshall Project published an investigation on June 1, 2026 reporting that quick and repeated ICE transfers have become more common in Trump's second term. From the final year of the Biden administration to the first year of Trump's latest term, the number of people transferred five or more times more than tripled, and the number transferred out of state within 24 hours more than doubled, according to the Project's analysis of ICE detention data obtained by the Deportation Data Project. In Trump's first year back in office, ICE transferred nearly 41,700 people to another state within 24 hours, more than double the number the previous year.

The article's named case is Abdullahi Mohamed. ICE detained him near Portland, Maine, and held him for more than seven weeks in Massachusetts. For about five days in December he then dropped out of his family's sight; he later told them ICE had sent him from Massachusetts to Mississippi to Louisiana and then to Texas, before a deportation flight to Somalia. His lawyer wrote that the transfers can block people like Mohamed from speaking with an attorney and make it difficult to file legal petitions in the right jurisdiction. The jurisdiction matters because habeas petitions generally must be filed in the federal district where a person is detained.

On ICE's own paperwork, the article describes a transfer checklist ICE produced in litigation: attorneys are to be notified of a transfer "as soon as practicable" and no later than 24 hours after it happens, and a senior official must approve it if the detainee has an attorney of record in that region, immediate family ties or a pending court case. Tess Hellgren, a supervising attorney at the National Immigration Project, told the Project she sees no evidence those factors are being considered. An ICE spokesperson said claims that transfers are being "weaponized" are "categorically false."

The article ties destination to the 5th Circuit. That court, which covers Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi, is one of two federal appeals courts that have backed the administration on its new detention policy. Those three states are home to 14 out of the 20 largest detention facilities, and nearly three-quarters of the people ICE deported in Trump's first year back were last detained in a 5th Circuit state. The forum-shopping reading is an attorney's: Dan Gividen, a former ICE deputy chief counsel, said "They're trying to get as many people to the 5th Circuit as possible."

In the first year of Trump's second term, ICE detained and deported 13,500 people in much the same way as Mohamed, more than twice the previous year's number.
