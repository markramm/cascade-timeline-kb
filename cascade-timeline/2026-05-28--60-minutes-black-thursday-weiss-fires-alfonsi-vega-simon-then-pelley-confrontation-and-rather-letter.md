CBS News fired "60 Minutes" correspondents Sharyn Alfonsi and Cecilia Vega, along with executive producer Tanya Simon, on Thursday, May 28, 2026, the day Fox News published its account. Staffers have taken to calling it "Black Thursday."

**Dates by person.**

- **Alfonsi: contract lapsed Saturday, fired Thursday.** "Over the weekend, my contract with CBS News expired," Alfonsi said in a statement. She remained employed until Thursday. The Thursday firing, not the lapse and not the day her statement ran, is the date this entry carries.
- **Vega: fired Thursday.** Fox News carries the Thursday firing in the same sentence as Alfonsi's and Simon's.
- **Simon: fired Thursday.** Same sentence.

**Pelley and Weiss.** Scott Pelley accused CBS News Editor-in-Chief Bari Weiss of "murdering" the newsmagazine at a staff meeting Monday with Nick Bilton, in a recording Status obtained. "She's murdering '60 Minutes,'" Pelley said. "She does not love this place. She was brought in to kill it." Status has Pelley using the name "Black Thursday" in that meeting.

**The Rather letter.** Dan Rather, Lowell Bergman and other journalists urged David Ellison on Monday to protect CBS News' editorial independence. The letter states: "Acquiring CBS News came with a legal requirement to serve the public interest, avoid political interference and maintain editorial independence."
