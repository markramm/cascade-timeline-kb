A Texas Tribune investigation published August 12, 2026 found that at least 60 children at the CoreCivic-operated South Texas Family Residential Center in Dilley have been detained 100 days or more — five times the 20-day limit set by the Flores settlement, the federal consent decree governing conditions for detained migrant children. In the year since the facility reopened in March 2025, more than 3,000 children passed through it, and more than half were held beyond the 20-day cap. Rep. Joaquin Castro, who has made seven oversight visits to the facility, called it in perpetual violation of the law and demanded its closure; immigrant-rights attorneys have renewed Flores contempt litigation against the federal government over the pattern.

This is a discrete, documented instance of the family-detention capacity buildout the timeline tracks through its contracting side — Dilley's five-year, $246 million CoreCivic contract restart ([[2025-03-05--target-hospitality-dilley-5-year-246m-contract-restart]]) and the broader 10,000-arrest ICE surge that filled LaSalle-area family detention ([[2026-07-01--ice-10k-arrest-surge-lasalle-family-detention-contract]]) — showing what sustained over-capacity operation looks like on the ground: children held five times longer than a binding federal consent decree permits, with contempt litigation now the only remaining enforcement lever.
