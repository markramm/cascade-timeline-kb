# Senate Confirms Rodney Scott as CBP Commissioner After the IACHR Held the United States Responsible in the Hernández Rojas Case

The Senate confirmed Rodney Scott as Commissioner of U.S. Customs and Border Protection on June 18, 2025, by a 51 to 46 vote, per the Senate roll call. Scott had led the San Diego Border Patrol sector in 2010, when Anastasio Hernández Rojas died after a beating and Taser use by border officials. This entry records what the documents show about Scott's role, who has characterized it, and what the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights found.

## What the record shows about Scott

- **His position in 2010.** Capital & Main reported that Scott "was acting deputy chief patrol agent of the San Diego Border Patrol Sector in 2010." KJZZ described him as the sector chief at the time, and Senator Ron Wyden said at his confirmation hearing that Scott "led the U.S. Border Patrol in San Diego." The sources differ on the title.
- **The investigative team.** Capital & Main reported that "a Border Patrol investigative team that Scott oversaw at the time began working the case even though the San Diego Police Department's homicide team had jurisdiction," and that "it's not clear whether Scott ever worked on the unit himself, but it is clear that he was in a supervisory position to the team." The reporter did not independently find that Scott directed any specific act by the team.
- **The subpoena.** Capital & Main reported that a briefing filed with the Commission by the family's attorneys "says that Scott signed the subpoena since he was in charge of the San Diego Sector at the time." Wyden told the hearing that Scott "signed a subpoena to obtain Mr. Rojas's medical records, which Customs and Border Patrol then refused to release to the San Diego Police Department," an action he said "the former head of CBP Internal Affairs called illegal and possibly obstruction of justice."
- **Scott's response.** Capital & Main wrote that Scott "did not respond to a request for comment." This entry did not find a statement by Scott on these points.

## Who characterizes it as a cover-up

The characterization is made by others, and the sources attribute it:

- Andrea Guerrero, executive director of Alliance San Diego and an attorney for the family, said on Democracy Now! that Scott "is implicated in the decision from the Inter-American Commission" and "directly participated in the cover-up." That is an advocate's account; this entry did not read the Commission's report, so whether it names Scott is not established here.
- Wyden wrote of "serious concerns about the gross failures of integrity and leadership he has shown."
- Democracy Now!'s headline said the Senate confirmed Scott "despite mounting accusations that Scott attempted to cover up and obstruct the criminal investigation" into the beating.
- Senator Mike Crapo said at the hearing that the case "had already been reviewed by seven different investigative and use-of-force policy bodies, who found the agents were not criminally liable," and that the Justice Department had declined to bring charges, per KJZZ.

## What the IACHR found

KJZZ quoted the Commission's release: "The IACHR found that the use of force in this case was unnecessary and disproportionate, given that Mr. Hernández-Rojas was unarmed, restrained, and posed no threat." KJZZ reported that the Commission ruled the U.S. government was responsible for the death and called for a new investigation.
