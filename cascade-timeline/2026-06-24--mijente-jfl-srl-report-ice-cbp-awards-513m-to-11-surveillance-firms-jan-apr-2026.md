A report by Mijente, Just Futures Law and the Surveillance Resistance Lab, all advocacy groups, tracked ICE and CBP contracts with 11 companies the authors said provide surveillance tech. The Guardian reported it, writing that it was released this week. The report's own release day is not stated.

The $513m is the coalition's tally, drawn from USAspending data, and it is not all ICE and CBP surveillance spending: the companies are the authors' own list. The 2026 bar reflects January-April only, per the report's chart note. The Guardian gives just over $310m for 2025.

The report counts awards. A ceiling is not spend, so read the figure as awards.

Truthout writes that the report calls Palantir "the backbone of ICE data surveillance." That is the coalition's characterization. Palantir's $97 million from ICE in 2026 is also a January-April figure. The $1 billion is a purchase agreement; it is a contracting vehicle and not money spent.
