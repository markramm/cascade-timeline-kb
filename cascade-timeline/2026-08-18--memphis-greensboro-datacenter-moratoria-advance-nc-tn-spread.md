A Memphis City Council committee advanced legislation on August 18, 2026 imposing a 12-month moratorium on new data center development, pausing rezoning applications, site plan approvals, and building permits; the proposal moves to the full council. In North Carolina, Greensboro City Council scheduled an August 17 public hearing on a 120-day moratorium covering data centers above 10 megawatts — after reversing course from a 5-4 rejection on July 21 to a unanimous 9-0 vote on August 3 to begin the moratorium process. WUNC reported on August 14 that Alamance and Yadkin counties were also considering moratoriums.

Greensboro's reversal from a one-vote defeat to unanimity in thirteen days is the more informative data point. Nothing changed about the underlying projects in that window; what changed was the political cost of being on the losing side, which is what a genuine constituency shift looks like at the municipal level. Neither the Memphis nor the Greensboro action names a triggering hyperscaler, which distinguishes this phase from the corpus's earlier entries where a specific project drove a specific fight — Effingham County residents confronting OpenAI's Project Camellia (2026-08-05--effingham-county-ga-residents-confront-openai-project-camellia), Jackson MS rezoning for Saxum (2026-06-23--jackson-ms-northwest-data-center-rezoning-saxum-june-24-hearing). Communities are now legislating against the category before a proposal arrives. North Carolina had already repealed the electricity sales tax exemption for data centers, and the NC moratorium wave now spans at least eleven counties plus Durham (2026-08-03--nc-moratorium-wave-11-counties-durham-hearing-aug24). The pattern of committee-advances-then-full-council (2026-07-13--indianapolis-committee-advances-data-center-moratorium, 2026-08-03--la-crosse-wi-city-advances-18-month-data-center-moratorium) is where these proposals most often get diluted — the exemption clauses tend to appear between the committee vote and the floor.
