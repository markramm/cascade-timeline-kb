The White House barred **CNN** from the television-pool seat on Air Force One for President Trump's trip to a college football game in Knoxville, Tennessee. CNN reported the move was "announced Friday night in a White House note that lays out the president's schedule" (CNN); the flight was the next day. The flight was the next day.

**What the reports say.** CNN reported that no outlet was listed as replacing CNN, "meaning there will be no television pool coverage of his trip" (CNN, Friday night). Al Jazeera/AP reported that **Real America's Voice News** replaced CNN "according to White House guidance"; NPR's Saturday broadcast said Real America's Voice was "manning this flight" and that the other pool members "are refusing to step in." The reports differ on the replacement; all agree the seat was taken from CNN. CNN credits the Washington Post as the outlet that was first to report the decision; the Post page could not be fetched for this correction, so it is not confirmed from the Post itself.

**Boycott.** Al Jazeera/AP reported that the other four pool members, **Fox, ABC, CBS and NBC**, "declined to participate in the pool until access was restored."

**Earlier exclusion.** CNN reported that the original ban also kept CNN off Air Force One when Trump traveled to the United Nations General Assembly in New York.

**Legal ambiguity plaintiffs are testing.** Al Jazeera reported that the judge's decision "addressed press passes to the White House and did not directly mention pool duties or Air Force One." Plaintiffs' counsel **Theodore J. Boutrous Jr.** said on CNN's *The Lead with Jake Tapper* that plaintiffs believe the ruling "also covers the issue of pool access" (Al Jazeera).
