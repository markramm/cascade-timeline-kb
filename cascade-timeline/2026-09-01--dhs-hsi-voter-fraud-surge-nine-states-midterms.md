DHS launched what an internal directive calls a "coordinated criminal voter fraud surge initiative" on September 1, 2026, running through mid-October — straddling the November 3 midterms. Homeland Security Investigations offices in nine states were directed to pursue investigative leads, conduct field interviews, and support timely prosecutorial review: **California, Connecticut, Georgia, Missouri, Nevada, New York, Pennsylvania, Washington and Wisconsin**. Agents were tasked in part with identifying naturalized citizens who may have registered or voted before completing naturalization.

HSI is the ICE component normally assigned to narcotics smuggling, human trafficking and transnational financial crime. It is now conducting field interviews about voter registration in the eight weeks before a federal election.

**The ratio is the finding.** DHS says that since April 2025 more than **24,000 cases** have been flagged by the SAVE verification system as potential noncitizens on voter rolls and referred to HSI. An earlier DHS preliminary review claimed more than **250,000 "potential" noncitizens** across four states — 190,832 in California, 35,152 in New Jersey, 15,903 in Nevada, 14,576 in Pennsylvania. Against those numbers, ProPublica data show **41 alleged noncitizen voters charged** between January 2025 and May 2026. The subpoenas issued under the surge are correspondingly small: roughly 13 individuals in Georgia, about 15 in Wisconsin, 2 in Washington.

Former HSI officials identify the mechanism generating the gap: many flagged individuals are **derived citizens** — people who acquired citizenship through a parent's naturalization — who have no citizenship-documentation problem at all but do not appear in the database the way a naturalization record would. A "potential match" in SAVE is a data-reconciliation artifact, not an allegation. One state official noted the information shared with DHS "is all publicly available data."

What the numbers describe is a screening system with a false-positive rate high enough that 250,000 flags have produced 41 charges, being used to justify armed federal investigators conducting field interviews in nine states during a pre-election window. The output of the investigation is not primarily prosecutions; on the record so far it is contact. This sits alongside the September ICE/HSI pre-solicitation to acquire voter files nationwide through a private contractor, and the Justice Department's September 10 preservation letters warning state election officials of criminal sanctions — three instruments, one apparatus, all arriving inside the same eight weeks.
