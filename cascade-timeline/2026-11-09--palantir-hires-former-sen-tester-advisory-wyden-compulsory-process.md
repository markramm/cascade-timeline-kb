NPR reported on November 9 that Palantir Technologies hired former Sen. Jon Tester (D-MT), who lost reelection to the Senate in 2024, as the company faces scrutiny in the incoming Congress. Tester described his agreement with Palantir as "advisory in nature, it is not lobbying". Sen. Ron Wyden, along with 30 other Democratic lawmakers, had earlier demanded answers about Palantir's contracts with the Department of Homeland Security; Wyden said that if Palantir is not responsive next year, "compulsory processes will be on the table". Palantir currently holds $2 billion worth of federal contracts, according to government records.

Tester was an outspoken critic of the PATRIOT Act during his time in the Senate, which makes the hire a former surveillance skeptic joining the company at the center of the DHS data-integration contracts. It follows the earlier oversight letters recorded in [[2026-04-14--goldman-wyden-30-lawmakers-demand-palantir-elite-immigrationos-oversight]] and the contract in [[2026-02-19--dhs-signs-billion-dollar-palantir-ice-surveillance-contract]]. Whether the arrangement is reportable lobbying is not established by the reporting.
