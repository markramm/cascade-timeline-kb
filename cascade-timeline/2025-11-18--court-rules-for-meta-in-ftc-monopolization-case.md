On November 18, 2025, Chief Judge James Boasberg ruled for Meta in the Federal Trade Commission's monopolization case. The court held that the agency had not carried its burden of showing a monopoly. The FTC had alleged that Meta "preserved its monopoly not by outcompeting its upstart rivals Instagram and WhatsApp, but by buying them", and the ruling turned on whether Meta holds a monopoly in the relevant market.

## What the FTC alleged

The FTC filed the case at the end of 2020. Its theory: there is a distinct market for "personal social networking" services, Meta holds a monopoly in it, and Meta kept that monopoly by buying Instagram (2012) and WhatsApp (2014). Those are the agency's allegations, as the court summarizes them. The FTC approved both acquisitions at the time.

## What the court held

- **The ruling.** "The Court ultimately concludes that the agency has not carried its burden: Meta holds no monopoly in the relevant market."
- **The market.** "The Court ultimately finds that YouTube and TikTok belong in the product market, and they prevent Meta from holding a monopoly." The opinion adds: "Even if YouTube is out, including TikTok alone defeats the FTC’s case."
- **The standard.** "Whether or not Meta enjoyed monopoly power in the past, though, the agency must show that it continues to hold such power now."
- **The record.** The court notes that its two earlier opinions on motions to dismiss did not mention TikTok.
- **Judgment.** The opinion ends: "A judgment so stating shall issue this day."

## Reactions

Meta's chief legal officer, Jennifer Newstead, said in a statement to NPR: "The Court's decision today recognizes that Meta faces fierce competition."

The FTC's spokesperson, Joe Simonson, told NPR the agency was "deeply disappointed" and said: "The deck was always stacked against us with Judge Boasberg." NPR reported that he questioned the judge's impartiality.

## The impeachment resolution

On March 18, 2025, Representative Brandon Gill of Texas and other members introduced House Resolution 229, which would impeach the judge. It was referred to the House Judiciary Committee. Its article of impeachment concerns the judge's handling of Alien Enemies Act matters, not this case. No later action on it is recorded in this entry.
