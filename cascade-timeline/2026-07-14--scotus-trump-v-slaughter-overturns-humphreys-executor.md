The Supreme Court decided *Trump v. Slaughter* on July 14-15, 2026, ruling 6-3 that Trump could remove FTC Commissioner Rebecca Kelly Slaughter — the ruling the Court had signaled was coming when it allowed the firing to proceed on the shadow docket the previous month ([[2026-06-27--scotus-trump-v-slaughter-firing-power-ruling-imminent]]). The decision overturns nine decades of *Humphrey's Executor* precedent that had insulated independent regulatory commissions (FTC, SEC, CFPB, and similar bodies) from at-will presidential removal.

The core fact (Trump prevailed, Humphrey's Executor no longer protects independent-agency removal) is corroborated across all three sources; the precise scope of the holding — full overruling versus a narrower agency-specific carve-out — should be verified against the opinion text before being treated as settled for downstream reporting. Notably, the Court allowed Fed Governor Lisa Cook to remain in her post the same term, suggesting an institution-specific carve-out rather than a categorical rule — see [[2026-06-29--scotus-overturns-humphreys-executor-presidential-firing-power]] for the companion ruling.

This directly enabled the EAC commissioner firings nine days later ([[2026-07-10--trump-fires-eac-commissioners-hicks-hovland-after-humphreys-executor]]) and effectively subjugates FTC, SEC, and CFPB leadership to White House removal-at-will while ongoing crypto self-dealing scrutiny of the administration continues.
