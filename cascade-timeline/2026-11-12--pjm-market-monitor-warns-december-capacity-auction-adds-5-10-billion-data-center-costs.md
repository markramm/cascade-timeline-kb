Monitoring Analytics, the independent market monitor for PJM Interconnection, warned on Thursday, November 12, 2026, in its report on the state of the PJM market, that PJM's capacity process has not been competitive, mainly because the demand from data centers, many not yet built, is still counted, the Pennsylvania Capital-Star reported. The report said the capacity auction set for December will again clear the maximum price, adding another $5 billion to $10 billion of data center costs to electricity bills across the region, and that capacity cost rose more than 80% in the first nine months of 2026 compared with the same period of 2025. The monitor recommended postponing the December auction. "PJM has failed to implement the broad public policy mandate reflected in the principles agreed to by all the PJM governors and the White House, as well as the Ratepayer Protection Pledge, to ensure that data centers pay their own costs," it said in a news release. A PJM spokesman said PJM fundamentally disagrees, and that how data center costs are shared is up to state utility regulators. PJM's decisions affect the electricity bills of 67 million consumers.

The $5 billion to $10 billion figure is the monitor's projection for an auction not yet held. The Ratepayer Protection Pledge was the administration's accord under which major tech companies agreed to obtain their own power supplies.
