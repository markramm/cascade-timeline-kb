ICE signed a **$9,975,502.40** task order on **September 4, 2026** with **Teya Diversified Solutions**, an Alaska Native corporation-owned firm, to keep security, power, and fire-detection systems running at the vacant warehouse outside **Williamsport, Maryland** that a federal judge barred it from opening as a 1,500-bed detention center five months earlier. The award period runs through **September 3, 2027**. It is the first task order issued under a broader caretaker contract vehicle worth up to **$28 million**, awarded to Teya **without competition** under the Small Business Administration program that lets Alaska Native corporation-owned companies win federal contracts without competitive bid. Project Salt Box, working from federal spending records, puts actual spending on the site at **$225.4 million** to date and total potential exposure at roughly **$753.4 million** if every option is exercised — the **$102.4 million** January purchase price, KVG's **$641 million** renovation option ceiling, and this **$9.9 million** caretaker order.

**The structural fact is that the injunction stopped the facility but not the spending.** Canon already records each upstream step: the purchase from Fundrise ([[2026-01-27--dhs-purchases-fundrise-williamsport-md-warehouse]]), the KVG renovation award ([[2026-03-07--ice-awards-kvg-113m-williamsport-contract]]), the NEPA injunction that halted construction ([[2026-04-15--judge-halts-williamsport-ice-construction-nepa]]), and the WEXMAC acquisition method that let DHS buy the building before the public knew ([[2026-04-20--wypr-exposes-williamsport-wexmac-contracting-method]]). What this entry adds is the *terminal* state of that sequence: a site legally unusable for its intended purpose, generating a nine-figure sunk cost and a fresh seven-figure annual maintenance obligation, with the maintenance itself routed through a non-competed vehicle. Litigation raised the cost of the program without reaching the appropriation behind it.

**Two procurement patterns compound here.** The site was acquired through a method designed to avoid the ordinary public real-estate process, and it is now maintained through a set-aside that avoids the ordinary competition process. The one-year award term is itself evidence: an agency provisioning a full year of caretaker service at a blocked site is not treating the injunction as the end of the project. That reading is consistent with the program-wide posture recorded in [[2026-08-19--ice-detention-reengineering-initiative-stalls-program-wide-11-purchased-sites]] — 11 purchased sites, $1.074 billion spent, no restart timeline — where the initiative stalled without being abandoned. Williamsport is the per-site version of that same holding pattern, and the NEPA theory that produced the injunction is the one mapped across the WEXMAC-TITUS census in [[2026-09-11--gilroy-williamsport-nepa-injunctions-share-categorical-exclusion-theory]].

**Independently verified beyond the ledger's original sourcing.** The $9,975,502.40 figure, the September 4 signing, the $28 million non-competed vehicle ceiling, the September 3, 2027 period of performance, and the $225.4M-spent / $753.4M-exposure arithmetic were confirmed against Project Salt Box's reporting on 2026-09-20; the same contract was independently noted in Austin Kocher's September 19, 2026 immigration-numbers digest. The judicial bar and the underlying Maryland suit are tier-1 sourced (Washington Post, March 11, 2026).
