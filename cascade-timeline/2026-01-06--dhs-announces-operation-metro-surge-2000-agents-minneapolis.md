On January 6, 2026, the Department of Homeland Security posted on X that "The largest DHS operation ever is happening right now in Minnesota," and ICE Acting Director Todd Lyons told Newsmax that the crackdown was the agency's "largest immigration operation ever." The 2,000 figure was not given on the record. The Associated Press reported that a person briefed on the operation said DHS planned to send as many as 2,000 officers to the Minneapolis-St. Paul area, and that neither Lyons nor DHS's spokeswoman said how many officers were involved. CBS News's "roughly 2,000" also came from officials who asked not to be named. Secretary Kristi Noem was also present and accompanied ICE officers during at least one arrest, per the AP; the sources cited here do not have her announcing the deployment or using the phrase.

Lyons said agents from Homeland Security Investigations were going door-to-door in the Twin Cities area investigating allegations of fraud, human smuggling and unlawful employment practices. The AP described the surge as a crackdown tied in part to allegations of fraud involving Somali residents.

Operation Metro Surge was not new that day. DHS said it launched on Dec. 1, and on December 4, 2025 DHS announced 12 arrests under that name, after Twin Cities leaders said more than 100 federal agents would be deployed (the Twin Cities Fox station's report). CBS News reported that as of Dec. 19, ICE had carried out nearly 700 arrests as part of the operation, according to DHS. The January deployment was a much larger one.

The AP's source said roughly three-quarters of the enforcement personnel were expected to come from ICE's Enforcement and Removal Operations; the force also included Homeland Security Investigations agents, specialized tactical units and U.S. Customs and Border Protection personnel including Cmdr. Gregory Bovino. CBS News's officials said as many as 600 HSI agents and up to 1,500 ICE ERO officers would rotate through over the month-long deployment, with Special Response Teams also slated to take part. By Jan. 11, WCCO counted more than 2,400 federal agents in the Minneapolis area, more than double the number of local police officers.

The next day, an ICE agent killed Renee Nicole Good, whom local officials described as a legal observer. As of Jan. 7, 2026, according to the city's crime dashboard data as summarized by Media Bias/Fact Check, hers was the only homicide Minneapolis had recorded in 2026. Federal prosecutors in Minnesota later resigned over the Justice Department's handling of the investigation; see [[2026-01-13--twelve-prosecutors-resign-over-renee-good-case]].

## Correction, 2026-11-16

Changed the wording from *an ICE agent killed legal observer Renee Good* to *an ICE agent killed Renee Nicole Good, whom local officials described as a legal observer*. Good's label as a legal observer comes from local officials, per the reporting cited in the corrections record; her family's attorneys said she and her wife stopped to observe with the intention of supporting and helping their neighbors; the family's complaint in *Ganger v. Ross* has Becca Good filming and Renee Good honking her horn to warn neighbors and does not apply the label to Renee. The entry now attributes it.

## Correction, 2026-11-17

Changed: who said what. The entry said DHS Secretary Kristi Noem announced the deployment of 2,000 federal agents and called it the "largest immigration operation ever." In the AP report (PBS) the phrase is ICE Acting Director Lyons's, from a Newsmax interview, and DHS's own post said "The largest DHS operation ever is happening right now in Minnesota." The 2,000 figure comes from a person briefed on the operation (as many as 2,000) and from officials who spoke to CBS News on condition of anonymity (roughly 2,000); neither Lyons nor DHS's spokeswoman gave a number. Noem was present at an arrest; the AP report does not have her making the announcement.

Changed: the start of the operation. Removed "quietly" and the 700-agent figure: CBS's 700 counts arrests as of Dec. 19. "Early December" stands.

Removed: the last paragraph's nationwide protests and count of resigning prosecutors; the prosecutors are in the linked entry. The homicide line now follows the Media Bias/Fact Check summary of the city dashboard, through Jan. 7.

Kept: the ERO share of the force (now "roughly three-quarters", the AP source's estimate), the Special Response Teams, the Homeland Security Investigations contingent (now as many as 600) and the Bovino involvement, all supported by the AP and CBS reports.
