Edward S. Herman and Noam Chomsky publish *Manufacturing Consent: The Political Economy of the Mass Media* (Pantheon Books, New York), proposing the **propaganda model** of the U.S. mass media. **Herman is the first-listed author**; the book is frequently miscited in secondary discussion as "Chomsky's" *Manufacturing Consent* or as "Chomsky and Herman," an inversion of the credited order. Herman — an economist, not a linguist — did the bulk of the model's empirical development; by Chomsky's own account (per secondary reporting on the book's authorship split, not independently verified against a primary statement in this pass) most of the manuscript, including the propaganda-model chapters, is Herman's work. This entry cites it as **Herman and Chomsky, 1988**.

**Date-precision note**: the archival catalog records located in this research pass (Internet Archive digitized first edition; Open Library edition record) confirm the year (1988) and publisher (Pantheon Books) but do not independently confirm a month or day. One Open Library aggregate search surfaced "1988-10" for this edition, but that figure could not be traced to a primary bibliographic record in this pass, so it is not asserted here. This is NOT a claim that the book was published on January 1.

**The propaganda model — five filters, in the authors' own terms.** The book's opening chapter, "A Propaganda Model," states that money and power filter out the news fit to print, marginalize dissent, and let government and dominant private interests get their messages across to the public, operating through five filters:

1. **Size, concentrated ownership, owner wealth, and profit orientation** of the dominant mass-media firms.
2. **Advertising as the primary income source** of the mass media.
3. **Reliance on information provided by government, business, and "experts"** funded and approved by these primary sources and agents of power.
4. **"Flak"** as a means of disciplining the media.
5. **"Anticommunism"** as a national religion and control mechanism.

(Herman and Chomsky later reframed the fifth filter more broadly as "fear" — of terrorism or of a designated enemy — in editions and commentary following the end of the Cold War; this entry states the filter as named in the 1988 first edition.)

**The model is explicitly structural, not a conspiracy claim.** Herman and Chomsky state that the filters "narrow the range of news that passes through the gates, and even more sharply limit what can become 'big news'" and that they "fix the premises of discourse and interpretation, and the definition of what is newsworthy in the first place." Critically, the authors are explicit that this does **not** require coordination or bad faith among journalists: "The elite domination of the media and marginalization of dissidents that results from the operation of these filters occurs so naturally that media news people, frequently operating with complete integrity and goodwill, are able to convince themselves that they choose and interpret the news 'objectively' and on the basis of professional news values." The claim is that the five filters constrain outcomes at a systemic level — ownership structure, revenue dependence, source access, organized retaliation, and ideological boundary-setting — regardless of individual reporters' intent, and that "the constraints are so powerful, and are built into the system in such a fundamental way, that alternative bases of news choices are hardly imaginable."

**Not the 1992 documentary.** *Manufacturing Consent: Noam Chomsky and the Media*, the 1992 documentary film by Mark Achbar and Peter Wintonick, is a separate work — a film about Chomsky's political thought broadly, not an adaptation of this book's propaganda-model argument — and is not the subject of this entry.
