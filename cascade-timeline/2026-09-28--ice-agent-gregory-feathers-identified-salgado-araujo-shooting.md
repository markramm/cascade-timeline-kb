## Opening

Nearly three months after ICE fatally shot **Lorenzo Salgado Araujo** during a traffic stop in Houston on July 7, 2026 ([[2026-07-07--ice-kills-lorenzo-salgado-araujo-houston-narrative-collapse]]), the Texas Tribune identified the agent who fired as **Gregory Feathers**, a retired ICE officer rehired within the prior two years — reporting published **September 28, 2026**, after the federal government had withheld his name for months. The same week, a Harris County grand jury began actively hearing the case.

## What Happened / Key Facts

**Identification came from records, not agency disclosure.** The Texas Tribune reports Feathers' name appeared in an ICE Form I-213 — the agency's own report documenting the pursuit and shooting — obtained by the outlet. DHS had not named him publicly in the nearly three months since the shooting.

**No charges filed; three separate investigations running.** As of this reporting, Feathers has not been charged with a crime. The FBI, the U.S. Attorney's Office, and DHS are each separately investigating — a multi-track, uncoordinated structure consistent with the pattern already documented for this case (see canon). Feathers' attorney said DHS policy prevents him from speaking publicly during a pending investigation.

**DHS has defended the shooting as self-defense; witnesses dispute it.** DHS's official position is that the shooting was a lawful act of self-defense. Witnesses and Salgado Araujo's family dispute this; two passengers who were in the van said ICE vehicles struck the van before the shooting and that Salgado Araujo did not threaten the agents.

**A Harris County grand jury is actively convened.** Law enforcement sources confirmed to ABC13 Houston that grand jury proceedings for this case began the week of September 21-24, 2026, meeting Wednesdays and Fridays. A witness who was in the van, Jose Trinidad Rojas Pliego, appeared before the grand jury. Harris County District Attorney Sean Teare has said he expects to conclude the grand jury process "soon," while noting that prosecuting a federal agent at the state level faces major jurisdictional hurdles.

## Why This Matters

This is the most procedurally advanced of the three Texas ICE-shooting cases surveyed in this research pass: unlike Ruben Ray Martinez (closed, no-bill) or Wilber Rafael Garces Perez (investigation just opened), Salgado Araujo's case has reached an active grand jury with witness testimony, and the shooter's name — contrary to the agency's practice in the other two cases — is now public via document-based reporting rather than agency disclosure. Whether this grand jury reaches an indictment, a no-bill, or the same jurisdictional obstruction the DA himself flags, is the next fact that will test whether Texas accountability mechanisms produce different outcomes for different cases or converge on the same non-indictment pattern already seen with Martinez.
