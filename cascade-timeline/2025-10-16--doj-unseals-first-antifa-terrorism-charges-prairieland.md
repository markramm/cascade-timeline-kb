On October 16, 2025, FBI Director Kash Patel announced that federal prosecutors in Texas had, for the first time, filed terrorism charges targeting antifa. The Epoch Times reported that two alleged members of a Texas cell had been charged with providing material support to terrorists, a charge that carries up to 15 years in prison. Reuters reported that both men had been arrested in July, with eight others, and charged with attempted murder and weapons offenses.

Patel wrote on social media: "First time ever: the FBI arrested Antifa-aligned anarchist violent extremists and terrorism charges have been brought for the July 4 Prairieland ICE attack in Texas." Attorney General Pam Bondi said in a statement that "Antifa is a left-wing terrorist organization" and "They will be prosecuted as such." Reuters noted that there had been "no mention of antifa" in the original charging documents or the prosecutors' announcement in July. It also reported that some national security law experts called the September executive order designating antifa a terrorist organization legally questionable, because antifa has no official leadership or organizational structure.

The Epoch Times summarized the October indictment: the criminal complaint had described 10 to 12 individuals in black arriving at the facility, while the indictment said at least 11 operatives from an Antifa cell in North Texas were there. CBS News Texas reported that, according to the indictment, the group was heavily armed with over 50 firearms.

The announcement followed National Security Presidential Memorandum 7 (NSPM-7), dated September 25, 2025. It lists "anti-Americanism, anti-capitalism, and anti-Christianity" among the "common threads animating this violent conduct" it describes. Reuters wrote that Patel, in announcing the charges, cited the President's designation of antifa.

The black-clothing and fireworks account belongs to a later indictment. In November, according to Wikipedia's summary, a new federal indictment labeled the Prairieland defendants "operatives" in a "North Texas Antifa Cell" and added the material support charge. The Justice Department's release on that twelve-count indictment lists, under the riot count, conduct such as shooting and throwing fireworks, spraying graffiti on property and vehicles and dressing in black bloc, and lists "personnel (including themselves)" among the support charged in the material support count. After the March 2026 trial, former Justice Department counsel Tom Brzozowski said prosecutors did not have to prove "terroristic intent" for the case.

Kyle Shideler of the Center for Security Policy testified at that trial as the government's expert on antifa. The Intercept reported that he was the author of a September article titled "How to Dismantle Far-Left Extremist Networks: A Roadmap for the Trump Administration," that he said he conferred with prosecutors in October, a month before they obtained an indictment, and that the Southern Poverty Law Center has branded the center a "hate group."
