HHS's Office of Refugee Resettlement awarded a $158 million base contract ($244 million ceiling) to Our Rescue — a Utah nonprofit founded by Tim Ballard, the anti-trafficking figure who resigned from his prior organization in 2023 amid multiple civil suits alleging sexual misconduct — to represent unaccompanied immigrant children in legal proceedings, replacing the Acacia Center for Justice's nearly two-decade relationship with the population. Our Rescue's current CEO, Derek Benner, is a former senior ICE official. The organization has no documented prior experience providing immigration legal services. Critics say the award dismantles established legal infrastructure for one of the most vulnerable detained populations and replaces it with a politically aligned group lacking relevant qualifications, at the exact moment ICE enforcement and unaccompanied-minor deportations are accelerating ([[2026-07-06--propublica-unaccompanied-minor-deportations-tripled-trump]]).

The award is a discrete instance of the accountability-elimination pattern the timeline tracks across the detention-industrial complex: contracting authority repeatedly flows to actors with personal or ideological ties to the enforcement apparatus rather than to organizations with track records serving the affected population, insulating the government's preferred outcome (fewer successful legal challenges to removal) behind a nonprofit's name. It follows ICE's attempted raid on legal-aid offices holding migrant children's files ([[2026-06-13--ice-hsi-attempt-raid-legal-aid-offices-migrant-children-files]]) — both events reduce the practical legal representation available to detained children within weeks of each other.
