On **August 7, 2026**, HHS's Office of Refugee Resettlement signed contract **7571MN26C00029** — **"Legal Services Bridge 2.0"** — obligating **$158,124,790** to **Our Rescue**, the Utah anti-trafficking nonprofit founded as Operation Underground Railroad by Tim Ballard and now led by **Derek Benner**, the former head of ICE Homeland Security Investigations. The federal contracting record codes the award **"NOT COMPETED,"** solicitation procedures **"ONLY ONE SOURCE,"** **one offer received**, under **FAR 6.302-2 — "unusual and compelling urgency."** Its NAICS code is **541110, "Offices of Lawyers."** Queried by UEI across all contract and grant types back to FY2008, **Our Rescue had never held a federal award of any kind**; this contract is its entire federal history, and it is roughly **three times the organization's total annual revenue**.

The urgency was the government's own. ORR was operating under a **preliminary injunction** entered 2025-04-29 in *Community Legal Services in East Palo Alto v. HHS* (N.D. Cal.) barring it from cutting off appropriated funding for children's legal representation, and under **8 U.S.C. § 1232(c)(5)**, the anti-trafficking statute requiring the government to ensure these children have counsel. **One day before signing** — on **August 6, 2026** — Judge **Araceli Martínez-Olguín** granted a motion to enforce that injunction, recording that the government **"concede[d] that they have withheld funds appropriated for direct legal representation… since December 2025 and continue to do so,"** rejecting its "invoice substantiation" defense as an attempt to **"utilize a contractual dispute as a shield,"** and ordering the withheld money released by **noon the following day — August 7**. She further found that because the incumbent contractor's contract had **expired July 31, 2026**, *"it appears to the Court that no mechanism presently exists by which Defendants are complying with the preliminary injunction,"* and that the government's description of its replacement plan **"raises more questions than it provides answers"** — citing **"two agreements recently reached for a seeming fraction of the legal services to be provided across the country."**

The displaced incumbent, the **Acacia Center for Justice**, had administered a subcontracted network of roughly 100 to 130 legal service providers serving more than 20,000 children, under awards including a **$832,386,516** ORR contract (2022–2025) and a **$356,310,289** bridge (2025–2026). One of the "two agreements" was almost certainly a **second single-source award**: on **August 6** ORR noticed its intent to award **up to $150,000,000** to **Burke Law Group, PLLC**, a Houston firm with roughly 30 lawyers, two of them in immigration, and ties to the Trump administration ([91 FR 50848](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/06/2026-16081/announcement-of-the-intent-to-award-a-single-source-cooperative-agreement-to-burke-law-group-pllc-in)). **Burke withdrew on August 11**, and ORR formally rescinded the notice on August 17.

Two non-competed awards totaling up to **$308 million**, four days apart, to two entities with no relevant track record, replacing a functioning provider network — one of which then walked away. Four U.S. senators wrote to Benner and Our Rescue's board on **August 18** demanding answers by **September 1**, citing "the nonexistence of legal services provided by your organization" and raising confidentiality concerns given the organization's ICE leadership. Michael Lukens of the Amica Center: *"HHS is causing chaos in the system, and that may be on purpose."*

The pattern is the one this timeline tracks across the detention-industrial complex: an accountability channel — here, a detained child's lawyer — is not abolished but **redirected** into hands structurally aligned with the enforcement apparatus, through procurement mechanics that avoid competition and disclosure. It follows ICE's attempted raid on legal-aid offices holding migrant children's files ([[2026-06-13--ice-hsi-attempt-raid-legal-aid-offices-migrant-children-files]]) and tripled unaccompanied-minor deportations ([[2026-07-06--propublica-unaccompanied-minor-deportations-tripled-trump]]).
