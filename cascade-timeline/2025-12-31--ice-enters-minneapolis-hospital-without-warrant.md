ICE agents entered Hennepin County Medical Center's emergency department without a judicial warrant over the holiday, remaining at a patient's bedside for more than 24 hours, according to a spokesperson for Unidos MN, and handcuffing the patient to the hospital bed. Agents gained access to staff-only areas including break rooms and parking lots before hospital security confronted them and confirmed no judicial warrant existed, at which point they were told to leave and complied.

Rep. Aisha Gomez stated: "Our Hennepin County commissioners worked with leadership of the hospital, including the leadership of security, established that there was no judicial warrant, and told them that they had to leave and they left." Rep. Mohamud Noor noted: "This is an individual with a high medical risk that that individual, if he was detained, he could have lost his life," per CBS Minnesota's transcription.

Other reporting, not tied to this incident, records fear of care among Twin Cities and Minnesota families that winter. Dr. Janna Gewirtz O'Brien, president-elect of the Minnesota Chapter of the American Academy of Pediatrics, said ICE raids were leading a growing number of people across Minnesota to cancel medical appointments (CIDRAP). Nurse practitioner Munira Maalimisaq told CBS Minnesota in an earlier report, published before the hospital episode: "My patients are canceling. They're afraid to come out of their home."
