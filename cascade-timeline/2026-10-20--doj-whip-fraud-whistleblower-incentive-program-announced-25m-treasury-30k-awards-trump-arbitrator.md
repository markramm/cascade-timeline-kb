Date: Tuesday, Oct. 20, 2026 (the day of the press release and the Oval Office event). In that press release the Justice Department and its National Fraud Enforcement Division "announced the creation of the Fraud Whistleblower Incentive Program (WHIP)." It calls WHIP "the first ever whistleblower rewards program that pays an up-front award for tips leading to criminal charges." Trump and Vance presented it the same day at an Oval Office event.

**What the primary document says.** The release states WHIP "will pay whistleblowers an award of $10,000 to $30,000 for information leading to criminal charges relating to certain subject areas," and that "The Department of the Treasury is committing $25 million" to the program. Awards "will be made in the Justice Department’s sole discretion following the filing of criminal charges". For health-care fraud it says the program "will prioritize submissions involving hospice, home health care, day care, and special services fraud."

**What Trump added at the event.** According to the Associated Press, Trump said "payments will amount to a 1% commission on the amount of fraud the tip helps uncover." Spectrum News quotes him: "we’re giving a 1% commission to that person." The 1% commission is not in the DOJ release. On disputes between claimants, the AP quotes Trump: "I will be the arbitrator of all of these decisions" and "It'll be a binding arbitration." Spectrum News reports he said whistleblowers would be paid "without the need for a criminal charge or conviction," which differs from the DOJ release, whose awards come after criminal charges are filed. Vance told the room, per the AP, "if you want to help us stop it, you get to share in the rewards."

**Where to report.** The DOJ release sends the public to "america.gov/fraud." The New York Post says tips "must be submitted on fraud.gov," and Spectrum News says the fraud.gov report tab links to an America.gov chatbot. When fetched for this entry, fraud.gov redirected to the White House "Fraud Ledger" page at whitehouse.gov/fraud.

**The Minnesota influencer.** The AP reports that "The White House had said Trump would present a $10,000 check to Nick Shirley," the influencer whose video said members of Minnesota's Somali community were running fake childcare centers, but "a check was not given to Shirley in the presence of journalists". The New York Post likewise says the award "wasn’t given publicly." Spectrum News, by contrast, reports Shirley "became the first recipient to receive a check." Whether and when a payment was handed over is therefore unresolved in the record; the AP and New York Post accounts agree it did not happen on camera. Shirley appeared on the record at the event; the AP quotes him: "When you expose fraud, you help save America."

**Context, kept separate.** The "primarily in those Blue States" focus of the fraud effort was stated by Trump, not Vance, in an earlier Truth Social post reported by Fox News: Vance's focus would be "primarily in those Blue States." This entry does not carry it as a Vance statement.
