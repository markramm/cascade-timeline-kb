On January 17-19, 2026 — roughly two months after UN Security Council authorization of the body in November 2025 — the Trump administration distributed charter invitations and publicly unveiled the governance architecture of the "Board of Peace," the entity chartered to administer Gaza's reconstruction through December 31, 2027. The charter, quoted directly by TIME, sets standard membership at a three-year rotating term at no cost, but grants permanent membership to any state contributing more than $1 billion in cash to the Board's fund within the charter's first year. Trump is named inaugural chairman with, per JNS's charter analysis, "sole authority to invite new members and appoint his own successor," plus "absolute veto power" over all strategic board decisions — a governance structure with no term limit or external check documented in press reporting. The founding Executive Board comprises Trump (chair), Secretary of State Marco Rubio, senior adviser Jared Kushner, Special Envoy Steve Witkoff, former UK PM Tony Blair, Apollo Global Management CEO Marc Rowan, World Bank President Ajay Banga, and deputy national security adviser Robert Gabriel. More than 60 nations were invited; 35 had accepted as of the announcement. Notably, the charter's mandate language does not name Gaza specifically but describes the board's purpose as promoting "stability" and "enduring peace in areas affected or threatened by conflict" — open-ended language that leaves the door open to non-Gaza deployments of the same governance architecture even as public messaging anchors the board's near-term work to Gaza.
