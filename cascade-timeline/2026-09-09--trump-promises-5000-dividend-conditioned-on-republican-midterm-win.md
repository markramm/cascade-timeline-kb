Speaking at the Republican Party's midterm convention in Dallas on the evening of September 9, 2026, Donald Trump promised a $5,000 "dividend" to every adult citizen, payable only if Republicans hold both the Senate and the House in the November 3 midterms. "I'm asking you to pretend that I'm on the ballot," he told the audience. The proposal would cost more than $1 trillion and would require congressional appropriation; Vice President JD Vance subsequently narrowed it, saying wealthy Americans would not qualify and suggesting tariff revenue as the financing source. Trump said the money must be spent in the United States.

The structural feature worth recording is the conditional. A promised federal payment contingent on a specific electoral outcome inverts the ordinary relationship between an appropriation and the electorate: the payment is not offered as policy to be enacted, but as a return on a vote. Commentators and legal observers raised federal vote-buying statutes in response; that framing is contested and untested here, and no charge or enforcement action has followed — this entry records the promise and its condition, not a legal conclusion about them.

Placed against the same week's election-capture record — a third emergency application to the Supreme Court seeking to make USPS mail-ballot restrictions operative before November, and an ICE procurement to build recurring nationwide voter-file acquisition — it belongs to a pattern this timeline tracks along two tracks at once: narrowing who can cast a ballot through administrative machinery, and altering what a ballot is worth to the person casting it. The proposal requires Congress to act and Congress has not; as of this entry it is a campaign promise, and its significance is in what it treats as offerable.
