After hours of emotional public testimony, DeSoto County, Florida commissioners on June 23, 2026 voted 4-0 to direct the county attorney to draft a one-year moratorium on new data center applications — but explicitly indicated intent to carve out an already-pending gas-turbine-powered hyperscale campus proposed by DCIP Group on a decommissioned power plant site that could reach 4 gigawatts. (Two outlets' June 25 framing of this as the direction-vote date is close but not exact per the primary Legistar record, which dates the directing vote 2026-06-23; June 25 is retained here as the capture date since that is when this pattern was first reported.) The carve-out means the moratorium protects residents from future projects while the single project residents actually mobilized to fight continues through the approval pipeline. The final moratorium vote followed on July 28, 2026, adopted as Ordinance 2026-016 — see [[2026-07-29--desoto-county-fl-moratorium-approved-loophole-preserved]] for the confirmed exemption text and [[dcip-group]] for DCIP's corporate and power-architecture detail. (4GW figure from tier-2 MySuncoast, consistent with tier-1 Suncoast Searchlight's scale reporting and DCIP's own marketing site.)

This is the documented local-community-resistance pattern: cities and counties enacting moratoria against fast-tracked data center expansion as ratepayer and water/power anxieties surface. It sits alongside the New York statewide S10642 moratorium, Arizona and Illinois tax moratoriums, and the Monterey Park municipal ban — a barn-door-after-the-horse dynamic where local pauses arrive only once a flagship hyperscale project is already entrenched.
