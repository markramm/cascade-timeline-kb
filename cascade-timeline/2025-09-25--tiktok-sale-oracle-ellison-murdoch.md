Trump signed an executive order on September 25, 2025 approving the sale of TikTok's U.S. operations. CNBC reported that Oracle, Silver Lake and the Abu Dhabi-based MGX would be the main investors, holding a roughly 45% stake in the new entity, with ByteDance retaining less than a 20% stake; Vice President Vance said the transaction values the business at $14 billion, but no purchase price was provided. Oracle would oversee the app's security operations and continue providing cloud computing services. That was the announced framework as of September 25, 2025; the structure that closed in January 2026 is below.

NPR reported on September 26 that the administration had asked the investor coalition for a payment to the federal government "in the low billions." The only source for the request is one person with direct knowledge of the talks, who was not authorized to speak publicly; Trump, asked about the fee, answered "We're going to be announcing different things, but the U.S. comes out great." The White House did not return NPR's request for comment. No amount has been documented in this entry's sources.

Who called it a shakedown: the word is not NPR's. NPR's headline attributed "shakedown scheme" to experts, and the quoted words are those of Yale School of Management professor Jeffrey Sonnenfeld, who was describing Trump's interventions in private business generally, not the TikTok fee specifically: "These are shakedown schemes." He added, "I'd even call it extortion." University of Delaware associate professor Dael Norwood said "There is no consistent principle at play, just the exercise of Trump's personal power over other people's money."

NPR described the investor coalition as including Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison, the Murdochs and a venture capital firm; CNBC said Fox News owner Rupert Murdoch and his son "could be involved" and named Dell Technologies CEO Michael Dell as another possible participant. Both outlets reported the investors' roles as reported or expected, not as completed.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

This entry was rechecked against NPR, CNBC and TikTok's own announcement. Corrected:

1. **Shakedown attribution.** The entry gave the word as NPR's own characterization of the payment demand. NPR's headline attributes the phrase to experts; the verbatim quote is Sonnenfeld's, about Trump's business interventions in general. Attributed to him above.
2. **Trump publicly demanding billions.** Removed. The request is reported through one anonymous source (via NPR, which cites an earlier Wall Street Journal report of the fee); Trump's public answer was evasive. Title changed to match.
3. **Ownership as of its date.** The 45% and under-20% figures were the announced September 25 framework, not an error, but it was stated without date or base and CNBC gave ByteDance under 20% and a further 35% for ByteDance investors and new holders. The structure that closed on January 22-23, 2026 differs in shape: TikTok's announcement says three managing investors, Silver Lake, Oracle and MGX, each hold 15%, and ByteDance retains 19.9%. The other investors it lists are the Dell Family Office, Vastmere (Susquehanna affiliate), Alpha Wave, Revolution, Merritt Way (Dragoneer partners), Via Nova (General Atlantic affiliate), Virgo LI (Milner foundation) and NJJ Capital (a French telecom entrepreneur's family office); the announcement names neither the Murdochs nor that venture capital firm.
4. **Removed as unsupported by the sources cited:** that control was "handed" to Ellison, the Murdochs and Dell as a completed fact (the January announcement does list the Dell Family Office); "Fox Corp" as a participant; Oracle managing "the algorithm" (CNBC says security operations and cloud; NPR says ByteDance will license the algorithm to the U.S. entity to be retrained); surveillance capabilities over Americans' data and a content pipeline favorable to Trump (our inference, not a documented finding; NPR's 170 million is the user count the algorithm would be retrained on); Ellison's donations to Trump.
