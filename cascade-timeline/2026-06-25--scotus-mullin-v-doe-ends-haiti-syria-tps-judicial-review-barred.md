On June 25, 2026, the Supreme Court, 6-3, allowed the Trump administration to terminate Temporary Protected Status for about 350,000 Haitians and 6,000 Syrians. The Court reversed and remanded, removing the lower-court orders that had postponed the terminations during litigation. In **Mullin v. Doe** (No. 25–1083, consolidated with **Trump v. Miot**, No. 25–1084), Justice Alito announced the judgment and delivered the opinion of the Court except as to Part III–A, which was joined by only three other Justices. The Court wrote that the TPS statute bars judicial review of non-constitutional claims, so the courts may not second-guess the Secretary on those grounds. The case was argued on certiorari before judgment, and the question presented was interim relief pending litigation; this is a signed opinion after argument, not a bare emergency order. The Haiti termination notice of November 2025 set a termination date of February 3, 2026, and lower-court orders had held it off.

The reasoning reaches beyond Haiti and Syria. The opinion says the Secretary has terminated every TPS designation that has come up for renewal, 13 in all. The Court did not bar the **constitutional** (equal-protection) claim; it ruled that the claim is unlikely to succeed. In dissent, Justice Kagan (joined by Sotomayor and Jackson) wrote that the President's statements were “so repellent and racially infected that the majority declines to put them in print,” and that they “fairly shout” that “race entered into the President's resolve to remove Haitians from this country.” The majority's answer was that none of the cited statements was “overtly racial.” The dissent set out the 2024 Springfield hoax ([[2024-09-09--vance-spreads-anti-haitian-immigrant-hoax-1757456537]]) in the President's words, Haitians “eating the pets of the people that live” in Springfield, Ohio, among the statements before the Court, so a slander that marked a community for action reached the U.S. Reports.

**Context / pattern.** This is the resolution of the Haiti/Syria TPS case the administration brought to the Court in March ([[2026-03-11--trump-asks-supreme-court-end-haiti-tps-350000]], cert granted [[2026-03-16--scotus-grants-cert-haiti-syria-tps]]), and it extends the path the Court ran on Venezuelan TPS in 2025 ([[2025-05-19--scotus-stays-noem-v-ntpsa-venezuelan-tps-termination]], [[2025-10-03--scotus-stays-ntpsa-venezuelan-tps-summary-judgment]]). It goes further, because it holds non-constitutional challenges to terminations unreviewable rather than merely staying relief. The case caption names Markwayne Mullin as Secretary of Homeland Security ([[2026-03-11--mullin-dhs-confirmation-hearing-march-18-scheduled]]). The use of immigration status as a removable, court-proof category, applied to a population already marked by a fabricated slander, fits the documented pattern of accountability-elimination and the deportation-pipeline beat. The harm lands hardest in concentrated communities like Springfield, Ohio. Gov. Mike DeWine (R) said the policy to remove these individuals is a “mistake,” and that the over 10,000 Haitians in Ohio were “working and contributing to our community and economy yesterday” while “today it is now illegal to employ them.”

**Update: the Haiti termination took effect July 27, 2026, and work permits ran through that day.** USCIS's Haiti TPS page states that the designation is terminated effective July 27, 2026, the day the Supreme Court's judgment issued in the consolidated case. Employment authorization documents under the Haiti designation stayed valid until then: an earlier USCIS release for employers says their validity “is extended per court order”. A later USCIS release says work permits in the Haiti TPS categories “are no longer valid”. Haitian TPS holders, as a class, therefore lost both work authorization and protection from removal on and after July 27, not in the days before it. The Syria termination date and any Syria-specific work-permit dates were not verified. This is the operational consequence the June 25 ruling made legally possible: about a month separated the Court removing judicial review of those claims from the termination taking effect.
