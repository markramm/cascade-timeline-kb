The Marshall County, Iowa Board of Supervisors voted unanimously (6-0) on September 23, 2026 to adopt a six-month moratorium on applications for data centers, AI computing facilities, data mining facilities, and cryptocurrency mining operations, remaining in place until March 23, 2027 unless extended or shortened. The Marshall County Zoning Commission had unanimously recommended the moratorium in early August; supervisors discussed the resolution at a September 9 meeting before voting at the following regular meeting. No hyperscaler project is named in the reporting reviewed as the proximate cause. The city of Marshalltown, within the county, has pursued its own parallel city-level data-center pause.

The vote is part of a broader county-level pattern this timeline has tracked closely — this same week, Pima County, Arizona ([[2026-09-22--pima-county-az-120-day-data-center-moratorium]]) and St. Joseph County, Indiana ([[2026-09-22--st-joseph-county-in-council-votes-6-3-to-begin-data-center-ban-process]]) each moved on data-center development pauses, following Linn County, Iowa's earlier 18-month moratorium ([[2026-07-01--linn-county-ia-18-month-data-center-moratorium-vote]]). Unlike several of those actions, this one passed unanimously and with no recorded dissent, and unlike Pima County's water-scarcity framing, no specific environmental trigger is cited in the available reporting — the moratorium reads as a standard pause-for-zoning-review measure rather than a response to a documented local strain.
