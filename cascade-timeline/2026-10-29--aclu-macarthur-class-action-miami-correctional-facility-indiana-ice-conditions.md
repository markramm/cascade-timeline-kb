Civil rights attorneys from Illinois and Indiana filed a federal lawsuit Thursday over conditions at Miami Correctional Facility in Bunker Hill, Indiana, which DHS is expanding for ICE use by 1,000 beds in partnership with the state. Since 2025, at least two immigrants have died in custody there, the Sun-Times reported. ACLU of Illinois staff attorney Kathleen Hickey said: "People are suffering, people have died, and more people will suffer and die if those continue." DHS denied the allegations.

Part of the pattern at [[2026-06-24--aclu-17-ice-custody-deaths-warehouse-expansion-96600-beds]].

## Update (2026-10-29, filing date): The complaint: Vasenin v. DHS, No. 3:26-cv-01582 (N.D. Ind.)

The case is Vasenin v. U.S. Department of Homeland Security, No. 3:26-cv-01582, in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Indiana, South Bend Division. The class action complaint was filed 10/29/26. The defendants, all sued in their official capacities, are DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, ICE Acting Director David J. Venturella, Marcos Charles, Frank Padula, ICE, DHS, the interim warden of Miami Correctional, Indiana Department of Correction Commissioner Lloyd Arnold and Dr. Adrienne Bedford.

The proposed class is "All civil immigration detainees who are currently detained, or who will be detained, at Miami Correctional Facility." The complaint pleads claims: Fifth Amendment conditions of confinement and inadequate medical and mental health care against the federal defendants, and Fourteenth Amendment conditions of confinement and inadequate medical care, under 42 U.S.C. § 1983, against the state defendants. Plaintiffs say they plan to seek "emergency discovery."
