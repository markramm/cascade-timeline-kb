KBR, then a Halliburton subsidiary, put out a press release on January 24, 2006 about an indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity (IDIQ) contingency contract it had won, to support Department of Homeland Security (DHS) Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) facilities in the event of "an emergency influx of immigrants into the U.S." or to support "the rapid development of new programs". The figure the story carries, $385 million, is a maximum total value (a ceiling), not money spent. Federal procurement data (USAspending, drawn from FPDS) place the contract (PIID in the frontmatter corrections) at the Army Corps of Engineers Fort Worth office, with orders totalling $2,668,546.38 in obligations over its life.

## What Happened / Key Facts

**Press-release date versus award date (date kind).** January 24, 2006 is the press-release date; the contract took effect at once, in KBR's words, and HalliburtonWatch carried it the same day. The federal record dates the earliest obligation sooner. A readiness order of $481,212 carries a USAspending date-signed in late December 2005, with a performance period running through late 2006. The master contract record itself carries only a migrated-data placeholder start in autumn 2005, which is not an award date. Best reading: the contract was in force by late December 2005 and publicised a month later.

**Ceiling versus obligated.** The $385 million is the contract's maximum value, per KBR, over a term of one base year plus option years. The contract's orders sum to $2,668,546.38 obligated in USAspending, well under one percent of the ceiling. They comprise the readiness order above; a nationwide contingency support order, with the most dollars; and smaller orders, including one titled "MASS MIGRATION PLAN" and one for a nonspecific temporary detention center line. Order dates run from 2007 into 2008, with the option years exercised in modifications through 2010 and period-of-performance extensions entered in 2012. These totals cover this PIID only, not any other DHS or Army vehicle paying KBR, and pre-2008 coverage of this contract is marked "migrated data".

**Vendor entity.** KBR's press release described KBR as "the engineering and construction subsidiary of Halliburton". USAspending today carries the vendor as KBR Services, LLC (parent KBR, Inc.), a normalized current name; which legal entity signed in 2005-2006 is not in the record or the press release. The spin-off from Halliburton is covered in [[2007-04-05--halliburton-spins-off-kbr-accountability-evasion]].

**Competition.** The federal record has the extent of competition as FULL AND OPEN COMPETITION, with three offers received. An earlier version of this entry had only one bidder responding; that is corrected above. KBR's own release uses the word competitively.

**Scope (per KBR's release).** Temporary detention and processing capability to augment ICE Detention and Removal Operations facilities in an emergency, a plan to react to a national emergency, and possibly housing for ICE personnel after a disaster. Type Investigations (March 2006) put it plainly: "KBR is not planning to build anything"; existing structures would be leased, not built. The same article has DHS ordering, during Hurricane Katrina, $7 million of temporary shelter for DHS and ICE officials in New Orleans from KBR under the earlier contract, not this one.

**Congressional response.** Rep. Bennie Thompson (D-MS), ranking member of the House Homeland Security Committee, learned of the contract from press coverage, per 2006 secondary coverage (his statement was not re-pulled for this update). A full-text search of the Congressional Record on govinfo for 2006 turned up floor statements on Halliburton in Iraq-contracting contexts, but none on this ICE contract, and no Senator Lautenberg statement or letter on it. That search was cut short when the shared govinfo DEMO_KEY hit its rate limit, so the Lautenberg question is open, not closed.

## GROWER-ASSERTED, UNVERIFIED

Bradley Grower (@wastedink), replying on Substack under Mark Ramm's detention-pipeline May-inflection post, put forward a standing Department of Defense KBR contract for pre-positioned pop-up detention capacity: a specified bed count, warehousing across a set of federal regions, and a rapid-deploy time standard, in place since 2006. None of those specifics appears in KBR's release, in the USAspending record, or in the Type Investigations article. The USAspending record cuts against the "pre-positioned" part: orders total a small fraction of the ceiling and the readiness order is a planning retainer, not a stockpile purchase. Treat the specifics as leads, not facts. On "ongoing since 2006": this IDIQ's last modifications are in 2012 and de-obligations in 2020. One USAspending search for DHS-awarded contracts to vendors matching "KBR" from 2011 on turned up nothing relevant, but vendor-name matching in that search is fuzzy, so a successor vehicle is not ruled out.

## Why This Event Matters

The 2006 award is an early clean example in this corpus of the contingency-as-standing-capacity pattern later seen in the 2025-2026 detention buildout: a large ceiling, a small retainer, and capacity held latent until an emergency.
