ICE officer **Christian Castro** pleaded **not guilty on September 4, 2026** in federal court in **McAllen, Texas** to **six counts of knowingly and willfully making materially false statements to the FBI** about a shooting during Operation Metro Surge. He was released on a **$75,000 unsecured bond with a GPS ankle monitor**.

The unsealed indictment supplies what the record previously lacked. On **January 14, 2026**, Castro and a partner tried to stop a vehicle driven by a Venezuelan citizen in north Minneapolis, pursued the driver on foot, and tackled him in the front yard of a duplex. **Julio Cesar Sosa-Celis**, 24, who lived there, came out to help during a struggle that lasted roughly **11 seconds**. As the two men ran back inside and began **closing the front door**, Castro stood up and **fired a single shot through the door**, striking Sosa-Celis in the leg.

**What the charges are actually about is the account, not the shot.** Castro is not charged federally with the shooting. He is charged with what he told investigators about it: that he was **on his back** when he fired; that he was attacked by multiple assailants wielding a **broom and snow shovel**; and that the encounter lasted about **three minutes** rather than eleven seconds. The indictment states flatly that **no person struck him with a broom as he described, and no persons wielded the broom and snow shovel as he described.**

Each of those three claims does the same work: it converts a shot fired at a door that was closing into a shot fired by a man pinned and under attack. The eleven-seconds-to-three-minutes stretch is the one that shows the mechanism most plainly — duration is what makes a struggle a siege, and it is the detail a reviewer cannot check without the video.

**Why the charge is the false statement rather than the shooting.** This is the structural point and it cuts both ways. Prosecuting the account is prosecuting the thing the paper record can prove — it requires no jury finding about whether force was reasonable, only that the officer's description was false. It also means the shooting itself remains federally unadjudicated. **Minnesota charged Castro separately with assault and false reporting of a crime**, which is where the use-of-force question actually sits; this corpus records Texas Governor Abbott **refusing Minnesota's extradition request** on August 27. The state that wants to try the shooting cannot reach him; the federal government charged what it could.

Castro has been **suspended without pay by DHS since February**.

Read against the rest of Operation Metro Surge in this timeline — a legal observer killed by an ICE agent on January 7, 1,300 collateral arrests, 578 of 4,000 claimed arrests documented — this is the first criminal accountability to reach an individual officer from that operation. It arrived eight months later, in another state, and charges the report rather than the act.
