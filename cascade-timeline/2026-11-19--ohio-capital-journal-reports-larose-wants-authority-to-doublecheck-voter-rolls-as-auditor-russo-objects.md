The Ohio Capital Journal reported that LaRose wants to keep power over the voting rolls when he becomes auditor. LaRose told the publication that an outside validator to audit the rolls would be useful. Russo, the secretary of state-elect, “didn’t buy LaRose’s story,” according to the Capital Journal.

The Capital Journal reported that Russo alluded to LaRose's decision in March to turn over millions of Ohio voter files to the U.S. Department of Justice. Russo said the plan could be “a backdoor attempt by Auditor-elect LaRose to continue sharing Ohioans’ private data with the federal government.”

The Capital Journal reported that LaRose's office did not respond to its questions, including one asking why, during his eight years as secretary of state, he never proposed that the state auditor doublecheck voting rolls maintained by his office.
