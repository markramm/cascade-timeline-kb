On December 18, 2025, Stateline reported that the Justice Department was offering states a confidential memorandum of understanding (MOU): in exchange for a state's full voter file, the state would remove from its rolls any voters the Justice Department flagged as ineligible within 45 days. The agreements were marked "confidential" by the Justice Department. The Stateline article itself returned HTTP 403 when re-read on 2026-11-16; its headline says the deal would "wipe voters flagged by feds as ineligible".

The MOU, as described in the Brennan Center's later analysis and in the court record, asked states to: provide complete voter registration files including names, addresses, birthdates, driver's license numbers and partial Social Security numbers; accept that the Justice Department would test and assess the lists; "clean" the list by removing voters the Civil Rights Division deemed ineligible within 45 days; and keep the agreement confidential. Colorado, which refused to turn over its full voter file, first made the proposed agreement public (Brennan Center).

A Justice Department official told a federal court in December 2025 that 11 states had expressed willingness to comply on the basis of the MOU; that is interest, not a signature (see Correction). Texas and Alaska later signed, and Tennessee and South Dakota provided their lists without signing (Brennan Center, Feb. 2026; see Correction for dates).

Legal experts identified problems with the agreements. The 45-day removal deadline conflicted with the National Voter Registration Act, which requires longer verification procedures and includes a 90-day quiet period before federal elections during which systematic removals are prohibited. The Brennan Center later found that data security provisions were inadequate, with files potentially shared with contractors lacking binding safeguards.

The confidentiality requirement was itself notable: the agreement asked states to commit, without public notice, to remove voters on the basis of a federal analysis. The files could be cross-referenced with DHS immigration databases.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

Three things in the earlier text are corrected; the file was also renamed (earlier id `2025-12-18--doj-secret-mou-voter-purge-agreements-exposed`).

1. **Who had signed by Dec. 18, 2025.** The earlier text said Texas and Alaska "signed the agreements" as of this entry. The statement that Texas and Alaska signed comes from the Brennan Center's analysis of Feb. 2026, not from the Dec. 18 Stateline article (which could not be re-read; per a search summary of it, the Justice Department did not answer Stateline's question whether any state had signed). The administrative record of *Common Cause v. DOJ*, D.D.C. 1:26-cv-01352, shows: Texas "EXECUTED MOU DOJ-TX 12.9.25" (CRT-0002054, ECF 30 index filed May 19, 2026), so Texas had signed before Dec. 18; Alaska "December 22 2025 Signed Executed Memorandum of Understanding - AK-DOJ" (CRT-0000001, ECF 24-1 and ECF 30), four days after Dec. 18. South Carolina's MOU is dated April 28, 2026 ("April 28, 2026 Executed MOU DOJ-SC State Election Commission", CRT-0000017), a different version that, per Brennan, has the state confirm ineligibility itself. Of the states the earlier text named, signing is established for Texas and Alaska only.
2. **The 11 states.** The "11 states" were named by the Justice Department's Voting Section chief at a Dec. 4, 2025 hearing in the California case as states that had "expressed a willingness to comply" on the basis of the MOU (Alabama, Mississippi, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, South Carolina, South Dakota, Texas, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia; from a search summary of Stateline, not re-read at source). Willingness is not a signature: South Dakota and Tennessee are on that list and, per Brennan, handed over lists without signing.
3. **Wisconsin and "secret" and "purge".** The earlier text said Colorado and Wisconsin released copies. Brennan names Colorado only; Wisconsin's release is not established in the sources read, and Wisconsin is removed from the actors. "Secret" was ours: the sources say "confidential" (a Justice Department designation), so the slug and title now say confidential. "Purge" was ours: the sources describe a 45-day clause to remove voters flagged by the Justice Department ("wipe voters flagged by feds as ineligible", Stateline headline; "clean", MOU text as quoted by the court); the agreements concern handing over voter lists, and the removal is a clause in them. Whether any removal happened under it is a separate question: Alaska told a state court none had been requested (Alaska Beacon, Oct. 21, 2026, via the Alaska race file).
