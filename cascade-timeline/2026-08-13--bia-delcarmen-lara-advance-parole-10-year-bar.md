The Board of Immigration Appeals issued a precedential ruling on August 13, 2026 in Matter of Delcarmen-Lara (29 I&N Dec. 830) overturning Matter of Arrabally (2012) — holding that travel on advance parole now constitutes a "departure" that triggers the 10-year inadmissibility bar for anyone with a year or more of accumulated unlawful presence. For 14 years, DACA and TPS holders routinely used advance parole to travel abroad and later adjust status to lawful permanent residence without exposure to that bar. The ruling applies prospectively only, but closes a pathway to residency that hundreds of thousands of DACA and TPS holders had relied on, and immigration attorneys are calling it one of the most consequential administrative immigration rulings of the decade.

The ruling functions as an administrative-law lever for the same immigration-system-capture pattern the timeline tracks through executive and judicial channels — a technical precedent reversal, issued by a body whose members serve at the Attorney General's pleasure, eliminating a legal pathway without new legislation or public rulemaking. It lands the same week as the Ninth Circuit's TPS-Venezuela ruling ([[2026-01-28--ninth-circuit-affirms-tps-venezuela-unlawful]]) narrowing protected status from a different angle, part of a broader 2026 pattern of administrative and judicial rulings foreclosing established immigration pathways rather than expanding enforcement directly.
