**Date kinds.** Oct 15, 2026 is the date line on the letter itself (c1). The sponsors' press release was republished Oct 16, 2026 01:58 GMT and says the letter went "Today"; the tracker cites an NYT story of Oct 15 (not fetched here; nytimes.com blocked). The requested reporting date is Nov 1, 2026 (c7). The events the letter asks about span Oct 12 to 14, 2026 (c3).

**What the letter is.** A 2-page letter to DoD Inspector General Platte B. Moring from nine senators, five Democratic-caucus and four Republican: Jeanne Shaheen (D-NH), Thom Tillis (R-NC), Dick Durbin (D-IL), Susan Collins (R-ME), Jack Reed (D-RI), Richard Blumenthal (D-CT), John Cornyn (R-TX), Chris Coons (D-DE), Lisa Murkowski (R-AK). Its opening request: "We urgently request an Inspector General investigation into these incidents" (c2).

**What it asks the IG to investigate (six items).**
1. Whether disruptions to intelligence sharing or threat warnings contributed to U.S. diplomatic, military or security personnel being unable to reach shelter during Russian attacks (c8).
2. Whether DoD or other U.S. officials "directed, authorized, approved, or had advance knowledge" of changes to intelligence sharing, warning capabilities, or Starlink/Starshield service (c5).
3. Whether the intelligence disruptions and the Starlink restrictions were connected, and whether deliberate policy, technical failure or contractor action caused them.
4. Whether affected U.S. personnel, the U.S. Embassy in Kyiv and Ukrainian authorities received adequate advance notice.
5. Whether restrictions were directed or implemented by SpaceX, DoD or other U.S. entities, whether consistent with U.S. contracts, and what DoD did.
6. What corrective measures are under way.
It also asks the IG to ensure relevant parties preserve communications, contracts, technical directives and correspondence, including DoD-SpaceX communications.

**What it says it does not know.** The cause of the shelter incidents "remains unclear" (c9). The letter treats the speed figures as reported ("reportedly", c4) and takes no position on who ordered the limit. Reuters (Oct 15) reported anonymous officials saying the order came from inside the White House, with a senior US official denying the president made it; see [Reuters entry](2026-10-15--reuters-starshield-limit-order-came-from-inside-white-house-anonymous-sources). The letter predates or ignores that report and does not mention it.

**Sec. 1244 question (the point this entry was opened for).**
- The letter does not cite Sec. 1244 of PL 119-60, does not say whether any notice was or was not received, and does not mention Congress being notified at all. A text search of the extracted letter for "1244", "notice to Congress", "notif" finds only the item-4 and contract-oversight "advance notification" language, which concerns notice to personnel, the Embassy, Ukraine (c6 context). Absence in a letter to the IG is weak evidence: this document could not be expected to state committee-notice status.
- Committee overlap, from the committees' own member pages (fetched Oct 16): the four committees named in the brief's Sec. 1244 text are SASC, HASC, SSCI, HPSCI. Signers on SASC: Reed (Ranking Member), Shaheen, Blumenthal. Signers on SSCI: Collins, Cornyn. Tillis, Durbin, Coons and Murkowski are on neither page. So five of nine signers sit on a Sec. 1244 notice-recipient committee, and none of them says in this letter whether notice arrived. (Reed's other listing on the SSCI page is a party-leader/ex officio entry, not counted.)
- Implication for the Oct 17 recheck: this letter does not decide between the brief's two paragraph versions. It supplies five named notice-recipient-committee members who have now acted publicly without asserting receipt; that is not a statement that notice was not received.
