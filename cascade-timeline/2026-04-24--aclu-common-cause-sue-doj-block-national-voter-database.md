The ACLU and Common Cause filed suit in federal court (*Common Cause v. U.S. Department of Justice*, No. 1:26-cv-01352-SLS, D.D.C.) in April 2026 to block DOJ's construction of what would be the first centralized national voter database in U.S. history. DOJ had demanded unredacted voter registration data — including partial Social Security numbers and driver's license numbers — from all 50 states and D.C.; multiple states refused, and DOJ sued them in response (documented separately in [[2026-04-06--doj-voter-data-campaign-reaches-31-suits-30-states-dc]]). The plaintiffs argue the database is unconstitutional and designed to enable a national voter-purge and surveillance operation ahead of the 2026 midterms. Reported the same week: a draft emergency executive order, confirmed by Democracy Docket and ABC News, that would declare a national emergency based on debunked 2020 election-fraud claims and ban mail-in ballots nationwide — evidence the voter-database demand was one component of a broader pre-midterm election-control strategy rather than a standalone data request.

This is the origin point of a litigation track the corpus tracks through its later procedural stages: Common Cause moved for partial summary judgment on May 19 ([[2026-05-19--common-cause-partial-summary-judgment-doj-national-voter-database]]), and the ACLU filed a second suit in July after DOJ completed the database, racing an August 5 purge-blackout deadline ([[2026-07-15--aclu-sues-doj-national-voter-database-nvra-blackout-deadline]]). The draft emergency EO reported alongside this filing did not surface again in the corpus's DOJ voter-database coverage as of this entry — its status is an open lead.
