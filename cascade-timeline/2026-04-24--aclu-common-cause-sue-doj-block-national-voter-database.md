The ACLU and Common Cause filed suit in federal court (*Common Cause v. U.S. Department of Justice*, No. 1:26-cv-01352-SLS, D.D.C.) on April 21, 2026 to block DOJ's construction of what would be the first centralized national voter database in U.S. history. DOJ had demanded unredacted voter registration data — including partial Social Security numbers and driver's license numbers — from all 50 states and D.C.; multiple states refused, and DOJ sued them in response (documented separately in [[2026-04-06--doj-voter-data-campaign-reaches-31-suits-30-states-dc]]). The plaintiffs argue the database is unconstitutional and designed to enable a national voter-purge and surveillance operation ahead of the 2026 midterms. Reported the same week: a draft emergency executive order, confirmed by Democracy Docket and ABC News, that would declare a national emergency based on debunked 2020 election-fraud claims and ban mail-in ballots nationwide — evidence the voter-database demand was one component of a broader pre-midterm election-control strategy rather than a standalone data request.

The database the suit targets is now cross-referenced against SAVE, an immigration-verification system with a documented history of false positives, creating substantial risk of mass wrongful purges of eligible voters. The threat is time-critical: the National Voter Registration Act's 90-day blackout on systematic roll purges before a federal election begins August 5, 2026, ahead of the November midterms — while Republicans simultaneously push the Supreme Court to gut that same blackout protection via an Arizona case (*RNC v. Mi Familia Vota*) that, per OPB, is "not expected until next year."

This is the origin point of a litigation track the corpus tracks through its later procedural stages: Common Cause moved for partial summary judgment on May 19 ([[2026-05-19--common-cause-partial-summary-judgment-doj-national-voter-database]]). The draft emergency EO reported alongside this filing did not surface again in the corpus's DOJ voter-database coverage as of this entry — its status is an open lead.
