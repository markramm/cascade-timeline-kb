On August 6, 2026, President Trump signed two executive orders on birthright citizenship and birth tourism. The first, titled "Continuing to Protect the Meaning and Value of American Citizenship," aims to exclude babies born to employees working for foreign governments, such as embassy or consulate staff who are foreign nationals. The second, Ending Birth Tourism, directs the Secretary of State and the Secretary of Homeland Security to prioritize the issue by denying visas to anyone suspected of engaging in the practice.

The Supreme Court had decided *Trump v. Barbara* weeks earlier ([[2026-06-30--scotus-strikes-down-trump-birthright-citizenship-executive-order]]): children born in the United States to parents unlawfully or temporarily present are "subject to the jurisdiction" of the United States and are citizens at birth under the Fourteenth Amendment's Citizenship Clause. The original order was the January 2025 one ([[2025-01-20--birthright-citizenship-order]]); the cert grant is at [[2025-12-05--supreme-court-grants-review-birthright-citizenship]].

Cato Institute analyst David Bier said the executive orders were "blatantly ignoring the Supreme Court's decision." White House staff secretary Will Scharf said the administration is taking "legally validated means" and that "There's absolutely nothing in here that runs afoul of any of the Supreme Court's opinions on the subject." Whether the new orders rest on the jurisdiction reading the Court rejected or on a different legal mechanism is the question a court will decide; the first court ruling on the second order is at [[2026-09-03--boardman-blocks-second-birthright-eo-no-order-can-undo-what-supreme-court-has-done]].
