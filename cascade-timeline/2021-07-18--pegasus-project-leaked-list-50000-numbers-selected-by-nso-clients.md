The Pegasus Project, a collaboration by more than 80 journalists from 17 media organizations in 10 countries coordinated by Forbidden Stories, with technical support from Amnesty International's Security Lab, published reporting built on a leaked list of more than 50,000 phone numbers. Forbidden Stories describes them as numbers selected for surveillance by NSO Group's customers. NSO Group contends that the reporting rested on "wrong assumptions" and a "misleading interpretation of leaked data", and says the list does not show who its clients targeted. A number on the list is a selection, not an infection.

The consortium identified potential NSO clients in 11 countries. Amnesty reports at least 180 journalists in 20 countries who were selected for potential targeting, and at least 25 Mexican journalists selected over a two-year period.

The device results have a base. Amnesty's Security Lab examined data from 67 phones whose numbers were on the list (OCCRP's account): 37 showed traces of Pegasus activity, 23 of them successfully infected and 14 with signs of attempted targeting, and the tests on the other 30 were inconclusive. Forbidden Stories gives the same finding as infection or attempted infection in 85% of cases, or 37 in total.

Amnesty reports that its Security Lab established that Pegasus was successfully installed on the phone of Jamal Khashoggi's fiancée Hatice Cengiz just four days after his murder. NSO Group responded that its technology "was not associated in any way with the heinous murder" of Jamal Khashoggi.

## Correction (2026-11-17)

The earlier version's title and body treated the list as surveillance of its numbers' owners and put 85% against 37 devices. This version states the list as selected numbers and puts each device count with its counter (OCCRP for the 67 examined phones, Forbidden Stories for its own percentage). The earlier Khashoggi-family sentence is now one phone, with NSO's denial. The Washington Post source was dropped because its page was not read for this pass.
