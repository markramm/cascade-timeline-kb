A Campaign Legal Center backgrounder published August 4, 2026 documented that ELLY — "The Elector List" — and Psephos, both rebrands of the EagleAI Network, were being actively pitched to county and state election officials as voter-roll cleanup tools while carrying forward the same error-prone matching methodology that discredited the original product. Per CLC, both tools routinely generate false positives, flagging eligible registered voters as deceased or as residing at nonresidential addresses. The Cherokee County, Georgia elections director found ELLY's voting-history data "consistently incorrect." NBC News reported the same pitches independently; American Oversight obtained records of state officials meeting with the EagleAI founder; and Democracy Docket reported that Georgia's Election Integrity Network demonstrated ELLY to state election board members. In North Carolina, activists tied to the Election Integrity Network — Cleta Mitchell's organization — were seeking authorization to run the tool against state rolls.

The rebrand matters because the underlying product already failed on the record. EagleAI's earlier deployments generated mass voter challenges that election boards threw out, which is the outcome a false-positive-heavy matching engine produces when its output is tested by a body obliged to hear the voter's side. Renaming the software does not change the matching logic, and a new name lets the pitch reach officials who did not follow the first round. That places it in a documented lineage: Georgia's exact-match system purged roughly 560,000 voters ([[2017-07-01--georgia-kemp-purges-560000-voters-exact-match]]), Wisconsin's 2019 purge targeted more than 200,000 ([[2019-12-13--wisconsin-voter-purge-200000-conservative-lawsuit]]), and Virginia's purge reached the Supreme Court on the eve of the 2024 election ([[2024-10-30--virginia-voter-purge-supreme-court]]). Georgia SB 189 was already the statutory channel converting private challenge lists into official action, as the Brennan Center warned ([[2024-07-09--ga-sb189-brennan-analysis]]).

The timing places this inside the federal roll-consolidation campaign that has been running through 2026 and losing in court. DHS ran a SAVE-database purge blitz across Ohio, Texas, and Idaho aimed at the midterms ([[2026-05-04--dhs-save-voter-roll-purge-blitz-ohio-texas-idaho-2026-midterms]]); Judge Sooknanan blocked the SAVE purge database as unlawful ([[2026-06-22--sooknanan-blocks-save-voter-purge-database-unlawful]]); DOJ's own roll suits ran to a 0-23 record by mid-August ([[2026-08-17--minnesota-judge-dismisses-doj-voter-roll-suit-fishing-expedition-0-23]]), including its first appellate loss in the Sixth Circuit ([[2026-06-24--sixth-circuit-doj-first-appellate-loss-voter-rolls-michigan]]) and a dismissal in Maryland by a Trump appointee ([[2026-06-23--doj-voter-roll-maryland-dismissed-0-9-trump-appointee]]). A privately licensed tool sold directly to county and state officials routes around all of that: it needs no federal court to approve it and no statute to authorize it, only an election administrator willing to run the list.
