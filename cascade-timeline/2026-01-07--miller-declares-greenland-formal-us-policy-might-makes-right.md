White House Deputy Chief of Staff Stephen Miller spoke in a CNN interview that CNN's analysis headlined as a new US mission statement of strength, force, and power. On Greenland, Miller told Jake Tapper: "obviously, Greenland should be part of the United States," and said it is "the formal position of the US government." On Venezuela, Miller defended Trump's assertion that the US is 'running Venezuela,' stating 'By definition, that's true' and 'We live in a world that is governed by strength, that is governed by force, that is governed by power.' Republican Senator Thom Tillis (NC) called Miller's comments 'insane,' 'amateurish,' 'absurd,' and urged Trump to fire him, saying Miller 'needs to know what he's talking about or get out of this job.'

Correction (2026-11-09): This entry's title and body presented "might makes right" as Miller's doctrine; that phrase is not Miller's, and the title is revised to his words. He said the world "is governed by strength, that is governed by force, that is governed by power." The "militarily" in "nobody is going to fight the United States militarily" is not in CNN's quote and, with "basis for their territorial claim" and "international niceties," could not be confirmed in the cited sources; all are removed.
