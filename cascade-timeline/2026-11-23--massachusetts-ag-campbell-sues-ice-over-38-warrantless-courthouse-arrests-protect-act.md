Massachusetts Attorney General Andrea Joy Campbell sued ICE on Monday, in Massachusetts v. US Immigr. & Customs Enf't, D. Mass., No. 1:26-cv-15219. The suit targets a May 2025 memo from US Immigration and Customs Enforcement authorizing courthouse arrests, which Massachusetts claims ICE is still carrying out. Through Nov. 20, ICE had made 38 civil arrests in Massachusetts courthouses without the warrants the state's PROTECT Act requires; the Act went into effect on November 3. Campbell said, "ICE's intentional violation of state law has created unnecessary fear, made it more difficult to operate our courts and hold defendants accountable," and argued that Massachusetts retains sovereign authority over its judicial system and its administration of justice. An ICE spokesperson said, "The Supremacy Clause makes it clear that sanctuary politicians do not control federal law enforcement."

The filing follows [[2026-11-20--judge-oliver-enjoins-four-sections-of-connecticut-sb-397-ice-mask-ban-and-inspector-general-power]], in which a federal judge held Connecticut's attempt to regulate ICE agents likely violates the Supremacy Clause; the Massachusetts complaint frames its claim on state control of its courts rather than on regulating federal agents. The complaint cites domestic violence survivors afraid to seek restraining orders as examples of the harm, as the state describes it.
