CNN reported that "it’s generally been assumed that any mass purges of voter rolls had to be completed at least 90 days out from an election". The article's subject is Republican officials and the Justice Department testing that assumption. In Ohio, Republican Secretary of State Frank LaRose is "making similar arguments to defend his list maintenance programs that use SAVE and state DMV data", per CNN.

**Idaho: flags versus referrals.** CNN reported that "an Idaho review of its voter rolls last year using the SAVE system initially found 760 potential noncitizens among its nearly 1.1 million registered voters". Per Idaho Secretary of State Phil McGrane, "after further investigation, only about three dozen were referred to law enforcement". This is the drop between a SAVE flag and a referral. It is not a measured error rate: the flags that were not referred were not shown to be citizens by this reporting.

**Later Texas data (a Texas Secretary of State release published after this entry's date).** The office said that "Last fall, the agency ran the names of the state’s 18 million registered voters through the federal Systematic Alien Verification for Entitlements (SAVE) database — a process that identified 2,724 potential noncitizens on the voter rolls". It said that "578 voters demonstrated citizenship" on additional review, and that "citizenship was proven for 506 because passport information was added to SAVE after the state’s initial run".
