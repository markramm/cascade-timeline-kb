Vice President JD Vance conducted the White House press briefing on September 3, 2026 — the first briefing in **42 days**, and delivered in place of a press secretary, a position left vacant after Karoline Leavitt's departure on August 27. As of mid-September no permanent successor has been named. Scott Jennings was reported as an early favorite and then as unlikely; Alina Habba publicly declined interest; Anna Kelly, the principal deputy press secretary, has been described as the natural internal successor but has not been appointed acting.

The pattern predates the vacancy. During Leavitt's maternity leave — she gave birth on May 1 and returned to the podium July 16 — the White House named **no interim press secretary**, distributing briefing duties among Vance, Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent. The same arrangement has resumed since her exit, now without the leave as an explanation.

Two things are worth recording, and they point in different directions.

The first is the 42-day gap itself. The daily briefing is not a statutory obligation and has been thinned by administrations of both parties, but six weeks without one, while the office sits formally empty, converts an institution of routine public accountability into an occasional event scheduled at the principals' convenience. Nothing was abolished. The function simply stopped being staffed.

The second is who fills it. A vice president at the podium is not a deputy press secretary: he cannot be fired for a bad answer, is not accountable to the press office, and speaks with an authority that makes the exchange less a briefing than an appearance. Cabinet principals absorbing a staff function is the same shape this timeline tracks elsewhere — a role whose value lay in its routine, replaceable occupant being performed instead by people whose position makes the routine impossible.

**Scope note.** A press secretary's resignation is ordinarily personnel churn and not this corpus's subject. Leavitt's own stated reason — that she could not give both the job and two young children what each required — is taken at face value here and is not treated as evidence of anything structural. What is recorded is the institutional consequence: an unstaffed briefing function, a six-week silence, and principals rather than staff at the podium. Importance is set at 6 to reflect that this is a pattern data point rather than a major event.
