Emiliano Andrade-Covarrubias, 56, a Mexican national who had lived in Dallas for decades, was found unresponsive in his cell at the Rio Grande Processing Center in Laredo, Texas, on Saturday, October 17, 2026, and died. The Texas Tribune reports that Rep. Henry Cuellar put the discovery at about 10 a.m. during a security check; a fetched summary of the Tribune's updated version attributes to ICE a discovery time of 9:48 a.m. and a pronouncement of death at 10:10 a.m. (not read in ICE's own words; no ICE release was found as of Oct 20). The Webb County Medical Examiner is performing the autopsy and the cause of death is unknown.

His daughter, Estefani Andrade, 29, a U.S. citizen, told the Tribune that her father had a heart condition, diabetes and high blood pressure, that he had medicine with him when he was detained, and that staff at the center would not "give him his medications." She said he had a cough, told her he was not feeling well, and that medical staff "would just ignore him" even when he reported trouble breathing and tightness in his chest. These are her account as relayed by the Tribune, not findings. DHS, per FOX 4 and the Tribune, says he received proper medical care. Rep. Cuellar told FOX 4 he has audio from people who said Andrade-Covarrubias asked for medicine and did not receive it, and that he could not verify what treatment he actually got.

Status and the arrest are reported differently. DHS says he was arrested at the Laredo port of entry on June 26, 2026 for allegedly not disclosing a 2004 theft arrest when applying for status, and did not say whether he was convicted; the family says he was a lawful permanent resident. He is the second death in a Laredo ICE facility this year after Felix Alcorta-Rodriguez (June 19), and the family told the Tribune they learned of the death from the Webb County Medical Examiner, not from a federal agency.

Not established: cause of death, whether medication was withheld, ICE's own death notice and congressional report. The New York Times story (Oct 19) that prompted this entry was paywalled and not read.
