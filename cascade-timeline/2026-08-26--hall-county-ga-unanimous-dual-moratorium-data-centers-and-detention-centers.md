The Hall County, Georgia Board of Commissioners voted **unanimously** to impose moratoriums on rezoning applications for **data centers, detention centers, and high-density housing** — three uses paused in one action. Staff will assess infrastructure capacity, water use, and cumulative impacts while the county adjusts its development code on industrial permitting.

**The pairing is what makes this entry worth writing.** Every other local moratorium this timeline records is single-purpose: a county pauses data centers, or it pauses detention. Hall County paused both in the same motion, which means its commissioners treated them as the same kind of problem — a large industrial use that arrives through a rezoning, consumes local infrastructure, and is negotiated by an outside party with far more capacity than the county government reviewing it.

That is the connection this corpus argues structurally, and here a county government made it procedurally without needing anyone's framework. The instrument does not care what the building holds. **A rezoning is the chokepoint for both**, and it is the one thing a county controls when it controls almost nothing else about either industry.

Note also what a rezoning moratorium is *not*. It does not reach a facility that needs no rezoning — an already-zoned industrial parcel, or a federal use that claims preemption. This corpus records exactly that limit at work in the detention context, where local zoning has repeatedly failed to reach a federal facility and NEPA has become the operative constraint instead. Hall County's pause governs applications, not outcomes.

Georgia is where the data-center half of this is concentrating: this timeline records **Walker County's** 180-day moratorium with a study committee (August 11) and **Dougherty County's** renewable 180-day freeze (August 31).

**Sourcing note.** The unanimous vote and the scope covering data centers, detention centers, and high-density housing are from The Gainesville Times, corroborated in outline by Yahoo News's earlier report of the commission debating a 180-day data-center moratorium. **The exact vote date is not confirmed** — the capture placed it at "approximately two weeks" before an August reference and this entry carries **August 26 as an approximate date**, which should be pinned to the commission's minutes before the date is cited. **The ordinance text was not read**: the moratorium's duration, whether the three uses carry the same term, and what definition of "detention center" it uses are all unresolved, and that last one decides whether it would reach an ICE contract facility at all.
