The Supreme Court ordered states challenging Trump's mail-voting executive order to file their response to the administration's emergency shadow-docket application by August 3, 2026, with a ruling expected imminently as the November midterms approach. The underlying order — issued March 2026, directing USPS to compile federal voter-eligibility lists and refuse delivery of ballots to voters not on them — was struck down by Judge Talwani in a 23-state challenge and then upheld on appeal by the First Circuit. The administration's emergency application, filed July 27, tests the Purcell principle: the norm against last-minute changes to voting rules close to an election, here inverted to argue the Court should act quickly rather than let it constrain the government.

This is the docket's next procedural step after the shadow-docket filing itself ([[2026-07-28--trump-races-scotus-shadow-docket-mail-ballot-restrictions]]), which followed Judge Talwani's June injunction blocking the EO across 23 states and D.C. ([[2026-06-25--talwani-blocks-trump-mail-voting-eo-23-states-dc]]) and the original 23-state suit against the order ([[2026-04-03--23-states-sue-trump-mail-voting-executive-order]]). If SCOTUS grants the stay, USPS could begin refusing non-listed ballots before the midterms regardless of how the underlying litigation ultimately resolves — the shadow docket functioning as a mechanism to install disputed policy ahead of and independent of merits review, with the composition of the Court itself the determinative variable.
