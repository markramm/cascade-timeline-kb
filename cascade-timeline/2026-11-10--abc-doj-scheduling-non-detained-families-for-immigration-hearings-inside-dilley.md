ABC News reported on November 10, 2026, in what it called a first for the Trump administration, that some immigrant families not in detention are being scheduled for in-person immigration hearings inside the South Texas Family Residential Center in Dilley, Texas. According to advocates and attorneys, the notices began arriving last week. Kristin Etter of the Texas Immigration Law Council said: "We've never heard of a detained docket, like Dilley, a detention center, having non-detained court appearances." A DOJ spokesperson told ABC there is "nothing novel about EOIR maximizing available space options" for hearings. In a notice ABC obtained, the immigration-court agency warns that people could be ordered removed if they fail to appear, if DHS proves the notice was delivered.

EOIR's public judge directory carries no Dilley court entry. This extends the Dilley pattern in [[2026-08-12--dilley-children-detained-100-days-five-times-flores-cap]]: the facility that holds families past the Flores cap is now also a place families outside custody are told to appear.
