On **March 31, 2026**, the Denver City Council voted **7-6** to approve a one-year, **$150,000** contract with **Axon Enterprise, Inc.** to deploy **50** automated license-plate-reader (ALPR) cameras citywide, replacing the city's prior vendor, **Flock Safety** (110 cameras). Council President **Amanda Sandoval** cast the deciding vote to break a 6-6 tie. The contract is Denver Legistar **File #26-0246**, a resolution created March 3, 2026, approved by the Health and Safety committee March 18, cleared for a floor vote by council rule after Councilman **Kevin Flynn** requested a postponement under Council Rule 3.6, and adopted/signed by the Council President the same evening, running through **March 31, 2027**.

## What Happened / Key Facts

- **Vote**: 7-6. Voting no: Stacie Gilmore, Serena Gonzales-Gutierrez, Paul Kashmann, Sarah Parady, Shontel Lewis, Jamie Torres. Sandoval broke the tie in favor.
- Because $150,000 sits well below Denver's $500,000 council-approval threshold, this contract did **not require** a council vote by charter — the administration sent it to council anyway, a step it did not take for the prior Flock extension (see below) or the September 2026 drone contract (see [[2026-09-26--denver-axon-drone-contract-drafted-under-500k-council-threshold]]).
- Councilwoman Sarah Parady objected to the process: "If we continue to authorize these technologies without a contract in place, this contract makes the executive branch and the police department the decision makers."
- ACLU of Colorado opposed the contract, arguing the cameras were approved before installation and before Denver's Surveillance Task Force had reviewed the deployment, calling it a swap of one "dragnet surveillance corporation" for another without new regulatory guardrails.

## Why This Event Matters

Establishes the contrast the September 2026 drone contract is measured against: Denver's administration *can* and *has* sent a sub-threshold Axon contract to council voluntarily when it judged the controversy warranted it. The 7-6 vote also shows council is closely divided on Axon as Flock's replacement, not uniformly supportive.
