A federal judge in Minnesota dismissed the Justice Department's lawsuit seeking the state's unredacted voter rolls on August 17, 2026, dismissing both counts of the complaint, under the Civil Rights Act of 1960 and the Help America Vote Act. The Civil Rights Act count failed because the statewide list "is not a record or paper that comes into Minnesota election officials' possession"; "fishing expedition" is the court's phrase for the government's Help America Vote Act theory, that it may use a civil action to seek discovery first and allege a claim later. The ruling brought DOJ's record in voter-roll litigation to 0-23. The Department has sued 30 states plus the District of Columbia.

The litigation scoreboard understates what the campaign has actually achieved. Twelve or more states have voluntarily surrendered voter data without any court order, which means a national voter database is being assembled outside the process that keeps producing losses — the courts block the coercive path while the cooperative path stays open and unlitigated. This is the pattern already visible in the corpus: DOJ secret voter-file agreements covering 37 million voters (2026-02-17--doj-secret-voter-file-agreements-37-million-voters), the ACLU's finding that 12 states complied while five suits were dismissed (2026-08-06--aclu-doj-voter-database-12-states-complied-5-suits-dismissed), a first appellate loss in Michigan (2026-06-24--sixth-circuit-doj-first-appellate-loss-voter-rolls-michigan), and a Colorado dismissal with prejudice (2026-08-04--doj-voter-roll-colorado-dismissed-with-prejudice-dc-dismissed). Minnesota is also where Bondi's January 24 letter to Gov. Walz put a voter-roll demand, stated as a compliance review under the Civil Rights Act of 1960, in the same letter as a demand for state cooperation with ICE (2026-01-24--bondi-voter-rolls-request-minnesota-ice-enforcement) — the same state where HSI ran Operation Puppet Master against the anti-ICE community. Losing 23 consecutive cases is only a defeat if the lawsuits were the point.

The Justice Department noticed an appeal to the Eighth Circuit on August 18, 2026.
