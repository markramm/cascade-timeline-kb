On October 20, 2026 (date of the email), a Civil Rights Division trial attorney emailed a local election board in Indiana that the division "intends to send election monitors to your jurisdiction on November 3, 2026", copying an assistant U.S. attorney for the Southern District of Indiana. It says "our monitors only observe." DOJ's own message is the primary document; the list below comes from local outlets.

What the local outlets report DOJ told jurisdictions:
- **Pennsylvania:** the Pennsylvania Capital-Star reports DOJ notified the state it plans to observe polling places in four counties: Allegheny, Lehigh, Luzerne and Philadelphia.
- **Georgia:** the Georgia Recorder reports monitors going to six counties: Bibb, Chatham, Cobb, DeKalb, Fulton and Gwinnett.
- **Maine:** Maine Public reports monitors for Lewiston and Portland, and then South Portland, with Secretary of State Shenna Bellows saying three municipalities have been notified.

Dhillon has said the department intends to send out 1,000 election monitors across all 50 states on Nov. 3. The Epoch Times reports the department has declined to publicly release the locations; Democracy Docket notes that in the past DOJ stated publicly where it was deploying monitors. By contrast, DOJ's press release on the Michigan primary named the places it was monitoring: Detroit, Hamtramck, Lansing, and East Lansing, four cities. See also the FOIA case entry: [[2026-10-22--dcd-denies-naacp-tro-dhillon-foia-election-monitors-records-sooknanan-doj-says-no-federal-law-enforcement-monitors-dcd-26-cv-03461]].

## Update (2026-10-29, publication date of the local reports): Clark County, Nevada; Wake, Alamance and Mecklenburg, North Carolina; Lowell and Boston, Massachusetts; Manchester and Nashua, New Hampshire

KOLO reports that the Justice Department notified Nevada's secretary of state and that DOJ observers will be in Clark County on Election Day. NC Newsline reports, citing the state Board of Elections, that monitors will be in Wake, Alamance, and Mecklenburg counties. NBC Boston reports that federal monitors are expected in Lowell and Boston, as well as in Manchester and Nashua, New Hampshire. In Butler County, Pennsylvania, the local paper quotes the elections bureau director: "We are not a county that has been alerted it is going to be monitored." None of these reports says which agencies the monitors come from, and none cites a DOJ release for the locations.

## Update (2026-10-31, publication date of the local report): Sangamon County, Illinois: 19 polling sites

Sangamon County Clerk Don Gray said his office was told that representatives of DOJ's Civil Rights Division "will monitor 19 polling sites in the county," the Illinois Times reported on October 31, 2026. "This is just the first time they've come to us," Clerk Gray said.

## Update (2026-10-27, confirmation date, per The Intercept): DOJ confirms Maine monitors to The Intercept; Bellows on federal-interference planning

The Justice Department on Tuesday confirmed to The Intercept that it intends to send election monitors to Maine and said: "We will have around 1,000 monitors in counties throughout the United States." Maine Secretary of State Shenna Bellows said: "This is the first time ever that we have had to engage in scenario planning to safeguard against potential federal interference in elections." She also said: "We have received complaints about the intrusive filming, photography, and harassment of voters in the City of Lewiston by political operatives." State law enforcement training materials were updated this year on barring armed federal agents from polling sites, The Intercept reports.

## Update (2026-10-30, publication date of the local report): New Haven registrars tell DOJ monitors they cannot enter polling places

The DOJ plans to send election monitors to New Haven, the New Haven Independent reported. The registrars' letter says "We do not permit election monitors to enter any polling places in New Haven."

## Update (2026-10-26, publication date of the local report): Arkansas: Washington County votes to keep DOJ monitors out of polling places

The Arkansas Advocate reports that DOJ will send monitors to Washington and Pulaski counties, and that the Washington County Election Commission voted not to allow them inside polling places, citing a lack of timely notification from the federal government. The Pulaski County Election Commission was to meet Thursday on the same decision; its outcome was not found in the sources read. Pulaski's election coordinator said DOJ officials told her they will send four to six monitors, in plainclothes, stationed 100 feet outside the polling place.

The report says the Justice Department has not disclosed why it chose those counties. A DOJ spokesperson told KATV: "We will be in all 50 states, including Arkansas, doing the routine election monitoring the department has done for decades."

## Update (2026-10-29, vote date (Pulaski County Election Commission meeting)): Arkansas: Pulaski County Election Commission votes 2-1 to keep DOJ monitors out of polling places

The Pulaski County Election Commission voted 2-1 on Thursday to deny the Justice Department's request to put federal monitors inside polling places, the Arkansas Times and Little Rock Public Radio report. Commission Chairman Raymond Jarvis cited the short notice given by the Justice Department; Commissioner Steve Harrelson made the motion, calling it a "State versus Federal" issue. The Arkansas Times reports the vote means federal monitors can still watch Arkansas elections from 100 feet away from polling entrances, like everyone else. DOJ's letter, read at the meeting, described a plan to send four monitors.

## Update (2026-10-29, DOJ reply date (Thursday letter to Colorado)): Colorado: DOJ declines state conditions for entering Adams County voting centers; monitors to stay outside

Colorado Secretary of State Jena Griswold's office detailed conditions for letting four federal observers into Adams County voting centers, and the Justice Department declined them on Thursday, CBS Colorado reports. The DOJ notice says its monitors will remain outside of polling places and not come within 100 feet of the building in accordance with Colorado state law. A DOJ spokesperson wrote that state or local approval is not required for Department monitoring.

## Update (2026-10-26, press-conference date (Jefferson County Clerk)): Kentucky: Jefferson and Fayette county officials say DOJ monitors cannot enter polling locations

Kentucky election officials said Monday they will not let federal election monitors inside polling locations in Jefferson and Fayette counties, Louisville Public Media reports. Jefferson County Clerk David Yates said his office may call local police if monitors persist in trying to enter past a standard 100 foot boundary. A Kentucky State Board of Elections spokesperson wrote that DOJ monitors, along with anyone else not casting a ballot, must remain at least 100 feet from the entrance to any polling site.

## Update (2026-11-03, statement date (Reuters report)): On Election Day, DOJ names the jurisdictions receiving monitors

On November 3, 2026, Reuters reported that the Justice Department said it was deploying 1,000 election monitors and observers to every state, and that a Justice Department statement identified which jurisdictions within the states would receive monitors and observers. Reuters reported that DOJ staff cannot conduct investigations on the spot or otherwise stop anyone from voting, and that the number is roughly triple the number in the last midterm. The list itself was not read for this update.

## Update (2026-11-03, release date): DOJ's own release names the jurisdictions

Today the Justice Department said in a release that it was engaging in its "largest monitoring effort in history" with approximately 1,000 election monitors and observers, "for the first time, in all 50 states," and listed them under the line "We intend to monitor or observe in the following jurisdictions." The release gives no reason for any jurisdiction.
