Sen. Dick Durbin released the Government Accountability Office's report "ICE Should Determine Cost Effectiveness of New Initiatives Before Continuing." According to Durbin's summary of its findings, ICE bought 11 warehouses for about $1.07 billion from January to March 2026, paying at least 50 percent above assessed tax value for nine of them and at least double for seven, and is now planning to sell seven of them. In July and August 2026 it bought four existing detention facilities for about $2.2 billion.

ICE has committed to pay for 46,688 beds at 64 facilities whether or not they are used, and from October 2025 to April 2026 an average of 9,733 of those beds sat empty each day. ICE and the Defense Department obligated about $111 million for detention at Guantánamo Bay. As of June 2026 ICE's spending plan still projected about $38 billion in fiscal 2026 obligations; through July it had obligated $9.1 billion. The staff who validate facility invoices had 55 of 87 positions vacant.

## Update (2026-11-19, report date): GAO's report read at source: life-cycle cost estimates and DHS concurrence

GAO says, in a letter dated November 19, 2026, that ICE did not manage detention facility investments in accordance with DHS acquisition practices, which include the development of life-cycle cost estimates. GAO says it is recommending that ICE develop life-cycle cost estimates, in coordination with DHS, before making new detention investments, and that DHS concurred with these recommendations.

GAO gives its purchase total as about $2.2 billion for four existing detention facilities that ICE bought in July and August 2026.
