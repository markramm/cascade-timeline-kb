Nick Turse reported in The Intercept on August 24, 2026 that a U.S. Central Command public affairs executive assistant, Richard Allee, told him during a phone call: "You'll be put on a list of people to disregard." Turse had been pressing CENTCOM for answers about Iranian attacks on U.S. bases — casualty counts and medical evacuations — and had been left on hold more than two hours; when he asked Allee to confirm that such a list existed, Allee answered "Yes," and declined to say which journalists were on it. Turse also reported that CENTCOM's media operations chief, Maj. Emma Thompson, repeatedly refused to answer his questions, and that an unnamed U.S. official said Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's office had standing orders not to engage with him. The Daily Beast and Editor & Publisher picked the story up the same week.

**CENTCOM denied the list exists.** The command's press office told The Intercept flatly that "CENTCOM does not have a 'blacklist,'" and CENTCOM spokesperson Capt. Tim Hawkins subsequently characterized Turse's account as "a complete misrepresentation of exchanges that occurred," accusing the reporter of repeatedly harassing staff and of "rude behavior." Hawkins additionally said Allee was a contractor rather than a CENTCOM employee, contradicting The Intercept's initial description of his role. The record therefore contains two things and not one: a documented on-record statement by a person answering CENTCOM's public affairs line, and the command's explicit denial that the practice he described is real. Nothing in the reporting independently establishes that a list exists, and this entry does not assert one — the denial is part of the event, not a footnote to it.

What the episode does document is the state of the record about press access at a specific combatant command during an active war, and it lands inside a sequence this timeline has been tracking. Hegseth's Pentagon imposed new press credential rules in October 2025 ([[2025-10-16--pentagon-press-credential-rules-hegseth]]) and replaced much of the resident press corps ([[2025-10-22--pentagon-press-corps-replacement]]); a federal judge struck the press policy as unconstitutional in March 2026 ([[2026-03-20--judge-strikes-pentagon-press-policy-unconstitutional]]). Turse's unanswered questions to CENTCOM stretch back to 2013, with one request from July 2015 still pending — a baseline of non-response that predates this administration and against which the August 2026 exchange has to be read. The wider environment is one the U.S. Press Freedom Tracker counted at 170 assaults on journalists in 2025 ([[2025-12-31--press-freedom-tracker-170-assaults-journalists-2025]]), and in which the country fell from 4th to 11th in the IAPA's press-freedom ranking ([[2026-03-10--press-freedom-us-drops-4th-to-11th-iapa-report]]).
