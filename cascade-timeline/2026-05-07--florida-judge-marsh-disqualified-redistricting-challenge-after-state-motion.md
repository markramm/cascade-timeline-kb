On May 7, 2026, Leon County Circuit Judge J. Lee Marsh granted his own disqualification from a state-court challenge to Florida's newly redrawn congressional map, after Florida Secretary of State Cord Byrd — a defendant in the case — moved to have him removed. The underlying suit, filed by 18 Florida voters and Equal Ground Education Fund and represented by the Elias Law Group, challenges Gov. Ron DeSantis's post-*Callais* congressional map under the Florida Constitution's explicit bans on partisan gerrymandering and non-compactness. Plaintiffs' own disqualification filing cited Florida Rule of Judicial Administration 2.330(e) ("the judge's impartiality might reasonably be questioned"), but reporting attributes the successful push to Byrd and state defendants, not the plaintiffs challenging the map — the direction ordinarily seen in redistricting litigation (a defendant state moving to remove a judge perceived as unfavorable, rather than a plaintiff moving to remove one perceived as biased toward the state). A May 9, 2026 opinion piece in the Tallahassee Democrat characterized the sequence as "one of the most extraordinary procedural twists in recent memory" and raised "judge shopping" concerns; the underlying reporting does not establish Judge Marsh's stated grounds for granting the motion beyond "an issue over its own lawyer" on the state's side (Center Square headline language), and this entry does not resolve that gap.

This is a state-court disqualification under Florida procedure, not a federal 28 U.S.C. § 2284 three-judge-panel recusal — worth distinguishing from the federal-panel composition question surveyed in [[2026-redistricting-panel-composition-and-recusal-survey]]. The case remained assigned to a different Leon County judge as of mid-May 2026 reporting. New Florida maps are part of the same post-*Callais* redistricting wave documented at [[2026-08-15--six-republican-states-schedule-august-special-sessions-post-vra-redistricting]], though Florida is not among the six states named in that entry — this is a seventh state's map fight, decided in state rather than federal court.
