MS NOW reported that on Monday, a senior political official told U.S. attorneys to get on a "wartime footing" for President Trump's final two years or consider leaving, according to two people with knowledge of the meeting. MS NOW named the official as Aakash Singh, a top lieutenant to Attorney General Todd Blanche.

According to the two people, Singh said Democrats' coming control of Congress in 2027 has put the department on a wartime footing. They said he added that prosecutors who do not want to aggressively tackle the president's priority cases should "probably consider resigning."

Leah Foley, the U.S. attorney in Massachusetts, told MS NOW, "I took it as a pep talk." She said Singh urged U.S. attorneys to "reflect as to whether this is still the job that you want."

A DOJ spokesperson said the "wartime" reference "had everything to do with public safety." The spokesperson also said, "He never said anyone would be forced out."

MS NOW reported that some departing officials were "pushed out for failing to deliver what the president or White House were seeking," according to five sources familiar with the departures. Those sources were granted anonymity.

Two people familiar with the departures said the removal of Jason Reding Quiñones, the U.S. attorney for the Southern District of Florida, was due to the president "growing weary of his failure to bring charges" in the "grand conspiracy" case. Another person disputed that and said he fell in trouble over frequent travel away from his office and a lack of instruction to his prosecutors.

MS NOW counted three assistant attorneys general announced as leaving in the days after the midterms, plus four other senior political appointees. Among U.S. attorneys, only Reding Quiñones is described as removed, and the account of why is disputed.
