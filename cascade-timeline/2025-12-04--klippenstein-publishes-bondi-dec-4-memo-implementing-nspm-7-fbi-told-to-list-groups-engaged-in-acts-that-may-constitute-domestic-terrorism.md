The Attorney General's memorandum is dated December 4, 2025 and is subject-lined "Implementing National Security Presidential Memorandum-7: Countering Domestic Terrorism and Organized Political Violence." Ken Klippenstein published it. The full text is recorded at [[2025-12-04--bondi-nspm-7-implementing-memo-primary-text-30-60-day-fbi-deadlines]].

**The document.** Its author line gives an office, not a name. I read the letterhead copy filed in the federal prosecution of the Southern Poverty Law Center, and the hosted text: no classification marking is printed on either (a reading of the page, not a quotation). The memo defines the term by statute: "domestic terrorism is criminal conduct that occurs primarily inside the territory of the United States." It then describes violent groups: "These domestic terrorists use violence or the threat of violence to advance political and social agendas, including opposition to law and immigration enforcement; extreme views in favor of mass migration and open borders;" and goes on to "adherence to radical gender ideology, anti-Americanism, anti-capitalism, or anti-Christianity; support for the overthrow of the United States Government;" and "hostility towards traditional views on family, religion, and morality; and an elevation of violence to achieve policy outcomes, such as political assassinations." That list overlaps with the findings language of NSPM-7, recorded at [[2025-09-25--nspm-7-countering-domestic-terrorism-and-organized-political-violence-signed]], and is not a copy of it. A footnote to the list adds: "No investigation may be opened based solely on activities protected by the First Amendment."

**What it directs.** The FBI "shall compile a list of groups or entities engaged in acts that may constitute domestic terrorism" and give it to the Deputy Attorney General; the initial report is due within 30 days and updates every 30 days after. All federal law enforcement agencies are told, within 14 days, to review their files for Antifa material. The FBI and its task-force partners "shall investigate matters from the past five years" involving potential domestic terrorism. The memo adds that "the FBI shall establish a cash reward system" for information on leadership of domestic terrorist organizations.

**What was reported.** Klippenstein quotes the list directive as "compile a list of groups or entities engaging in acts that may constitute domestic terrorism"; the memo's own wording is "engaged in." He writes that "The target is those expressing" the views the memo lists, and describes NSPM-7 as "a declaration of war on just about anyone who isn’t MAGA." The memo's sentence ties the listed agendas to groups that use violence or the threat of it. Whether a group could be listed for the views alone is the critics' and the reporter's reading; the memo does not say so.

## Correction

Corrected in the recheck of the epistemic gap-catalog entries (date in the corrected field above). The earlier entry (id 2025-12-04--bondi-fbi-domestic-terrorism-memo, kept in previous_ids) called the memo classified, said it defined domestic terrorism to include the listed views, and described a retroactive review of all FBI files over five years. The document, read in the letterhead copy and the hosted text, carries no classification marking, defines the term by statute and lists the views as agendas of violent groups, and orders a look-back by the FBI at domestic-terrorism matters, separate from the all-agency file review limited to Antifa-related holdings. The earlier title is replaced. The earlier entry's links to Democracy Now, Democracy Docket and Arnold & Porter were removed because they were not re-read in this pass.
