Democratic congressional candidate Eliott Rodriguez demanded on Thursday that Miami-Dade County Supervisor of Elections Alina Garcia recuse herself from the recount in Florida's 27th Congressional District, where Republican Rep. María Elvira Salazar leads by 1,116 votes. His campaign cited Miami Herald coverage of Salazar's election-night party, where Garcia was quoted as saying "María Elvira is the winner, with or without a recount" and that a flip was "possible but not probable."

The campaign's lawyers formally demanded that Garcia step aside from all canvassing board proceedings and recount decisions in the race. The campaign also alleges that Salazar's recount attorney is Garcia's son-in-law. "A candidate can declare victory. An election official responsible for the canvass should know better," campaign attorney Jason B. Blank wrote in his letter.

## Update (2026-11-06, complaint filing date): Garcia refuses to recuse; Rodriguez sues in federal court to remove her from the canvassing board

On Friday the head of Miami-Dade's elections office refused to recuse herself from overseeing the review of ballots in the race, and a few hours later Rodriguez filed a lawsuit in federal court asking a judge to force her to step down, WLRN reported. Garcia sits on the canvassing board, and the margin stood at 1,090 after the initial count. The complaint says the representation "creates an objective conflict between the Supervisor's duty to decide CD27 matters impartially and her family connection to the lawyer advocating for one candidate." A spokesperson for Garcia's office said: "She is not recusing herself. There is no conflict." If the board finds the race too close to call, a recount will be held on Saturday, Nov. 7.
