Columbia County, Georgia commissioners voted to approve Google's White Oak and Pumpkin Center data-center complexes — 33 buildings across roughly 3,500 acres near Harlem and Appling — while denying a third proposed site at Byrd Farm. As part of the approval, Google committed to cover power expenses for the approved sites. County leaders then took up a property-tax-relief mechanism under House Bill 439, state legislation written specifically for Columbia County: if voters approve it in the November 2026 referendum, residents could see property-tax credits as early as 2027, ahead of the data centers becoming fully operational. This marks the first case in this timeline's coverage of a hyperscaler-linked property-tax-relief measure being put directly before voters as a referendum, rather than negotiated purely as a county-commission incentive package.

Google's framing casts the tax-relief plan as a path toward eventually eliminating homestead property taxes for residents — a claim that, per the underlying capture-report sourcing, comes from Google and county press materials rather than a certified county fiscal estimate, and should be treated as **disputed/unverified** pending independent review. Likewise unverified is any claim that Google's power-cost commitment fully offsets the transmission and substation costs the buildout will impose on Georgia Power's ratepayer base; no independent ratepayer-impact analysis or utility tariff-docket review is yet in the public record. The referendum mechanism itself joins a broader pattern of hyperscaler-adjacent fiscal capture spreading through state and local fiscal policy — incentive packages and exemptions increasingly designed to buy public consent ahead of election cycles ([[2026-06-11--data-center-fiscal-capture-tax-exemptions-state-budgets-electoral-salience]], [[2026-06-02--shapiro-releases-pennsylvania-data-center-incentive-conditions-2b-exemption]]) — set against a countervailing wave of county-level pushback and moratoriums elsewhere in Georgia and nationally ([[2026-06-22--arizona-illinois-data-center-tax-moratoriums-300-bills-nationally]], [[2026-07-07--bell-county-tx-residents-demand-end-meta-data-center-abatements]], [[2026-07-15--palm-beach-county-rejects-project-tango-data-center]], [[2026-06-10--abbott-directive-puct-ercot-data-center-infrastructure-costs]]). Watch the November vote as a bellwether: if the tax-relief framing succeeds, it becomes a template other counties hosting hyperscaler campuses can replicate; if it fails, it strengthens the moratorium/resistance track this timeline has been tracking county by county.
