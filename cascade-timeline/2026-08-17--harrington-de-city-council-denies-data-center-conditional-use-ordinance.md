## What Happened

On Monday, August 17, 2026, the Harrington, Delaware City Council held the **second reading** of **Ordinance 25-08** — "An Ordinance Amending Chapter 440-96 D – Conditional Uses – of the Code of the City of Harrington to Add Data Centers as a Conditional Use in the Manufacturing Zone" — at a meeting at the Harrington Public Library. Per the city's own agenda packet, the ordinance's operative text would have added a single line, "(j) Data Centers," to the list of conditional uses permitted in the city's Manufacturing (M) zone, alongside existing conditional uses such as grain elevators, meatpacking facilities, recycling centers, and waste-to-energy plants. Under Delaware's Second Reading process this was the ordinance's final-passage vote, not a preliminary step.

City Council **voted unanimously against** the ordinance, per WBOC (same-day report) and Spotlight Delaware (two-days-later report, which put the crowd at roughly 100 people in a meeting Assistant City Manager Amanda Marlow said was the city's largest in at least six years). This is a **denial**, not a tabling, deferral, or withdrawal: the ordinance was brought to a floor vote and failed. Neither outlet reports a specific tally, only "unanimous."

A second, unrelated item was also on the same agenda: **Ordinance 26-04**, a first-reading rezoning of dozens of separate parcels across the city (residential, commercial, industrial reclassifications unconnected to data centers). This is a general zoning-map update, still at first reading (not voted to passage at this meeting), and should not be conflated with Ordinance 25-08 — the agenda packet lists them as separate, sequential items.

### The proposal and the developer

Per Spotlight Delaware, the conditional-use change was sought by developer **Maguire Hayden**, who proposed converting a former cold-storage warehouse site to a data center that would draw approximately 24 megawatts — described in the article as roughly equivalent to the electricity use of 20,000 homes. Delaware Business Times had reported on Harrington's exploratory stage with this same prospect as early as January 2026 ("Harrington considers possible data centers," Jan. 9, 2026), corroborating the developer's name and the timeline predates the August vote by months.

Per Spotlight Delaware, Maguire Hayden's team had also circulated a proposed **community benefits agreement** — a document dated "7.13.26" in its filename — offering the city, population roughly 4,000, contributions totaling **$1 million**. The article frames the offer as unusual because in Delaware, governments more typically pay incentives to attract companies rather than the reverse. The ordinance vote rejected the zoning change despite this offer being on the table; neither outlet reports the offer being formally withdrawn or separately voted on.

### Who spoke, and what they said

- **Vice Mayor Darrin Simpson**, per Spotlight Delaware, said of the council's decision: "It was a council's opinion that it's not something that would be in the best interest of the citizens in the city of Harrington." He also called for the city to consider a data center ban.
- **Barbara Bullock**, a resident and former planning commission member, per WBOC: "Our infrastructure is outdated anyway, and they have not improved anything here."
- **Bill Brode**, a longtime resident, per WBOC, on anticipated facility noise: "From what I've been reading up on, it's going to be a constant hum."
- **Ardyn Overko**, a resident, per Spotlight Delaware: "It's really a shame that you guys would even consider a cute town like this to have a horrible data center."
- The city was represented by attorney **Max Walton**; the developer's team included attorney **John Paradee**, per Spotlight Delaware.

Resident concerns reported by both outlets centered on water and sewer capacity, electrical infrastructure, and noise — the same infrastructure-capacity register found in the parallel Dover and Kent County actions in this corpus, though Harrington's residents framed it in terms of the city's own aging systems specifically, not statewide rate exposure.

## Status and reintroduction

Neither outlet, nor the agenda packet, states whether Ordinance 25-08 can be reintroduced in a future session or whether this denial is treated as final within city process. No public record located in this pass addresses that question either way — this is a **gap**, not a negative finding. Under Delaware municipal ordinance procedure a failed ordinance is not typically subject to any statutory cooling-off period the way some jurisdictions' zoning re-application rules work, but nothing in the sourced record confirms or forecloses a future resubmission by Maguire Hayden or another applicant.

**Instrument status for this entry: DENIED.** The specific conditional-use change proposed under Ordinance 25-08 failed on its second reading and is not adopted or in effect. No data center is currently a permitted or conditional use in Harrington's Manufacturing Zone as a result of this vote.

## Why This Event Matters

Harrington is a genuine counter-example to the corpus's build-anywhere pattern: unlike Kent County's permanent-but-permissive siting ordinance or Dover's still-pending moratorium, Harrington's council was presented with a specific, named developer's specific proposal — plus a direct $1 million financial inducement — and voted it down at first opportunity, on the same infrastructure-capacity and noise concerns raised elsewhere in the state. The corpus's convergent-not-coordinated finding continues to hold: no evidence here of shared drafting or timing with Dover, Kent County, or any other jurisdiction's action, only the same short menu of concerns (water, sewer, power capacity, noise) recurring because the underlying siting proposals are structurally similar, not because anyone coordinated the response.
