**Jason Frazier** — an activist who challenged the registrations of **nearly 10,000 Fulton County voters**, most of whose challenges were thrown out — **quietly started work as an investigator for the Georgia State Election Board on May 14, 2026**, with authority to investigate election processes across the state. The hiring was not publicly announced.

The sequence is the finding. Georgia passed a law in 2024 making it easy for private citizens to challenge another voter's eligibility. Frazier used it at volume: nearly 10,000 challenges in Fulton alone, by his own claim more than 25,000 in total. Among those he challenged were a **homebound cancer patient**, a woman whose **street name had been changed by the city**, and a **sitting member of the Fulton elections board**. Fulton County commissioners **twice denied him a seat** on the county election board, citing concern that he would undermine public confidence in elections.

He now holds an investigative post at the state body that oversees those same elections.

**What the pathway actually is.** This is not an activist who persuaded an institution. It is a mechanism used from the outside, a rejection by the body closest to the evidence of how it was used, and then placement inside a body one level up — where the job is to investigate, which is the formalized version of the challenge. The county that watched the challenges land said no twice. The state hired him without announcement. A private challenge tool becomes public investigative authority, and the record that would have counted against him at the county level did not reach the state's decision.

Note what makes this durable rather than episodic. This corpus records the Georgia State Election Board adopting rules later found illegal (July 2024), the Georgia Supreme Court blocking its hand-count rule (October 2024), and Trump naming board members from the stage at an Atlanta rally (August 2024). Staffing is the step that outlasts all of those: rules get struck down and majorities turn over, but an investigator remains, and the investigations opened are not reversed by a ruling about a rule.

Two named officials went on record. Fulton Commissioner **Dana Barrett**: "He is a proud MAGA operative whose entire goal is to undermine elections by spreading lies and conspiracy theories." SEB Democrat **Sara Tindall Ghazal**: "The majority members of the State Election Board appear determined to double-down on their MAGA credentials."

**Sourcing note.** The May 14 start date, the investigator role, the nearly-10,000 Fulton challenge figure, the twice-denied county seat, and both quotes are carried by the Atlanta Journal-Constitution and Democracy Docket independently. The **"over 25,000 successful challenges" figure is Frazier's own claim** as relayed by the reporting — not a verified count, and the venue is unspecified; it is recorded here as his assertion, not as fact. **Who hired him, through what process, and whether the board voted** are not established in these sources and are the obvious next question — a Georgia Open Records Act request for the hiring file would settle it.
