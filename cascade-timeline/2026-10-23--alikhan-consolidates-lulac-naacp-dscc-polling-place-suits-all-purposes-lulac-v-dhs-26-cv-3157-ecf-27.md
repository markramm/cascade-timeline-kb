On Oct. 23, 2026, Judge Loren L. AliKhan ordered that the NAACP v. DHS and DSCC v. ICE suits "shall be consolidated for all purposes" with LULAC v. DHS; filings go on the LULAC docket and bear each case's number. The order says "The parties all agree that consolidation would be appropriate," and finds the claims turn on "whether Defendants are authorized to deploy law enforcement officers to polling locations."

This is the court's ruling on the question it raised in its Oct. 9 order ([[2026-10-09--alikhan-contemplates-consolidating-lulac-naacp-dscc-polling-place-suits-lulac-amends-to-add-doj-fbi]]) after the parties' joint status reports ([[2026-10-16--all-parties-tell-alikhan-consolidation-appropriate-lulac-naacp-dscc-polling-place-suits-joint-status-report]]). The order sets no schedule and rules on no injunction request.

## Update (2026-10-30, filing date): Corrected joint status report sets out the dispute over expedited discovery

In a corrected joint status report filed 10/30/26, the parties dispute the expedited discovery. The government states it has "complied with this Court's Order" and proposes another report "on November 5, 2026." The filing notes that Election Day is November 3, 2026. The plaintiffs say the defendants "have not complied with the Court's expedited discovery deadlines" and that missing material includes "DHS Weeklies." They ask the court to order a privilege log, review withheld documents in camera, and hold a status conference. These are the parties' positions in a status report, not findings by the court.

## Update (2026-10-30, filing date): DHS calls an August "Voter Fraud Surge" email its "best guess" at the guidance CNN reported

An exhibit to the corrected joint status report filed 10/30/26 shows a government lawyer telling plaintiffs that an August 28 email with the subject line "Voter Fraud Surge - Broadcast Message Request" is DHS's "best guess" at the internal guidance CNN reported. The email itself is not on the docket.

## Update (2026-10-30, filing date): After conferring twice, the parties still dispute production and file for a status conference

In a joint motion dated October 30, 2026 (Document 49), the parties say they conferred "at 1:30 p.m. and 4:45 p.m." Plaintiffs "maintain that Defendants have failed to produce responsive materials." The government says "it has complied with this Court's Expedited Discovery Order" and disputes the need for a conference. These are each side's positions.

## Update (2026-10-30, filing date): DHS says it produced over 1,600 pages under the expedited-discovery order; plaintiffs say the production is incomplete and ask for a status conference

Defendants say they served their responses and a production on October 26 and a second on October 28, and that they produced over 1,600 pages in all. Plaintiffs say the production is incomplete, that DHS Weeklies are absent, and that no privilege log came with the October 26 production. The documents produced were served on plaintiffs and are not on the public docket.

## Update (2026-10-31, minute order date): Hearing set for Nov. 2 unless discovery disputes in the NAACP case are resolved

Judge Loren L. AliKhan ordered the parties in NAACP v. DHS, 26-CV-3263, to continue to meet and confer and file a notice of any updates by 10:00 p.m. on Sunday, Nov. 1. Unless they have fully resolved their discovery disputes by then, she ordered them to appear for a motion hearing at 10:30 a.m. on Monday, Nov. 2, in Courtroom 21. ## Update (2026-11-01, filing date): FBI guidance on foot patrols near polls surfaces in a joint status report; government says no polling-place policy exists

According to the statement quoted in the November 1, 2026 joint status report, personnel were directed "to not engage in foot patrols at or near any polling location where voting is occurring." The government's section says the statement explained "the presence of FBI agents near a polling place in Washington, D.C., on October 30, 2026" and was not a discovery response. On a sworn commitment to obey the armed-presence statute nationwide, plaintiffs write: "As of this filing, Defendants have not provided any such statement or declaration." The government's section says plaintiffs are "apparently dissatisfied with the fact that the policy they challenged in this lawsuit does not exist."

## Update (2026-10-31, email date): The FBI's own account of the D.C. polling place, in the exhibit

An exhibit to the joint status report is a DOJ email relaying the FBI's response, under the subject "Armed Agents at Polls in DC." It says any personnel observed "were part of a roving interagency foot patrol assigned to that neighborhood" under the DC Safe & Beautiful Task Force and were not there "for the purpose of conducting any investigative activity." It says FBI Washington Field Office leadership "did not become aware of the potential presence of armed FBI personnel in the vicinity of an active polling location until the morning of 10/31/2026." It says personnel were told "the only exception to that prohibition is if there is an active shooter at a particular polling location."

## Update (2026-11-03, filing date): DOJ says Walton's ruling "does not immediately impact the present case"

Filed 11/03/26, the joint status report AliKhan ordered: plaintiffs said Walton's decision "does not change the representations that Plaintiffs have provided to the Court." Defendants said it "does not immediately impact the present case" because "there is no pending motion for emergency relief before the Court," and "reserve the right to reassess" if the plaintiffs move for emergency relief.
