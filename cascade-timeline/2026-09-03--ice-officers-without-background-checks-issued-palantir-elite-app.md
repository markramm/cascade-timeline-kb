The Intercept reported on September 3, 2026, citing five ICE officials, that deportation officers who have **not completed their background investigations** continue to work in all jurisdictions — in some cases nearly a year after hiring — and are routinely issued **ELITE**, the Palantir-built mobile application that surfaces addresses, criminal histories, immigration records, court rulings and other sensitive data on both immigrants and, per the reporting, U.S. persons whose records intersect theirs.

The structural problem is the ordering. A background investigation is the mechanism that determines whether a person should hold access to restricted data. Issuing the access first and completing the check later — if at all — does not weaken that mechanism, it inverts it: the vetting becomes a retrospective audit of a decision already made and already acted on. Every query run in the interval is a query by someone the agency has not yet cleared.

Scale is what makes the interval consequential. This timeline records ICE's $1B Palantir blanket purchase agreement (February), the LexisNexis contract piping 82 billion-plus data points into Palantir for bulk facial recognition (August), and the September HSI pre-solicitation for recurring nationwide voter-file acquisition. ELITE is the handheld end of that accumulation — the interface at which an aggregated national dataset meets an individual officer in the field. NPR separately documented in July that ICE gained access to **Medicaid data** through Palantir, which is the kind of record a background check exists to gate.

Note what this sits beside. In the same week, ICE was reported paying ZeroFox to scrub its own officers' personal information from the web. The agency is simultaneously expanding what its unvetted personnel can see about the public and contracting to reduce what the public can see about its personnel.

**Sourcing note.** This rests on a **single tier-1 report** sourced to five unnamed ICE officials; no document has been produced and ICE is not recorded responding. The claim that officers work "in all jurisdictions" and the "nearly a year" figure are those officials' characterizations as relayed by The Intercept, not agency data. The NPR Medicaid story is separate reporting cited here for context, not corroboration of the vetting claim. A FOIA for ICE's ELITE provisioning policy — specifically whether a completed background investigation is a prerequisite — would settle whether this is a policy gap or a policy violation, and that distinction is not established.
