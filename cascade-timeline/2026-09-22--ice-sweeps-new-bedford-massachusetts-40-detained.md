A coordinated ICE enforcement surge hit New Bedford, Dartmouth, and Fall River, Massachusetts, beginning September 22, 2026. Advocates and local officials reported at least 40 people detained in the New Bedford area alone, with at least 31 confirmed ICE arrests across southeastern Massachusetts (the 40 figure comes from advocates via WGBH; the 31 figure is independently confirmed by Boston.com and the Boston Globe). Advocates described agents working in teams and stopping people based on appearance rather than specific warrants at gas stations, street corners, and parking areas. New Bedford Mayor Jon Mitchell said ICE "continues to overreach" and that at least eight of the people detained had no criminal records beyond minor motor vehicle violations. The Boston Globe characterized the sweeps as arriving at a critical time for the agency's broader expansion strategy.

The racial-profiling characterization is the mayor's and advocates' claim, not an independently adjudicated finding — the sourcing preserves that attribution. The sweep is consistent with the pattern the corpus tracks nationally: ICE's July arrest surge approached a 600,000-deportation pace for the fiscal year ([[2026-07-23--ice-nears-600k-deportations-july-arrest-record]]), sustained in part by expanded detention capacity contracts such as the LaSalle Family Foundation deal ([[2026-07-01--ice-10k-arrest-surge-lasalle-family-detention-contract]]). New Bedford's sweep adds a Northeast, sanctuary-adjacent-city data point to that national arrest-volume pattern.
