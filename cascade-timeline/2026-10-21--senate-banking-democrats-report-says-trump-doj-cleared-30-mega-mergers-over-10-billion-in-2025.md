Scott MacFarlane Reports published (this entry's date is the outlet's publication date, not a confirmed release date of the report) an account of a 44-page report from Senate Banking Committee Democrats, issued by Sen. Elizabeth Warren, whom MacFarlane describes as vice-chair of the committee. According to MacFarlane, the report argues the Trump administration "has abandoned even its previous rhetorical commitment to antitrust enforcement." The Democrats say their review found: "In 2025, President Trump's first year back in office, there were 30 corporate mega-mergers valued at over $10 billion—significantly more than in any single year between 2022 and 2024." The report says the Justice Department replaced rigorous scrutiny with "minimal review," that Trump "inserted himself into the transaction while Paramount was still bidding against Netflix," and that corporate profits reached "a record annualized high of $4.42 trillion in the first quarter of 2026." This is a partisan minority report; every characterization here is Senate Banking Committee Democrats' own. Quotations are as printed by MacFarlane. The Justice Department did not respond to a request for comment.

On specific deals, MacFarlane quotes the Democrats saying Antitrust Division staff raised concerns about Capital One-Discover before DOJ allowed the transaction to proceed unchallenged in April 2025, and that the Department of Transportation cleared a United-JetBlue partnership in July 2025. Related entries record the departure of the Antitrust Division's chief, the Paramount Skydance close of WBD, the HPE-Juniper settlement and the House hearing on DOJ antitrust.
