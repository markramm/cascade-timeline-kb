The Associated Press reported on March 31, 2025 that the White House itself, not Justice Department leadership, had carried out the latest firings of career prosecutors. Per AP, from a person familiar with the matter, an assistant U.S. attorney in Los Angeles was fired on the Friday before, in an email from the White House Presidential Personnel Office, without explanation and shortly after a right-wing activist posted about him on social media. That followed the White House's firing the week before of a longtime career prosecutor who had been serving as acting U.S. attorney in Memphis.

**The count.** White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt said in a statement that the White House, in coordination with the Justice Department, has dismissed more than 50 U.S. attorneys and deputies in recent weeks. That is a White House statement quoted by AP, with the group described as U.S. attorneys and deputies. Leavitt's stated rationale: "The American people deserve a judicial branch full of honest arbiters of the law who want to protect democracy, not subvert it".

**Context.** NPR, earlier that month, described the preceding weeks as including "demoting attorneys who worked on cases related to the Jan. 6 attack on the U.S. Capitol, and firing officials who investigated the president himself".

## Correction (2026-11-17)

Earlier text: 50 prosecutors fired on this date, with a justification quoted from the acting attorney general. Now: the 50 is Leavitt's statement about U.S. attorneys and deputies over recent weeks, per AP, and the individual firings are described by post.
