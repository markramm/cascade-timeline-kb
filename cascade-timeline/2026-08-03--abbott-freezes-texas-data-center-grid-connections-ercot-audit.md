Gov. Greg Abbott directed the Public Utility Commission of Texas and ERCOT on August 3, 2026 to audit every data center in the grid interconnection queue before approving new connections, immediately freezing large-load tie-ins statewide. ERCOT suspended its "Batch Zero Large Load" classification notifications that had been scheduled for August 7, halting grid access for active Texas projects from Meta, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, and xAI.

This escalates the ratepayer-cost-shift fight Abbott himself opened two months earlier when he directed PUCT and ERCOT to require data centers to fully fund their own infrastructure costs ([[2026-06-10--abbott-directive-puct-ercot-data-center-infrastructure-costs]]) — that directive set a study process in motion; this freeze stops new grid connections outright while the audit runs. It follows the same demand-side logic Texas set in SB 6's large-load interconnection reform ([[2025-06-20--texas-sb-6-large-load-interconnection-signed]]) and lands amid the broader multistate pattern of grid operators and utility commissions confronting hyperscaler-driven ratepayer cost shifts, from Pennsylvania's PPL settlement ([[2026-03-13--ppl-electric-pa-puc-275m-rate-hike-large-load-data-center-tariff]]) to PJM's record capacity-auction costs ([[2025-11-01--pjm-capacity-auction-record-data-center-40-percent-costs]]). ERCOT is expected to seek a "good cause exception" to its own suspension at a PUCT open meeting August 20 — the next test of whether the freeze holds or becomes another negotiated carve-out.

**Coverage update (2026-08-05):** One day after Abbott's order, Brazos County commissioners tabled a tax-abatement vote for the RELLIS Campus data center — a project already four years behind schedule after its original developer's November 2025 Chapter 11 filing. A vote is now expected August 18, after Abbott's audit disclosure requirements (tax incentives, power, water, cooling) apply.
