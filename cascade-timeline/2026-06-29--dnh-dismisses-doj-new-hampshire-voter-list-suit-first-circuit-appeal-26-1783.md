On June 29, 2026, Judge Joseph N. Laplante of the District of New Hampshire dismissed the Justice Department's suit for New Hampshire's unredacted statewide voter registration list. The court grants New Hampshire's motion to dismiss and denies as moot the United States' motion to compel. The United States noticed its appeal on July 6, 2026, and the appeal is pending as No. 26-1783.

**The ruling.** The order dismisses the Title III (Civil Rights Act of 1960) count on a threshold reading of the statute, and the Help America Vote Act (HAVA) count separately.

- *The list is not a covered record.* Title III reaches records that come into an election officer's possession. The court read that to mean records obtained from outside sources such as voters, and held the list is a continuously maintained, state-generated compilation of data that does not qualify. The court notes the Sixth Circuit reached the same conclusion in the Michigan case (see [[2026-06-24--sixth-circuit-doj-first-appellate-loss-voter-rolls-michigan]]).
- *The demand stated no factual basis.* The court read the statute's separate basis requirement as a factual predicate distinct from purpose, and found DOJ's demand letter gave only its legal authority. It called the letter facially invalid and treated the point as an independent ground for dismissing the Title III claim.
- *HAVA.* The United States has accordingly waived the claim, the court held, by not answering the motions to dismiss it, and the court said it would have dismissed it regardless: HAVA has no disclosure provision, and the complaint's one relevant fact, New Hampshire's 2024 duplicate-removal rate against the national average, was too thin. The court does not address the privacy and preemption arguments.

The order lists nine similar lawsuits dismissed to that date; Minnesota's dismissal came later (see [[2026-08-17--minnesota-judge-dismisses-doj-voter-roll-suit-fishing-expedition-0-23]]), and this suit is among those described in [[2025-12-12--doj-expands-voter-data-lawsuits-to-18-states]]. Four New Hampshire voters intervened as defendants, by endorsed order of the magistrate judge.

**The appeal.** The United States is the appellant. The appellees are the Secretary of State and the State, plus the four intervening voters, whom the State's brief captions as Intervenors - Appellees and who filed a separate brief. On September 16, 2026, the appeals court denied a motion for summary disposition by a subset of the appellees, denied without prejudice to their arguments in briefing. The United States filed its reply brief on November 6, 2026, asking the court to reverse and remand with instructions directing the district court to compel production of the list forthwith. It argues the demand does not violate federal or state privacy laws and that federal law preempts any contrary state privacy law, points the district court did not decide. The reply is a party's position, not a finding.
