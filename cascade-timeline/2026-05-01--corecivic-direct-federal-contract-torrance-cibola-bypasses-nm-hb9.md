## Opening paragraph

Effective May 1, 2026 — nineteen days before New Mexico's Immigrant Safety Act (HB9) took effect — the Department of Homeland Security/ICE signed a sole-source, non-competed single-award indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity (IDIQ) contract directly with CoreCivic, Inc. covering "detention and transportation support services" at both the Torrance County Detention Facility (Estancia, NM) and the Cibola County Correctional Center (Milan, NM). The base IDV, PIID `70CDCR26D00000029`, is valued at $135,421,013.76 (base and all options) over a one-year period of performance (2026-05-01 to 2027-04-30). By removing Torrance and Cibola counties as contracting parties, the deal put both facilities outside the reach of HB9, which bars only *public entities* from ICE detention contracts and has no authority over private companies.

## What Happened / Key Facts

- **Contract vehicle and structure.** The base IDV `70CDCR26D00000029` is a single-award IDIQ, solicitation `70CDCR26R00000014`, competed with a single offer received, awarded under FAR 6.302-1 sole-source authority ("ONLY ONE SOURCE-OTHER"). A first delivery order/task order against it, PIID `70CDCR26FR0000058`, was issued the same day (signed 2026-04-30) specifically for Torrance, funding $14,085,950 for the period 2026-05-01 to 2026-09-30 (subsequent task-order records in the KB show incremental funding actions of $6,326,650 and $9,490,050 against the same PIID, reflecting partial-period fundings/mods rather than three separate contracts).
- **Recipient identity, verified.** CoreCivic, Inc., UEI `HJGMJN1JKL46`, headquartered at 5501 Virginia Way, Brentwood, TN 37027. Same UEI on both the IDV and the task order — no subsidiary or shell entity holds the award.
- **PSC/NAICS (contracting officer's own classification).** Product/Service Code S206 ("Housekeeping-Guard"), under toptier S / midtier S2 ("Housekeeping Services"); NAICS 561612 ("Security Guards and Patrol Services").
- **Awarding structure.** Awarding office: DHS/ICE "Detention Compliance and Removals." Funding office: ICE "Enforcement and Removal Operations El Paso." Place of performance for the Torrance task order: Estancia, Torrance County, NM 87016 (FIPS county code 057; NM state FIPS 35 → combined 35057).
- **Entity-precision chain, confirmed against CoreCivic's own facility page and Santa Fe New Mexican reporting:** CoreCivic, Inc. holds **title** to the Torrance County Detention Facility ("Owned by: CoreCivic since 1990"), **operates** it directly ("Managed by: CoreCivic" — no separate operating subsidiary), and (as of May 1, 2026) holds the **ICE contract** directly. Santa Fe New Mexican reporting confirms the same for Cibola: "CoreCivic owns the land and buildings for the facilities, which makes it easier for ICE to contract directly with the company." This is a single-entity chain — unlike the four-deep title/lessee/contract-holder/landlord-parent structure documented at GEO's Big Horn facility, there is no separate REIT subsidiary standing between CoreCivic and the property.
- **Why the direct contract exists.** Reporting from April 2026 (Santa Fe New Mexican) quotes Torrance County Manager Jordan Barela confirming the county's April 2 one-month IGSA extension was an explicit stopgap "while it waits for the direct contract to be finalized," anticipating the IGSA would "sunset and would not be renewed" once ICE-CoreCivic contracting concluded. Cibola County Manager Kate Fletcher gave a parallel account. The direct contract materialized as anticipated, effective 2026-05-01.
- **The predecessor IGSA.** Prior to the direct contract, ICE held an intergovernmental service agreement (IGSA) with Torrance County, under which ICE paid the county (roughly $2.4 million/month per February 2026 reporting) and the county paid CoreCivic as the private operator — the "county as middleman" structure. That IGSA was the instrument Torrance County's commission extended on 2026-02-04, twelve hours after the NM Senate passed HB9 ([[2026-02-04--torrance-county-nm-corecivic-igsa-extension-12-hours-after-ban]]).

## Why This Event Matters

This is a documented instance of the direct-federal-contracting bypass pattern: a state ban that reaches only public entities is defeated by relocating the ICE relationship from the county (a public entity, reachable by the ban) to the private operator (unreachable by the ban) — while the facility, the population, and the operator all remain unchanged. The timing (contract effective 9 days before Torrance's April 2 stopgap extension would have otherwise lapsed, and 19 days before HB9's May 20 effective date) indicates the bypass was executed to beat the statute's clock, not coincidental timing. Both facilities remained open and operational as of this writing.

## Broader Context

This corrects and extends [[2026-05-20--new-mexico-immigrant-safety-act-hb9-takes-effect-bans-ice-detention-contracts]], which stated "As of reporting, DHS had not filed a preemption challenge to the statute" and treated the mechanism by which Torrance/Cibola stayed open as an open question. The direct-federal contract — not a preemption lawsuit — is the actual bypass mechanism for these two facilities; DOJ's separate federal preemption suit against New Mexico (filed 2026-05-08, per detention-pipeline site sourcing) targeted the statute more broadly and specifically enabled Otero County's continued defiance ([[2026-07-01--otero-county-nm-renews-igsa-defies-state-detention-ban]]), a different mechanism (county defiance vs. county removal) at a third New Mexico facility. All three New Mexico ICE facilities — Otero, Cibola, and Torrance — remained open as of this event, despite HB9 nominally targeting closure of all three.
