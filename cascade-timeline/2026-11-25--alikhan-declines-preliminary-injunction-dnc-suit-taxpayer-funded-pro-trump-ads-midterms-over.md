On November 25, 2026, U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan in Washington, D.C., declined to extend a temporary ban on the federal government's taxpayer-funded ads, saying the Democratic National Committee hadn't shown a likelihood to prevail in the case. The DNC sued in October over ads paid for at least in part by a $20 million Department of Homeland Security contract, and it challenged at least 11 of them. Her earlier temporary restraining order blocked some of the challenged ads through Nov. 25. She wrote, "But the November 2026 midterm elections have come and gone," and "At this juncture and on this record, the court is unpersuaded." The Justice Department had argued there was no threat of harm because the government had already ceased using public funds to air the ads and the midterm elections had passed.

The Associated Press says the ruling comes after another judge in a similar case in New York also declined a long-term ban on the ads. Our timeline holds the earlier New York stay of the $20 million ad purchase at [[2026-10-11--sdny-rochon-stays-cbp-lmd-20m-ad-purchase-order-common-cause-v-dhs]] and the contract at [[2026-09-20--cbp-awards-lmd-agency-20m-national-media-campaign-contract-trump-ads-begin]]. The later New York ruling is not yet recorded.
