summary: A magistrate judge in the Western District of Pennsylvania held federal respondents in civil contempt in their official capacities and set attorneys' fees as the sanction, leaving willfulness undecided.
---

The docket text of the court's order, entered 10/26/2026, says the respondents, including Rife and Blanche, were held in civil contempt in their official capacities. Magistrate Judge Maureen P. Kelly signed the order. The petitioner, a private person, had asked the court to enforce its earlier order granting a writ of habeas corpus after a detention by DHS agents.

The docket text says the sanction is reasonable attorneys' fees and costs incurred by the petitioner and his counsel.

In the order the court wrote that the declarations of the respondents' agents lacked independent knowledge to support the statements they made under oath. The court wrote: "At worst, it is possibly perjury." The court also wrote that it "need not determine today the depth of this disconcerting obfuscation," because compensatory sanctions in civil contempt do not require a finding of willfulness. The question of willfulness is therefore undecided in the order.
