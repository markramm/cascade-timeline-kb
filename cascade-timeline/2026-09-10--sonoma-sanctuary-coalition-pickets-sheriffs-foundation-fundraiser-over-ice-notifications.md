About **three dozen** members of the **Sonoma County Sanctuary Coalition** picketed a fundraiser for the new **Sonoma County Sheriff's Foundation** outside **Balletto Winery** in Santa Rosa on **September 10, 2026**, over Sheriff **Eddie Engram's** policy of responding to ICE jail-release notification requests.

Organizer **Renee Saucedo** put the demand precisely: "**We're here to say, if you're going to form a public safety foundation, it cannot include the policy of turning people over to ICE.**"

**The numbers are the useful part, and they cut in an unexpected direction.** Per **IOLERO**, the county's independent law-enforcement watchdog, as reported in July 2026: the Sheriff's Office received **325 ICE notification requests** in 2026, **responded to 46**, and those responses produced **21 arrests**.

Read that ratio carefully. The office declined roughly **86%** of requests — which is what Engram means when he calls the policy limited, and he is describing it accurately. Cooperation is confined, per the reporting, to people convicted of serious or violent felonies or cases with judicial warrants. But 46 responses produced 21 arrests, so **once the office answers, an arrest follows about 46% of the time.** A notification is not a neutral administrative act; it is the step that makes the arrest possible.

That is the disagreement in one statistic. The sheriff points to the 279 requests he refused. The coalition points to the 21 people taken. Both are looking at the same policy, and neither figure contradicts the other.

**The venue matters too.** The protest targeted a **fundraiser**, not a jail or a courthouse — the Sheriff's Foundation being a separately governed entity that raises private money for equipment and staff assistance outside the department budget. The coalition's argument is that private philanthropy for the office cannot be walled off from the office's policies. Engram's response defended the protest itself: "**It's a really important thing for people to come out and freely express their ideas whether it's something that is more pro-law enforcement or something that is not necessarily in favor of law enforcement.**"

Sonoma County holds **no IGSA**. This is the quieter form of cooperation this corpus tracks — no contract, no beds, no fee, just a notification practice that functions as the handoff. It is also the form that a county's own elected sheriff controls directly, which is why the fight is local and why it resembles the Monroe County, Indiana dispute over what a sheriff must do with a detainer.
