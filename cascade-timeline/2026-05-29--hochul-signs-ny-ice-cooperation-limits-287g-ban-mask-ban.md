New York Gov. Kathy Hochul signed several immigration protection measures into law as part of the state budget on Friday, according to NY State of Politics. The legislation prohibits local jails from holding individuals on behalf of ICE and bans formal agreements between local governments and ICE, known as 287(g) agreements. The measures also prohibit ICE agents from wearing masks and protect sensitive locations from ICE enforcement activity without a judicial warrant. Pace University Law professor Bennett Gershman said states cannot create laws that interfere with how federal agencies like ICE operate because federal law pre-empts state law, and that the law may not hold up in court.

The package combines several kinds of provision: a ban on agreements, a rule on jail holds, a mask rule and sensitive-location protections. Earlier bans on 287(g) agreements: New Mexico's governor signed the Immigrant Safety Act, House Bill 9, on February 5, 2026; its supporters' release says it bans 287(g) agreements and also prohibits state and local governments from entering into agreements to detain individuals for civil immigration violations. Spotlight Delaware reported on July 14, 2025 that Delaware's House Bill 182 barred 287(g) agreements and made Delaware the seventh state to ban the agreements through state policy.
