Mother Jones reported August 2026 that ICE's official death count significantly understates actual in-custody mortality, profiling Prisciliano Trejo Ricano, 29, who was held at Georgia's GEO Group-operated Stewart Detention Center and described being treated "like a number or a placeholder." The piece documents how deaths occurring post-transfer, after release, or in understaffed medical facilities fail to register in ICE's official count.

The methodology gap the piece identifies — deaths counted only at the moment of custody, not post-transfer or post-release — is the same structural gap formalized by the Venturella memo documented at [[2026-06-04--venturella-memo-eliminates-post-release-death-reporting-ice-accountability-erasure]]. Stewart Detention Center recurs as a chronic oversight-failure site in the detention death record; this profile adds a named individual to the pattern of undercounted mortality that lets ICE report a lower official death toll than independent tracking indicates even as 2026 tracks worse than the 2025 record year.
