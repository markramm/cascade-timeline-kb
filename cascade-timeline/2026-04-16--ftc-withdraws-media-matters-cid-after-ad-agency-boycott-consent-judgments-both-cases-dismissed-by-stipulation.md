Sequence only, with date kinds. This entry corrects a framing in circulation: the FTC settled with the advertising agencies, not with Media Matters. No settlement between the FTC and Media Matters appears in the filings read.

- April 15, 2026 (complaint and consent-judgment date): the FTC and eight states filed a complaint in N.D. Tex. against three advertising-agency holding companies and the court entered stipulated final orders the same day. In the Dentsu order, Dentsu "neither admits nor denies any of the allegations in the Complaint." The order bars Dentsu from agreements that "Directs Advertisers’ advertising spend based on Covered Bases," and defines the term to start with "Political or ideological viewpoints."
- The complaint names Media Matters among the groups it says pressed the ad industry, describing it as an advocacy group. It is not a defendant, and the complaint is the FTC's allegation, not a finding.
- April 16, 2026 (withdrawal date, per the FTC's own motion and attached letter): FTC staff withdrew the civil investigative demand issued to Media Matters. The letter says the settlement "resolved the issues related to Media Matters that gave rise to the CID" and that "the Commission no longer requires compliance with the CID."
- The FTC's motion called the settlement a "landmark settlement" and said other demands in the same investigation were withdrawn for the same reason, citing a filing in the NewsGuard case "noting withdrawal of CID issued to NewsGuard."
- Background dates from the same motion: the demand was issued May 20, 2025 (issue date) and the district court entered a preliminary injunction against it on August 15, 2025 (order date). The earlier stage is in [[2025-05-22--ftc-probes-media-matters-over-musks-x-boycott-clai]].
- The FTC's motion argued that "the injunction remains in place" after withdrawal. The FTC asked to dismiss its own appeal rather than ask the court to reverse; Media Matters initially opposed the motion.
- May 4, 2026 (clerk order date): the D.C. Circuit's per curiam order noted that "this case is dismissed" on the parties' joint stipulation, and no mandate issued. The stipulation recites that the parties "jointly stipulate to the dismissal of the captioned appeal."
- The district-court case ended by stipulation the same day: the plaintiff's complaint "voluntarily dismissed pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 41(a)(1)(A)(ii)," each side bearing its own fees and costs.

What the record does not show: any terms of an agreement between the FTC and Media Matters (none is on either docket), whether the withdrawn demands will be reissued, or why Media Matters moved from opposing the FTC's motion to joining a dismissal stipulation. The mechanism worth keeping: an investigation that a court had enjoined as likely retaliatory ended not by ruling but by the agency settling a different case against different parties, which mooted the enjoined demand before the appellate court could decide it.
