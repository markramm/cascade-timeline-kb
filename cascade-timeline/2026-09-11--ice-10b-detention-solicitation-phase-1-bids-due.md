Phase-1 proposals for ICE's turn-key detention construction solicitation — **70CDCR26R00000026**, worth up to **$10 billion** — were due at 5:00 p.m. ET on September 11, 2026. Phase-2 proposals are due September 18, and award is expected before September 30, the end of the fiscal year. The structure is a hybrid firm-fixed-price and labor-hour IDIQ with a **120-month ordering period**, meaning whoever wins holds the vehicle for ten years.

The two-phase design is worth understanding because it explains the compressed calendar. Phase 1 is scored **pass/fail per proposed facility**; only bidders passing every Phase-1 criterion for a given site are invited to submit a Phase 2 proposal for that site. Seven days separate the two deadlines, and twelve days separate Phase 2 from the expected award. A ten-year, ten-billion-dollar vehicle is being competed on a three-week clock that ends when the fiscal year does.

The solicitation prices 208 cells across eight named existing Service Processing Centers — "Secure Housing Units" at roughly $24–42 million each, about $247 million combined — and reserves **14 additional locations** where work can later be ordered without a new competition, including Guantánamo Bay, Honolulu, Broadview (IL), and Batavia (NY). This is the contracting arm of the Detention Reengineering Initiative documented here since June: the shift off county-leased jail beds toward federally owned, warehouse-converted processing hubs.

Two features reported in the solicitation deserve tracking rather than assertion. Project Saltbox reports it contemplates **mobile detention sites and courtrooms inside the facilities**, and contains **a clause asserting that state law does not apply**. If accurate, the second is the more consequential: it would state on the contracting instrument what federal-facility preemption has so far accomplished case by case — and it bears directly on the counties currently weighing whether to keep their names on IGSA agreements, where the operative question is precisely what leverage local law retains once the federal government owns the building.

**Sourcing note.** The solicitation number, the $10B ceiling, the Phase-1/Phase-2 deadlines, the pass/fail evaluation and the 120-month ordering period are confirmed across procurement listings and TIME's reporting on the underlying documents. The mobile-courtroom and state-law-preemption clauses are reported by Project Saltbox (tier 2) and have **not** been read against the solicitation text directly; a SAM.gov pull of 70CDCR26R00000026 would settle both and is the obvious next step. No award had been announced as of this entry.
