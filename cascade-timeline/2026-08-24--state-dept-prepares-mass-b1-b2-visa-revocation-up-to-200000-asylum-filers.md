The State Department is preparing to revoke B1/B2 tourist and business visas of up to 200,000 people, according to State Department documents obtained by the Associated Press and reported August 24. The action targets B1/B2 visas issued between 2016 and 2026 whose holders subsequently filed asylum claims — what officials describe as the largest single mass visa revocation in U.S. history, though that superlative comes from officials quoted in the reporting rather than an independently established record. Deputy Secretary of State Christopher Landau framed the move as targeting "bogus asylum claims"; State Department spokesman Tommy Pigott said the agency is "coordinating with DHS to identify and revoke" the visas. Revocation would not automatically trigger deportation — holders with pending asylum cases would be recategorized but would lose business/tourism status. As of the AP's reporting, the formal announcement had not yet been made; State told reporters it was expected "in coming weeks," and no specific legal authority for the mass action had been cited in the documents or communications made available to the press.

The scale reframes a program already running for most of a year: [[2025-12-08--state-department-85000-visa-revocations]] recorded 85,000 visa revocations across all categories since January 2025 — more than double the 2024 rate, including over 8,000 student visas — with roughly half attributed to safety-threat findings (DUIs, assaults, theft) and the rest to expirations and terrorism-support allegations. A contemplated action of up to 200,000, aimed specifically at the asylum-filing population, would be more than double that already-record figure, and unlike the earlier wave it targets people for a status they voluntarily disclosed to the government — filing for asylum — rather than for a flagged offense. That specificity is the structural escalation: cross-referencing a decade of visa issuance records against asylum filings to build a revocation list turns an immigration-benefit application into the basis for punitive action against the underlying visa.
