Loudoun Now reported on **November 17, 2026** that the Board of Supervisors approved a 12-month pause on processing legislative data center applications. The motion by Supervisor Briskman passed 7-1-1, with Supervisor Umstattd opposed and Supervisor Kershner abstaining.

FOX 5 DC, also on November 17, reported a 10-month pause. The outlets differ on length. The adopted resolution has not been read.

Loudoun Now reported that the resolution says the board "will not act on any data center or substation application" until the county finishes its data center standards and location rewrite. It reported that substations deemed necessary for "grid reliability" are exempted. It also reported that staff will keep accepting applications, which will stay in a backlog.

Umstattd said the board was poised to "deprive an industry and different entities within that industry of their constitutional rights." County Attorney Leo Rogers told the board, "There's a real threat of litigation here." Briskman, the motion's sponsor, said residents "they really have been screaming about the data centers impacting their quality of life."

The Board voted, after the earlier deferral. The pause's length and effect on applications since September need the adopted resolution.

Not read: the signed resolution and the county's own release.
