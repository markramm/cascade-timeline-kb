The government's supplemental brief (Doc. 263, filed 09/10/26) describes CUC Operation Keyhole. In its words, after the August 27, 2026 discovery hearing "it became clear how important" Keyhole is to the defendants' motion for discovery. The government moved for leave on September 1, 2026; Magistrate Judge David Schultz granted it on September 2, 2026 and set a deadline of September 11, 2026.

**What the government says Keyhole is.** A certified undercover operation administered exclusively by the Special Agent in Charge of HSI St. Paul, whose area of responsibility the brief gives as Minnesota, North Dakota and South Dakota, and historically Iowa and Nebraska. It was first certified on December 22, 2008, by the then Director of the ICE Office of Investigations, following a review and recommendation by the Undercover Review Committee. The brief says HSI's statutory authority to establish, maintain, and operate certified undercover operations derives from 8 U.S.C. § 1363a and 19 U.S.C. § 2081.

The brief's account of its function is financial. HSI undercover investigations "often bear higher costs than other investigative activities", and the brief says a certified undercover operation lets an SAC office lessen the financial burden by sharing undercover assets between investigations. The brief's own section heading says Keyhole "functioned as a funding source, not an investigative tool" into the defendants' conduct. It says inclusion of Puppet Master and Whipple Shield "enabled the use of CUC Keyhole funds to provide investigative support", such as funding the deployment of personnel and the acquisition of equipment.

**The figures the brief gives:**

- 77 separate criminal investigations have been approved for undercover activity under CUC Keyhole by a St. Paul SAC.
- The brief lists the areas as "including but not limited to" narcotics trafficking, child sexual abuse, financial investigations and counter-proliferation; it gives no division of the 77 by area and does not say what Keyhole was certified for.
- Keyhole was last reviewed by the Undercover Review Committee in June 2026 and recertified by the HSI Executive Associate Director on June 29, 2026; the next reauthorization review is due by December 31, 2026.
- Operation Puppet Master was approved for inclusion in January 2026 and Project Whipple Shield in March 2026.
- As of the June 2026 reauthorization, six active criminal investigations were using Keyhole, one of them Project Whipple Shield. Puppet Master "ceased undercover activity under CUC Keyhole" by then.

The brief says the remaining active investigations are unrelated to the prosecution and the defendants, and that disclosure would endanger informants and undercover agents in those matters. On the law it argues the defendants have not shown materiality under Rule 16, calls the request a "fishing outing into an unrelated operation", and proposes in-camera review if the court finds some disclosure warranted.

**How to read it.** This is the government's account in a brief opposing discovery, not a produced record. Taken as written, the brief describes Keyhole as an accounting-and-assets vehicle shared across investigations, which is a narrower thing than the sentence in this corpus's January entry on Puppet Master suggested (see [[2026-01-28--puppet-master-accepted-under-cuc-op-keyhole]]); our reading is that the earlier entry's refusal to overclaim holds. What the brief does not settle is why a vehicle it dates to 2008 took on Puppet Master and Whipple Shield in 2026, or what the funds paid for; the defence has asked for receipts (see the update below).

**The defence side of the September exchange.** Sant opposed the government's motion for leave. His opposition (Doc. 258) quotes an email from AUSA Tory Sailer to defense counsel Jill Brisbois on September 1, 2026, in which Sailer wrote that he "looked into this further yesterday" and that the operational purpose of Keyhole was not as characterized at the hearing. Judge Schultz's order records that Sant asked to respond in writing and "asks the Court to schedule an evidentiary hearing on the relevant scope and purpose" of Keyhole; the order says Sant must respond within 7 days of the date the supplemental briefing is filed and that the court will rule on the evidentiary-hearing request after the briefing is filed.

**Ann Arbor and Chicago.** Doc. 263 names only Puppet Master and Whipple Shield as investigations included in Keyhole and does not mention collateral cases or the Ann Arbor and Chicago operations. Whether either drew on Keyhole funds is not established here. See [[2026-04-08--hsi-undercover-operation-zion-lutheran-church-ann-arbor]] and [[2026-04-02--hsi-chicago-undercover-operation-breaking-the-ice-tour-co-prosperity-whipple-shield-collateral]].
