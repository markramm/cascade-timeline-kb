USASpending's record for the parent award shows an indefinite delivery / indefinite quantity contract to The GEO Group, Inc. for detention services at the Big Horn Detention Facility in Hudson, Colorado, signed on this entry's date. CBS Colorado called it a five-year agreement. The record's solicitation_procedures_description is ONLY ONE SOURCE and its extent_competed_description is NOT COMPETED. Its product_or_service_code is S206 (HOUSEKEEPING- GUARD) and its naics is 561612. Its base_and_all_options is 528678643.44, a ceiling; its total_obligation field is zero.

The first delivery order under it has a base_and_all_options of 106502322.6 and, in the record as last modified in November, a total_obligation of 58653711.99. Its description says it "ESTABLISHES THE BASE PERIOD CLINS AND PROVIDES FUNDING FOR THE TRANSITION PERIOD." Obligated and ceiling are different numbers: the ceiling is what ICE could spend across the full term, and the obligation is what it had committed as of that record.

CBS Colorado reported that the new center is intended for 1,200 more people than GEO's Aurora facility, which can house over 1,500 people. The facility sat empty since 2014, per CBS, and the complex is owned by Highlands REIT.

A Highlands subsidiary, IVT PPD Hudson Associates L.L.C., signed an 88-month lease with GEO Secure Services LLC, per AltsWire's report of Highlands' filing; the lease begins Aug. 1. Base rent starts at $250,000 a month for the first four months, then rises to $958,333.33 a month, and rent then increases 3% annually. A contingency drops rent to $250,000 a year if a separate agreement between GEO and an unrelated third party is terminated; neither the filing nor AltsWire names the agreement. GEO said the facility will generate $85 million in annual revenue in its first full year.

The Colorado Sun reported, citing documents obtained by the ACLU of Colorado, that the nearest fire station is two miles away and the nearest hospital is 12 miles away.
