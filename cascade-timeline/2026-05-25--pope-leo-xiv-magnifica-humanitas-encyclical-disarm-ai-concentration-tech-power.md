On May 25, 2026, Pope Leo XIV presented his first encyclical (its dateline is earlier than the presentation), **‘Magnifica Humanitas: On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence’**. The encyclical's own word for its demand on AI is in §110: "To disarm does not mean rejecting technology, but preventing it from dominating humanity."

**What the Pope said at the presentation (Vatican News, Linda Bordoni, May 25).** Speaking in the Synod Hall, Leo said: "In a similar sense, artificial intelligence now demands to be 'disarmed,' freed from logics that turn it into an instrument of domination, exclusion or death." Vatican News's own lead sentence summarizes this as calling for AI to be "disarmed" from logics of domination, exclusion and war. That lead is the reporter's wording. Neither the lead nor the Pope's sentence is in the encyclical text, which contains no passage with the words "logics of domination".

**Verified verbatim (primary text, vatican.va):**
- **Data colonialism (§178):** "Even today, colonialism assumes new forms. It no longer dominates only bodies, but appropriates data, transforming personal lives into exploitable information." And: "Otherwise, the digital age will not be post-colonial, but colonial in another form."
- **The hidden bodies (§173):** data labeling, model training and content moderation, "often involving disturbing material"; children and adolescents "crushing the materials from which rare earth elements are extracted." "The bodies of these people are scarred, injured and worn down so that computational flow may continue uninterruptedly."
- **Power not with States (§95):** "control over platforms, infrastructure, data and computing power does not rest with States, but with major economic and technological actors." Of such power: "When such power is concentrated in the hands of a few, it tends to become opaque and evade public oversight."
- **A handful of actors (§72):** "we cannot allow a handful of actors to dictate these processes on their own."
- **Algorithmic exclusion (§80):** "Justice demands that we prevent the emergence of new forms of exclusion and deprivation of freedoms: individuals and peoples hindered or denied access to basic technologies, communities exposed to invasive surveillance and social groups penalized by opaque algorithms that perpetuate prejudice and discrimination."
- **Disarm (§110):** "To disarm does not mean rejecting technology, but preventing it from dominating humanity."

**What the press said (not the encyclical's words).** Catholic News Service (Josephine Peterson) described it as "the 82-page encyclical" that boosts the Church's voice on "the concentration of technological power among a handful of corporations." Page counts and that phrase are CNS's; the text says "a handful of actors" (§72) and "major economic and technological actors" (§95), and it names no company. CNN reported that Leo "is the first pontiff to personally present an encyclical letter to the world at the Vatican."

**Discipline.** The encyclical mentions migrants (§16, §78, §81) but does not mention detention centers; the detention-datacenter pairing in RAMM coverage is Mark's structural framing, not a papal attribution.

## Correction (2026-11-17)

An earlier version of this entry gave the encyclical credit for words that are not in its text, and its title repeated one of them. Corrected against the vatican.va English text:
- The summary said the encyclical called for AI to be disarmed "from logics of domination, exclusion, and war." That wording is Vatican News's summary of the Pope's May 25 remarks, and the Pope's own sentence ends "domination, exclusion or death." Neither is in the encyclical.
- The body gave `the concentration of technological power among a handful of corporations` as the encyclical's phrase. It is Catholic News Service's description. The text's nearest wording is §72 (a handful of actors) and §95. The title now says technological power concentrated in the hands of a few.
- The body gave `deny access to healthcare, employment or security based on unjust and prejudiced data` as an encyclical quotation. It is not in the text, and none of the readable outlets (NPR, CNN, National Catholic Reporter, Catholic News Service) carries it. It is cut. The text's nearest passage is §80, quoted above.
- `82-page` is now attributed to Catholic News Service; the vatican.va text states no page count.
- `very troubling voices` was set in quotation marks as a report of the encyclical. It appears in none of the sources read, and is cut.
