# Ukraine Presidential Decree 465/2025: 60 entities and 73 individuals sanctioned; Bitfury and Vavilov are not on it

**Correction (2026-10-20).** This entry was first published (June 2026) with the title "Bitfury Group and Vavilov Sanctioned Among 60 Crypto Entities for Alleged $3 Billion Russia Facilitation," and its Gould section called Bitfury "a sanctioned entity." Both were wrong. The decree's two annexes, read in full on 2026-10-20, name neither Bitfury Group nor Valery Vavilov. The claim traced to two low-tier outlets (AssayNews and Trinity Bugle) citing unnamed sources, repeated by crypto-news sites.

## What happened

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy signed Decree No. 465/2025 on July 6, 2025 (signing date), putting into force a National Security and Defense Council decision of the same date that applies sanctions to 60 legal entities and 73 individuals. The decision states that it supports proposals submitted by the National Bank of Ukraine. Ukraine's announcement said the 73 individuals are Russian citizens.

The 60 entities were described by Ukraine's sanctions commissioner as crypto miners, digital-asset system operators, crypto exchange operators and firms in Russia's payment infrastructure, including five registered outside Russia: Token Trust Holdings Limited (Cyprus), EXMO RBC Limited (Kazakhstan), AWX Solutions (UAE), Crypto Explorer DMCC (UAE) and Bitpapa IC FZC (UAE).

## The "billion" figure

In his July 6, 2025 address Zelenskyy said that through "one single company, now included in the sanctions list," Russians had moved "several billion dollars" since the start of that year. He did not name the company. A "$3 billion" figure attached to Bitfury appears only in the low-tier outlets above, attributed to unnamed sources, and is not supported by any primary document.

## Bitfury, Vavilov and Jonathan Gould

Jonathan Gould was Bitfury's chief legal officer from February 2022 and was confirmed as Comptroller of the Currency on July 15, 2025. No primary record shows a Ukrainian sanction on Bitfury or Vavilov, and this entry no longer draws any connection between the decree and Gould. Bitfury is also absent from the Ukrainian sanctions register as indexed by OpenSanctions, consistent with its absence from the decree.

## Open

Whether any later, separate Ukrainian act names Bitfury or Vavilov has not been checked; it would need its own primary document. UK or EU follow-on designations: none found.
