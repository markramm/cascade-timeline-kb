In *D.N.N. v. Liggins*, Judge Julie R. Rubin ordered on the 6th day of March 2026 that the Baltimore Hold Rooms be run under a preliminary injunction, and certified a class of "All persons who are now, or will be, detained at the Baltimore Hold Rooms." The order bars detaining anyone there with less than 31 square feet of personal space, and requires a basic medical screening within 12 hours of entry, prescription medication access within 24 hours following intake, and written notice of rights in English and Spanish within one hour following arrival. Defendants had to adopt written compliance procedures within 60 days of entry of the order, and "shall not retaliate" against a detainee for complaining. Amica Center for Immigrant Rights, the National Immigration Project, and Crowell & Moring LLP were appointed class counsel. The named plaintiffs proceed by initials.

This order is the text later enforcement fights turn on: the screening and notice-of-rights provisions are what the government asked the court to revisit in [[2026-11-13--rubin-denies-ice-partial-reconsideration-baltimore-hold-room-enforcement-order]]. It is one of several conditions injunctions against ICE sites, alongside the Adelanto order in [[2026-07-16--sykes-enjoins-ice-adelanto-conditions-two-independent-monitors]].
