Senators Ron Wyden (D-OR) and Mike Lee (R-UT), with Representatives Warren Davidson (R-OH) and Zoe Lofgren (D-CA), introduced the Government Surveillance Reform Act on March 12, 2026. The sponsors' release says Wyden, Lee, Davidson and Lofgren "introduced an updated version of the Government Surveillance Reform Act". The bill text records that Mr. Wyden, for himself, Mr. Lee, Ms. Warren and Ms. Lummis, introduced the bill, S. 4082, on March 12, 2026 and that it was referred to the Committee on the Judiciary. The release says it "is cosponsored by Sens. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., and Cynthia Lummis, R-Wyo., and Reps. Sara Jacobs, D-Calif., and Pramila Jayapal, D-Wash."

**What the data-broker section does.** The sponsors' release says: "The bill bans the federal government from buying Americans’ data from data brokers without a warrant." The text of the section, Title II, Section 201 of the bill as introduced, is a prohibition. It provides that "a Federal law enforcement agency may not obtain in exchange for anything of value covered personal data" from a covered organization, and lists exceptions in the clauses that follow. The word warrant does not appear in Section 201. A separate part of the bill, in the release's words, "requires federal law enforcement to get a warrant to surveil Americans’ location information". The release's wording on purchases and the text of the section are not the same, and this entry follows the text.

**Whether a purchase is a search is open.** In Carpenter the Court held that "accessing seven days of CSLI constitutes a Fourth Amendment search" and wrote "Nor do we address other business records that might incidentally reveal location information." See [[2018-06-22--scotus-carpenter-v-united-states-cell-site-records-are-a-fourth-amendment-search]] and [[fourth-amendment-and-location-data-law]]. No court order is required for a purchase today; the bill would prohibit it by statute.

Wyden said: "Advances in technology, from AI to the explosion of Americans’ data available for purchase, have far outpaced the laws protecting Americans’ privacy and civil liberties". Lee said: "It is imperative that Congress enact real reforms to protect our civil liberties, including warrant requirements and statutory penalties for privacy violations". The release says the bill is "endorsed by privacy and civil liberty advocates across the political spectrum".

The purchases the bill addresses are documented in [[2026-03-18--patel-fbi-confirms-warrantless-purchase-commercial-data]], [[2026-03-25--npr-documents-data-broker-loophole-purchase-instead-of-warrant-fisa-702]], [[2026-03-03--72-lawmakers-demand-dhs-oig-probe-warrantless-location-purchases]] and [[2026-06-08--ice-awards-thundercat-9-9m-itin-data-subscription-after-courts-blocked-irs-channel]]; related events are [[2026-06-29--scotus-chatrie-location-data-fourth-amendment-geofence-warrant]] and [[2026-06-12--fisa-702-lapses-first-time-since-2008-pulte-dni-house-vote-fails]]. This entry records no markup, floor vote or committee action on the bill.

## Correction (2026-11-17, second pass)

See the corrections field above. The title and filename said the bill requires a warrant before agencies buy data from brokers. Section 201 as introduced prohibits a federal law enforcement agency from obtaining covered personal data in exchange for anything of value, with listed exceptions, and does not use the word warrant; the sponsors' release uses the warrant wording, and is now quoted as theirs. The title and filename now say what the section does and the old id is in previous_ids. Lines on the timing of other events and on a ledger's date and bill number were removed with the sourcing note, because the bill number and date are in the bill text and the dates of the other events are in the linked entries.

## Update (2026-03-12, bill introduction date): The bill text: S. 4082 carries the Fourth Amendment Is Not For Sale Act as Title II

The Senate bill, S. 4082, was introduced on March 12, 2026 and referred to the Committee on the Judiciary. Its Title II is titled the Fourth Amendment Is Not For Sale Act, and Section 201 is titled "Prohibition on Federal law enforcement purchase of personal data from data brokers."
