Georgia's Senate Bill 189 became law on July 1, 2024, according to the Georgia Recorder, and bolsters 2021's Senate Bill 202, which further spelled out that citizens can challenge as many voters in their county as they wish. Senate Majority Leader Steve Gooch said, in the Recorder's account, that SB 189 "establishes the types of evidence needed to challenge a voter’s status." The Recorder reported that under the law there is "enough probable cause to challenge an elector who is dead" and in other listed cases.

## What the Brennan Center reported

On July 1, the Bibb County party chair challenged 243 voters, and the local board put 45 of them into challenged status, the Brennan Center reported. In early July, an individual submitted two lists comprising around 34,000 voter challenges that alleged only that these people may have a new address; Brennan does not name the county. The county elections supervisor began mailing those voters notices but was clear that he "would not remove voters who don’t respond."

Brennan's analysis argues that the law "makes it easier for people to attempt to kick their neighbors off the rolls through voter challenges." That is the Center's reading, set against Gooch's. Brennan also wrote that "county election boards have consistently rejected these challenges over the last few years."

## A separate figure, not about SB 189

ProPublica found that about 89,000 of 100,000 challenges since SB 202 became law were submitted by just six right-wing activists. That figure comes from data ProPublica obtained for 30 of the state’s 159 counties, and it predates SB 189.
