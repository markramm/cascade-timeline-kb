On September 24, 2003, the House agreed to the conference report on the Department of Defense Appropriations Act, 2004 (H.R. 2658). The Senate agreed to the conference report on Sept. 25, and the Act was approved on September 30, 2003 into law.

Section 8131(a) of the Act, the Act's bar on the program, reads: "none of the funds appropriated or otherwise made available in this or any other Act may be obligated for the Terrorism Information Awareness Program". The bar carried a proviso for the program "hereby authorized" for processing, analysis and collaboration tools for counterterrorism foreign intelligence, with funds expressly provided in the National Foreign Intelligence Program. Subsection (b) then limits those tools: "None of the funds provided for Processing, analysis, and collaboration tools for counterterrorism foreign intelligence shall be available for deployment or implementation except for" lawful military operations outside the United States, or lawful foreign intelligence activities conducted wholly overseas or wholly against non-United States citizens.

The joint explanatory statement says the conferees agree with the Senate position, which "eliminates funding for the Terrorism Information Awareness (TIA) program within the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency", and that they "direct that the Office be terminated immediately". The statement lets research projects continue under DARPA: Bio-Event Advanced Leading Indicator Recognition Technology, Rapid Analytic Wargaming, Wargaming the Asymmetric Environment, and Automated Speech and Text Exploitation in Multiple Languages. It also says "The conference agreement does not restrict the National Foreign Intelligence Program from using processing, analysis and collaboration tools for counterterrorism foreign intelligence purposes".

So the Act barred obligations for the program by name and left a funded intelligence-community track for the same kind of tools, limited by subsection (b) to overseas use or use against non-U.S. citizens.

The earlier text of this entry gave the wrong dates for the vote and the signing: it put the Senate vote and the signing after the dates in the Act's legislative history. The earlier text also said the language terminates the program permanently and orders the Information Awareness Office closed; the section sets no end date, and the closure order is in the joint explanatory statement, not in the section.
