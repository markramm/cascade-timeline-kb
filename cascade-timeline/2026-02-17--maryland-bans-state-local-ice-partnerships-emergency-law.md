Maryland Gov. Wes Moore signed the cross-filed emergency bills, Chapter 1 (Senate) and Chapter 2 (House), on Tuesday, February 17. The legislature's synopsis describes them as prohibiting the State, a unit of local government, a county sheriff, or any agency, officer, employee, or agent of either from entering into an immigration enforcement agreement, and requiring termination of existing ones immediately on the Act taking effect. CBS Baltimore called them emergency bills. The kind of law is a ban on agreements.

The House bill passed the House 99-40 and the Senate 32-13; the Senate bill passed the Senate 32-12 and the House 99-38. WMAR's breakdown reported that in the House only one Democrat voted no, and that the Senate vote on both bills was party-line.

CBS Baltimore reported that nine counties participated in 287(g) agreements, naming Allegany, Carroll, Cecil, Frederick, Garrett, Harford, Washington, Wicomico and St. Mary's, and that the law orders existing agreements to end by July 2026.
