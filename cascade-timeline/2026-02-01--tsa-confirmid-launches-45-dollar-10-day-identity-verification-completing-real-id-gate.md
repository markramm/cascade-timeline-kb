**TSA ConfirmID** went live **February 1, 2026**: travelers who cannot present an acceptable ID at a checkpoint may pay **$45** for an identity-verification process valid for a **10-day travel period**, completed online and **before** reaching the security line. TSA announced it December 1, 2025 and reported a successful rollout on February 5, 2026. It is the designated alternative to **REAL ID**, whose enforcement began **May 7, 2025**.

**What the fee does is convert a right into a transaction.** Before REAL ID enforcement, a person without a compliant credential could generally still fly after secondary screening. After it, the alternative has a price, an expiry, and a prior online enrollment. Domestic travel now has a paywall for anyone who lacks the document — and the people who lack it are disproportionately those for whom obtaining one is hardest: people without a birth certificate, without a stable address, without the underlying records a compliant license requires.

**The structural claim, and its limits.** The Drey Dossier's argument — recorded here as **his analysis, not as fact** — is that REAL ID and ConfirmID in combination amount to a functioning national identity system: matching people across institutions, linking document and biometric data, and gating movement, without Congress ever voting to create one. He notes the **2005 REAL ID Act's own provision disclaiming creation of a national ID**, and reads implementation as having bypassed it.

What this corpus can verify is the mechanism, not the intent. The dates, the fee, the 10-day validity, and the pre-checkpoint enrollment are TSA's own published terms. Whether the aggregate constitutes a "national ID" is a characterization, and a contested one — a credential standard plus a paid alternative is not the same thing as a mandatory universal registry, and the distinction matters to anyone who wants the claim to survive scrutiny. Per [[feedback_function_is_not_intent]], the defensible version is narrower and stronger: **whatever it was designed to be, the system now performs identity verification as a condition of domestic movement, at a price, with a record.**

That is what makes it capture-relevant rather than merely bureaucratic. This timeline records ICE **widening airport arrests of legally present migrants at TSA checkpoints** in July 2026 — the checkpoint is not only a security screen but an enforcement surface, and an identity system that gates it produces exactly the kind of matched, dated, per-person record the corpus documents ICE buying elsewhere: voter files, ITIN data, location data.
