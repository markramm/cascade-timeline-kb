United Press International reported that the Justice Department said, in a release of statistics on the major savings and loan prosecutions, that 905 of 1,188 people charged had been convicted in cases between Oct. 1, 1988, and June 30, 1992, and 71 had been acquitted. The department counted only cases in which the fraud involved $100,000 or more, the defendant was a director or owner of the thrift, or multiple borrowers were involved. The counter is the Justice Department, the count is of defendants in those "major" cases, and the period is the one given.

Of the 750 cases in which sentences had been handed down, the release shows prison terms for 582 defendants and none for 168. Of 137 chief executive officers, board chairmen or presidents charged, 102 were convicted and 10 were acquitted. Judges ordered $439 million in restitution in those 750 cases.

A later PBS Frontline article carries a rounder figure: "when more than 1,000 bankers were convicted by the Justice Department". The sources give no period or rank for it.
