Abu Dhabi's MGX, an Emirati investment firm, invested $2 billion in the crypto exchange Binance. World Liberty Financial's USD1 stablecoin was the currency of that payment. World Liberty did not receive the $2 billion.

Zach Witkoff, a World Liberty co-founder, announced the arrangement at a crypto conference in the United Arab Emirates, in a fireside chat with Eric Trump: "USD1 has been selected as the official stablecoin to close MGX's $2 billion investment in Binance." will use of the announcement says MGX "will use stablecoins issued by World Liberty Financial" to pay for the investment. The reporting leaves one point open. A Columbia Business School professor quoted by Fortune raised the possibility that MGX had not yet sent the balance to Binance. Fortune estimated that if Binance held on to the USD1, World Liberty's yield on the underlying reserves could net as much as $80 million over a year.

Fortune reported in February 2026 that the coin received "an immediate bump in market capitalization after MGX, another venture firm tied to the Abu Dhabi royal family, invested $2 billion into the crypto exchange Binance with USD1."

The link to the Trump family runs through the stablecoin and through the people on both sides, not through an investment in World Liberty. The separate purchase of a stake in World Liberty by Aryam Investment 1, a Tahnoon-backed company, is covered in [[2025-01-16--tahnoon-uae-buys-49-percent-wlfi-500m]].

## Correction (2026-11-16)

This entry previously said MGX committed $2 billion to invest in World Liberty Financial itself, carried a date in March, and cited a Financial Times URL that cannot be read. The record says otherwise. The $2 billion went into Binance, and the stablecoin was the payment currency. The entry now carries the publication date of the Rolling Stone article. The unreadable FT citation was dropped. A line about the SEC and Binance was removed.
