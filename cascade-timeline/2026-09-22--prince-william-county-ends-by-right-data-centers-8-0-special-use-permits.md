On **Tuesday, September 22, 2026**, the **Prince William County (Va.) Board of County Supervisors** voted **8-0** to adopt a zoning text amendment ending **by-right data center development** countywide. Future data center projects must obtain a **special use permit** and come before the board. The **Data Center Opportunity Zone Overlay District**, established in **May 2016**, will be drastically reduced; existing and under-construction data centers, and projects whose site plans have passed quality control, have a **90-day** window to remain in the overlay. Brentsville District Supervisor **Tom Gordy** initiated the directive in March. Chair **Deshundra Jefferson** said she "felt the need for the board to seek legal guidance ahead of the vote."

**Prince William is one of the two largest data center markets in the world, so a shift from permission to discretion here is a change in kind.** By-right zoning let projects proceed on staff review without a public hearing; a special use permit puts each one in front of elected supervisors, with conditions and a record. That is a procedural lever, not a moratorium — its effect depends on how the board uses it.

**It is also a lever Virginia counties can actually pull.** Neighboring Loudoun's attempt at a moratorium ran into state law ([[2026-07-22--loudoun-county-va-moratorium-blocked-by-state-law]]); rezoning a county's own overlay district is squarely within local land-use authority. The move sits in the broader local turn against automatic approvals, alongside Montgomery County, Md.'s 18-month moratorium ([[2026-07-28--montgomery-county-md-final-vote-18month-datacenter-moratorium]]), while federal ratepayer legislation stayed advisory ([[2026-09-16--house-passes-ratepayer-protection-act-417-3-consider-not-mandate]]).

**Open:** the special use permit review criteria and timelines; which pending projects qualify for the 90-day window; whether Loudoun follows.
