In No. 26A476, FCC v. Brown, Chief Justice Roberts ordered that the Fourth Circuit's October 7, 2026 judgment in case No. 26-2230 is stayed pending further order of the undersigned or of the Court, in an order dated the 8th day of October 2026, and set a response to the application for Saturday, October 10, 2026, by 5 p.m. (EDT). The Fourth Circuit's order had granted the mandamus petition and directed the Commission "to resolve by 12:00 noon on Friday, October 9, 2026, the Federal Candidates’ Application for FCC Review." SCOTUSblog reports that Solicitor General D. John Sauer had asked the Court to block that order; a divided panel granted the candidates' request on Wednesday. Sauer urged an immediate administrative stay and wrote that the court of appeals had "seriously erred in trying to short-circuit the Commission’s deliberations," calling the panel's order an "egregious intrusion into the nation's electoral process."

The docket for No. 26A476 records a response from the respondents on Oct 10 2026 and a reply from the Federal Communication Commission on Oct 11 2026. A separate application, No. 26A477, was filed the same day by the National Republican Congressional Committee, et al.

This is a pause on a lower-court order, not a ruling on the merits. The FCC's Media Bureau issued the underlying notice on March 30, indicating that political parties and joint fundraising committees should also receive the favorable rates, and on Sept. 4 the Supreme Court had put the Fourth Circuit's earlier decision on hold.
