On July 20, 2026, ICE issued a Request for Expressions of Interest (RFEI) seeking contractors to build standardized 26-cell detention units at 14 locations: Guantanamo Bay, the U.S. Virgin Islands (St. Thomas), El Centro CA (closed since 2014), Honolulu, Miami, Batavia NY, Broadview IL, Oakdale LA, El Paso and Huntsville TX, Florence AZ, Aguadilla PR, and additional sites. Each unit is priced at $24M–$40M, with total expansion capped at $10 billion; contract award is expected by September 2026. A companion Time investigation published August 4 documents the strategic logic behind the RFEI: ICE is shifting from renting county-jail and private-prison bed space toward directly acquiring and building dedicated, ICE-owned facilities — a move that removes local jurisdictions' ability to cancel contracts or withdraw cooperation as a check on detention capacity.

This is a structural escalation of the private-detention buildout tracked across 2024-2026: GEO Group's $529M Hudson/Big Horn Colorado contract ([[2026-07-09--geo-group-529m-hudson-colorado-big-horn-detention-contract]]), CoreCivic's $300M ICE contract award ([[2025-10-05--corecivic-wins-300-million-ice-contracts]]), and the private-prison stock surge following the 2024 election ([[2024-11-06--private-prison-stocks-surge-trump-2024-victory]]) all involved ICE renting or contracting capacity from existing private operators. The RFEI marks a shift toward ICE-owned infrastructure that is harder to contest through local political pressure — the same leverage exercised, for example, by hunger strikers and elected officials at Delaney Hall ([[2026-05-26--delaney-hall-newark-hunger-strike-geo-group-protests-pepper-ball-clashes-sen-andy-kim]]) — even as documented conditions problems continue to accumulate across the existing GEO/CoreCivic-operated facility network ([[2026-05-27--ap-investigation-ice-detention-suicides-record-fy-pace-corecivic-geo-facilities]]). The inclusion of Guantanamo Bay and the reopening of El Centro (shuttered in 2014 over conditions) both extend the pattern first established with the first private-prison contract at a Houston INS facility in 1984 ([[1984-01-22--first-private-prison-houston-ins-facility]]): permanent physical infrastructure outlasts any single administration's stated policy.
