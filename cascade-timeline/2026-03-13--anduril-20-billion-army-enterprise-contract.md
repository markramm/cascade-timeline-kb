The U.S. Army and Anduril Industries signed a single-award indefinite-delivery contract on March 13, 2026, with a ceiling of $20 billion. USAspending records nothing obligated on the contract itself. The Army's own release is dated the same day.

## What the record says

Washington Technology reports the Army is bringing together its contracts with Anduril under a "potential $20 billion ceiling value over up to 10 years". Army Recognition reports that the Army states the figure is "a maximum potential value rather than an obligated amount" and that the agreement "will not replace competition for future programs". It describes the contract as consolidating "more than 120 separate procurement pathways".

USAspending records the contract as "NOT COMPETED", a single award made under "ONLY ONE SOURCE-OTHER". It is a contract under the Federal Acquisition Regulation, not an other-transaction agreement. The notice for the award gives the reason: "The justification for this sole-source award stems from the lack of qualified responses to a Sources Sought notice."

Money moves through orders placed under the contract. When pulled for this correction, USAspending listed 9 orders under the contract with 174,331,959.14 obligated in total.

## Who is tied to it

ABC News reports that 1789 Capital "lists Donald Trump Jr. as one of six partners" and "invested $50 million into Anduril Industries as part of a larger $2.5 billion funding round last year", according to Anduril's founder, and that the investment "marked a relatively small stake in Anduril".

Senators Warren and Blumenthal wrote to Secretary Hegseth after the Defense Department's reply of March 10, 2026 that "It also suggests that DoD appears to have no effective processes in place to ensure that DoD contracts are being fairly awarded to companies based on our national security and defense requirements—rather than the financial interests of the President’s family." That sentence is the senators' reading of the reply. It is not a Defense Department statement.
