As of **September 18, 2026**, President Trump's super PAC **MAGA Inc.** and two new Trump-aligned groups, **No Going Back PAC** and **Safety and Affordability PAC**, had placed more than **$150 million** in initial ad reservations over the preceding two weeks, per NPR. An NPR analysis of **AdImpact** data found Republican candidates and groups had pledged more than **$557 million** to Democrats' **$343 million** in the nine most competitive Senate races. MAGA Inc. ended July with more than **$403 million** in cash (CNBC, Sept. 18) and ended August with about **$415.8 million** after modest spending (CNBC, Sept. 21). Trump told reporters he expected to allocate "probably $400 or $500 million" to the midterms.

**Where the money goes is the finding.** NPR reports the Senate targets include Alaska, Texas, North Carolina and Ohio, and House spending includes Texas' 15th (Trump +18 in 2024), Kentucky's 6th (+15) and Alabama's 2nd (+14). In Texas alone, pro-Paxton reservations for September exceeded **$94 million** against roughly $20 million for Democrat James Talarico — more than Democrats had spent on Texas ads all cycle. Outside money is being used to defend seats drawn or presumed safe, which means the reservations are a measure of the party's exposure as well as its resources.

**The concentration is in groups the president controls, not in the party.** A sitting president directing a nine-figure super PAC, alongside newly created affiliated committees, into specific races extends the transactional pattern canon has logged this cycle — including a promised $5,000 payment conditioned on a Republican midterm win ([[2026-09-09--trump-promises-5000-dividend-conditioned-on-republican-midterm-win]]) and nine-figure AI-industry super PAC spending to block regulation ([[2026-03-12--ai-industry-super-pacs-flood-2026-midterms]]). NPR notes the dynamic reverses for candidate campaigns, where Democrats hold more cash.

**Sourcing note.** The capture ledger also reported that only about $1 million of $160 million in Republican TV ads mentioned Trump against about $23 million of Democratic ads; that figure was not located in the sources read at promotion and is omitted.
