On Nov. 18 the Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to block a ruling keeping Temporary Protected Status in place for Venezuelans; Al Jazeera reports that some 600,000 Venezuelans in the US were protected under TPS programmes. Solicitor General D. John Sauer told the court that the order by U.S. District Judge Edward Chen "will spring into effect, inflicting severe and immediate harm," and said his office had been informed that the government could be forced to release some 1800 or so Venezuelan aliens from detention. The court of appeals then issued an order extending its administrative stay, keeping Chen's ruling on hold until seven days after it issues a decision, and on Thursday the administration withdrew its application, per SCOTUSblog.

The administration relies on Mullin v. Doe, in which the Supreme Court ruled in cases involving Haitian and Syrian TPS beneficiaries that courts generally cannot review the Secretary of Homeland Security's decision to designate or terminate a country under TPS ([[2026-06-25--scotus-mullin-v-doe-ends-haiti-syria-tps-judicial-review-barred]]). Earlier litigation on the Venezuelan designation is in [[2026-01-28--ninth-circuit-affirms-tps-venezuela-unlawful]].
