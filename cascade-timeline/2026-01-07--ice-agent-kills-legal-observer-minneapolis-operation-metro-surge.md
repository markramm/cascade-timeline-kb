ICE agent Jonathan Ross shoots and kills Renee Nicole Good, 37, during an Operation Metro Surge immigration enforcement action in Minneapolis. Good was a poet and a mother of three who graduated from Old Dominion University in 2020; family and friends told ABC News she was a stay-at-home mom. She and her wife, Becca Good, had dropped her six-year-old son at school that morning. Becca Good said they "stopped to support our neighbors."

**What she was doing, as the record describes it.** Local officials said she was acting as a legal observer, per Al Jazeera, and attorneys for her family said she and Becca Good decided to "observe with the intention of supporting and helping their neighbors." The family's complaint in *Ganger v. Ross*, filed 10/01/26, says Becca Good got out of the car and filmed ICE activity while Renee Nicole Good stayed in the car and honked her horn to warn neighbors. This entry does not use the label as her title.

Good was in the driver's seat of her Honda Pilot in the middle of a residential street when an ICE agent opened fire. ABC News's analysis of verified video, including the footage taken by the agent who shot her, shows Good turning the steering wheel to the right, away from the agent, just over one second before the first shot; ABC counts three shots in total. The agent's own video, a 47-second clip first posted by Alpha News and shared on Friday by the White House, was confirmed authentic by a DHS spokesperson to the Guardian, which reported that the clip is consistent with another video showing the agent slightly brushed by the car, moving so slowly that he easily kept his balance. The Twin Cities Fox station reported on October 16, 2026 that, according to court documents, federal authorities over the summer turned over body camera video from all three Metro Surge shootings.

Her last words to the agent, through her open window: "That's fine, dude" and, the Guardian reports, "I'm not mad at you." The Guardian describes her as speaking calmly.

Ross testified in a trial the month before that he deployed to Iraq from 2004 to 2005 with the Indiana National Guard and joined the Border Patrol in 2007; AP, using records, reported that he has served as a deportation officer with ICE since 2015. He said he is assigned to fugitive operations, and a DHS spokesperson said he had been selected for ICE's special response team.

The complaint alleges Greg Bovino was, at the time, Commander-at-Large of the U.S. Border Patrol and the field commander of Operation Metro Surge. An analysis of federal data by the Project On Government Oversight and American University's Investigative Reporting Workshop found El Centro Sector agents used force over 3.6 times for every assault they faced, against just over a 2-to-1 ratio across the Border Patrol.

DHS said in a social media statement that a "violent" rioter "weaponized her vehicle"; its spokesperson told the Guardian that the "officer was in fear of his own life." Secretary Kristi Noem called it an "act of domestic terrorism." The White House shared the agent's video; Vice President JD Vance said Good "tried to run someone over with her car and the guy defended himself when that happened," and President Trump wrote that the driver "seems to have shot her in self defense." Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey called ICE's self-defense claim "bulls---."

Her wife Becca Good said "Renee was made of sunshine." Becca Good's statement: "Renee leaves behind three extraordinary children; the youngest is just six years old and already lost his father." Loved ones remembered her as "an amazing human being."
