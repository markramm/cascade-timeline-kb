White House adviser Stephen Miller, who played a significant role in crafting Executive Order 13769 (SPLC), implemented Trump's campaign promise of a "Muslim ban" just seven days into the administration, creating immediate chaos at airports across the United States as customs agents detained travelers, separated families, and denied entry to legal permanent residents. The executive order, developed with Steve Bannon, suspended entry of all Syrian refugees indefinitely, banned entry from seven Muslim-majority countries for 90 days, and reduced refugee admissions by more than half. Airports erupted in protests as lawyers rushed to assist detainees, immigration agents received no advance guidance on implementation, and even green card holders were turned away mid-flight. Federal judges across the country issued emergency stays. The first, on Jan. 28, 2017, was Judge Ann M. Donnelly's stay of removal in Darweesh v. Trump (E.D.N.Y., 17 Civ. 480, ECF 8), which found that the petitioners "have a strong likelihood of success in establishing that the removal of the petitioner and others similarly situated violates their rights to Due Process and Equal Protection guaranteed by the United States Constitution"; it was a stay, not a final ruling on the merits. The Office of Legal Counsel did review the order: it "approved the order for 'form and legality'" (BuzzFeed News, on the Justice Department's release of the OLC memo). Miller defended the ban in media appearances, telling CBS's Face the Nation on Feb. 12, 2017: "our opponents, the media and the whole world will soon see as we begin to take further actions, that the powers of the president to protect our country are very substantial and will not be questioned." When courts blocked the order and the administration prepared a revised version, Miller said the new order would reflect "mostly minor technical differences" with the "same basic policy outcome for the country," as the Fourth Circuit en banc quoted him in IRAP v. Trump (May 25, 2017). Courts later cited Miller's own statements as evidence of the order's purpose.

Correction (2026-11-09): This entry called Miller the "primary architect" of the order and said it was developed without standard input from the Office of Legal Counsel, that Judge Ann Donnelly ruled the government's actions violated due process, and that no terrorist attacks were perpetrated by nationals of the banned countries. SPLC says Miller played a significant role in crafting the order alongside Bannon; the OLC, Donnelly-ruling and no-attacks sentences could not be confirmed in the cited sources and are removed. The "will not be questioned" quote is from Face the Nation, Feb. 12, 2017, and the "mostly minor technical differences" quote is as quoted by the Fourth Circuit in IRAP v. Trump (both now cited). The framing that the order realized Miller's white nationalist ideology was our characterization and is removed.
