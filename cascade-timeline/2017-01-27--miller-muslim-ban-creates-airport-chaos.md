White House adviser Stephen Miller, who played a significant role in crafting Executive Order 13769 (SPLC), implemented Trump's campaign promise of a "Muslim ban" just seven days into the administration, creating immediate chaos at airports across the United States as customs agents detained travelers, separated families, and denied entry to legal permanent residents. The executive order, developed with Steve Bannon, suspended entry of all Syrian refugees indefinitely, banned entry from seven Muslim-majority countries for 90 days, and reduced refugee admissions by more than half. Airports erupted in protests as lawyers rushed to assist detainees, immigration agents received no advance guidance on implementation, and even green card holders were turned away mid-flight. Federal judges across the country issued emergency stays. Miller defended the ban in media appearances, telling CBS's Face the Nation on Feb. 12, 2017: "our opponents, the media and the whole world will soon see as we begin to take further actions, that the powers of the president to protect our country are very substantial and will not be questioned." When courts blocked the order and the administration prepared a revised version, Miller said the new order would reflect "mostly minor technical differences" with the "same basic policy outcome for the country," as the Fourth Circuit en banc quoted him in IRAP v. Trump (May 25, 2017). Courts later cited Miller's own statements as evidence of the order's purpose.

Correction (2026-11-09): This entry called Miller the "primary architect" of the order and said it was developed without standard input from the Office of Legal Counsel, that Judge Ann Donnelly ruled the government's actions violated due process, and that no terrorist attacks were perpetrated by nationals of the banned countries.
