ICE announced four new detention centers adding 5,500 beds as it races toward a stated 100,000-person capacity target. June 2026 arrests topped 43,000 — the highest monthly total of the second Trump administration — with 65,765 people held in detention as of July 11, at a pace of 1,593 arrests per day in early July. The expansion represents the largest structural build-out of immigration detention infrastructure in US history, continuing the trajectory documented in the ICE detention re-engineering initiative ([[2026-06-23--ice-detention-reengineering-initiative-100k-warehouse-network]]).

The four-facility, 5,500-bed expansion referenced here is detailed in the same-week SAM.gov solicitation for Seattle, Denver, Philadelphia, and Miami sites ([[2026-07-29--ice-issues-emergency-solicitation-5500-beds-four-cities]]). Combined, the arrest-rate and capacity-build data confirm the administration is scaling both enforcement throughput and detention capacity simultaneously, rather than one lagging the other.
