## What happened

A follow-up research pass independently verified, directly against federal court dockets (not
secondary or state-pleading citations), the two NDNY docket numbers a prior pass had sourced only
to the state's own verified complaint against Sheriff Bourgault. The verification produced a
significant correction and surfaced a live discrepancy in Nassau County's compliance status.

## Correction (lead finding)

The Aug 3, 2026 NDNY ruling (ECF No. 53) that grants in part and denies in part a preliminary
injunction is captioned **United States v. Russo**, not "United States v. State of New York" as
the prior work-log entry and this corpus's 2026-08-28 timeline entry both stated (both citing the
state's verified complaint, which used the shorthand caption).

Verified directly from the federal docket (CourtListener/RECAP mirror,
`docket/73606612/united-states-v-russo/`):

- **Docket: 1:26-cv-01360, N.D.N.Y.** Filed **June 22, 2026 in W.D.N.Y.** (before Judge Lawrence
  J. Vilardo), then **transferred to N.D.N.Y. on July 13, 2026** and assigned to Judge Mae A.
  D'Agostino — which resolves the prior pass's "filed ~June 2026" uncertainty.
- Named defendant **Michael Russo** (a New York State official; role not established this pass)
  **was dismissed from the case on July 10, 2026** ("granting 8 Motion to Dismiss Defendant
  Michael Russo. The Clerk of Court shall remove Russo as a defendant") — before transfer to
  NDNY, but the case caption was never updated and still carries his name.
- **The complaint originally referenced 287(g) agreement counts** — on July 8, 2026 the court
  ordered the government to file a letter "identifying any references in the complaint to the
  number of 287(g) agreements in the Western District of New York" — so 287(g) is contextually
  present in this litigation's background, but is **not the statute the Aug 3 ruling adjudicates.**
- **The Aug 3, 2026 order (ECF 53) itself concerns New York Civil Rights Law § 101 (the "Face
  Covering Act") and § 102 (the "Identification Act")** — statutes restricting ICE agent conduct
  (mask-wearing, identification requirements) in the field. This is a **different, though
  related, New York statute from the Local Cops, Local Crimes Act** that bans local 287(g)
  participation. The order grants the United States' preliminary-injunction motion in part and
  denies it in part as to those two statutes specifically. **Whether this ruling has any direct
  bearing on the Local Cops, Local Crimes Act's 287(g) ban was not established this pass** — the
  prior framing ("denying DOJ's injunction request against the Local Cops, Local Crimes Act") is
  not supported by what this docket actually adjudicates and should not be repeated without
  further verification.

**Confidence**: high on docket identity, filing history, and subject matter (all read directly
off the federal docket's own entries). Lower on how this ruling relates to the Local Cops, Local
Crimes Act specifically — that connection, if any, was not confirmed and needs its own pass
against the actual order text (ECF 53), which was not independently pulled this session (PACER
paywall; RECAP has not purchased it).

## Second docket confirmed cleanly

**Bourgault v. Hochul, 1:26-cv-01637 (N.D.N.Y.), filed Aug. 25, 2026** — confirmed directly
against the federal docket, matching the state's complaint citation exactly. Sheriff Bourgault
and 14 other named plaintiffs (sheriffs/officials) sue Hochul, James, and the State of New York.
Assigned to Judge Mae A. D'Agostino. The docket's own automated "Notice of Potential Related
Case(s)" entry lists **"1:26-cv-1360, 1:26-cv-1281"** as related cases — this is what led to
identifying (and correcting) the Russo docket, and to locating the third case below.

## A related docket located, not yet read

**State of New York v. The U.S. Department of Justice, 1:26-cv-01281 (N.D.N.Y.), filed June 22,
2026** — confirmed as a distinct real docket via CourtListener's search index (court, date, and
exact docket number all match), listed as related to Bourgault v. Hochul by the court's own
notice. The docket detail page returned CourtListener's WAF JS-challenge (HTTP 202, zero bytes)
on every attempt this session — an access blocker, not a verified absence. Its docket entries and
subject matter are not yet established. Given the filing date (same week the Russo case was
transferred) and the state-as-plaintiff-against-DOJ posture, this is a plausible candidate for
where a Local Cops, Local Crimes Act challenge proper would live, but that is a hypothesis, not a
finding.

## Nassau County: the Aug 25/Aug 28 roster still shows both agreements active

ICE published a newer roster snapshot since the prior pass (download ID changed from `208496` to
`208609`; new file `ParticipatingAgencies08282026pm.xlsx`, server last-modified 2026-08-28
16:48:45 GMT). Parsed the same way as the prior pass (direct XML extraction, no `openpyxl`
available): 2,417 data rows, three New York agencies still listed, unchanged from the Aug 25
snapshot:

| Agency | Model | Signed |
|---|---|---|
| Nassau County Police Department | Task Force Model | 2025-03-10 |
| Nassau County Sheriff's Office | Warrant Service Officer | 2025-02-28 |
| Rensselaer County Sheriff's Office | Jail Enforcement Model | 2020-06-10 |

Negative controls (Otsego, Mohawk, Camden, Allegany Village, Niagara, St. Lawrence/Lawrence) were
re-run against the new file and are true zeros for New York — the matching logic is validated by
real hits for the same fragments in other states (e.g., "Lawrence" → Alabama, Arkansas, Missouri,
Pennsylvania, Tennessee county agencies; "Camden" → Camden County, Georgia).

**This sits in real tension with multiple Aug 26 tier-2 reports** (ABC7 New York, Politico, PIX11,
LI Press, Fox 5 NY) quoting Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman directly ("saddest day")
announcing he ended Nassau's ICE partnership. **ICE's own roster, current as of three days later
(Aug 28 PM), still lists both Nassau agreements as active.** No Nassau County government primary
document (press release, executive order, or official statement page) was located this pass —
the `web` search tier (local SearXNG) was down entirely (connection refused, not merely rate-
limited) for the whole session, and a targeted curl of Nassau County's own site did not surface
one. This gap is not resolved; report the tension rather than assuming either side is wrong. A
possible explanation not yet confirmed: ICE's roster may lag real-world terminations by more than
three days, or Blakeman's announcement may cover only one of the two Nassau instruments, or the
termination paperwork may not yet have reached ICE. None of these is verified.
