A whistleblower complaint sent to Senator Richard Blumenthal's office, made public September 1, 2026, alleges that the technical system the U.S. Postal Service assembled to vet voters was developed in a "rushed, chaotic, and fundamentally flawed process." Blumenthal forwarded the complaint to Postmaster General David Steiner with a request for information.

Two allegations carry the weight. First, **the system was built in weeks** — against the nine months to a year the whistleblower says a project of this complexity normally requires. Second, **work continued after Judge Indira Talwani's August 27 order**, which the whistleblower alleges is still ongoing notwithstanding it.

The system implements Executive Order 14399, which directs the federal government to generate lists of confirmed citizens eligible to vote and directs the Postmaster General to propose a rule under which USPS will not transmit ballots to people absent from their state's list. A system that decides which ballots move through the mail is, functionally, a determination of who votes by mail — and the complaint's claim is that the determination rests on software assembled in weeks and, in its own characterization, untested.

Both allegations are about process rather than outcome, and that is what makes them checkable. Whether the system is flawed is a technical question no one outside USPS can yet answer; whether work continued past a court order is a factual question with a documentary answer, and it is the one that would matter to Talwani. This timeline records her August 26 vacatur-and-reinstatement sequence and the September 3 third emergency application to the Supreme Court. The complaint alleges that while that litigation ran, the build did not stop.

**Sourcing note.** The complaint's existence, its characterization of the process, the weeks-versus-months comparison, the allegation that work continued past the August 27 order, and Blumenthal's referral to Steiner are confirmed across NBC News, Votebeat, PBS and Axios. **The complaint itself is not public** and has not been read here; every allegation is the whistleblower's as relayed by Blumenthal's office. The capture ledger additionally described a July 15 letter from Steiner and USPS Board Chair Amber McReynolds telling Senate Democrats the work had stopped — that detail appears in none of the tier-1 reporting reviewed and is **not carried into this entry**. USPS has not been recorded responding to the substance.
