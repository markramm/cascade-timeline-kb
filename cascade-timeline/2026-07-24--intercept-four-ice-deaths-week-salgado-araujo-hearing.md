The Intercept synthesized four ICE-related deaths in a single week as evidence the agency has evolved into an unaccountable paramilitary force, centering the case of Lorenzo Salgado Araujo — shot while driving a Houston construction crew to a job site ([[2026-07-07--ice-kills-lorenzo-salgado-araujo-houston-narrative-collapse]]). At a July 24 Congressional Hispanic Caucus field hearing in Houston, Harris County DA Sean Teare and Salgado Araujo's sons testified under oath; the three men who were in the van with Salgado Araujo remain in federal detention rather than having testified themselves, but witnesses have disputed ICE's "weaponized vehicle" account, per the DA, who called them "the most material witnesses." Salgado Araujo was not the operation's target — ICE had been tipped off about someone inside the white van who resembled the person agents were looking for.

The hearing is the first instance of elected officials taking sworn testimony that directly disputes an ICE public account of a fatal shooting, arriving in the same week that ICE's detention and deportation data blackout was independently confirmed ([[2026-07-21--ice-data-blackout-detention-deportation-stats]]) — an agency escalating in lethal field conduct while simultaneously suppressing the public data that would otherwise measure it. The "weaponized vehicle" narrative pattern recurs across at least four 2026 incidents, including the July 13 Biddeford, Maine shooting ([[2026-07-13--ice-fatal-shooting-biddeford-maine-weaponize-vehicle]]).

Corrected 2026-10-01: PBS NewsHour reports ICE was tipped off about "someone" (singular) resembling its target, not "two other people"; corrected accordingly. Click2Houston reports the three van passengers remain in federal detention and did not testify at the hearing — sworn testimony came from DA Sean Teare and Salgado Araujo's sons — so the entry no longer attributes testimony to the passengers directly.
