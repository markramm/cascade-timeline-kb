On January 22, 2026, Anthropic filed a letter with the FCC in the FCC's submarine cable licensing proceeding. The FCC's Second Report and Order cites it as the "Anthropic Ex Parte".

The Order quotes the filing: "Adversarial SLTE or fiber owners could selectively deny service, degrade performance, or power down infrastructure supporting AI workloads."

The International Connectivity Coalition disputed the technical premise, and the Order quotes its letter: "the functions Anthropic describes occur at higher network layers beyond the control of the submarine cable system itself." The Order's reply: "We agree these features can be done at higher layers in terrestrial networks." 

This is Anthropic's documented participation in a national-security rulemaking. The sources do not say whether the filing was solicited or whether Anthropic's commercial interest in compute connectivity shaped it.
