On March 11, 2026, DHS issued a notice of intent to award a sole-source contract to Amentum Services, Inc. for detention, transportation, and medical services at Camp East Montana, the largest immigration detention facility in the United States. Amentum took over operations on March 12. The no-bid contract runs through September 30, 2026 (a 180-day term); Public Citizen characterizes it as "a no-bid, sole-sourced contract of an undetermined amount" — the contracting notice did not state a ceiling value, and tier-1 reporting (AP/WTOP) does not cite one. **The federal contracting record now supplies both the amount and independent confirmation of the no-bid characterization** (verified 2026-08-28): USAspending PIID `70CDCR26C00000016`, recipient Amentum Services, Inc. (UEI QEMLRQA7PLG4), a DEFINITIVE CONTRACT with **$452,855,435.04 obligated**, period of performance 2026-03-13 to 2026-09-30. `extent_competed` = **"NOT COMPETED"**, `solicitation_procedures` = **"ONLY ONE SOURCE"**, `number_of_offers_received` = **1**, `other_than_full_and_open` = **"ONE"** (only one responsible source), `type_set_aside` = NONE, no parent IDV — i.e. this is a standalone ICE sole-source contract, **not** a WEXMAC task order. The sole-source characterization here is confirmed against the award's own record, in contrast to the WEXMAC warehouse task orders (GardaWorld, KVG), which the same audit found were competed.

## What Happened

DHS terminated its contract with Acquisition Logistics LLC (ceiling about $1.34 billion; see Correction) — a Richmond, Virginia company with 8-50 employees and no prior detention experience — following three detainee deaths in 44 days (December 2025–January 2026), a homicide ruling from the El Paso County Medical Examiner, and, per the Washington Post, violations of at least 60 federal detention standards in the facility's first 50 days of operation.

The replacement contract was awarded without competitive bidding. DHS justified the no-bid award by citing the "proprietary nature" of the camp's infrastructure and asserting that "no other vendor" could provide uninterrupted services. The justification we read (posted for the later extension of this award) rests on termination of the incumbent contract, Acquisition Logistics', and on Amentum's infrastructure and operational control at the facility; it does not cite Amentum's status as a subcontractor (see Correction). USAspending subaward records do list Amentum Services as a subawardee on the Army order ($207,318,836.04, dated July 2025).

Amentum Services is a subsidiary of Amentum Holdings Inc. (Chantilly, Virginia), a major federal contractor with 50,000+ employees and billions in primarily DOD contracts. The company does not appear to have prior experience running civilian immigration detention facilities. Amentum's various subsidiaries have accumulated 112 federal regulatory violations historically, including a 2024 fatal workplace accident in Virginia and a 2023 OSHA citation for exposing CIA headquarters workers to toxic vapors.

## Why This Event Matters

The Amentum transition is the third contractor replacement in thirteen months at Fort Bliss — none awarded through open competitive bidding:

1. **Deployed Resources** ($3.8B ICE, April 10-13, 2025) — cancelled three days after award, stated reason never disclosed
2. **Acquisition Logistics** (Army order, ceiling about $1.34B, July 18, 2025 — March 2026) — terminated after three deaths, reported violations of at least 60 standards, illegal Texas business registration
3. **Amentum Services** (DHS sole-source, undetermined amount, March 12, 2026 — September 30, 2026) — sole-source, no competition

Amentum was already on site as a subcontractor under the terminated order, and the justification we read names its control of the facility's infrastructure as the reason no other vendor could take over. The effect is continuity: an entity that took part in operations under the terminated contract became the only eligible replacement, foreclosing competition. Whether that obscures responsibility for prior abuses is our reading; the justification does not discuss it.

## Broader Context

A February 2026 inspection by ICE's Office of Detention Oversight (released April 2, 2026) found 49 deficiencies at Camp East Montana in areas including use of force, security, medical care, tuberculosis isolation, and documentation of force incidents. The inspection documented inherited conditions that predate Amentum's takeover, creating ambiguity about which contractor bears responsibility for which violation period.

The pattern — inexperienced or improper prime contractors, undisclosed subcontractors performing actual operations, then subcontractor elevated to prime when prime fails — insulates operational actors from accountability while maintaining continuity of the same personnel and conditions.

## Correction (2026-11-16)

The contract figure, the account of the justification and the violation counts in this entry did not match the record. Acquisition Logistics' work at Camp East Montana is recorded on an Army order and on an ICE order that administratively transferred it, so the ceilings are not added. The Army order carries a ceiling of $1,342,742,059.48 and total obligations of $253,178,228.81. The ICE order carries a ceiling of $1,339,875,022.97 and total obligations of $598,444,871.04. A ceiling is the most the government could pay; an obligation is what it has committed.

The justification posted on SAM.gov for the extension of this award does not rest on Amentum's status as a subcontractor. It says competition is limited "due to a termination of the incumbent contract", meaning Acquisition Logistics', and that because of the facility's infrastructure "only they possess the unique qualifications and immediate operational control necessary" to continue. AP, reading the same notice, reported that a different contractor would require replacing Amentum's "proprietary infrastructure".

The 49 comes from a three-day February inspection by ICE's Office of Detention Oversight, which counted 49 deficiencies and gave the camp an "acceptable/adequate" rating. The 60 comes from a Washington Post report on an inspection that ended Sept. 5, which found conditions that violated at least 60 federal standards. We have not read that inspection report. El Paso Matters called the February report the first inspection released by that office for the camp. The counts come from different inspections.
