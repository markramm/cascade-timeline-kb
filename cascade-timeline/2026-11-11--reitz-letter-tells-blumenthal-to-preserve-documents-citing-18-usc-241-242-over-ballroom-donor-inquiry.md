Aaron Reitz, the federal attorney for the Southern District of Texas, sent Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., a letter on Wednesday and posted it on X. Read from the image of the letter, it gives Blumenthal formal "notice of your obligation to preserve all documents, communications, and other electronically stored information relevant to your intent to deprive citizens of their core constitutional rights in violation of 18 U.S.C. §§ 241 and 242," and says he must preserve evidence related to his "efforts to suppress associational activities for philanthropic projects throughout the nation’s capital." It asks him to confirm in writing by 6:00 p.m. Eastern Time on Nov. 18, 2026 that he has received the letter and taken steps to preserve the materials. Blumenthal answered on social media that "the implied threat of an investigation is baseless."

The body of the letter does not mention the White House ballroom. Its only reference is a footnote citing a Blumenthal press release titled "Blumenthal Presses Secret White House Ballroom Donors." Democracy Docket writes that Blumenthal spearheaded an investigation into donors to Trump’s ballroom. Democracy Docket wrote the statutes differently from the letter, which reads "18 U.S.C. §§ 241 and 242."

Reitz also sent letters to Sen. Elissa Slotkin, D-Mich. ([[2026-11-12--reitz-letter-tells-slotkin-to-preserve-documents-over-go-hard-on-accountability-comments]]) and Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass. ([[2026-11-15--reitz-letter-accuses-warren-of-wielding-gavel-like-tomahawk-demands-preservation-by-nov-23]]). Democracy Docket reports that legal experts say Reitz's letters make specious legal arguments. The letter text is as transcribed from the X image, which is a screenshot, not a DOJ-hosted original.
