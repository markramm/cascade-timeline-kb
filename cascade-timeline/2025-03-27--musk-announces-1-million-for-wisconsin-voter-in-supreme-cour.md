Musk posted on X late on a Thursday, according to AP, that he planned to give $1 million each to two voters at a Sunday event; NBC News dated his announcement to Friday morning, when he said he would "personally hand over two checks for a million dollars each in appreciation for you taking the time to vote". The two accounts differ by a night; the file's date follows AP's. Musk later deleted the post and posted a clarification, AP reported, that the money would go to people who would be "spokesmen" for his online petition against "activist" judges, with attendance limited to those who had signed it. On Friday his PAC said it had awarded $1 million to a Green Bay engineer for signing the petition.

Attorney General Josh Kaul filed the lawsuit in county circuit court on Friday, per AP's story of Mar 28, 2025. Kaul asked the court to order that Musk stop promoting the Sunday giveaway and not make any future payments to Wisconsin voters, arguing that any payment to voters violated state law. The case was assigned to Susan Crawford, a candidate in the April 1 election for the seat of retiring Justice Ann Walsh Bradley; her spokesman said "Judge Crawford will recuse from this case." Ben Wikler, the Wisconsin Democratic Party chair, called it an "illegal election bribery scheme". No source read here decides whether the payments were lawful: AP quoted a University of Wisconsin law staff attorney saying "there can be arguments made on either side of that question". What the Wisconsin Supreme Court did on Sunday is on [[2025-03-30--elon-musk-hands-out-1-million-payments-after-wisconsin-supre]].

AP also wrote that Musk and groups he funds had already spent more than $20 million to elect candidate Brad Schimel. That is AP's figure for Musk and the groups he funds as of its Friday story, tallier not named; it is not one PAC's total, and it is not on the same base as the figures on [[2025-03-25--musk-and-trump-ratchet-up-involvement-in-wisconsin-supreme-c]].

The earlier text gave other dates and figures for this event, named the recipient, labelled him as backing one candidate, and gave the attorney general's filing a different description.
