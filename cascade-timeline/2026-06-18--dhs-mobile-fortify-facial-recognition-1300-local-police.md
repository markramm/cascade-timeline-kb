NPR reported in June on a DHS Privacy Threshold Analysis (PTA) for a mobile face-scan app called the ICE Task Force Module, which allows local police to scan the faces of people they stop. NPR says the document was first reported earlier in the month by 404 Media. The app is for local police working under ICE's Task Force Model, about 1,300 agencies nationwide; that figure counts participating agencies and is not a rollout target.

NPR reports the app compares a scan against more than 250 million government records and that the photos are stored in an internal DHS system for 15 years. NPR reports that the document says the app launched last September, which suggests police are already using it. The PTA itself is posted on ice.gov and states that it provides coverage to the app for three years.

This is the Task Force Module, not Mobile Fortify. NPR says the app seems to work similarly to Mobile Fortify, but it is unclear whether it uses the same technology. The PTA's own submission, launch and approval dates are in the related entry on the app's PTA.
