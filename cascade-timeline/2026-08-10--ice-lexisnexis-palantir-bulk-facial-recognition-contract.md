ICE signed a $6.7 million procurement for LexisNexis commercial records — more than 82 billion data points — with an explicit API requirement feeding directly into Palantir, mandating AI-driven identity inference and bulk facial matching across large-scale image databases. First reported August 10, 2026, the contract extends Palantir's deportation-targeting infrastructure (widely believed to be its ELITE system, though that link is inference rather than confirmed procurement language) with a commercial data-broker layer that adds social-media-scale facial-recognition capability on top of ICE's existing biometric tools.

Note on sourcing: the original reporting is credited to 404 Media but is available here via a syndicated re-post (allaboutcookies.org); the 404media.co original could not be independently confirmed live at time of writing due to bot-blocking on direct access, and this entry is flagged for a follow-up check that the syndicated text matches 404 Media's original reporting.

This is the latest layer in the Palantir-ICE buildout the timeline has tracked continuously — the $513M CBP/ICE Palantir-backbone spending ([[2026-07-14--ice-cbp-surveillance-spending-513m-palantir-backbone]]), the largest-ever Clearview AI contract ([[2026-06-20--ice-largest-clearview-ai-contract-dhs-deletes-oversight-policy]]), and ImmigrationOS's tracking of U.S. citizens through Medicaid, IRS, and DMV data ([[2026-04-01--immigrationos-palantir-tracking-us-citizens-medicaid-irs-dmv]]). Each new contract adds a distinct commercial data source to the same integration backbone, consistent with the Brennan Center's finding that the integration layer — not any single tool — is what makes the architecture a persistent-tracking system ([[2026-08-12--brennan-center-dhs-29b-domestic-surveillance-architecture]]).
