On February 23, 2026, the Department of Homeland Security answered a September 18, 2025 letter from Sen. Patty Murray about pregnant, postpartum and nursing women in ICE custody. The 19th reported the answers on March 18, 2026. This entry is dated to that report. The counts below are DHS's, each with its own window; they should not be read as one series.

**363 removals.** DHS's table of ICE removals of pregnant, postpartum and nursing women totals 363, with monthly rows from January through the following February. The table's heading prints February 16, 2025, but the rows run through a second February, and the 19th and Human Rights Watch both read the window as January 1, 2025 to February 16, 2026. The table carries DHS's note: "An alien may have multiple removals in the given time frame." So 363 counts removals, not necessarily women.

**16 miscarriages, through September 23, 2025.** The senator asked about miscarriages from January 1, 2025 to the date DHS received her letter. DHS answered for January 1, 2025 to September 23, 2025, the date it received the letter, and gave sixteen miscarriages in that window. The enclosure gives no miscarriage figure for the months after. The 19th wrote that "Sixteen miscarriages were recorded during that time period", right after giving the window ending February 16, 2026. The DHS document puts the sixteen in the earlier window, and Human Rights Watch's reading, late September 2025, matches it.

**498 booked out, and 86 and 121 in detention.** DHS also gave 498 pregnant, postpartum and nursing women recorded as booked out of ICE custody from January 1, 2025 to February 16, 2026; a book-out can be a release or a temporary trip, and it is not a deportation count. As of February 16, 2026, DHS counted 86 detainees identified as pregnant, and 121 identified as pregnant, postpartum or nursing; nine of the pregnant detainees were in their third trimester. The 86 and the 121 are different groups.

**A separate count, from the Guardian.** In a later report, drawn from records ICE released in the Guardian's records lawsuit, the Guardian counted at least 18 women who miscarried in custody from January 2025 through September 2025, and said ICE provided miscarriage data only up to early October 2025. It adds that experts consider this very likely an undercount. The Guardian's count is higher than DHS's. The records behind it are not public, and we do not know why the counts differ. The reconciliation is in the research note on the 16 and 18 counts.

**What advocates documented.** An ACLU post of October 27, 2025 summarized a letter to ICE built on interviews with detained women and described women "Shackled and chained while miscarrying, denied prenatal care, given inadequate food and water." A Physicians for Human Rights and Women's Refugee Commission report, released days later, rests on interviews in Honduras. Medical staff at the reception center there described a 25-year-old pregnant woman who bled for several days inside a detention facility, and another woman, diagnosed with a missed miscarriage, who was deported without treatment. No account here is named.

## Correction

This entry carried an earlier date and a title built on the sixteen. The sixteen is DHS's count to Sen. Murray through September 23, 2025, and the entry is re-dated to March 18, 2026, the day the 19th reported the response. The 363 is a count of removals, not of women. A given name was removed from an account the ACLU had already pseudonymized. What else changed is listed in the corrections field above.
